0 ATTEMPT to tell in a few paragraphs, or even within 
the covers of this book, of the beauty and completeness 
of Palos Verdes Estates is to attempt the impossible. 
Yet one of the most comprehensive statements ever 
made about Palos Verdes is the single remark of a resident who 
says: “I live here because I get so much more out of life.” 

The house we live in, to many of us, is not necessarily our 
home. In this age, and especially here in California where sun¬ 
shine spreads its golden veneer, we seem to be willing to accept a 
different kind of living—a life in which the home itself plays a 
relatively minor part. Our pursuit of pleasure or recreation often 
leads us to places far distant from where we live—to mountains, 
to distant beaches, to golf clubs or to far-off woods and bridle trails. 

Not so in Palos Verdes. 

Here, to a greater extent than in any other residential com¬ 
munity in the West, if not in all America, it is possible to have a 
home, a playground and a preferred vacation place all within a 
five-minutes’ journey. 

Palos Verdes, as you will see it today, is already the most com¬ 
plete suburban community ever attempted in the history of city 
planning. Richly endowed by Nature in beauty of location, climate 
and scenery, it is peculiarly adaptable to residential development. 

So intelligently has it been laid out, so carefully designed, that 
not one single attractive feature has been lost or even spoiled in 
this huge Ten Million Dollar con¬ 
struction project which, as the Na¬ 
tional Conference on City Planning 
says, is “an object lesson to the whole 
country.”

A1 URE has given Palos Verdes Estates an exceptionally 
temperate climate and very fertile soil conditions, as 
is shown in the reports of Dr. F. A. Carpenter, meteor¬ 
ologist, late of the U. S. Weather Bureau. (Circular on 
request.) Observation, over a period of six years, reveals the fact 
that the average variation between winter and summer tempera¬ 
tures has never exceeded 10° (52°-62°). Sunshine in Palos Verdes 
averages 358 days a year. 

Frost below the 500-foot levels is unknown. 

Contrary to general belief, wind velocity is far less than in 
either Redondo or San Pedro lying to the north and south. The 
sheltering slopes of the Palos Verdes hills act as a break for all 
air movements originating either on land or at sea, and lessen the 
wind pressure to an average of no more than 5.6 miles per hour— 
identically the same average as Los Angeles, 20 miles inland! 
These hill formations also split the ocean fog banks, giving Palos 
Verdes an almost complete immunity from cloudy days. Daily 
climatological records all show that general weather conditions 
are consistently better than at any point in the Los Angeles city 
limits. (See Climate Chart beloiv.) 

For many years prior to the development of the Estates, Palos 
Verdes soil was known to possess abnormal moisture-retaining 
qualities, so much so that horticulturists spoke of this district as 
“the most fertile, unirrigated region in Los Angeles County.” 

Supplementing this gift of Nature, the Palos Verdes Water 
Company—a half million dollar corporation under California State 
Law—now owns and operates a complete plant, wells and five 
reservoirs and has developed a water system capable of yielding- 
over Z]/ 2 million gallons daily. With vast water-bearing lands in 
reserve, Palos Verdes Estates can ultimately furnish sufficient ir¬ 
rigation for a suburb of 100,000 people.

HERE are many famous golf clubs in California, but a 
more distinctive course than Palos Verdes does not exist. 

Nationally-known experts have praised these links 
but as yet no one has adequately described the thrill 
which a game here provides for professional and novice alike. One 
of the very few seaside courses in California, Palos Verdes tests 
both the skill of the good player and encourages the best efforts of 
the beginner. Every hole is a feature hole and the average player 
finds that this fascinating layout, with its steep canyon and hillside 
shots, is so intriguing that it amply compensates for penalties 
incurred. Similarly there is rich reward for accurate judgment. 

The course comprises some 213 acres of beautiful rolling 
country at an elevation of from 400 to 500 feet above sea level. All 
the endless variety of natural hazards have been preserved and 
developed by the best golf engineering talent in the West. A dis¬ 
tinct flavor of the Scottish Highlands, the homeland of golf, is 
noted in the large quantities of heather along the hillsides. From 
12 of its 18 scenic holes there is a clear view of the Pacific Ocean 
and you might search the length and breadth of California and not 
find a golf links so ideally situated, or so fascinating at any season 
of the year. It has been truthfully said that to play the course at 
Palos Verdes is to improve one’s game, whatever one’s handicap 
may be. 

The golf course, together with the magnificent clubhouse, are 
all deeded to and owned outright by the residents of the Estates. 
Every landowner automatically becomes a member of the golf 
club. There are no initiation fees and no annual dues—no expenses 
other than greens fees which, for residents, are nominal monthly 
charges and, of course, such expenses as members incur on their 
house account. 

Tennis courts located in the 7-acre plot surrounding the school 
and, adjoining, a fine, full-sized baseball diamond, are further trib¬ 
ute to the completeness of the provision made for outdoor sport.

Think what it would mean to live practically next

the development of a polo team and the usual weekly and bi-weekly 
rides for members and friends.

HE public parks and playing spaces which comprise one- 
fourth of the entire Estates are, essentially, the very 
foundations upon which Palos Verdes itself is built. 

When, some few years ago, the creators of the Palos 
Verdes of today—a group of architects, engineers and financiers 
—stood on the brown hillsides of “Rancho” Palos Verdes, they 
visualized not a new subdivision, but a seaside residential park. As 
they planned for beauty, so they planned for permanence, and con¬ 
sequently ample provision was made for sufficient park areas to 
serve the maximum growth of the community. The system of 
main boulevards and major traffic roads, with the secondary 
avenues and streets, provides for all future traffic conditions. Sim¬ 
ilarly the parkways, decorative landscaping features and play¬ 
ground areas have all been reserved as of legal record and are 
located at scenic points all over the Estates in proper proportion to, 
and in correct artistic relation with the 6000 homesites in the 
present community limits. 

The original city plan upon which Palos Verdes Estates came 
into being and upon which it is now being carried to completion 
sets aside one-fourth of the entire acreage for purely decorative 
or recreational purposes. This reservation is in addition to the 
500-odd acres which have been allotted to the construction of 
major traffic boulevards, streets and avenues which provide access¬ 
ibility to every homesite. These park areas alone amount to some 
800 acres and are legally dedicated so that they can neither be sold 
nor built upon. Because of these unchangeable restrictions, future 
generations of Palos Verdes residents will enjoy their golf links, 
their parks, their playgrounds and their “open spaces” for all time.

OTHING has been built in Palos Verdes—whether pri¬ 
vate residence, Project development or public utility— 
that was not planned in advance. 

The planning' of the entire Estates at one time made 
it possible to allocate the various school and playground areas to 
serve the needs of the ultimate capacity population. Ten acres in 
every square mile is set aside for an elementary school and play¬ 
ground; every two miles, 25 acres for a junior high school and ball 
park; every three miles, 40 acres for a senior high school and a 
community playground. Edward T. Hartman, State Consultant 
on Housing and Planning for Massachusetts, says: “This method, 
which should really prevail everywhere, offers a solution to one 
of the most interesting problems facing our planning boards and 
school committees.” 

The business-zoning plan employed in Palos Verdes is unique 
in the history of community building, because here it has been 
possible to group shopping districts into convenient units, approx¬ 
imately two miles apart. Shops, stores, garages, etc., in keeping 
with prevailing type of architecture, are located in plazas just off 
main boulevards, permitting comfortable parking space and thus 
preventing traffic congestion. 

Motor-busses operate on regular schedule between principal 
points in the Estates and Redondo. 

The “Palos Verdes Estates” Post Office has been in operation 
since August, 1925. 

There is a Public Library and the Palos Verdes branch of the 
County Free Library in Malaga Cove Plaza. 

A fire crew with truck, hose and chemical tank, gives ample 
fire protection. 

Palos Verdes Water Company has already developed an inex¬ 
haustible supply of water. Electricity and telephones are generally 
distributed through underground conduits. Gas is furnished to 
every homesite. Storm drains, culverts, curbs, sidewalks, are in¬ 
stalled and are designed to meet all future requirements.

A.LOS VERDES ESTATES is entirely 
different from any previous attempt at 
community building. It combines all 
that is most desirable in the exclusive 
garden suburb with the attractions of a magnifi¬ 
cent scenic location and an exceptionally tem¬ 
perate climate. 



Just what Palos Verdes is—its aim, its charm, 
its completeness, its tremendous appeal to those 
who know how to enjoy life, can never be por¬ 
trayed by pictures and print. Nothing short of 
actual vision can convey the atmosphere or the 
individuality which characterizes canyon and 
hillside and red-tiled roof, and makes music out 
of roads and building materials. 

The future of Palos Verdes Estates is assured, 
in many ways. Its permanency is proven. Its 
desirability as a homeplace is evident at the first 
visit. With a little over one-third of its develop¬ 
ment program now actually completed, it is 
today attracting international attention. The 
three and a quarter million dollars’ worth of 
construction work that has already been put into 
roadways, school, golf club, Inn, and all the cus¬ 
tomary basic improvements, has won for Palos 
Verdes a distinction among the world’s best 
known suburban communities. 


Such famous districts as Roland Park, Balti¬ 
more; Forest Hills, Long Island; St. Francis 
Wood, San Francisco, have no better environ¬ 
ment or more open spaces, better recreation 
areas, or more careful control of architecture. 

Unlike the English garden cities, Palos 
Verdes is not planned as a self-contained indus¬ 
trial and residential unit. It was originally con¬ 
ceived and is now building up as a strictly 


residential suburb, serving a metropolitan area 
containing more than two million people. 

Adequate provision is made for future growth. 
Palos Verdes will never have to submit to the 
usual unsightly encroachment of business in¬ 
terests, because 90% of the Estates is restricted 
to single family residences. Necessary business 
activities—shops, garages, service stations, etc.— 
are artistically located in a few compact plazas. 

The administration of the affairs of the 
Project is in the hands of a group of architects, 
engineers and financiers, with the Bank of 
America as Trustee for the property. Similarly, 
the municipal affairs of the residents are admin¬ 
istered by the Palos Verdes Homes Association, 
a committee of property owners. A legally con¬ 
stituted and endowed Art Jury supervises all 
building operations. 

Clearly defined standards of architecture and 
rigid building restrictions have been established 
and allotted to certain districts, thus assuring 
continuity of house design and maintenance of 
property values. Your neighbor’s house in Palos 
Verdes will never offend your artistic taste—it 
will neither detract from the value nor spoil the 
appearance of your own. Nothing unsuitable to 
the decorative design of the Estates is permitted. 
No billboards or general advertising signs are 
allowed. Trees above a certain height and 
growth cannot be cut down where their absence 
would destroy a natural beauty spot. 

In such a manner does the Art Jury, repre¬ 
senting group-control, and not personal fancy, 
prevent inartistic development or the infringe 
ment of individual tastes. It is very evident that 
the idealistic methods which characterize the

As is shown in the foregoing pictures, Palos 
Verdes has made an unusually generous provi¬ 
sion for open spaces and for recreation areas. 
Some 400 acres of this legally reserved land is set 
aside for schools, for community playgrounds, 
for business plazas, for entrance parks, for street 
plantings and for various boulevard landscaping 
features which may be noted here and there over 
the property. Another 213 acres was deeded to 
the residents for the golf course, which is now 
one of the sportiest links in California. Four 
miles of ocean shore park was also dedicated, and 
200 acres of additional parks, gulches, hillside 
campgrounds and bridle trails—in all 800 odd 
acres, or one acre in every four, is a public park 
and can never be used for any other purpose. 

Title to these recreation areas is vested in the 
Palos Verdes Homes Association, a non-stock, 
non-profit, community organization formed to 
direct municipal affairs. This association levies 
and collects an annual maintenance tax from all 
property owners for upkeep of parkings, street 
plantings, etc., and said tax is limited by legal 
restriction to never exceed the city tax rate of 
municipal Los Angeles.

next 18 months will see a greater expansion than

double that of 1925 and the number of applica¬

during 1927 is already (January 1, 1927) greater 
by 150% than the total home building program 
of last year—with twelve months still to go! The

There are many reasons why you should con¬ 
sider this unique place for your future home, 
chief of which is the fact that it permits of more 
desirable living conditions than are possible else¬ 
where. It has a location as fine as anything that 
California offers, with the beauty of the French 
Riviera, the climate of Sunny Italy, the intimacy 
of rural England, with all the facilities and the 
completeness of the modern American city. 

As a homeplace, Palos Verdes offers you the 
ideal suburb, developed along sound, practical 
lines. As an investment it is even more practical 
because here idealism pays large dividends in 
both prosperity and happiness. The expansion 
and growth of Los Angeles is all in the direction 
of the ocean, toward Palos Verdes, and to own a 
piece of the Estates is to lay the foundation of 
future independence—financial and personal.

Verdes project we assume that you already are

something of its favorable recognition by inter¬

whose services are soug'ht on two continents.
