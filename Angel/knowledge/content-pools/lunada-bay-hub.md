# Lunada Bay — Your Guide to Life on the Bluffs

*By Angelique Lyle | Updated April 2026*

---

I moved to Palos Verdes not knowing a soul, and Lunada Bay is where I finally understood why everyone calls this place "the Hill." It's not the elevation — it's the feeling. You park your car, walk the plaza, grab a coffee, wave at three people you know, and realize you haven't left your neighborhood all day. That's Lunada Bay. It's the kind of place where your kids ride bikes to the creamery after school and you don't check your watch.

---

## The Neighborhood

Lunada Bay sits on the western bluffs of Palos Verdes Estates, and the ocean is everywhere — not just the views, but the salt air, the morning fog that burns off by ten, the sound of waves when your windows are open. The streets are hilly and winding, most homes were built in the '50s through '70s, and the lots are generous by LA standards. You'll see ranch-style homes next to mid-century moderns next to full tear-down rebuilds with walls of glass. It's not cookie-cutter — every block has its own personality.

The median home price landed around $2.38 million in early 2026, though the average sale price for 2025 was closer to $3.18 million — the range stretches from $1.6M starter homes to $9.5M oceanfront estates. Sixty-one homes sold last year, up from 45 the year before, so activity is picking up even as prices hold steady. If you're looking at Lunada Bay, know this: inventory is tight, the best homes move fast, and the ones with ocean views rarely need open houses.

About 4,500 people live here. It's families, mostly — the kind of community where the elementary school parking lot is the social hub and everyone ends up at the farmers market on Sunday morning.

## Schools — The Real Reason Families Move Here

Let me be direct: the schools are what close the deal for most families considering the Hill. Lunada Bay Elementary feeds into Palos Verdes Intermediate, then into Palos Verdes High School. That's the pipeline, and it's a strong one.

**Lunada Bay Elementary** (K-5) has an 8/10 on GreatSchools — 82% of students are proficient in reading, 77% in math. It's small (around 336 kids), the parents are deeply involved, and there's a Gifted & Talented program. But the numbers don't tell you what matters most: the campus feels like a neighborhood school, not an institution. Drop-off is chaotic in the best way — everyone knows everyone.

**Palos Verdes High School** is the one that shows up in rankings — 10/10 on GreatSchools, A+ on Niche, 97% graduation rate, average SAT of 1330. Both my kids went through PVPUSD and I can tell you what the stats miss: it's the extracurriculars, the parent network, and the fact that the district genuinely invests in keeping things excellent. PVPUSD serves about 10,300 students across 19 schools, and it's ranked in the top 5% of California districts.

Enrollment opens early — the 2026-27 cycle started in February via their Virtual Line system. If you're planning a move, start early.

## Lunada Bay Plaza — The Village Center

Every great neighborhood has a gathering spot, and this one has the plaza. Lunada Bay Plaza sits at Yarmouth and Palos Verdes Drive West, and it's the kind of place where you can handle Saturday morning in one stop: coffee, hardware store run, quick haircut, maybe sushi for lunch.

**What's there now:**

The newest addition is **Java Wave**, which opened last August in the old St. Honore space. Beth Parwar — a former art teacher from the neighborhood — serves Intelligentsia coffee, small plates, and pastries. It's become the spot for cyclists, surfers, and the before-school-drop-off crowd. Opens at 7am weekdays.

**Black Bamboo Sushi** is tiny — seven tables and a bar — but the rolls are enormous and impeccably fresh. The Alaska Roll is a local favorite. Dog-friendly patio, reservations recommended on weekends.

**Salsa Verdes** does regional Mexican in the courtyard. The crab enchiladas are the move. Firepits outside, the owner knows your name by your second visit.

**Lunada Kitchen** handles the brunch crowd with scratch-cooked food, local coffee, and a patio where you can see Catalina on a clear day. Open until 4pm daily.

And then there's **Lunada Creamery** — bespoke soft serve with rotating creative flavors, opened by Julie and Jason Anthony because they wanted their kids to be able to walk to a treat shop. That's the kind of place this is.

The plaza also has **Lunada Bay Hardware** (50+ years and they still cut lumber), **Lunada Bay Barbers** (since 1956), a pharmacy, a deli, boutiques, and a fountain courtyard. It's not trying to be anything it isn't — it's a real village center, not a lifestyle brand.

*Note: Lunada BayHouse restaurant closed in early 2026. No word yet on what's replacing it.*

## Where to Eat Nearby

Beyond the plaza, you're ten minutes from some of the best dining on the peninsula:

**Yellow Vase** in Malaga Cove Plaza is where I take every new client. French-California cafe, florist, and bakery all in one — the brioche French toast is worth the wait on weekends. Two PVE/RPV locations.

**Nelson's at Terranea** is Angelique's-approved sunset dining. Cliff-top, casual, fire pits, Baja fish tacos, and the kind of view that makes people decide to move here. This is where I take clients when I want them to fall in love with the area.

**mar'sel** is the fine dining option at Terranea — Chef Fabio Ugoletti's seasonal tasting menus are serious, and the Sunday brunch is worth the drive from anywhere in the South Bay.

**Terra Mia Woodfire Kitchen** in PVE does wood-fired Italian that competes with anything on the westside. **Avenue Italy** in Golden Cove is lively, family-friendly, and the pasta is excellent.

And don't sleep on **Neptune's Frozen Treats** at Malaga Cove Plaza — opened late 2025, family ice cream tradition going back to 1914, rotating flavors. Perfect after a walk on the bluffs.

## What's Happening Right Now

### This Month (April 2026)

**41st Annual Whale of a Day** — April 11, Point Vicente. Free. Gray whale migration celebration with food trucks, kids' activities, beer/wine garden, and tours inside the Point Vicente Lighthouse (celebrating its 100th anniversary this year). The shuttle from Ken Dyda Civic Center saves you the parking headache.

**PVPLC Lunada Canyon Volunteer Day** — April 4, 9am. Habitat restoration right here in Lunada Bay. The Land Conservancy runs these monthly and they're a great way to meet neighbors while doing something meaningful.

**Trolls: A Field Study** — through October at South Coast Botanic Garden. Large-scale troll sculptures scattered throughout the garden. My kids loved it; my neighbors won't stop talking about it. Worth the membership.

**Earth Day at White Point** — April 18. PVPLC hosts planting, weeding, kids activities, and raffle prizes. Bring the family.

**PVPLC Nature Walks** — Every week through April. Birdlife walks, canyon discovery walks, native plant tours. Free or minimal cost. Best way to explore the preserves if you're new to the area.

### Every Week

**PV Farmers Market** — Every Sunday, 8am-1pm at Peninsula High School (Hawthorne & Silver Spur). Year-round. This is where the neighborhood shows up — produce, artisan goods, prepared food. Get there before 9 or the good stone fruit is gone.

### Coming Up

**SOAR: Tropical Butterflies** opens in May at the Botanic Garden. Sixth year of the immersive butterfly experience — this one sells out.

**Lunada Bay Summer Concert Series** starts later this summer at Lunada Bay Park, sponsored by LBHOA. Bring a blanket and a bottle.

## Community Pulse

A few things worth knowing if you're considering Lunada Bay:

**The coastal access question is settled.** PVE resolved the Lunada Bay access litigation — public access to the bay is ensured. The old surf localism issues are gone. The city enforces a zero-tolerance policy and the Bay Boys are history.

**PVE runs quiet and well.** Goat grazing for fire safety (yes, really — it works), a new police transparency map, and the General Plan Safety Element is in public review. City council meets regularly and the vibe is engaged, not contentious.

**The Peninsula Center Library** is getting a renovation — groundbreaking was March 6, completion expected by June 2026. The new main entrance will feel like a community living room.

**The Lunada Bay HOA** is active and community-focused. They sponsor the summer concerts, maintain neighborhood communications, and organize events like the annual Diwali celebration at Lunada Bay Park.
