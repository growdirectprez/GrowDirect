This Week on the Hill

What's happening on the Palos Verdes Peninsula and across the South Bay this week.

This week is a big one on the Peninsula. The Point Vicente Lighthouse turns 100 on Saturday — a full-century celebration with tours, history, and ocean views that remind you why you live here. PVPLC has guided nature walks running all week, and the 41st Annual Whale of a Day hits the Interpretive Center. If you're still exploring whether the Hill is right for your family, this is the week to come see it in its element. — Angelique

This Week, 100 Years Ago

In 1926, Malaga Cove Plaza was taking shape as the heart of Palos Verdes Estates — the only one of five planned business centers that was fully realized. Frank Vanderlip had hired the Olmsted Brothers to lay out the community, and architect Myron Hunt's Art Jury was enforcing the Mediterranean style that still defines every street: stucco walls, tile roofs, archways. The Neptune fountain — a 17th-century marble sculpture imported from Italy — would be dedicated four years later.

Photo: Malaga Cove Plaza, 1926. The Etter residence (foreground) won the Art Jury's Most Notable Structure award in 1925. The Olmsted residence on Rosita Place is visible in the background. — Palos Verdes Library District Community Archives
