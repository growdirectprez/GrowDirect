Neighborhoods

Every neighborhood on the Hill has its own personality. Here's what it's actually like to live in each one.

Palos Verdes Estates

Malaga Cove

The heart of PVE. Spanish arches, the Neptune Fountain, Saturday mornings at Yellow Vase. If you want to walk to the library, grab coffee, and run into three neighbors — this is it. The architecture has rules for a reason, and the result is a neighborhood that actually looks like a neighborhood.

Lunada Bay

The blufftop neighborhood. Ocean views from streets you didn't know existed. Quiet mornings, big skies, and a community that shows up for each other. Lunada Bay Plaza has your basics — market, restaurants, the creamery. But you're really here for the space and the light.

$2.38MMedian

Palos Verdes Estates

Valmonte

The eucalyptus streets. Flat lots, mature trees, families who've been here for generations alongside new ones who found it on accident. Cape Cod meets California ranch. The most walkable neighborhood on the Hill for kids — flat streets, no through traffic, everyone knows everyone.

$1.28MMedian

Rancho Palos Verdes

Miraleste

Harbor views, city lights, and a different energy than the west side. Miraleste is where you get the panorama — Catalina to downtown LA. Larger lots, more variety in home styles. Less village, more estate. If you want space and views without the RPV premium, look here first.

$2.24MMedian
RPVCity
ViewsLifestyle
Rancho Palos Verdes

Silver Spur

The practical choice that surprises people. Good schools, easy access to the freeway, a real mix of home styles from ranch to Mediterranean. Silver Spur is where families who want to live on the Hill without the PVE price tag land — and then never leave.

Rolling Hills Estates

Horses and hiking trails five minutes from the freeway. Larger lots, genuine green space, corrals in backyards. RHE is the South Bay's best-kept secret for people who want land without going to Santa Clarita. The equestrian trails connect everything.

Dining

Nelson's at Terranea for sunset drinks. Red Onion for the margarita that's been the same for 61 years. MB Post when you want to impress someone. Fishing with Dynamite for oysters. The South Bay punches way above its weight.

Coffee

Yellow Vase for the full Saturday morning experience — flowers, pastries, ocean view. Reverent in Rolling Hills if you care about the actual coffee. miss.coffee for the creative lattes. Each one has its crowd.

Schools

PVPUSD is why half the families moved here. PV High and Peninsula High are both A-rated. The feeder patterns matter — your elementary school determines your friend group through graduation. I can walk you through it.

Trails & Parks

Portuguese Bend Reserve has 10+ trails and you'll never see all of them. Abalone Cove tidepools are the best classroom your kids will ever have. Point Vicente for whale watching. Founders Park for the view you show visitors.

Shopping

Malaga Cove Plaza is the most photographed spot on the Hill — and the Ranch Market is actually good. Riviera Village in Redondo for the afternoon browse. Manhattan Beach downtown when you want to spend money and not feel bad about it.

Events

PV Street Fair in June is the one weekend everyone comes out. Whale of a Day at Point Vicente in April. PVPLC guided walks every second Saturday — free, and the naturalists know everything. Picnics in the Park on summer Wednesdays.
