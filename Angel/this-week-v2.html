From the Archives

The Peninsula, then and now

The Heart of the Hill, 1930

The Neptune Fountain at Malaga Cove Plaza — a 17th-century Italian marble sculpture imported by collector Arnoldo Adolfo di Segni and dedicated on February 16, 1930. Behind it, the early homes of Palos Verdes Estates climb the hillside, built to the Mediterranean standards enforced by Myron Hunt's Art Jury. The Olmsted Brothers designed the streets. Frank Vanderlip funded the vision.

Ninety-six years later, the fountain still runs. The Art Jury still reviews every exterior change. The Mediterranean style Vanderlip insisted on still defines every street, roof, and archway on the Hill.

Hand-tinted postcard, ca. 1930. Neptune Fountain, Malaga Cove Plaza, Palos Verdes Estates. — Palos Verdes Library District Community Archives

The Lighthouse and the Bean Fields, 1937

Point Vicente Lighthouse, eleven years after it first lit in 1926. Looking northwest — the lighthouse tower rises beyond the keeper's quarters, radio antenna masts, and utility poles. In the foreground: bean fields. The Peninsula was still agricultural land. No Terranea. No subdivisions. Just beans, a lighthouse, and the Pacific.

This Saturday, the lighthouse turns 100. The bean fields are long gone, but the light still works.

Photograph, 1937. Point Vicente Lighthouse looking northwest. Security Pacific National Bank Collection / Los Angeles Public Library. — Palos Verdes Library District Community Archives
