Your Neighborhood, Your Schools

On the Hill, where you live determines where your kids go to school. Every school in PVPUSD is excellent — the real question is which neighborhood fits your family's daily life.

Every parent who calls me starts with the same question: "Which school is the best?" And I always say the same thing — they're all good. Genuinely. PVPUSD is the #1 district in LA County. The real question is: what does your family's daily life look like? Do your kids walk to school or do you drive? Are you close to their friends? Is the after-school pickup 5 minutes or 25? That's what actually matters — and it all depends on where you live. — Angelique

The One Thing to Know

On the Palos Verdes Peninsula, your home address determines your elementary school, which determines your middle school, which determines your high school. There are two high schools — Peninsula High (PVPHS, "Pen") and Palos Verdes High (PVHS, "PV High") — and both are outstanding. If you have a preference, pick your high school first, then work backwards through the feeder pattern to find your neighborhood.

Two Paths, Both Excellent
The Peninsula has two high school feeder systems. Here's how they work.

The "Pen" Path

PVPHS — Peninsula High

PVE and Rolling Hills Estates families. Slightly smaller feel, strong academics and performing arts reputation.

E

Lunada Bay, Montemalaga, Cornerstone, Silver Spur, Dapplegray

Five elementary schools feed into this path. Lunada Bay and Montemalaga are in PVE; the others are in Rolling Hills Estates.

M

Malaga Cove Middle or Ridgecrest Intermediate

PVE elementaries feed to Malaga Cove; RHE elementaries feed to Ridgecrest. Both feed to Pen.

H

Palos Verdes Peninsula High School

Rolling Hills Estates campus. Known for robotics, debate, performing arts, and a tight-knit community feel. ~2,200 students.

The "PV High" Path

PVHS — Palos Verdes High

RPV families primarily. Larger campus, strong athletics and AP programs, more geographic diversity.

E

Mira Catalina, Vista Grande, Rancho Vista, Point Vicente, Soleado

Five RPV elementary schools feed into this path. Spread across the city from Miraleste to the southern coast.

M

PV Intermediate (PVI) or Miraleste Intermediate

Southern RPV elementaries feed to PVI; eastern RPV feeds to Miraleste. Both feed to PV High.

H

Palos Verdes High School

PVE campus (yes, the RPV-feeder high school is in PVE). Strong AP program, athletics, and a larger student body. ~2,400 students.

Neighborhood by Neighborhood
What your family's school path actually looks like — based on where you live.

Lunada Bay

Palos Verdes Estates

This is the neighborhood I send every young family to look at first. Your kids walk to Lunada Bay Elementary — literally walk, on sidewalks, past their friends' houses. By middle school they know every kid on the block. The school-to-neighborhood bond here is tighter than anywhere else on the Hill. It's the kind of place where the crossing guard knows your name.

Lunada Bay Elem → Malaga Cove Middle → PVPHS (Pen)

Daily Life

Morning drop-off: 5-min walk for most families
After school: Kids walk home or to the cove
Neighborhood feel: Everyone knows each other through school
Parent culture: Very active PTA, block parties, holiday traditions
The catch: Premium pricing. You're paying for walkability + the Bay

Malaga Cove & Valmonte

Palos Verdes Estates

Montemalaga Elementary is small and it stays small — your kid's class might be 20-25 students, and they'll know all of them for years. Valmonte families are a short drive up, and Malaga Cove Middle is right in the neighborhood. This part of PVE has the Olmsted-planned feel — winding streets, big trees, the historic plaza. It's quieter than Lunada Bay but just as connected.

Montemalaga Elem → Malaga Cove Middle → PVPHS (Pen)

Daily Life

Morning drop-off: Short drive or walk depending on the street
After school: Malaga Cove Plaza for ice cream run
Neighborhood feel: Old PVE — established families, big lots
Parent culture: Close-knit; small school = you know every parent
The catch: Fewer kids per block than Lunada Bay; playdates require more driving

Rolling Hills Estates

Rolling Hills Estates — Peninsula Center Area

RHE is where a lot of families land when they want great schools at a more accessible price point than PVE. Cornerstone, Silver Spur, and Dapplegray are all solid — and they all feed to Ridgecrest, which feeds to Pen. The Peninsula Center area means you're close to everything: the farmers market, the shopping, the library. It's the practical choice that doesn't feel like a compromise.

Cornerstone / Silver Spur / Dapplegray → Ridgecrest Intermediate → PVPHS (Pen)

Daily Life

Morning drop-off: 5-10 min drive for most; some can walk
After school: Ernie Howlett Park, Peninsula Center shopping
Neighborhood feel: Family-first, active, community sports leagues
Parent culture: Strong booster clubs, youth sports focus
The catch: More suburban feel; less of the ocean-bluff romance of PVE/RPV

Portuguese Bend & Point Vicente

Rancho Palos Verdes — Western RPV

The views out here are unreal — Catalina on a clear day, the lighthouse, the open space. Point Vicente Elementary has an ocean view from the campus. Your kids grow up with more sky and more space than anywhere else on the Hill. The trade-off is distance — you're driving a bit more for everything. But families who choose this side of RPV chose it for a reason, and they never look back.

Point Vicente Elem → Miraleste Intermediate → PVHS (PV High)

Daily Life

Morning drop-off: 5-15 min drive depending on where in the area
After school: Trails, PVPLC preserves, wide open space
Neighborhood feel: Rural California 30 min from LAX. Equestrian trails.
Parent culture: Outdoor families, conservation-minded
The catch: More isolated; fewer kids walking distance for spontaneous play

Miraleste & Central RPV

Rancho Palos Verdes — Eastern RPV

Miraleste is RPV's most neighborhood-y neighborhood. Vista Grande, Rancho Vista, and Mira Catalina serve this area — good schools, steady families, and the most affordable entry point on the Peninsula. You're closer to San Pedro and the harbor, which means more dining options and a different energy than upper PV. Still PVPUSD. Still the Hill.

Vista Grande / Rancho Vista / Mira Catalina → Miraleste or PVI → PVHS (PV High)

Daily Life

Morning drop-off: Short drive; some walking neighborhoods
After school: Parks, nearby San Pedro harbor area
Neighborhood feel: Diverse, established, good value
Parent culture: Grounded, less status-conscious than upper PV
The catch: Less "prestige" cachet; but the schools are the same district

Pen vs. PV High — The Honest Comparison

Both are in PVPUSD. Both are excellent. The choice is feel, not quality.

PVPHS — "Pen"

Rolling Hills Estates campus. ~2,200 students. Known for robotics, debate, performing arts, and a strong academic culture. Draws from PVE and RHE — tends to be a slightly more homogeneous student body. The campus has a "small town high school" energy. Friday night football at Pen is a community event.

PVHS — "PV High"

Palos Verdes Estates campus (despite being the RPV feeder). ~2,400 students. Strong AP program, athletics — especially water polo, tennis, and track. More geographic diversity in the student body. Bigger-school energy. The campus sits above the coast and the views from the athletic fields are genuinely stunning.

What About Private Schools?

Some families look at Chadwick School (K-12 in RPV, ~$45K/year) — it's excellent and progressive. Rolling Hills Prep and Peninsula Heritage are smaller options. But here's the thing: most families who move to the Hill for the schools end up in PVPUSD. The public schools are genuinely that good, and being in the district school means your kids' friends are their neighbors. That's the real value — not just the education, but the community that comes with it.
