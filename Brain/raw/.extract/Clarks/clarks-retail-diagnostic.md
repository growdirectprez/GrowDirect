Background, Scope & Approach
# Background to this Report
Summary of Key Themes from Initial Workshop from Clarks employees
Retail Strategy
Clarks business has grown primarily from a wholesale business, and thus does not always operate as a typical retailer
The Top 100 stores return 48% of total retail sales (but only 41% of the profit); Many successful retailers have a much higher proportion of profit form their top stores
Market conditions including competition from the supermarkets have caused erosion of market share in Men’s and Women’s categories
There is a feeling that cost pressure rather than driving sales is the greater imperative for the Clarks retail business
Range Profile:
The Top 20 lines account for approximately 25% (Men's) 21% (Women's) and 18% (Children's)  of sales volume
Significant risk and complexity exists around managing end-sizes
There is a large ‘tail’ of slow and non-moving SKUs in the business
Supply Chain
Supply chain is geared towards internal operating efficiency. This causes sub-optimal retail operations
There is currently no robust, business-wide figure for measuring at-store availability
It is estimated that stockholding is around 14 weeks in store
Replenishments will typically be made on a Day 1 for Day 3 cycle
Store deliveries are made 5 days per week (but larger stores operate 7 days per week)
Retail Operations
Store processes have not changed significantly for many years
Clarks pay store staff minimum wage.  Increases are dictated by legislation and therefore difficult to plan for.
Staff turnover is running at approximately 40%. (Many high street retailers operate with greater than 50%) Turnover of management >30%( Many high street retailers are in excess of 35%)
The rate of personnel churn can lead to poor retailing disciplines, with no obvious best-practice adherence
Store labour costs as a % of sales is 14.1% (which is up to 2% above many speciality retailers)

# The Roadmap of Opportunities Breaks Down Into Three Phases
The road forward, in our view, is straightforward and revolves around getting the basics right, enhanced consistency in execution and developing or obtaining the required skills and competencies.
Phase 1 ‘‘What are our quick wins?’
Robust measure for availability
Short-term replenishment ‘quick wins’
KPIs for DC stock carry over and order well
72 hour lead-time for customer orders
Investigate causes of gross stock variance
Quantify lifecycle distress and plan to clear
Conduct store labour benchmarking
Formal process to understand staff attrition
Benchmark manager T’s and C’s
Formal career planning for store personnel
Remove double counting from stocktake
Product labelling
Phase 2
‘What are the tactical initiatives?’
Segmented replenishment model
Variable delivery frequency and lead-times
Customer order availability measure
Direct store deliveries
Stockfile visibility and control
Review and revise store delivery schedules
Pre-advice for allocated & despatched stock
Process revision for customer orders
Cost-to-Serve modelling
Understand optimal store labour mix
Labour management tools
Consistent store labour processes
Store-friendly deliveries
Single national call-centre
Phase 3
What are the strategic initiatives?’
Visibility of stock by location
Advanced replenishment system
In-day sales polling
Home delivery for customer orders (as part of multi-channel strategy)
Range rationalisation
Strategic review of upstream supply chain
Labour management programme
Improve retail-focus of central functions
Retail consultation in multi-channel

# Action Plan
Action Plan – Where to Start
Pre-requisites
Define the key measures for availability – Ensure business-wide buy-in to the calculation method
Develop Programme Management principles to ensure initiatives are effectively managed
Create governance framework to determine who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted & Informed
Ensure the required resource is committed, and the best people are allocated to the programme
Develop and execute communications plan to ensure availability is on everyone’s agenda
Develop a cross-functional steering committee with executive sponsorship
Phase 1
‘Quick Wins’
Undertake rapid assessment of implementation costs, benefits, risks etc.
Agree which Phase 1 initiatives can be achieved within 6 months without major business change
Assign initiative ownership (cross-functionally where relevant)
Agree deliverables, milestones and critical success factors
Undertake rapid assessment to understand which initiatives can deliver a trial within 6 months
Develop Terms of Reference for initiatives to be taken forward, and gain sign-off from functional owners
Define and mobilise small cross-functional teams to focus on delivering a low-cost, measurable trial
Phase 2
‘Tactical Initiatives’
Review Tactical Initiatives considered too large to deliver trial within 6 months
Agree strategic initiatives to investigate further and develop Terms of Reference.  This should focus chiefly around development of a business case, with clear understanding of go / no-go decision factors
Identify milestones for business case sign-off and individual initiative roadmap
