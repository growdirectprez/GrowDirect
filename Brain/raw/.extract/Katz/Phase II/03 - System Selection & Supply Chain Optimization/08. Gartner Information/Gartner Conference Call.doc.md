Gartner Conference Call – Maria Jimenez
October 30, 2003

Has been researching the retail software sector for the last 3 months (3-4 hours a day) talking to customers and vendors

Corporate Viability 
SAP’s overall corporate viability is positive plus
SAP financials are mixed, not selling as much as they could
SAP going after co-development deals, leveraging customers for development
For marketing, JDA is best of breed and don’t have as many retailers as perceived (80% revenue = retail, 80% customers = non retail)
SAP is recognized everywhere but their reputation is tarnished in North America retail (marketing perception is bad)
SAP is spending a lot to rebuild their image
SAP is an organization with a global presence but do not get the mindshare in the US.  US companies perceive that all decisions in Germany
SAP is recruiting many more Americans
Need to close some big retailers in the United States
Retail is very strategic for SAP board
SAP’s goal is kill Retek

Market Offerings
Product/Service capabilities are dependent on volume and how important merchandising function is
SAP had difficulties in the past due to volume issues
Both Retek and JDA have mature solutions
SAP functionality is not a major problem
Initially 37 customers implemented SAP retail, 35 of them had trouble
Software covered 80% of requirements in merchandise management, the problems stemmed from inability to scale and interface problems
Don’t deploy merchandise management prior to finance, it can be a problem
Many French retailers had problems integrating legacy finance applications with SAP
One of the major changes is that R/3 enterprise has been separated from the retail application
The SAP application today doesn’t compare to what it was before (much better)
SAP doesn’t want to become a merchandising management vendor and fight head to head against point solutions
SAP will focus on delivering an integrated solution
Replenishment – SAP is ahead of JDA in CPFR, JDA is ahead of SAP in forecasting (using your own rules) even though E3 has been stagnant for some time, E3 is quick to deploy
Include deadlines in SAP contracts to prevent troubles
SAP has 2 different demand planning solutions (microforecasting from SAS), at store level or DC level
No other product can forecast at store level, differentiator for SAP
In-store systems – POS from SAP for low volumes only
Analytic / Optimization – SAP is not building BI tool solely for retail, JDA has better retail specific functionality but SAP is very committed.  SAP challenging best of breed vendors with their integrated BI
BI integrated with financials is a competitive advantage
JDA is acquiring companies without looking at how technologies overlap
Technology / Methodology – JDA does implementations themselves, clients get a better deal, seem like custom projects but limited in what business they can take, not exposed to different methodologies
SAP methodology is more strict
Goodyear article – SAP problems
Pricing Structure – SAP is very good at mining revenue from clients, good at selling big deals
Be careful with contracts with SAP, speak to Alexa Bona who is specialized in licensing structure

Customer Service / Support
Service level from SAP is directly proportional to distance from Walldorf
If you are the only retailer in your subsector implementing SAP, you will be very well supported since SAP needs to have very happy retail customers
If you are the number 2 retailer in your subsector implementing SAP you may have some problems
JDA customers are very satisfied if they have 1 product
JDA customers who add multiple products from JDA are slightly less satisfied (2 best of breed products does not result in excellent process, problems between two areas)
Two different cultures between SAP and JDA
SAP has stable and integrated functionality with rigidity but gives you clear business process.  SAP more expensive in the beginning
JDA has a better understanding of retail and can be rapidly deployed
Not aware of any company taking one of these products for all processes
More implementations in softlines, Grocery – Drug Retail expertise in Europe is different from North America
Grocery – Drug is not a strong subsector for JDA or SAP
Currently SAP is training/retraining people on a global basis for SAP Retail 4.7
Still to be seen if SAP will get support from partners
SAP is not agile in US for support
2 weaknesses, pricing and support (if not number 1 retail)
SAP cannot afford any unhappy customers
Dennis Moore is the xApps leader at SAP, business unit that develops composite application when something is missing
SAP pricing is complex, find out 1 year later that something was not included

Take Aways
Conference call with Alexa Bona, Grant, Katz Legal, IBM contract specialist to understand SAP pricing contracts and pitfalls
Implement finance first, implication on timelines
Katz does not have resources available for co-development work
Is IBM being trained in SAP Retail 4.7?
