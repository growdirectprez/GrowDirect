1.3 Strategic Objective

Katz’s overall business strategy is:

Our corporate stores will be the pharmacy of choice for the time-constrained female shopper seeking everyday health solutions, basics and seasonal merchandise for herself, her home and her family.  We will be conveniently located, community stores that will provide current and innovative merchandise in an efficient, friendly and appealing shopping environment.
1.4 Why Retail Supply Chain Management Systems?

Process improvements enabled by new merchandising and supply chain systems are required to enable Katz to achieve its business strategy and respond to anticipated consumer demand.

Katz needs to deliver superior customer service and added value through the effective flow of physical goods, visibility of associated information and management of the entire business.  Systems and processes must enable efficient merchandise and inventory planning within the extended supply chain.  Performance measurement, transactional and decision support capabilities must assist in revenue enhancement and cost containment efforts.

Katz’s supply chain initiative has mandated the selection of a retail supply chain management system for Katz.  The selection phase follows a strategic supply chain assessment completed in July 2003.  The recommendations from the study specified a comprehensive road map that requires implementation of a corporate retail supply chain management system.
1.5 Project Scope

The scope of this system selection project is focused predominantly on the business requirements of Katz’s head office and the corporate stores but also considers those of the independent store network.  The intent is to find one cost-effective applications architecture (i.e. one solution or one set of solutions) that will meet the requirements of the complete Katz retail organization.

Vendors are asked to submit proposals on the best implementation strategy for their respective solutions given the organizational model currently deployed in Canada.  Again, while this initiative is focused on Katz’s Canadian business, Katz’s expectations are that the selected system will be eventually rolled out to Katz’s US operations.

1.6 Key Business Differentiators

Key functional requirements were identified through cross-functional workshops.  Katz’s key business differentiators include:

Multi-banner organization
Katz operates multiple banners, multiple store sizes (200 to 15,000 sq feet) with regional and location-specific variances in the merchandise mix.  Katz must be able to cluster stores based on demand or by user defined criteria.  Katz needs the ability to plan merchandise at a store department or category based on unique demand.  The objective is to optimize store sales through store specific assortment and planograms.

Entrepreneurial spirit
Katz operates as an entrepreneurial organization which results in a need to support rapid change.  This requires a system that can be rapidly reconfigured with a minimal time and effort.

Usage of distribution channel
The acquisition of the majority of Katz product is outsourced to multiple distributors.  Katz has a requirement to increase control and visibility over its supply chain processes requiring systems with the ability to collaborate with external systems (i.e. forecasting, product planning, replenishment orders, invoices, visibility of external inventory, allocation, etc.).

Proprietary POS systems in stores
Katz operates over 300 corporate stores which have proprietary point of sale (Auto-Star) and dispensary systems (Nexxsys).  Katz requires an interface to synchronize data (item, price, sales, inventory, replenishment orders, etc.) between both systems.

Variety of product identifier tags
Product identification varies by commodity type and includes item #’s, UPC #’s, PLU #’s, DIN and Pack size.  The system must be able to support these identification types.  This is due to the fact that Katz sells drug, grocery and sundry items. 

Multiple units of measure
Planning and execution modules will need to operate using multiple units of measures.  Katz sells and inventories items by units, weight and volume.

3.3.2 Product Integration

Please describe the integration capability of your solution with external applications.  What features of your solution make it a good fit in a multi-application environment?

The primary hub for integration with external solutions comes from the JDA merchandising and transaction engine PMM.  PMM is designed to feed information to other JDA systems as required through the use of flat file and XML based files depending on the business process requirements and timeliness of the information required.
  
The module within PMM that is designed to manage the two-way flow of external information is called System Data Interchange (SDI).  The way this module works is it will capture audits of data maintenance activity through Oracle triggers.  Based on configuration PMM will determine which external systems need which pieces of information, and at what level of detail.  SDI will then process the required information, into a comprehensive set of standard SDI Export tables ready to be picked up and/or delivered to each specific external system.  PMM also has a large number of standard SDI Import tables that are can be used for initial data loading or data conversion, as well as for ongoing interfaces to bring data from external systems into PMM. Please note that before any data is inserted into PMM’s base tables, it is fully validated to guarantee the integrity of the PMM database. Invalid data is rejected and on-line screens are provided for viewing and correcting rejects.

When deploying point solutions, like Intactix or E3, these solutions have their own internal capabilities of accepting and sending information back to non-JDA systems and therefore do not require PMM to be live for interfacing data.

4.1.22 Auditing

Describe what system auditing features and reports are available for your application and provide examples where available. Does the auditing capability cover both application data as well as application configuration and security?

PMM utilizes Oracle triggers to automatically capture audits of database changes, whether they were performed from within the PMM application of via some other means (e.g. direct database manipulation).  There are two types of audits captured:
One is used to drive the interfaces to external systems via SDI. These audits are discarded after being processed by SDI. 
The second set of audits, which are user configurable, are for longer-term retention. Client’s generally write their own reports against these retained audits.  

4.1.23 Upgrades

Describe the process of upgrading from one minor version to the next.  Describe the process of upgrading from one major version to the next.  When upgrading, can a company skip major or minor releases, or is the only upgrade path from the previously generally available version?  Comment on database server(s), application server(s) and client desktops.  

An upgrade CD is provided, in both cases, that automates the upgrade process including any required data conversions. Any components that need retrofitting would need to be re-applied after the upgrade. The upgrade usually comprises of two phases 1) database changes (tables, procedures etc) and 2) client changes (Uniface source repository, Uniface client objects). Normally, the Uniface objects are housed on a network file server so that deployment to individual desktops is not required.   Occasionally a major release may require a new version of Uniface, which may need to be reinstalled on individual desktops if a thick-client deployment was used.  Upgrades need to be deployed incrementally by version, as each upgrade CD only includes changes from the previous release.
