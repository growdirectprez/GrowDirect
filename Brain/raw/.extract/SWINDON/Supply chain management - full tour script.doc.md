Key messages
World class retailers recognise that they have many different supply chains based on product characteristics and manage these in different ways. 
In managing a supply chain, there are a few key performance measures that are reviewed on a regular basis. However, where there are significant exceptions, it may be necessary to drill down to lower levels of detailed information to pinpoint the problem. An integrated system provides this capability much more easily than a set of disparate systems
To get a step change increase in supply chain performance, you need to work collaboratively with suppliers and trading partners in the entire value chain.
Supplier management forms a key part of supply chain management.







Introduction

Hello, I am Chuck Flowplan, the T&K Supply Chain Management controller, and want to welcome you again to our Swindon Distribution Centre. I now want to talk to you about the way T&K manages its supply chain, in fact its different supply chains, and the way we work with our suppliers.

In the last 3 to 4 years, T&K experienced an exponential growth in business. However, with this came a sharp increase in logistics costs and some tensions within the business about how logistics were not doing their part in increasing profitability. 

We realised that we had to be much smarter in managing our supply to handle our increased business profitably. We had reached a point where we simply could not cope with the increased business volumes going through the business. With the help of PwC, a new supply chain strategy was developed. A board level director was appointed for Supply Chain and every category team went through an extensive re-skilling process in managing the supply chain in a new way.

Today, supply chain strategy forms a core part of the overall business strategy, proposition development and category management. As part of the annual strategic review, two key questions are asked:
What is the role of supply chain in the total strategy of T&K?
What competitive advantage does the supply chain offer to T&K?

There is a belief that successful retailers have two key advantages:
Their channel propositions and a good way of delivering these propositions;
Fundamental competitive advantage in managing their supply chain and leveraging their suppliers.

Managing Different Supply Chains

Uncertain business future

The only certainty in retail is that there is always an uncertainty about the business future which must be acknowledged and understood. Therefore, our T&K supply chain must have the necessary flexibility to be able to handle this uncertainty. In the past, we had a very traditional approach to configuring our supply chain. The commercial function would be asked what they expected to buy in the following year. The entire supply chain was then configured to meet that requirement, however flawed or volatile that might have been.

Supply Chain Management

How has this changed? Today, there has been a major transformation in the way that T&K manages its supply chain. The most significant part of this has been in getting people to think about supply chain management in a new way, developing appropriate skills to manage in this new way and providing the necessary information support and tools to manage in this new way.
 
T&K category teams now use an analytical approach to optimising the supply chain and understand that there must be trade-offs associated with this (in terms of balancing supply chain capacity, service levels and inventory within supply chain). In the future, as our selling channel proposition becomes more complex the nature of our supply chains will also become more complex.

These more complex T&K supply chains must be managed more subtly, in particular:

Optimising how the supply chain network is used; 
Deciding which products are sent down which routes;
Determining how the flow of products in the supply chain is handled; 
Recognising parameters in the supply chain; 
Managing individual products to optimise performance against constraints.

Reporting

Having managed to get our people to manage different supply chains differently, we then turned our attention to reporting. It is true to say that we had absolutely hundreds of pages of supply chain reports, most of which were rarely looked at. However there was resistance to stopping these. Furthermore, because we did not have integrated systems, people often requested reports containing overlapping information but where the data originated from different systems. The amount of time and effort that went into reconciling these different versions of the truth was horrendous. We had a whole industry of spreadsheets used for analysis containing information sourced from different systems.

Thankfully all of this changed as a result of our new business strategy and the decision to implement an ERP system. After having developed our new business strategy, we engaged PwC to help us define a new Company Balanced Scorecard as the primary vehicle for our performance management. [Optional : show one scorecard - Powerpoint presentation containing balanced scorecard and the approach used in developing it].

From a supply chain viewpoint, there were two key performance measures – on-shelf availability (expressed as a percentage) and number of days of stock (defined as the amount of stock that would satisfy x days of sales). These two metrics now appear on our corporate balanced scorecard. These are broken down to lower levels of detail where each buyer / supply manager can see the numbers for every product that they are responsible for. Our exception reports can tell us who is not meeting corporate targets and allow us to take remedial action, be it training, supplier management or other appropriate interventions.

For the balanced scorecard, we used SAP’s strategic enterprise management module (SEM). Each function and every individual within that function can see their performance and exceptions are highlighted. We have limited the number of standard reports produced by the system and instead, gone for an approach where if an exception is highlighted on the balanced scorecard, then a number of standard or adhoc reports can be run to get further information. C.  Collaboration with suppliers

We recognised that we could only go so far in optimising our supply chain and that further value would come in applying the ECR philosophy of working collaboratively with our suppliers to minimise inventory and maximise availability across the value chain. Like most retailers, our relationships with our suppliers have historically been adversarial and there has been a lot of resistance in turning this round. 

Today, we have different types of relationships with different suppliers, and have an ongoing programme to develop these for key suppliers. We work jointly with our suppliers in a number of areas, for example :
Joint category management including assortment planning, product introductions and promotions management.
Joint supply management including vendor managed inventory, real time access to daily stocks and sales figures. 

Lets take a look at two specific examples :

Every supplier can via a web browser and either an extranet or the internet, log into our SAP system and review sales and stock figures for their product daily. [Demonstrate on SAP].
We are able to log into those suppliers that have web-enabled their systems and review stock levels for products that we stock, our orders and expected shipments. [Demonstrate on SAP if possible].

This has given us a number of benefits :

The onus is on both us and our suppliers to jointly reduce inventory and maximise availability.
For many products, we have been able to lower the stock held. 
Our suppliers and manufacturers are able to more accurately project raw materials and finished goods inventory needs. 
We are now able to avoid problems such as over-shipments, excessive mark-downs and out-of-stock positions and improve our inventory turns and profitability.
We are also now able to have vendor managed inventory for products where this makes sense. Here the supplier, having visibility to our daily sales and stock levels, decides on our replenishment needs within an agreed framework. Replenishment is not done on SAP, no purchase orders are sent – we simply receive stock and pay the supplier based on what we sell. Every year, we review the arrangement.

D. Supplier management

Supplier management is another key part of managing our supply chain. It is not restricted solely to annual discussions and contracts. We used to be very weak in this area and it meant that we were not really sure which suppliers were good and which were bad. We had no way to get this information to help us in negotiations and we did not know whether problems in the supply chain were our fault or the fault of the suppliers.

We can now monitor the performance of suppliers in a number of different ways, the most common being: 
correct delivery quantities, 
on time deliveries
quality of product
agreed pricing. 
We can also create our own criteria if we want to.

We can now manage our suppliers on an ongoing basis so that we can go back to suppliers with this information, help them to improve their performance and also as an aid to determining new suppliers.

We will have a look to see how SAP has helped us:

For our crisps, we analyse the performance of supplier AAAA and decide that AAAA is performing poorly in 2 main areas: orders are consistently arriving late and the full order quantity is not being delivered.

Therefore we investigate other existing suppliers who could supply our crisps. Use source lists of other similar products. By reviewing their performances in these 2 areas, we see that one has an excellent record while the other is extremely poor and we have been reducing our orders on this supplier. We decide that one supplier could also be used. We add supplier to our source list for the crisps.

The system also gives us source lists which are an easy way to maintain a list of possible suppliers for a particular article. 
Summary

We have now demonstrated some of the key issues of managing the T&K supply chain, both internally and collaboratively with suppliers. Through the use of improved reporting, better collaboration with suppliers and the recent implementation of SAP, we have resolved these problems and now:
Our buyers are able to negotiate improved terms with suppliers which allow flexibility of deliveries and quantities so that we can receive a constant flow of deliveries as required
Improved reporting have helped the supply chain managers to manage their stock levels and improve the way that they bring product in
Suppliers now have access to sales and stock levels and are able to manage the supplier managed products better
Through the improved use of supplier evaluation we are able to agree targets with our suppliers, effectively monitor them and use this information in negotiations or in finding more suitable suppliers.
