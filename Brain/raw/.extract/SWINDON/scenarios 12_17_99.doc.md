Kiosk Scenario




Kevin uses the web to pre-shop at T&K. He has identified a sweater that he likes but wants to try it on before purchasing it. After checking prices and confirming in-store availability Kevin makes a trip to a nearby T&K.
Roger heightens the level of customer service T&K offer by placing a Kiosk in the store. Roger identifies the area of the store with the highest pedestrian traffic as the ideal location for the kiosk.



Upon arriving at the store, Kevin realizes that he has no idea where the sweater section is located. He doesn’t want to bother with asking someone or trying to find a clerk. Kevin goes to T&K’s Kiosk Korner to access its touch-screen intranet PC.
Roger intends the Kiosk to act as an extension of T&K’s customer service focus. It is to serve multiple customer service functions such as: (i) to provide store layout / product location information; (ii) to provide up to the minute product inventory data; (iii) to allow customers to browse the full line of T&K products; (iv) to allow customers to purchase items from the Kiosk; (v) to allow customer to begin the product return process or to allow them to purchase gift certificates from the Kiosk



Through this computer Kevin is able to access floor layouts and product locations. He also decides to double check inventory level for the sweater before heading off to find it. Much to his dismay, however, someone beat Kevin to the last sweater and there are no more sweaters in stock.
The Kiosk is similar to the website in regard to its ability to interact with the customer. Upon transmitting a message that indicates the sweater is out of stock, the Kiosk displays an allows Kevin to purchase the sweater via the Kiosk while waiving the shipping costs. The Kiosk in this fashion aids in sales retention and avoids a lost sale due to out-of-stock store merchandise.



Before logging off the Kiosk, Kevin notices that T&K is offering him to waive the shipping fees if he uses the store intranet to purchase the sweater. Kevin decides to check inventory levels and upon finding the sweater is in supply, orders it on the PC.









Product Manager Scenario




Roger is the business manager for the T&K website. He is responsible for monitoring web traffic and analyzing customer reaction to the site as well as customer buying patterns.




-After running a custom report which tracks first-time visitor behavior, Roger notices that approximately 30% of first-time visitors do not offer personal profile information. He also learns from a content utilization report that the same percentage of first-time visitors browses and ‘picks up’ 3-5 different items but does not buy anything.
-To remedy first-time visitors not offering profile information, Roger creates an incentive offering a $5 gift certificate for first-time visitors who provide profile information.
-To remedy first-time visitors not buying anything during the first visit, Roger offers a 15% discount on initial purchases by first-time visitors.
Roger plans to perform further analysis in one week to determine the effectiveness of his new initiative.




-Although Roger has programmed the system to offer products to cross sell with primary purchase items such as printers, Roger notices that customers are only purchasing a printer cable and toner. Roger realizes that the special paper that is being offered may be too expensive for the printer. Roger assumes that customers are buying printers, cables, and toner from T&K but going to the competitors for paper purchases.
-To remedy the situation, Roger offers a different line of paper as well as paper from numerous manufacturers. Roger feels that if the customer had a wider range of paper to choose from that the customer would be more inclined to purchase paper from T&K.
---Roger is monitoring competitors’ paper prices to determine if they are being undersold. 




-Roger’s visitor analysis is becoming more comprehensive and targeted to the general home office customer. He is able to continually segment the current visitor and other home office customers by using past and current observation tracking. Roger utilizes tools like the incentive utilization report to learn that the visitor and similar customers are not taking advantage of the coupon offerings as Roger hoped they would.  

--To remedy this situation, Roger divides the current visitor and her peers into groups: the group that only wants to buy Californian wine and the group that is seeking other varieties of wine. Roger then, while incorporating data he has been able to gather so far, continues his efforts to target each subset of home office customer.




-Roger has identified one subclass of wine buyers in which his current visitor falls: the customer that buys not only wine, but other complimentary products to go with wine. Roger selects and markets to this subclass wine accessories, cheese and fruit, and related products. 




-Roger again uses the content utilization reports to learn that his visitor and her peers frequently ‘pick up’ items other than wine and related items. Because the visitor has not historically purchased such items, Roger decides to contact the visitor, as well as others who have provided limited profile information, via targeted email to attempt to do some high-level market research. After emailing this subclass with a coupon for cheese products, he surveys them for their current preferences and comments. Roger learns that his visitor and others in her subclass are often seeking additional offerings to compliment wine and cheese for such events as parties, dinners, and in his visitor’s case, for catering events.




Roger creates a marketing plan, incorporating all the information gathered to date on his current visitor and her subclass. Roger gathers items that would be good upsell candidates for those who frequently buy wine, cheese, and other associated products. Roger decides to begin offering cookbooks, decorating information, linens, and gift giving ideas to compliment the wide range of activities that are associated with wine and cheese events. After monitoring analyzing a few purchases but the subclass, Roger should be able to more accurately target the group’s decorative needs and brand preferences.
