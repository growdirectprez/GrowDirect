Introduction

This document details the story-line for the new CRM tour at the Retail Solution Centre.

The tour will follow a similar format to the existing RSC tours. It will begin in the boardroom with an introduction to T&K. The RSC team will then take on their roles as T&K customers and the tour will follow the couple as they interact with each of the relevant channels and technologies in the street, home and store.

The clients will then go to the HO and be shown how all the data generated is processed and analysed.

In each pod one or more of the key messages underpinning the CRM story-line will be highlighted.

The key messages are:

Segmentation of customers
Permission based marketing
Managing customers by Value Measure
Seamless integration of channels
Personalised proposition

T&K’s Strategy
Customer
Characteristics

The story line is based around a married couple with two children: Mr Simon & Mrs Julia Jones aged 45 and 42, with two children, William aged 13 and Victoria aged 9.

The characteristics defining each member of our family are detailed below:

Simon – is an Account Director for a large corporation. He is a traditional husband who leaves the shopping and household management  to Julia. He likes branded goods particularly top quality shoes, shirts and ties. Coupons or promotional offers rarely tempt him. He enjoys sports, golf, tennis and skiing in particular and is knowledgeable about wines.  He is interested in “gadgets” and technology and particularly likes the T&K offers by e-mail and the “key date” reminder service He has given them a list of about 10 key dates he needs to remember; family birthdays, wedding anniversary, secretary’s birthday etc. He subscribes to the T&K’s free on-line monthly updates; he receives these by e-mail.
Julia – is quality and value for money conscious, time starved and fashion aware. She is a Solicitor for a small local practice. She enjoys sports tennis and skiing, has an Open University degree in Italian and a passion for everything Italian.  She likes all the T&K offers whether by e-mail, paper mail or in-store offers.
William – is obsessed with sports and fashion, always wanting the newest equipment and cool clothing labels. He is technologically clued up and enjoys the Internet and computer games. He has his own mobile phone.
Victoria – is interested in sport particularly skiing and tennis. She follows latest teen bands – Steps, S club 7, Westlife etc. and is under peer pressure to wear cool clothes (not T&K’s!)


Segmentation and Value

Segmentation


Simon and Julia are individuals, each in a different one of T&K’s four customer segments. These four segments were originally determined using the SAS Enterprise Miner data mining tool-set. Customers have been identified as being in one of four segments based on their answers to a number of questions. When customers join the loyalty scheme (whether via the web or in store) they answer eight questions. The first five questions are factual while the next three are qualitative and determine which category the customers are in.

Age Range
Marital Status
Income Range per household
No Adults in household
No Children in household
Price preference (1-5)
Brand preference (1-5)
Function preference (value for money vs. luxury item; 1-5)

For the preference questions the customers select a value of 1 through to 5 to indicate their purchasing preference relating to product prices, brand preferences and product function, where 1 means that the customer prefers coupons while 5 means the customer prefers everyday fair pricing.

The four customer segments identified by T&K and the related behaviours are:


Bargain Hunters:
Prefer sales and coupons
Buy Branded goods when on sale, generics otherwise
Rarely buy luxury items
Some families in the middle and all the families in the low markets
Impulse Buyers:
Buy on an Every Day Fair Price basis, rarely use coupons
Prefer brands over generics
Tend to splurge on luxury items in each category
Young couples and singles in middle and up markets
Time Starved Professionals:
Will use coupons as available but not very often
Prefer brands, never buy generics
Purchase basic goods, rarely splurge
Families and households in middle and up markets
Budget Conscious
Use coupons and buy sales items with average frequency
Mostly purchase generics
Purchase basic goods, rarely splurge
Younger parents in middle and down markets and older households in all markets 
Julia is a category 3 shopper and Simon a category 2. William and Victoria do not have loyalty cards and are therefore not classified.
It is possible for a customer to express different preferences for the same attribute across categories. e.g. prefers brands for clothing but not for food. For the purposes of the demonstration, however, both William and Julia will have the same preferences for each category
Because Julia and Simon are in different categories they will be offered different promotions over the different channels

Value

Both Julia and Simon will be allocated a card (Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum) based on their value to T&K. This is determined by their total spend in the 12 months prior to the 1st June.

The value categories are:

Platinum card holder 	Retain and extend 	>£3,500 pa – (2.5k food, .5k clothes, .5k home)
Gold card holder 		Extend		>£1000 ≤ £3,500 pa
Silver Card Holder		Not Loyal		>£300 ≤ £1000 Bronze card holder		Visitors		≤£300 pa

Julia and Simon and platinum and silver card holders, respectively.

The type of card affects the level of rebate given by T&K’s loyalty card scheme and the promotions offered. 


Other information relating to the customer

Potential value – also known as “Headroom” is the expected total purchases for a given period based on demographic info and lifestyle data, minus the current actual expenditure. For example, Simon according to demographic and lifestyle data should spend around £200 pw on food on average. Therefore if he is currently spending £120 then the headroom is £80pw.
Responsiveness to offers -  promotions
Profitability last year – this needs some thought as T&K’s will have to record all costs to deliver the goods, services, promotions etc. to the Jones family. So what is the algorithm for profitability in this case? It is likely that the real profits = the total net sales – product costs – product distribution costs – proportionals (store ops cost + promotion costs + loyalty card +CRM costs + infrastructure costs).

Answers to these questions are held in the BV data model and could be used by BV and Valex to determine systematic rule based promotional pushes.

Loyalty Card

T&K has decided to implement a Loyalty system. There are 2 drivers behind this decision:
It is a method of gathering data about customers
To provide incentives for customers to spend more money
Points are earned on the following scale basis. Assuming the majority of customers spend under £1000 pa. 
Expenditure under £1,000 = 0.5% or 1 point for every £2, 
Once the spend reaches £1,000 there is a bonus of 1% on the £1,000 i.e. £10 voucher, 
For every £1 spent between £1,000 and £2,000 1%
For every £1 over £2,000 1.5%
Redemption will also be on the basis of  points ….
Once earned the points can be converted to on-line vouchers which can be redeemed over the web, through the kiosk for another companies vouchers (like a health spa) and/or through the POS where the customer can get T&K's vouchers or money off the bill.
As mentioned above Simon and Julia have separate loyalty cards.  
The Loyalty points will be maintained within the BroadVision database. Functionality will be limited to the basic earning and redemption of loyalty points. The process will be managed within BroadVision.  As for POS transactions, Nixdorf will provide further information with regards to the new Loyalty System functionality that will be added. 
It is intended that the Loyalty Card scheme will be real time, with purchases and returns from the POS and the web being immediately registered in a Central Data Store and visible to other channels
The points total for the transactions will only be displayed on the receipt. To view the points total in store the customer will need to use the kiosk or the service desk or if possible to implement, the POS.
BV will have an account review functionality, detailing a rolling 12 months purchase information history.
Promotions

Promotions are key to T&K's strategy with the regular use of supplier funded coupons in the press, direct mail offers and online personalised promotions
Channels
The Store

The store base will contribute approximately 97% of the turnover and will therefore remain a key focus in T&K’s strategy.

Customer Service is a high priority, with the focus being on the recognition of platinum cardholders and other key customers at local level by key store personnel e.g. customer service desk. 
Store layout is key: for instance placement of correlated products such of power cables next to the electric garden mower.

The Kiosk

Kiosks will be situated in store, train-stations, campuses and airports and will have the same user interface and functionality as the web page except that points can be redeemed for paper coupons at the kiosk. The kiosks will also have a swipe card reader.

The Internet

Internet access to a range of T&K services is provided. These include home shopping, gift registry, reminder service etc which is enabled through using BroadVision technology.

The WAP Mobile Phone

Mobile phone access to a limited range of T&K services will be available using WAP technology.

The Catalogue

The existing T&K catalogue will remain allowing a discussion to evolve based around the use of call centre technology to process catalogue orders. A call centre will not be implemented.
A full personalised catalogue will not be produced, however, category based mail shots would be possible for a range of promotions, e.g. back to school, summer holiday etc. 
Direct Mail

Personalised Direct Mail will be used as part of a multi-channel marketing campaign orchestrated using Valex (campaign management tool). 


Products

T&K offers a range of own brand & branded merchandise.
Own Brand counts for about 60% of the range in homewares, 40% in fashion and 20% in food – RRJ to check food ratio. An exclusive competitively priced range is available in clothing.
It emphasises quality and value for money of the T&K’s Brand.
The products on offer differ depending on the T&K store grouping: high street, out of town, seaside, small store or convenience. Within each of the above formats the store size will also effect the breadth of the product range on offer.
Fulfilment
Internet and Home Delivery

More detail – pick for home vs. pick from store, do we charge for delivery, what products are we using, etc.
Returns Policy

Returns are guaranteed with proof of purchase.
Delivery charges are refunded when T&K has made a mistake.
There will be no recouping of Loyalty Points if the return is worth less than £100. Points will be recouped for returns in excess of £100.
