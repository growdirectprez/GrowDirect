Introduction

This document details the storyline for the new CRM tour at the Retail Solution Centre.

The tour will follow a similar format to the existing RSC tours. It will begin in the boardroom with an introduction to T&K. The RSC team will then take on their roles as T&K customers and the tour will follow the couple as they interact with each of the relevant channels and technologies in the street, home and store.

The clients will then go to the HO and be shown how all the data generated is processed and analysed.

In each pod one or more of the key messages underpinning the CRM storyline will be highlighted.

The key messages are:

Managing customers by Value Measure
Permission based marketing 
Seamless integration of channels
Segmentation of customers
Personalised proposition

T&K’s Strategy
Customer
Characteristics

The story line will be based around a married couple with two children. Mr Simon & Mrs Julia Jones aged 45 and 42 with two children William aged 13 & Victoria aged 9.

The characteristics defining each member of our family are detailed below

Simon – is an Account Director for a large corporation. He is a traditional husband who leaves the shopping and household management  to Julia. He likes branded goods particularly top quality shoes, shirts & ties. Coupons or promotional offers rarely tempt him. He enjoys sports, golf, tennis and skiing in particular and is knowledgeable about wines.  He is interested in “gadgets” and technology and particularly likes the T&K offers by e-mail and the “key date” reminder service He has given them a list of about 10 key dates he needs to remember; family birthdays, wedding anniversary, secretary’s birthday etc. He subscribes to the T&K’s free on-line monthly updates; he receives these by e-mail.
Julia – is quality and value for money conscious, time starved and fashion aware. She is a Solicitor for a small local practice. She enjoys sports tennis & skiing, has an Open University degree in Italian and a passion for everything Italian.  She likes all the T&K offers whether by e-mail, paper mail or in-store offers.
William – is obsessed with sports and fashion always wanting the newest equipment and cool clothing labels. He is technologically clued up and enjoys the Internet and computer games. He has his own mobile phone.
Victoria – is interested in sport particularly skiing and tennis. She follows latest teen bands – Steps, S club 7, Westlife etc. and is coming under peer pressure to be wearing cool clothes (not T&K’s!)


Segmentation and Value

Segmentation


Simon and Julia are individuals, each in a different one of  T&K’s four customer segments
The four segments were originally determined using SAS Data mining tools.
Customers are identified as being in one of the 4 segments below based on the answers to a number of questions. 
When customers join the loyalty scheme (whether via the web or in store) they answer 6 questions. The first 3 questions are factual; the next three determine which category the customers are in.

Age Range
Marital Status
Occupation
Price preference
Brand preference
Function preference

The customer selects a value of 1 to 5 to indicate their strategy preference relating to; product prices, brand preferences and product function e.g. 1 meaning customer prefers coupons vs. 5 meaning customer prefers Everyday fair pricing

The four customer segments identified by T&K and the related behaviours are:



Sales Lovers:
Prefer sales and coupons
Buy Branded goods when on sale, generics otherwise
Rarely buy luxury items
All families in middle and down markets
Splurgers:
Buy on an Every Day Fair Price basis, rarely use coupons
Prefer brands over generics
Tend to splurge on luxury items in each category
Young couples and singles in middle and up markets
Trusting Folk:
Will use coupons as available, less than average
Prefer brands, never buy generics
Purchase basic goods, rarely splurge
Families and households in middle and up markets
Pragmatists:
Use coupons and buy sales items with average frequency
Mostly purchase generics
Purchase basic goods, rarely splurge
Younger parents in middle and down markets and older households in all markets 


Julia is a category 3 shopper and Simon a category 2. William and Victoria do not have loyalty cards and are therefore not classified.
It is possible for a customer to express different preferences for the same attribute across categories. E.g. prefers brands for clothing but not for food. For the purposes of the demonstration, however, both William and Julia will have the same preferences for each category
Because Julia and Simon are in different categories they will be offered different promotions over the different channels

Value

Both Julia and Simon will be allocated a card (Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum) based on their value to T&K. This is determined by their spend in the previous year.

Julia is a platinum cardholder and Simon is a Gold cardholder.

The type of card will effect the level of rebate given by T&K’s loyalty card and the promotions offered 


Other information relating to the customer

Potential value – known as “Headroom” it is the expected total purchases for a given period based on demographic info and lifestyle data minus the current actual spend e.g. the Jones family according to demographic and lifestyle data should spend around £200 pw on food if they are currently spending £120 then headroom is £80pw.
Responsiveness to offers -  promotions
Profitability Last Year – this needs some thought as T&K’s will have to record all costs to deliver the goods, services, promotions etc. to the Jones family. So what is the algorithm for profitability in this case. Net sales – product cost – product distribution cost – proportional (store ops cost + promotion costs + loyalty card +CRM costs) = real profit

Answers to these questions are held in the BV data model and could be used by BV and Valex to determine promotional pushes.

Loyalty Card

T&K has decided to implement a Loyalty system. There are 2 drivers behind this decision
A method of gathering data about customers
An incentive to customers to spend more money

Points are earned on the following scale basis. Assuming the majority of customers spend under £1000 pa. 
Spend under £1,000 = 0.5% or 1 point for every £2, 
Once the spend reaches £1,000 there is a bonus of 1% on the £1,000 i.e. £10 voucher, 
For every £1 spent between £1,000 and £2,000 1%
For every £1 over £2,000 1.5%
Redemption will also be on the basis of  points ….
Once earned the points can be converted to on-line vouchers which can be redeemed over the web, through the kiosk for another companies vouchers (TBD) and/or through the POS where she can get T&K's vouchers/money off her bill.
Simon and Julia will have separate loyalty cards
The Loyalty points will be maintained within the Broadvision database. Functionality will be limited to the basic earning and redemption of loyalty points. The process will be managed within Broadvision, for POS transactions a file will be generated to be picked up by Broadvision. 
It is intended that the loyalty card scheme be real time with purchases / returns from the POS and the web being immediately registered and visible in the other channels
The points total for the transaction only will be displayed on the receipt. To view the points total in store the customer will need to use the kiosk or the service desk or the POS?
BV to have an account review page within including current and previous period balances.
Promotions

Promotions are key to T&K's strategy with the regular use of supplier funded coupons in the press, direct mail offers and on-line personalised promotions

Direct Mail

Product

T&K offers a range of own brand & branded merchandise
Own Brand counts for about 60% of the range in homewares, 40% in fashion and 20% in food – RRJ to check food ratio. An exclusive competitively priced range is available in clothing.

It emphasises quality, value for money and the exclusivity of T&K’s Brand
The product on offer differs depending on the T&K store grouping – Highstreet, Out of Town, SeaSide, Small Stores, Convenience. Within each of the above formats the store size will also effect the breadth of the product range offered.
