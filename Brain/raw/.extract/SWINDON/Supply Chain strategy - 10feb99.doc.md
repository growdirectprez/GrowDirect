Strategic Supply Chain Planning
Draft Tour Script 
Key Messages

The following are the key messages from this tour:
Supply chain strategy should be regularly reviewed in the light of changes in market, business strategy and technology
A review of supply chain strategy should consider infrastructure/facilities, processes, systems and organisation
At the core of designing an optimum supply chain is designing the right physical network, supported by appropriate processes.  Computer modelling of network options is a valuable tool in such design work. 

Introduction

T&K’s business has changed radically in the last year or two and even more change is expected in the next few years. For example :

T&K is no longer just a UK retailer, with stores now in France and Germany and more planned across Europe over the next five years.
The product mix has changed drastically – T&K no longer sells just clothes to middle-aged people; today there is a variety of products & services targeted at multiple segments.
The store is no longer the only channel – T&K is introducing different formats, each with its own unique proposition. These include different store formats, a variety of catalogues and more recently electronic channels via the Internet and eventually with interactive TV.
The service proposition has also changed fundamentally – today customers can order products and services specifically tailored to their needs, delivered to a location of their choice be it a store, their home, office or any other pick up point. 24 hour trading has become the norm for the electronic channels and store opening hours have also increased with the liberalisation of trading laws. A major development is the emergence of a pan-European customer base buying from multiple countries. At the same time consumers are increasingly demanding about product range and customer service.

All of these factors have created enormous challenges in the “supply chain” – the physical network, organisation, and information flows which get the right products to the customer or point of sale. Something had to be done to reconfigure the supply chain to support these new business requirements.

The current physical distribution infrastructure was unable to cope with the demands of the business.  T&K had to address a number of issues from a strategic point of view :

How should we segment the logistics operation – by geography, customer, product, customer service proposition, etc. ?
What supply chain capacity was necessary to run the business ?
Of this capacity, how much should be managed internally and how much should be managed by third parties ?
What physical infrastructure was necessary in terms of distribution centres, etc. ?
Where should this physical infrastructure be located given that the business is becoming pan-European ?
Should we pick from DCs for the mail order / internet business or should we pick from store ?
If we pick from DCs for mail order / internet customers, then should we have a dedicated picking centre or pick from our existing DCs that support stores ?
How should this infrastructure be configured given that T&K now delivers large loads to stores and smaller loads to specific customers ?
Can greater utilisation be made of existing infrastructure to get more throughput ?
What further automation is appropriate ?

As a result of all of this, T&K developed a new supply chain strategy. We recognised that this is something which they would need to repeat from time to time and so what they have done is to introduce a more formal strategic supply chain planning process as part of their annual strategic review. It is expected that in most years, they will be able to make minor changes to their strategy but every now and again there will be a set of circumstances that force a major change. The main reason for reviewing this annually is that implementing changes may involve initiatives which require long timescales to implement.  These may include new supply chain facilities or contracts (e.g. in warehousing, transport), or significant new processes/systems (e.g. computer systems).
T&K have introduced a new 5 tier strategic supply chain planning process :

Every year, a number of indicators such as segment split, service levels, cost inflation, etc. are examined to assess whether or not the current supply chain strategy is meeting the needs of the business. External assessments are also carried out on technology, the competition and market trends.
If it is felt that the current strategy is no longer providing the best cost / service balance in its existing form, then a wider ranging review of business requirements is conducted and various scenarios are modelled to understand requirements in more specific terms.
If the business review indicates that more capacity, lower costs or different service is required, then the supply chain network is modelled to establish where best to have how much capacity.
If the network model indicates the need for new distribution centres, then various configuration options are modelled to help develop a business case for the right design.
If the requirements indicate that more needs to be obtained from existing capacity, then a more detailed machine model is developed to simulate the effects of actual automation and the options in how to configure this.

Step 1 : Triggers to review Supply Chain Strategy

Every year, T&K looks at the following indicators to establish whether or not the current supply chain strategy is delivering the required support to the business :
Performance indicators which are worse than industry best practice: for example service levels, distribution costs per unit, stock levels, etc.
Over- or under-capacity in current facilities
Substantial changes in the market place, for example rising volumes, longer opening hours, different store formats
Technology changes, such as EPOS data availability, e-commerce

Step 2 : Current and Future Business Requirements
 
The second step is to assess the challenge for the T&K supply chain, both currently and into the future. To do this we must look at what the T&K business will require from the supply chain.   Any supply chain strategy should consider requirements for 5-10 years ahead, especially if substantial investment is required. 

At the core of the challenge for the supply chain are the future demand and market requirements: the basic data for T&K to look at this is:

Projected sales for the organisation over the next 5 years, by
Region/area of demand
Demand channels (stores, mail order ..)
Product category (apparel, foods …)
Product churn & diversity
Market Positioning
Financial/capital investment constraints
Current supply chain facilities
Characteristics of future business:
Supplier locations
Customer service levels /response times required or planned
Market requirements
Legislative restrictions (e.g. environmental)

Step 3 : Supply Chain Network Option Analysis
 
We have seen that projected changes in the market, and the plans that T&K have for using alternative sales channels, will lead to over-capacity for the current Distribution Centres, and will provide new challenges for the Supply Chain.  It is clear that there is a requirement to set up new facilities, and to review the positions, sizes and functions of existing facilities.   Overall, we need to define what the best network is for T&K, and how it will operate to support the business requirements.

To help in this task we can set up a detailed computer model of the alternative network options for T&K, simulating the effects of each option on costs and service.  This uses the ‘demand scenarios’ which we developed in the “sales analysis’ model.

Step 4 – Distribution Centre Configuration

The analysis from the previous steps indicates that T&K is going to need at least one new Distribution Centre for servicing stores and another for the growing home shopping business. The capital expenditure request for £60m per DC has been taxing the board with the main concerns being :

Do we really need 2 new DCs ?
What if our home shopping business does not take off as forecast ?

The Supply Chain function were set with the challenge of establishing whether or not one dual purpose super DC would meet the requirements. The attraction from their point of view was that it would be a lower investment and also optimise the sale of stock through both store and home shopping channels. However, it was not easy to decide how much space to allocate to each, how to configure the DC in detail e.g. how much space to use for cross-docking versus flow racking, etc.

Step 4 – Distribution Centre Configuration

T&K’s analysis showed that adding another DC would still leave a capacity shortfall. More throughput was required from current DCs. A small parts singles picking system had already been installed. The Distribution function had various ideas on different types of automation that could be installed to increase throughput. The suppliers of these types of systems were quoting various throughput rates that sounded a little too optimistic. The T&K board wanted a degree of comfort on whether or not the favoured options would deliver the desired result since the investment ranged from £10m – £18m.

A detailed machine model was developed to simulate this and verify the suppliers estimates.

As it turned out, the suppliers suggested configurations would not have delivered the required throughput. However, with a few adjustments, T&K found a way of achieving more than was required. 


Conclusion

We have seen that T&K cannot stand still in its Supply Chain: changes in its business and customer demands that change is essential.  The cost of poor decisions can be huge in terms of high costs and poor service.  Using sophisticated modelling techniques, we can develop solutions which provide

Improved return on investment (ROI)
Reduced business risk (investment & viability)
More informed understanding on the cost of flexibility
Improved service levels

Today, T&K has to use specialist systems and tools to model supply chain requirements. Tomorrow, a new breed of Advanced Planning & Optimisation systems are expected to be available to do this What If scenario modelling. T&K will review these products, including SAP’s SCOPE module and use the most appropriate technology going forward.
