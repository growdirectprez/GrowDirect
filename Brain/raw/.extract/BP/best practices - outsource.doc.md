Definition of Outsourcing

Outsourcing – The long term contracting of services, to an oustide provider, that was previously performed by internal personnel.  Outsourcing is the redefinition of the corporation around core competencies and long-term outside relationships.  Outsourcing shifts the corporation’s focus from “how to do” to “what to do”.
Business Process Outsourcing – The term used to describe the emerging trend of companies to outsource discrete business processes (financial, accounting, tax, human resources, etc.) in order to allow a refocus on defined corporate care competencies.
Best of Breed Outsourcing – The term used to describe the selection of multiple outsourcing providers based on skills knowledge of specific business process or IT process areas in a bundled outsourcing transaction.
Selective Outsourcing – An outsourcing agreement of more limited nature and shorter duration than a traditional outsourcing contract.  It blends in-house and vendor resources to optimize IT operations.
Transitional Outsourcing – The use of 3rd party vendors to handle management of legacy systems, transition to new systems, and stabilization and management of new processes

Economists Intelligence Unit survey results:
26% of the executives interviews have at least one financial function outsourced currently
42% of executives expect to outsource at least one financial function in the next 3 years
The most widely outsourced financial functions include pension management (42%), tax (40%), payroll (26%), and asset appraisal/valuation (24%).
Other outsourced financial functions include organizational training, leasing, internal audit, investment management, accounts payable/receivable, accounting, and treasury management.
Size of the company or function is not a major factor in the outsourcing decision
85% of executive surveyed outsource all or part of at least one business function

Examples of organizations who have employed outsourcing include

Accounts Payable
Houghton Mifflin, a publisher, realized substantial savings through outsourcing accounts payables to Wachovia Bank.  The incremental cost of writing checks has decreased from $.65 - $.70 to $.20 and $.08 for payments made by electronic funds transfer (EFT).  The volume of checks before the decision to outsource checks was 600-700 checks, with 1,000 during peak times.  Although initially scheduled for 90 days, the transition to Wachovia bank processing took 50 days.
      The process involves Houghton Mifflin preparing payment vouchers, including the amount to be paid and the format of the payment.  Wachovia outsources print and mailing checks to First Pay, who screens addresses for accuracy by comparing them against updated lists from the UP Post Office.  When checks are presented for payment, Wachovia compares the amounts to a master list to guarantee accuracy.  If these controls fail, Wachovia takes responsibility.  Houghton Mifflin pays $.20 per check and $.08 per EFT (plus a one-time fee per vendor).  Discounts from the bank are only available for larger volumes, upwards of 10,000 checks per month.
      Houghton Mifflin also outsources the management of its pension fund and is considering payroll after the experience with Wachovia.


General Ledger
Alcatel Italia, an European producer of telephone equipment, outsources the inputting of transaction data into their general ledger and transaction control systems.  Alcatel purchased its business from US-based ITT several years ago and, to handle the increased volume, decided to outsource this function to achieve greater flexibility



Accounting Services
Cyprus Amax Minerals Company, a $3 billion coal and copper mining company, contracted to provide accounting services including certain transaction processing, general ledger, financial statement drafting, and some analysis through the design creation and operation of a shared services center.  All employees affected by the transition were offered employment to the contractor or were redeployed within Cyprus.


Payroll and General Accounting
A traditional manufacturer outsourced procurement administration, payroll, HR administration, and general accounting.  Their primary findings included legacy accounting systems that needed improvement, financial reports required three weeks to produce, and the cost of financial management was increasing.  This company conducted a detailed business case analysis for outsourcing financial transactions, drafted a proposed Service Level Agreement, and developed a contract with an outsourcing provider.  As a result there was a 30% cost reduction, 75% reduction in reporting cycle time, and higher levels of accuracy.

Payroll
Microsoft outsources payroll processing, a significant portion of benefits administration, offshore compliance for financial and accounting taxes, and various treasury functions, such as the custody and management of share certificates.  In addition, Microsoft asserts that they are looking for additional opportunities to “create scale economies by outsourcing financial functions”
