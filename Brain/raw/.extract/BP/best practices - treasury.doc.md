Best Practice Option

Clear Policy Guidelines
Clear policy on risk appetite
Performance goals monitored
Establish well-defined business objectives
Establish clear guidelines for investment and debt management
Degree of centralization is well-defined and understood
Employ suitable passwords for funds transfers
Perform periodic audits and implement appropriate controls

Integration with Other Financial Systems
Maintain investment/debt transactions in an integrated treasury management workstation and link with accounting and risk management systems

Streamlining Treasury Processes
Treasury work station integrated with other financial systems
Automation of cash management, trading, currency, and interest rate management
Use of EFT and EDI
Establish a streamlined and cost-effective process for managing investment/debt transactions
Coordinated with project planning to ensure proper mix of short and long-term funds
Utilizes cash flow forecasts to identify amount and timing of investable cash or cash shortfalls
Automated bank reconciliation

Examples of organizations that have utilized best practices include:

An electric company found treasury input (i.e., knowledge of EFT/EDI/Lockboxes) invaluable when it re-engineered nine core finance functions. "Quick hits" (early successes) were all treasury related and provided momentum and backed up effort to sell re-engineering within the organization. The banking perspective helped focus on cost/benefit analysis by freeing up $15m cash held in travel advances by replacing them with ATM cards. This action saved the company $2 million per annum.  This company found that common systems and consolidated practices are a necessity to cut out the complexity of a banking network. Once reengineering is completed, monitoring and reconciliation is simplified, allowing substantial savings in fees and cashflow.

A U.S. company’s restructuring aimed to focus on core businesses and managing corporate functions on a global basis to leverage the company's size. As the new management information system was implemented, they changed Disbursement accounts, bank relationship and authorizing signatures were changed with the implementation of a new management information system, resulting in huge the cost savings. Treasury success led to involvement of treasury in reengineering eight other functions.

The treasury department in a major gasoline distributor sold the idea of EFT straight out of Accounts Payable via EDI to cut paperwork, errors and time. New system envisions variety of payment options, automatic journal entries and payment files sent electronically to the bank

Cash Management Policy/Process
Best Practice Option
Best practices for cash management centers on establishing a well-defined treasury policy, streamlining the cash management process, and improving the management of cash.  Analysis reveals the following best practices in treasury management:

Establish Well-Defined Policy
Clearly defined and communicated cash management guidelines
Well-defined risk philosophy
Establish cash disbursement limits

Streamline the Cash Management Process
Maintain streamlined and cost-effective bank account structure
Utilize EDI, EFT, lockboxes, sweep accounts, controlled disbursement to more efficiently manage cash flows
Centralized banking facilities with minimal number of bank accounts
Outsource aspects of cash application to bank to improve efficiency of cash application

Improving Cash Management
Daily, monthly and yearly rolling cash flow forecasts
Daily forecasts used to support investing/short-term borrowing decision
Medium-term forecasts used to plan operating cash utilization
Periodic update of cash forecast to meet the needs of the organization (twelve month forecast updated monthly)
Accurate assessment of daily operational cash requirements and activities
Use of cash ratios to monitor sufficiency of cash and efficiency of use
Bank compensation monitored and analyzed regularly
Use excess cash in one unit to minimize funding costs in other units

Examples of organizations who have utilized best practices for cash management:

An international manufacturer launched a Cash Management Improvement Program that eliminated local payroll account and as many local depositories as possible. The depository accounts are still there, but the amounts running through them are significantly less since most transactions are redirected to the lockboxes. One lead bank is used to provide concentration, fund transfer and disbursement services. The number of banks used was reduced from 150 to about 85. The balances of these banks were reduced from $25 million to $3 million.  Cost management at the operating companies has become more effective because the low balances mean companies pay fees and control their expenditures more carefully. This company reduced bank exposure and reduced outstanding bank balances via zero balance accounts and fees in lieu of balances. They also increased efficiency and communications via automation.

Cash Management Technology
Best Practice Option
Best practices for cash management centers on automating bank reconciliation, producing management reports, and producing an effective treasury workstation with other financial systems.  Analysis reveals the following best practices:

Automating Reconciliation
Automated linkage with bank information and accounting system
Automated bank reconciliation
Electronic downloading of transaction data from banks

Produce Management Reports
Variance reporting of actual cash position against forecast position
Ability to generate customized reports
System that produces reports on all bank accounts and associated information

Effective Treasury Workstation
Integrated treasury management workstation
Integrated cash flow forecasting station with accounting systems (general ledger, accounts receivable, accounts payable)
Inputs to forecast generated directly from accounts payable and receivable modules
Ability to monitor bank charges electronically
Development of treasury management systems which retrieve account information same day or previous day directly from the banks, store the data for all future reference
Internal company/division netting systems which avoid the use of internal checks, reducing bank charges, and eliminates the float/overdraft requirements
On–line systems to issue electronic one–day payments through an automated clearing house (ACH) for third party and inter company transfers (transaction cost is 8 cents)

Examples of organizations that have utilized best practices include:

While changing from a wholesale orientation to a retail orientation, a major company re-engineered their cash management process.  The change began by identifying “quick wins” that will not hinder longer-term solutions and will save the company currently, for example by changing from bank transaction detail to general ledger.  Within a few weeks, 75% of bank transactions were posting automatically.  In addition, the company was able to reconcile transactions to the general ledger four to six weeks earlier than previous practices.
