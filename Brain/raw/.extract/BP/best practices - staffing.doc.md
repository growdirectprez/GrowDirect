Structure and Role of Finance Function

Best practices shows that the traditional finance structure is not meeting current business needs and calls for a centralized/matrixed finance structure.  A centralized/matrixed finance organizational structure is viewed as more responsive to the customer than the traditional finance organization.  The characteristics of the traditional and centralized/matrixed finance structure are listed below:

Characteristics of Traditional Finance Structure (less cost effective, outdated):
Emphasis of the finance division’s role is as gatekeeper and auditor  
Accounting specialist with highly standardized procedures
Headquarters has knowledge, authority, and control
Historical number crunching, centralized decision making

Characteristics of Centralized/Matrixed Finance Structure (most cost effective, best practice):
Team member, coach, advisor
Business perspective and operational exposure, more flexible procedures
Knowledge, authority and control distributed throughout business
Prospective analysis and problem-solving
Majority of transaction processing is automated and/or outsourced, required little manual intervention

World-class finance departments demonstrate the following key trends:
Link finance organizational structure to the business strategy
Build a small core finance staff with deep skills
Educate business partners within organization in financial concepts
Centralize transaction processing
Distribute decision support

World-class finance organizations spend 25% of their time on transaction processing (formerly manual systems are automated) and 75% on decision support.   In a decision support role, finance has the following roles and responsibilities:
Shift emphasis of role from scorekeeper to strategic business partner with decision makers of organization
Influence decisions
Understand all processes
Build consensus
Practice horizontal management
Maximize value 
Concerned with balance sheet, cash flow, total shareholder return
Focus on effectiveness
Marketplace focus
Customer and people focused

Finance Staff Skills

Staff of world class finance organizations are business partners in their organization and posses the following skills in priority order:
Problem solving and creativity
Teamwork and coaching
Financial accounting/control
Business/line experience
Change/project management
Financial systems
Communication

Finance Function Costs

Given this new, strategic role for the finance function, best practices indicates that world class organizations spend the following proportional percentages on finance staff.

The total cost of the Finance function varies as a percent of sales:
Best in class                  0.35%
Above average             0.75%
Average                         1.4%
Inefficient companies  2.0% and above

Examples of organizations that have utilized best practice include:

Kmart has implemented Category Teams that integrate traditionally separate functional divisions.  Merchandising, logistics, finance, and planning personnel are included in a Category Team in order to “achieve parity with competitors, get different perspectives, and expedite decision-making.”  In addition, both Target and Wal-Mart use this same team structure.
