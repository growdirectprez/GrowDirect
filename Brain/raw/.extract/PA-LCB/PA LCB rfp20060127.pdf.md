I-1.  PURPOSE  -  This  Request  for  Proposal  (“RFP”)  provides  interested  parties

with sufficient information to enable them to prepare and submit proposals for

consideration  by  the  Commonwealth  of  Pennsylvania,  Pennsylvania  Liquor

Control Board (“PLCB”), to satisfy a need for category management analysis.

I-2.

ISSUING OFFICE - This RFP is issued by the PLCB.  The Issuing Officer for

this  RFP  is  Ms.  Debra  L.  Brinser,  Chief,  Procurement  Division,  Room  413,

Northwest  Office  Building,  Harrisburg,  Pennsylvania  17124-0001;  telephone:

(717)  787-9851;  facsimile:    (717)  783-8927;  e-mail:  dbrinser@state.pa.us.

The  Issuing  Office  is  the  sole  point  of  contact  in  the  Commonwealth  for  this

RFP.

I-3.  SCOPE  -  This  RFP  contains  instructions  governing  the  proposals  to  be

submitted and the material to be included therein; a description of the service

to be provided; requirements that must be met to be eligible for consideration;

general evaluation criteria; a proposed contract; and other requirements to be

met by each proposal.

I-4.  PROBLEM  STATEMENT  -  The  PLCB  recognizes

the  opportunity

for

efficiencies  and  resource  and  cost  savings,  as  well  as  increased  customer

satisfaction, within the structure and processes of category management as it

1

relates  to  the  sale  of  wine  and  spirits.    The  PLCB  is  seeking  a  contractor  to

assist  the  PLCB  with  analyzing  its  current  category  management  practices,

policies and procedures, and evaluating completed merchandising test results.

The PLCB wishes to ensure that policies, procedures and required tools based

on  industry  best  practices  are  put  into  place  to  increase  service  delivery,

customer  satisfaction  and  store  operational  efficiency  in  each  of  the  PLCB’s

wine and spirits stores.  The selected proposer will conduct detailed category

management  analysis  and  will  benchmark  the  PLCB  category  management

processes  against  proven  industry  standards  and  norms.    The  selected

proposer  will  provide  the  PLCB  with  detailed  process  workflows  and

recommendations  for  category  management  efficiencies  and  improvements.

Additional detail is provided in Part IV of the RFP.

IV-1.   OBJECTIVES  -  The  PLCB  is  seeking  a  contractor  to  assist  the  PLCB  with

analyzing its current category management practices, policies and procedures,

and  evaluating  completed  merchandising  test  results.    The  PLCB  wishes  to

ensure  that  policies,  procedures  and  required  tools  based  on  industry  best

practices are put into place to increase service delivery, customer satisfaction

and  store  operational  efficiency  in  each  of  the  PLCB  wine  and  spirits  stores.

The  selected  proposer  will  conduct  detailed  category  management  analysis

and  will  benchmark  the  current  PLCB  processes  against  proven  industry

standards and norms.

General  and  specific  objectives  are  provided  below  to  show  what  the  PLCB

expects to gain from the service.

A.

General.

It  is  the  PLCB’s  desire  to  incorporate  research-based

strategies  into  all  of  its  business  practices  so  ultimately,  the  right

products are available in each store and merchandising guidelines are

implemented that create a consumer-centric shopping environment.

and  preferences  of

the  beverage  alcohol  consumer  and

stores.

To find the most efficient way to maximize space in the PLCB’s

wine  and  spirits  stores  so  a  strict  inventory  model  can  be

adhered to.

3.

To  find  a  layout  for  stores  that  makes  the  most  sense  to

in

the  appropriate

timeframes.

APPENDIX I

OVERVIEW OF THE PENNSYLVANIA LIQUOR CONTROL BOARD

Pennsylvania Liquor Control Board Mission

The Pennsylvania Liquor Control Board (“PLCB”) is an independent state agency that
is also one of the largest retailers of wines and spirits in the world.  It is a modern,
dynamic  organization  which  consistently  strives  to  serve  the  public  interest  as  it
fulfills its three (3)-pronged mission of regulation, retail sales and customer service,
and alcohol education.

In  order  to  sell  alcohol  in  Pennsylvania,  a  permit  or  license  is  issued  by  the  PLCB’s
Bureau  of  Licensing  (“Licensing”).    Licensing  analyzes  and  applies  the  appropriate
statutory and regulatory criteria to all applications for licenses and permits issued by
the PLCB.  Licensing scrutinizes tax status by processing tax clearance certifications
through the Pennsylvania Department of Revenue and the Department of Labor and
Industry.    The  Pennsylvania  State  Police,  Bureau  of  Liquor  Control  Enforcement,
works  with  Licensing  to  enforce  compliance  with  the  Pennsylvania  Liquor  Code  [47
P.S. § 1-102 et seq.] and the PLCB’s Regulations [40 Pa. Code § 1.1 et seq.].

The  Bureau  of  Alcohol  Education  (“Alcohol  Education”)  seeks  to  accomplish  its
portion of the PLCB mission by providing leadership and support to communities and
state  organizations  to  employ  prevention  and  education  strategies  that  reduce  the
misuse or abuse of alcohol.  Some of the misuses and abuses that the PLCB seeks to
prevent  are:  underage  drinking,  high-risk  college  drinking,  impaired  driving  and
other  alcohol-related  consequences,  such  as  sexual  assault,  property  damage,
violence, crime, suicides and addiction.  Its programs have become models for other
states  and  locales  for  the  development  of  approaches  to  reducing  alcohol  misuse,
and its staff are important resources for those organizations.

The  Bureau  of  Product  Management  (“Product  Management”)  is  responsible  for  the
marketing of wine and spirit beverages and accessories sold through the PLCB’s wine
and  spirits  store  system.    This  responsibility  involves  analyzing  the  marketing,
pricing,  proper  inventory  levels,  category  management  development  and  refining
product  merchandising  policies.    Product  listings  and  delistings,  special  purchase
allowances,  promotions  and  product  price  change  recommendations  are  developed
by Product Management as well.  Product Management also acquires and controls the
movement of special liquor order merchandise from vendors’ premises directly to the
PLCB’s Pennsylvania wine and spirits stores.

The Bureau of Store Operations (“Store Operations”) is responsible for the operation
of  approximately  six  hundred  fifty  (650)  wine  and  spirits  stores  located  throughout
the  Commonwealth  of  Pennsylvania.    The  PLCB  has  organized  the  Commonwealth
into three (3) regions and twenty-eight (28) districts.  Each region is responsible for
supervising the overall operation of the stores in their region, including sales policies
and  procedures,  inventory  and  loss  control,  ordering  of  merchandise,  display  of
merchandise, customer service and control of inventory levels.

110

PLCB Initiatives

The  PLCB’s  wine  and  spirits  stores  are  almost  entirely  self-service,  allowing  the
customer  to  browse  and  comparison  shop  amongst  an  expansive  product  mix.
Stores  feature  bright,  appealing  interiors  equipped  with  electronic  retail  technology
and  custom-built  shelving.    Employees  are  trained  and  knowledgeable  about  wine
and spirits products.  Competitive prices obtained through the PLCB’s position as one
(1)  of  the  nation’s  largest  purchaser  of  wine  and  spirits  are  continuously  offered.
Approximately twenty-five percent (25%) of the stores are open on Sundays.

The  PLCB  currently  has  approximately  sixty-eight  (68)  Premium  Collection  stores.
These  stores  carry  thousands  of  products  in  all  price  ranges  and  categories,
displayed in a modern and tasteful environment.  In addition, they feature collections
of  finer  and  hard-to-find  wine  and  spirits.    Most  of  these  stores  now  feature  a
temperature-controlled environment for premium products that require special care,
to  ensure  that  customers  receive  the  utmost  in  quality.    Each  premium  collection
store has a knowledgeable wine specialist to help customers make selections.

One-Stop  Shops  are  wine  and  spirits  stores  located  within  supermarkets,  but  are
staffed  by  PLCB  employees.    This  concept  was  initiated  as  a  means  to  provide
shopping convenience to PLCB customers.  The first One-Stop Shop was a Premium
Collection  store  located  in  the  Clemens  Family  Market  in  Blue  Bell,  Montgomery
County.  To date, the PLCB has opened seventeen (17) One-Stop Shops, with plans
to open more in 2006 due to the success of these stores.

The  Outlet  store  concept  is  one  of  the  PLCB’s  key  initiatives.    Outlet  stores  feature
brand-name  products  at  heavily  discounted  prices  aimed  at  addressing  border
bleed—the  exodus  of  Pennsylvanians  who  in  years  past  have  sought  lower  prices
across  the  state’s  borders.    Currently,  there  are  seven  (7)  Outlet  stores  in
Pennsylvania.

The  PLCB  E-Commerce  website,  (www.pawineandspirits.com)  offers  enhanced  wine
education  information  and  an  expanded  selection  of  more  than  one  thousand  five
hundred  (1,500)  wines,  spirits  and  accessories.    The  site  includes  ratings  from
magazines such as Wine Enthusiast, Wine Spectator and The Wine Advocate.  Users
can  research  information  about  specific  varietals  or  wine  regions  and  compare
different vintages.  Orders are delivered to the store of the customer’s choice within
one (1) or two (2) business days for a small shipping fee.

The website is also a portal for the Wine Connection, the exclusive wine club of the
PLCB.    The  wine  club  offers  consumers  the  opportunity  to  select  a  monthly
membership  category  (red  wine,  white  wine  or  a  mixed  selection)  and  receive  two
(2) bottles of fine wine each month or every other month.  Wine club selections are
chosen  carefully  by  wine  experts  and  are  available  exclusively  for  club  members  at
superior prices.

Category Management

The  PLCB  began  implementing  the  principles  of  category  management  into  its
business practices in 1997, with the assistance of the vendor community and the use
of outside consultants.  Category management is the process of managing a product

111

category  or  service  and  its  related  items  as  a  strategic  business  unit.    As  a  result,
merchandising decisions can be made based upon stated category goals which have
been  developed  considering  the  needs  of  consumers.    This  means  that  consumer
usage and purchase patterns will drive the organization of the categories.

Within  the  framework  of  category  management,  the  PLCB  wine  and  spirits  stores
were examined for similarities and “clustered” accordingly.  Clustering places stores
that  perform  similarly  in  groups,  taking  into  consideration  driver  categories—those
categories that demonstrate a strong performance within a store relative to the total
PLCB.    Demographic  data  is  used  to  validate  the  clustering  decisions.    The  goal  of
clustering is to get in touch with the customer and to be able to analyze and evaluate
the  market.    Using  this  data-driven  approach  allows  for  a  thorough  analysis  of
product placement, sales, promotions and inventory management in individual stores
and in the distribution centers.

In  1999,  the  Pennsylvania  wine  and  spirits  stores  were  organized  into  four  (4)
clusters.    These  original  clusters  were  developed  using  demographic  indicators
(education,  income,  professions,  etc.)    Over  time,  it  was  realized  that  there  was  a
drastic  variance  in  the  performance  of  stores  within  certain  clusters.    This  led  to
further  research  and  a  resulting  re-clustering  for  a  total  of  eight  (8)  clusters  in
August 2004.   This number of clusters was deemed manageable, yet profitable, for
the effort needed to put the right product mix in front of the right customer.

The current clusters are as follows:
Cluster 1: Wine Enthusiasts
Cluster 2: Premium Wine Development
Cluster 3: Popular Wine Development
Cluster 4: Mainstream Spirits & Low Tier Wines
Cluster 5: Metro Ethnic
Cluster 6: Brown Goods & Low Tier Wines
Cluster 7: Brown Goods Development
Cluster 8: Urban Ethnic

The PLCB conducted a three (3) month merchandising test (May-July 2005) to assess
the  impact  of  a  cluster-based  assortment  and  new  merchandising  concepts.    The
results of this test will guide much of the upcoming category management work.

Elements of the merchandising test included studying the benefits of:

•  Moving  products  at  certain  price  points  from  the  wood  sections  to  the  metal

shelves.

•  Maintaining a seven (7)-day supply of all products on the shelves.  Based on
available sales data, planograms were designed so that facings of all products
would be adjusted to allow for a full seven (7)-day supply at minimum.

•  Quality as a driver for spirits.  For most spirit categories, vertical price blocks
were  implemented  to  display  the  products,  rather  than  the  “top  shelf”
approach.  This allows the customer to shop a section of the category instead
of walking along the shelf line.

•  Size as a more distinguishing characteristic than brand for consumers making
wine purchase decisions.  This model resulted in the separation of 750ml and
1.5L sizes.  Two (2) concepts were tested:

o  Above/below where 750s were blocked on the top two (2) shelves and

1.5’s on the bottom two (2) shelves.

112

o  Side  by  side  where  750’s  and  1.5’s  were  blocked  separately,  side  by

side.

For the merchandising test, twenty (20) test stores were matched with twenty (20)
control  stores.    Resulting  data  show  that  the  stores  were  extremely  well  matched.
Control  store  schematics  were  not  adjusted.    Store  specific  schematics  were
developed  for  the  test  stores,  using  consumer  purchase  decision  hierarchy-based
merchandising principles.  Customer intercepts were conducted in both the test and
control  stores  to  evaluate  the  customers’  response  to  the  merchandising  change
perception,  the  variety  perception  and  any  changes  to  in-store  purchase  behaviors.
Details on the merchandising test can be found in Appendix K.

Enterprise Resource Planning

The  PLCB  is  pursuing  the  implementation  of  an  integrated  Enterprise  Resource
Planning  (ERP)  system  that  will  accommodate  the  collection  of  financial  and
inventory  transactions  and  post  them  for  financial  reporting,  centralized  inventory
control  and  management,  and  end-to-end  supply  chain  management.    As  such,  the
replenishment  of  store  inventory  will  be  accomplished  by  tracking  product  sales  at
the  store  level  and  establishing  efficient  orders  to  the  PLCB  warehouses,  with  the
aggregation  of  store  demand  being  utilized  to  derive  efficient  order  quantities  from

inventory data will further be integrated with merchandise assortment planning and
shelf  space  management  to  gain  the  maximum  benefit  of  inventory  allocation  in
support of category management objectives.

The  project  is  scheduled  to  commence  in  March,  2006  with  a  goal  of  complete
conversion of financial reporting by July 1, 2008, the start of a new fiscal year.  The
timing of the retail business component implementations will be driven by the ability
to  implement  such  components  separately  from  the  financial  components,  and  with
least  risk  impact  on  the  target  date  for  financials.    As  such,  business  components
may be addressed as early implementations where they may reduce risk, as well as
deferred implementations that will follow the financials implementation.

The  PLCB  is  pursuing  a  best  practices  approach  to  its  ERP  project  and  desires  to

APPENDIX L

CORE METHODOLOGY

“Core” items are the most profitable items in the cluster and are required for
PLCB wine and spirits stores to carry.  These items are identified one time per
year.  Following is the methodology used to identify core items.

•  The retail sales (and therefore retail profit) for each product within

each cluster and within each warehouse within each cluster is available.
•  The number of periods that each product has been active within each cluster

•

•

is available.
If a product has been active for less than 26 periods, retail profit for the most
recent 13 periods is calculated.
If a product has been active for 26 periods or more, projected retail profit is
calculated for the upcoming 13 periods using the trend of the most recent 26
periods.

For each cluster, in turn:

1.  For each warehouse, which services stores in the cluster, each
product is ranked and receives a percentile ranking relative to the
warehouse.

2.  Every active product is ranked, by its projected or actual retail
profit, with each active code receiving a percentile ranking relative to the
cluster.

3.  A product is determined to be a Core Product within a cluster if it is in
the top 65% ranking for the cluster and the top 80% ranking of every
warehouse which services stores within that cluster.
