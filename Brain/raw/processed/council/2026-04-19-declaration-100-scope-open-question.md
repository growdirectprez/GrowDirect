# Declaration 100 Scope — The Open Question

**Author:** Condor
**Date:** 2026-04-19
**Status:** Standing note. Folds into Foundation's research mandate.
**Extracted to:** `Brain/wiki/cove-declaration-100-scope-open.md`

---

## The claim that is not actually proven

Most secondary writing on Declaration 100 — including prior versions of the
Brain wiki — describes it as "covering all Palos Verdes Corporation land
outside Palos Verdes Estates" or "covering Lot H." Those are convenient
summaries. They are not proven.

## Why it's not settled

The property-description page of Declaration 100 is page 155 — the one page
of the 23-page document that is partially illegible on the LA County
Recorder's microfilm. The county recorder has not been able to produce a
clean copy. That page is where the metes and bounds are defined.

Fragments visible on the smeared page:

- References to Lot H and the Bixby partition (Case 2373)
- Bearings and distances: 2,304.72 ft, 1,779.84 ft, 2,209.30 ft, plus shorter segments
- Mean High Tide Line of the Pacific Ocean, with a general easterly direction
- Triangulation Station "Redondo"
- References to Rancho Palos Verdes and Malo Pinito Ranch
- A "true point of beginning"
- An illegible acreage figure

From what is visible, the covered area is plausibly a *superset* of what
became the later named tracts — the long distances suggest the full
coastline, not a subset. That is the working hypothesis. It is not the same
as proof.

## What it likely excludes

With reasonable confidence:

- **Declaration 1 territory (1923)** — Palos Verdes Estates and Miraleste,
  which already had their own declaration scheme before Declaration 100 was
  recorded
- **Named tracts that recorded their own declarations between 1923 and
  1929** — any area that had already been carved out by its own recorded
  instrument

With less confidence:

- **Rolling Hills** — incorporated 1957, but the land-use framework for
  what became Rolling Hills may or may not have been under Declaration 100
  in 1929. The Gatewood / Rolling Hills Community Association records
  would need to be pulled to know for sure.
- **Parts of Rolling Hills Estates** — similar uncertainty. Some portions
  may have been under Declaration 100 before RHE incorporated (1957) and
  adopted its own framework.
- **Various Bixby heirs' holdings** — portions of Lot H that were
  partitioned out before 1929 may or may not have been PV Corp's to burden.

## Why this matters now

1. **0 Clipper Road (APN 7573-006-024)** — the coverage question is live.
   The 1950 Lot H Declaration clearly applies, auto-renewed through 2035.
   Whether Declaration 100 *also* applies (with different duration and
   amendment thresholds) depends on whether page 155's boundary includes
   that parcel. A clean Declaration 100 could strengthen or weaken the
   restrictive-covenant case depending on what page 155 says.

2. **Foundation cover.** The Abalone Cove Foundation (501(c)(3)) has a
   legitimate public-interest rationale for funding the archival work: a
   non-advocacy research effort to recover a damaged recorded instrument
   of peninsula-wide historical significance. That is the kind of project
   a 501(c)(3) can credibly host, and the Foundation's posture means no
   specific property owner is visibly driving the research for their own
   interest.

3. **Other affected parcels.** The same boundary question that affects 0
   Clipper likely affects other parcels scattered across the peninsula
   that were never brought under a subsequent declaration. Resolving page
   155 clarifies the status of every one of them.

## Paths to resolution

In rough order of probable yield:

1. **Alternate microfilm sources.** Title companies often retain their own
   microfilm or paper copies of recorded instruments from the 1920s-30s.
   First American Title, Chicago Title, CTC, Stewart. A clean copy from
   any of them solves it.
2. **Certified copies in old title policies.** Any title policy issued on
   peninsula parcels between 1929 and 1955 that attached Declaration 100
   as an exhibit may still exist in insurer archives.
3. **Quoted in later instruments.** If any recorded deed, declaration, or
   lease after 1929 quoted Declaration 100's property description in full
   or by reference to its exact metes, that quoted language establishes
   the boundary without page 155.
4. **Bancroft Library / Columbia (Vanderlip papers) / LOC (Olmsted
   records).** The drafting files may contain a legible version of the
   property description.
5. **LA County Surveyor records.** Declaration 100 references Triangulation
   Station "Redondo" and specific bearings. The Surveyor's historical
   control-point records may allow reverse-construction of the missing
   corners once partial data is matched against survey monuments.
6. **Reverse engineering from Declaration 101's seven parcels.** The 1929
   Declaration 101 places specific parcels under Declaration 100's
   framework. Those parcels' legal descriptions are fully legible in the
   101 transcription. They are subsets of 100's coverage, so they
   constrain (but don't define) the outer boundary.

## Posture for the wiki and the public

- **State clearly that the scope is open.** Do not write summary articles
  that flatten this into "covers Lot H" or "covers all non-PVE land." The
  uncertainty is the point.
- **Tag inference vs. verbatim.** Wiki claims traceable to legible source
  text are citable. Claims that rest on inference from the smeared page or
  from later implementing instruments should be labeled as inference.
- **Frame the Foundation's role correctly.** The Foundation is not
  advancing a theory. It is funding the archival work to find out what
  the document actually says, and providing institutional cover while
  that work is done.
