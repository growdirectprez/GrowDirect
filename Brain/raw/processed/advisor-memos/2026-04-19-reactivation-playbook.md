## Distilled Working Positions

### 1. Article II §5 is the procedural reset button

Vanderlip drafters wrote Article II §5 as a dormant reactivation clause, not a routine operating rule. Condition precedent: the Community Association of Palos Verdes has failed to meet/act for >90 consecutive days. That condition has been true for decades. Fifteen (15) owners of record title of fifteen (15) separate parcels within the original Declaration 100 boundary may lawfully:

1. Publish notice (3× Los Angeles daily newspaper + 1× San Pedro paper)
2. Call and hold the meeting
3. Elect a Board of three Trustees by majority vote (weighted by acreage per the original declaration — but modernizable; see Article V section below)

Those Trustees automatically step into the shoes of the original Association and inherit all 26 enumerated powers under Article II §4 — enforcement of restrictions, assessment liens, Art Jury interpretation, zoning/building code functions, park/street/water/sewer oversight.

This is self-executing once the condition precedent is met. Language sits in the recorded chain of title waiting for residents to invoke it.

### 2. What gets brought forward procedurally

The Trustees' first substantive act at or after the meeting: record a **Restated Declaration of Covenants, Conditions & Restrictions** modeled on the 2009 Restated Declaration. In that document they:

- Expressly incorporate by reference surviving, non-void portions of Declaration 100 (Book 9436, p. 155) and Declaration 101 (Book 9482, p. 37):
  - Single-family residential use only (Art. III §1(a) of 100)
  - Density and minimum dwelling-size rules from Declaration 101 as amended by the 1949 Modification
  - Natural drainage / storm-water channel protections (Art. III §1(l) of 100)
  - Building setback, height, Art Jury approval requirements
  - Prohibition on mining/drilling and noxious uses
  - Non-racial, non-abandoned covenants that still serve the original Vanderlip plan
- Add modern recordable clarifications referencing the 1980 Wong subdivision, 1978 Coastal Specific Plan Subregion 4, 1946 USGS report, 1982 Ehlig paper, 2011 LGC Valley study — treating 0 Clipper as flood-hazard / open-space / avoidance-corridor land
- Reaffirm the reversionary interest (successor-in-interest to PV Corp / Filiorum) and the Association's enforcement rights

Courts treat a properly noticed, recorded restatement as valid ratification of original covenants (2009 WPBCA restatement precedent; also Nahrstedt, Citizens for Covenant Compliance). Declarations 100 and 101 resurface — current, enforceable instruments on the same chain of title.

This is NOT the high-threshold amendment vote under Article V §2(a) (80% by area + 70% of all owners + Association approval + Corporation consent). It's the low-threshold Article II §5 reactivation — a constitutional-crisis reset for exactly this situation.

### 3. Boundary — negative-space exclusion drafting

PV Corp drafted master instruments with the largest-possible metes-and-bounds, then express exclusions:
- "All of Lot H except the parcels heretofore subdivided and recorded as Palos Verdes Estates…"
- "except Tract 14649 as shown on the map recorded in Book…"
- "except those portions conveyed to Filiorum Corporation or to the Portuguese Bend Community Association…"

Covenants in Declaration 100 automatically attach to everything inside the master boundary that has not been expressly carved out. Exclusions are exceptions leaving remaining land burdened by the original master covenants.

The Restated Declaration must include a **Boundary Recital** that quotes the original Declaration 100 metes-and-bounds verbatim, lists every negative-space exclusion recorded, and attaches a modern plat overlay (Wong map + current assessor parcels) showing remaining burdened land in highlighted "negative space."

This turns strange title chains from weakness into strength.

### 4. Article V equity modernization

Original Article V ties voting power and enforcement authority to proportional equity / acreage under the old PV Corp master holding. In 2026, with only a subset of parcels reactivating and negative-space exclusions fragmenting original acreage, the acreage-weighted model is impractical.

Restated Declaration modernizes intent: shift to **one vote per parcel (or equal shares)** among participating reactivated owners for all future Association/Trustee actions. Preserves the protective purpose without altering underlying covenants or reversionary interest. Defensible under Nahrstedt and Citizens for Covenant Compliance.

Proposed recital language:

> "The original Article V equity and vote-share provisions of Declaration 100 were intended to ensure fair, proportional governance among all burdened parcels under the Vanderlip master plan. Because only a subset of parcels is now reactivating via the self-executing Article II Section 5 mechanism, and because the negative-space exclusions have fragmented the original acreage holdings, the Trustees hereby modernize the intent by adopting one vote per parcel (or equal shares among participating owners) for all future Association/Trustee actions. This preserves the protective purpose without altering the underlying covenants or reversionary interest."

Pre-draft Restated Declaration with negative-space boundary recital + Article V modernization + creek/fault-line recital + ACLAD partnership language + surviving Dec 100/101 covenants. ### 6. ACLAD partnership — same-side strategy

Abalone Cove Landslide Abatement District mandate: public safety, slope stabilization, open-space preservation. Operates ~22 dewatering wells. Recent data: Abalone Cove decelerating to ~2.14 in/week, Portuguese Bend ~1.37 in/week (early March 2026). City spends ~$1M/year on road repairs.

Reactivated CoAC covenants are the private enforcement counterpart:

- CoAC: recorded equitable servitudes running with the land, not preempted by Housing Element mandates (Nahrstedt, Citizens for Covenant Compliance)

Unassailable layered defense: public abatement + private single-family / geologic restrictions. 1-acre west-of-creek parcel becomes demonstration project — left undeveloped or converted to public-access geologic interpretive overlook.

### 7. YIMBY landscape — adversarial

YIMBY (Yes In My Backyard): pro-housing-supply movement aligned with developers across LA and South Bay. California YIMBY, LA YIMBY, South Bay YIMBY, YIMBY Law.

Tactics against NIMBY/neighborhood opposition:
- Pressure cities to keep high-density sites in Housing Elements
- Oppose downzoning or site removal (including geologically constrained sites)
- Support Builder's Remedy when cities fall out of HE compliance
- Frame opposition as "anti-housing," "elitist," "exclusionary SFR preservation"

Silver Spur precedent: Neighborhood Voices of Silver Spur Inc. (NVSS, `savethecanyon.net`) 501(c)(4)-style nonprofit fighting 482-unit / 11-story Akhilesh Jha / Verdes Estates project at 5323 Ironwood. 400–450 neighbors mobilized. YIMBY-aligned advocacy pushed back on them.

Same adversarial pattern will apply to Abalone Cove. Strategic response: asymmetric private-covenant layer + factual geologic reality + ACLAD partnership. Don't match YIMBY rhetoric — hold factual high ground, let the recorded instrument speak.

### 8. RM-22 track record — eye-sores, incompatibility, danger

City assured residents RM-22 is "for density calculations" only. Track record on the peninsula shows otherwise once applications are filed:
- **Palos Verdes Bay Club** (240-unit, 32821 Seagate Dr) — bulk/scale dwarfing SFR, parking/traffic impacts, architectural clash with peninsula's low-profile view-preserving aesthetic
- Similar overlays near Trump National — zoning/density debates despite conservation easements
- Once rezoned + Builder's Remedy / vested rights invoked, local discretion evaporates

Naive to expect the City to resolve the dual mandate (Sacramento production pressure vs. on-the-ground stewardship) in favor of stewardship. The private covenant layer is the non-naive response.
