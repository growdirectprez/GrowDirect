# Processes
       The core of the operating model is the end to end processes for providing property, building services and facilities management (property services). The end to end processes cover all the activities required in delivering property, services to the business. This includes everything from translation of business strategy into property strategy, putting together programmes of work and financial forecasts, to delivery and payment for services such as cleaning and utilities.
The structure
       The organisational structure describes the organisations, internal and external, which carry-out the processes, and how the interfaces or relationships between them are to be managed
Financial structure
       Responsibility for budgets need to be aligned with process responsibilities. An important principle is to align budgets with responsibility for decision making. Budget responsibility should not be given to parts of the organisation that have no influence on the process and how budgets are spent.
Performance Measures
       Processes and organisations should be supported by a performance management framework which measures the outputs of the end to end processes to; understand the effectiveness of the processes and organisations and to make changes where required, and to incentivise those carrying out activities to focus on what is important for the business

# Values & Culture

Preferred
     Implement
-

Options
introduction
Method

Option
-ation
There are many definitions of organisational culture.  One of the most useful is: “what people collectively believe is important for their organisation and themselves to succeed”.  Beliefs of what it takes to succeed are often subconscious, because people will have behaved and thought in a way for so long they are almost not aware of it.
Culture is important because it affects the decisions and behaviour that drive performance.
There are two aspects of culture that will need to be addressed during implementation:
Aspects of the existing culture which do not fit with new ways of working and will therefore block the change.  For the preferred option the following may be difficult cultural challenges:
 Working for benefits across JLP in a strong Divisional culture
Working with clearly defined and measured responsibilities, particularly where current responsibilities may have been given to others and it may be tempting to shadow or second guess
Working within an end to end process, where although responsibilities are defined there is a requirement to understand process steps that lead to and from and individuals own activities. A process culture requires people to actively help interfaces to succeed and not to make process and responsibilities a new opportunity for a silo mentality.
Secondly, at a more strategic level, the culture of providing property services must keep up with business and market requirements and opportunities. The property services operating model must capable of innovating and evolving to match ever-changing demand which will mean accepting change as a natural way of working  rather than occasional step changes in structure and performance. The organisational model must be capable of looking at change as an opportunity rather than a threat.
In the next phase work will need to be carried out to understand the differences between current culture and values and the culture that will be needed to support the new processes and organisation. Once these differences are clear ways will need to be devised to introduce the values and culture with the more obvious changes.
