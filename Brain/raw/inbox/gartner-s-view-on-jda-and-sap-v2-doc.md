Vendor Rating: JDA Software Group
Oct 23rd 2003
Answers to Retailers' Top 10 Questions on 'SAP for Retail'
Sept 23rd 2003
JDA’s portfolio is a patchwork of different technologies, which can make it hard to implement and support.

Companies that are willing to buy more than one application from the JDA Portfolio should evaluate more than individual product functionality. Otherwise, the cost of maintaining disparate platforms could make advanced functionality less relevant and lead to failures in deployment.

JDA proved itself to be a visionary by anticipating the need to provide merchandise management, optimization and collaboration solutions globally through a broad retail and distribution suite.

JDA claims the broadest product suite in the retail market. It also maintains that every product in its portfolio can be implemented as a stand-alone solution.  This strategy meets customer needs nicely when they are based on application-by-application implementations, but it does not provide the lowest total cost of ownership for enterprisewide implementations or when enterprises are trying to implement a broader suite of solutions.

JDA has been reluctant to work with implementation partners, preferring to do most of the deployment work internally.

Supply chain execution is an area where JDA lags behind Retek and SAP. We do not believe that JDA has sufficient understanding of the growing needs of retailers to integrate planning and execution processes tightly.

JDA has some of the most recognized products in each of the stand-alone application areas.

Integration between different pieces of the solution is still one of the major concerns for users and prospects and it is often unclear what functionality sits with what product.

There is no evidence that JDA has had to lower its prices when competing against SAP in retail, which shows that users recognize and value JDA’s expertise more highly than pure product integration.
After difficulties with earlier versions of its merchandising management solution, SAP is now developing a robust, integrated retail solution. But, it is not yet ready to cover all functional domains, countries or retail sub-segments.

Retail is considered a very strategic vertical for SAP. Its executive board has dedicated more attention and funding to promote aggressive delivery of new retail functionality during the next 18 months.

SAP will be the most prevalent software vendor for worldwide retail applications by 2006 (0.7 probability).

SAP’s centralized organization makes it less agile than pureplay vendors. Its culture is more "SAP-centric" than "customercentric."

SAP has found it difficult to compete for two reasons:
The first versions of SAP's retail solutions had no merchandise management functionality.
Pureplay vendors had a deeper understanding of business requirements.

SAP has an opportunity to be a one-stop-shop. But it faces challenges:
Its centralized organization makes it less agile than pureplay vendors.
SAP has great vision, but needs to deliver its solutions rapidly to its key retail sub-segments.
SAP has the potential to deliver a collaborative, trade funds and promotions management offering, but this will require concerted effort to align its customer relationship management and SCM offerings with SAP for Retail.
Point-of-sale systems and collaborative planning, forecasting and replenishment (CPFR) are other areas where SAP is weak.

It has the broadest integrated retail solution.  Its solution focuses on SCM (planning and execution) and SAP plans to deliver more advanced capabilities by 2005.

Integrated solutions can lower their total cost of ownership significantly, as long as the applications are suitable for their businesses.  It is still too early to tell if SAP for Retail is less expensive than best-of-breed deployments.

In retail, SAP has good vision and the potential to execute well. When SAP for Retail is fully delivered, it will be ahead of the competition in terms of integration and functionality, because other players have not invested as heavily as SAP.

Retailers that want to adopt SAP for Retail for all their core processes should proceed only if they are early adopters and are willing to accept the risks associated with joint development.  Unless they wish to be early adopters, prospects should carefully monitor what functionality is already available for their retail subsegment.
