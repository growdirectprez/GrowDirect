# Note to the RACI tables ( Key –R(m) responsible manage, R(d) responsible deliver, A- accountable, C- consult, I -inform
The exercise of populating the RACI tables has concentrated on assigning accountability and responsibility for activities to maintain clarity. For now consult and inform have only been used in the tables for minor projects and facilities management where it was felt important to indicate how end users would be brought into the process through consultation and information.
Responsibility has been broken down into two categories, R(m) shows who will be managing the work, R(d) shows who will be undertaking the work, (m,d) indicates where an organisation will be both managing and delivering.
Responsibilities may also be broken down between those responsible for operational activities and those responsible for property, building services or FM delivery. This is indicated in the table.
Accountability – where the tables indicate that more than one organisation could be accountable for an activity this indicates that the accountability would be allocated to different organisations depending on the size or sensitivity of the output I.e making use of levels of delegation. Levels of delegation have not been defined

# Define Business Requirements - Notes
This is the first and most important process, without which other parts of the process cannot properly meet business need.
The accountability for the majority of this process lies with the informed client in each Division.
The informed client will collect requirements from through-out the Division, requirements would range from new stores, new fit-outs eg lingerie roll-out,or the need for changes to cleaning routines. Requirements would be collected using regular meetings. The informed client would also be expected to lead the innovation process on behalf of the Division to develop fresh property solutions to business need eg increased requirement for selling space met through new selling formats, or fit-out with greater capacity for stock.
The informed client would challenge the value of requirements to the business to ensure that expenditure on property, building services and FM yields the most value for the business
The Central Property Services unit would be expected to bring innovative ideas to the informed clients on ways to better meet the needs of the business, this would include any asset development initiatives eg selling or developing parts of sites to provide extra income for JLP in addition to operational income.
The Central Property Services Unit would provide ideas and information to help the  informed clients make decisions on accepting or rejecting requirements.
The results of this process would be fed into processes to produce policy, to procure services or to put together programmes of work as part of the business planning process.

# Create an operating strategy - Notes
 The operating strategy is a high level document outlining the major outputs of the end to end process and describing any activities that would be undertaken to improve the outputs of the end to end process eg
changes to the processes,
changes to organisations eg more or less outsourcing,
improvements in data quality eg installation of a new information system.

The operating strategy would be underpinned by key performance indicators which would be supported by performance measures for parts of the process, organisations and individuals
The operating strategy would act as guidance for all organisations with property, building services and FM accountabilities and responsibilities. It would probably be updated annually as part of the business planning process, although changes might be needed within year in response to new requirements or poor performance
Since the delivery of property, building services and FM would be cross Division and would involve more than one organisation the accountability for the majority of this process would reside with the JLP Corporate Centre, who would define the scope of services covered by the strategy
Both the Informed Clients (DS and Waitrose) and the Central Property Services Unit would contribute to the development of the strategy, although overall production would be managed by the Central Property Services Unit

# Developing and maintaining programmes of work - Notes
 The process divides into two, the first part of the process covers putting the programmes of work together as part of the annual planning process to inform Divisional business plans. The second part of the process covers any updates to the plans which will occur through-out the year as circumstances change.
The programmes of work will cover 2-3 years and will therefore contain very high level indications of activity or expenditure as well as detailed activities and costs eg for projects already underway or planned maintenance for the year.
The programmes of work will be rolling programmes, which once put together will be updated yearly rather than completely changed.
The programmes of work will be put together by the Central property unit in response to a list of requirements put together by the Informed Clients. The Central property Unit will produce plans for the acquisition of new sites as well as plans for each existing property. The plans for each property will enable the Central Property Unit to look at the impact of a variety of requirements on a particular building eg making sure that cleaning regimes and budgets for cleaning are adjusted for buildings where build projects are to be undertaken, or that the requirements for minor spend are carefully evaluated against longer term needs for total refurbishment.
Once requirements for each building are understood the Central Property Unit will put all requirements together into programmes of work, major projects, minor projects, property management, and FM. The programmes will look at total activity for both Divisions to assess the potential for economies of scale from procurement. Budgeted programmes of work will be developed  in consultation with informed clients.
Once the programmes of work have been agreed by the informed clients, Divisional Boards will be accountable for approving them through the business planning process.

# Create a property policy and standards
Business requirements may be for policies and standards rather than projects or delivery of services. Policies and standards for property, building services and FM can cover a wide range of requirements, for example:
Policy on ways of working to ensure consistency and spread best practice
Fit –out or service standards to ensure consistent value for money
Policy on funding property
Policies and standards may be particular to a Division or be applicable to John Lewis Partnership as a whole.Where policies are applicable to a Division either the informed client or the Divisional Board will be accountable for it. Where the policy is cross Division the JLP Corporate centre or a representative of the centre will be accountable for it.
The Central Property Unit will be responsible for producing policy and standards to meet the requirements of the Divisions or Corporate centre. The Central Property Services Unit may either produce policy and standards itself or manage consultants to produce policy
An important part of the policy process is deployment of the policy without which the policy process is of little use. The Informed clients will be accountable for deployment of policy and also the  monitoring of the policy. The responsibility for collecting data on the benefits of the policy will be with the informed client or the central property services unit depending upon the type of policy or standard. Where measurement is cross organisation the Central Property Services Unit will be responsible for managing the collection of data and reporting.

# Procure call-off contracts
 Call –off contracts are an efficient way of procuring services, once in place services such as, for example, rent reviews can be requested from the consultant without having to re-tender a contract. Call-off contracts can be put in place for a wide range of services from major projects to cleaning.
The production of programmes of work should allow JLP to negotiate better terms in the full knowledge of likely requirements either for one Division or where possible for both Divisions. The overall management of procurement for property, building services and FM should also enable JLP to take advantage (where appropriate) of suppliers who provide a combination of services.
 Once programmes of work have been agreed and funded by the Divisions the accountability and responsibility for procurement for all services for JLP will lie with the Central Property Services Unit. The Central Property Services Unit will be responsible for obtaining the best procurement routes to meet the agreed needs of the business.
Occasionally JLP Corporate Centre approval may be required for particularly large or sensitive contractual commitments.

# Acquire and Build Major Projects
 This process covers a wide range of projects from store acquisition to refurbishment of single departments. (A monetary definition may be required as there is a separate process for minor projects with less steps and a different allocation of responsibilities).
The process will be started within the programming process, either the project will have been entered in the programme to start at a specific point, or an opportunity may arise, for example the offer of a new site, which will mean accelerating a project. Where some time has elapsed between inclusion of the project in the programme and the start of the project then the business requirements for the project should be revisited by the informed client to confirm that the project is still required. Occasionally a project may arise through an ad hoc  opportunity not previously considered, in this case it will need to be taken through the definition of business requirements process( slowly or quickly) and put into the programme (taking out or delaying other projects if necessary).
The informed clients are accountable for major projects, and are responsible for their overall management from feasibility through to successful operation, they are also responsible for the overall relationship between JLP and the Developer.
 The informed clients may also be responsible for carrying out particular project activities eg appointing research consultants.
The Central Property Services Unit will be responsible for carrying out a range of property, building services and FM activities through-out the project. The table on the following page shows the range of activities that the informed client should be responsible for at particular stages of a project and those that the Central Property Services Unit should be responsible for, remembering that the Informed Client has overall accountabiliy for the project and manages the input of all functions working on the project including the Central Property Services unit.

# Asset development where no impact upon operations
 The asset development process covers the activities which add value to the business through exploiting the property asset. This can be through physical development of a site eg selling part of a site for development, or through non-physical activities such as raising finance through sale of freeholds.
 Asset development opportunities put forward by the Central Property Services Unit will be considered by the Informed clients as business requirements and projects to realise value will be put into the programmes of work in the same way as operational projects. In certain circumstances unplanned opportunities will arise these however must still be approved by informed clients before starting this process.
Depending upon the type and size of the opportunity the Informed client,  Divisional Board  or JLP Corporate centre will be accountable for approving the feasibility stage  and business case for these projects.
The Central Property Services Unit will be accountable and responsible for management and delivery of the project once this approval has been given, using external suppliers as required.
Asset development projects which have an impact upon operations will be managed according to the acquisition and build major projects process where Informed Clients will be accountable and responsible for project management.

# Deliver Facilities Management (unplanned)
This process covers unplanned requirements for small works, maintenance and cleaning that are received by the Maintenance Management Ltd’s help desk. The process shows two flows. The first flow shows requirements that are actioned immediately by the help desk without the need for approval, the second flow shows how requests are dealt with if they require approval
The help desk will receive all unplanned requirements, how the help desk responds to these requirements should be governed by policy. Most requirements will be dealt with through the first flow and the help desk will mobilise contractors on receipt of the call. There will however be some work which should require further approval, either:
 because it might have legal/construction implications (eg health and safety – example of the Milton Keynes wall); or
because it is a new requirement and its value to the business should be assessed;
 or the work is potentially of high value and may require adjustments to other programmes.
Requirements that need further approval should either be passed by the help desk to the Central Property Services Unit, if they have legal or construction implications, or to the informed client for approval.
To make a decision to go ahead with some requirements may require joint working with the informed client and the Central Property Services Unit to assess costs and the impact on other programmed work.

# Measure performance
This process gathers high level data on the performance of all parts of the end to end process against measures specified in the operational strategy. In practice the success of parts of the process will be measured continually for example through project reviews and contract management. This data however should be aggregated for regular quarterly review to monitor the strategy and to be used to trigger improvements.
The JLP Corporate Centre is accountable for setting the scope of the measures as the measures will be across different organisations taking part in the end to end process.
Informed clients will be accountable for the majority of the process and will be responsible for collecting data from the Divisions, for example data from end user surveys.
The Central Property Services Unit will be responsible for collecting data (eg from contract management) and for managing the process of pulling together all the measures and their analysis for review
