General Ledger (G/L) Policy/Process
Best Practice Option
Best practice for G/L updating focuses on streamlining the process, increasing accuracy, and creating a proactive finance and accounting.  Analysis reveals the following best practices for the G/L process:

Streamlining
Use one general ledger
Use global standardized chart of accounts
Periodic review of chart of accounts to reduce redundant account codes
Record reversals only at year-end
Establish the goal of completing every transaction within 48 hours of the event it represents
Perform all GAAP adjustments and currency conversions at the field locations where the transactions originate

Increasing Accuracy
Charge data providers a fee for correcting erroneous data to minimize edit time
Set an accuracy threshold to eliminate over-analyzing of subsystem data
Hold departments accountable for major errors such as failure to notify accounting of payroll changes
Emphasize the first-time quality of journal entries by ensuring that all required data is available for the entry to be made correctly

Proactive Finance and Accounting
Focus on analysis versus transaction processing
Liaison teams linking business and accounting
Continually challenge accounting policies for materiality

Key performance indicators and benchmarks include the following:

Performance Measures/Benchmarks		Best Practice

Percent of G/L time spent on corrections                                                                         1.31% (average)


Examples of organizations who have utilized best practices for closing:

A U.S. government department requires all U.S. departments and agencies to maintain a standard general ledger and chart of accounts and standardization of core financial accounting systems (including the general ledger, accounts receivable, and accounts payable).

A large multinational consumer goods manufacturer consolidated 5 general ledgers into one and to reduce their closing cycle from 2 weeks to 2 days.  In addition, they redeveloped financial reports to include both financial and non-financial data, sharply reduce the number of errors, and developed 60 standardized reports in place of the over 6,000 user written reports.

General Ledger (G/L) technology
Best Practice Option
Best practice for G/L updating focuses on improving access to real time information, streamlining the updating process, and improving maintenance.  Analysis reveals the following best practices for G/L technology:

Improving Access to Real-time Information
Provide on-line, real time data access
Perform on-line or daily updating of the G/L for remote locations

Streamline Updating Process
Standardized accounting system within the organization
Integrate the G/L and inventory systems to facilitate reporting permit seamless transactions without manual entries
Minimal effort in modification of chart of accounts
Electronic approval of journal entries
Automatic reversal of journal entries
Upload automated databases in journal entry form to eliminate re-keying
On-line interface from supporting systems
Automatic account reconciliation
Automatic posting of accruals
Continuous or single day closing of accounts
Journal entry data is staged throughout month to avoid bottlenecks in the IT system

Improving Maintenance
Enterprise-wide financial information data warehouse
Supports complex allocation methods for indirect costs and overhead based on volume, hours, prior year’s history or percentages.  Allocation is performed independently of the accounting data base leaving original balances intact
Track manual journal entries as a measure of quality for the G/L process

An international oil company implemented regular reviews of the Chart of Accounts (COA) to reduce redundant costs, standardized the COA, centralized control functions, made input to the G/L at the source rather than through centralized data entry, and changed emphasis from corrective entries and manual journals to up-front controls. Closing Policy/Process

Best Practice Option
Best practices for the closing process focuses on streamlining and shortening the closing process, eliminating re-keying or correcting data, and proactively identifying any issues that might slow the process.  Analysis reveals the following best practices for the closing process:

Streamline and Shorten Closing Process
Implement a soft close for non-quarter months that focuses only on key performance indicators and uses large materiality tolerances
Missing data does not stop closing process
Set an accuracy threshold to eliminate over-analyzing of subsystem data
Edit information in source systems, not the G/L
Schedule each accountant’s time and tasks during the closing cycle to ensure only the critical tasks are worked on during closing
Cross-train employees to assist in closing process
Do not accrue unpaid invoices at month-end; amounts are not material, and it simplifies the closing process

Eliminating Re-Keying or Correcting Data
Charge data providers a fee for correcting erroneous data to minimize edit time
Enforce a standard list of monthly close dates for all units

Proactively identifying issues
Establish a “pre-close” meeting to discuss any unusual result
Conduct formal post-mortems after each monthly close to document lessons learned and improvement opportunities

Key performance indicators and benchmarks include the following:

Performance Measures/Benchmarks		Best Practice

Percent of G/L time spent on corrections		1%
Cycle time to close G/L	                                                                                         	3

91% of companies close monthly; 9 % of companies close quarterly

Average number of days to close the monthly books among companies that have undertaken some financial reengineering project: 6.8 days

Examples of organizations who have utilized best practices for closing:

A large entertainment company began a firm wide initiative to reengineer quarterly closing, including moving toward soft monthly close, which ultimately resulted in cycle time being reduced from 23-28 days to 13 calendar days and annual closing hours being reduced by 55%.

A large energy company closes its monthly books in 2 days and has a 5 day year-end close, with the annual report released the following Monday. By shifting to a soft close, the closing process in business units has been simplified.  In addition, no extra resources are allocated at the end of the month to finish up near completed orders before the month‑end just for the sake of getting it on the books (although it is expected that this practice still occurs at the quarter‑ends).   In addition, analysts retrieve their own reports and no repetitive data entry occurs.

A large multi-national consumer goods manufacturer has restructured its Financial Management function, including eliminating monthly closing, which resulted in reducing closing from 26 to 7 days.
