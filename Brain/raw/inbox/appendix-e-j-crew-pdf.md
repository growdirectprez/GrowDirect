J. Crew Enhances
Business Agility with ERP Solution

mySAP Retail solutions save retailer time and money by eliminating
redundant business processing and providing consistent data

Business agility is the new buzzword.

Corporations aspire to it and indus-
try analysts have now added it to the
cache of benchmarks they use to eval-

uate business success.

In retailing, an agile company is one that
is flexible enough to respond swiftly to the
dynamics of the market, nimble at capital-
izing  on  opportunities  as  they  arise  and
fixed on streamlining business processes. J.
Crew’s  CIO,  Paul  Fusco,  reports  that  the
New York-based  specialty  retailer  fits  the
bill.

Since  1998,  J.  Crew  Group,  the  multi-
channel apparel company that evolved from
direct  mail  origins,  has  been  transforming
its  software  applications,  migrating  from
home-grown  custom  applications  toward
mySAP  Retail  solutions.  Fusco  maintains
that one of the key benefits of switching to
an enterprise resource planning (ERP) solu-
tion is enhanced business agility.

“When  you  have  one  system  with  very
few interfaces into legacy solutions, it’s pos-
sible to take requests and turn them around
quickly.  Just  recently,  we  fielded  a  request
that would have taken three or four months
to complete, prior to SAP. Now, we’re able
to handle requests in a matter of weeks, or
sooner,” says Fusco.

“Using SAP puts rigor into the business.
In  retailing,  there  are  a  lot  of  people  who
say  they  don’t  feel  like  they  own  the  data.
SAP gives you a structured, rigid approach
to data and, in doing so, provides the user
with  greater  visibility.  How  you  enter  data
and how you use it make you more agile,”
he adds.

Another fundamental benefit of J. Crew’s
mySAP implementation has been the abili-
ty to access consistent data across all of the
company’s  nearly  200  stores  and  factory
outlets.  By  integrating  information  and
data across the supply chain, Fusco feels the

company has achieved more effective inven-
tory  management  and  better  control  of
daily costs and operations.
THE ERP PATH J.  Crew  started  down  the
ERP  path  with  a  measured  rollout  of
mySAP Financials. After closing the books
on  1999  using  the  SAP  financial  systems,
the  company  added  the  mySAP  Human
Resources  module.  Six  months  later,  J.
Crew  began  utilizing  SAP  for  inventory
management  and  replenishment  and  as  an
integrated system to help manage data and
incorporate  information  with  legacy  soft-
ware  systems.  Last  year,  J.  Crew  added
another  building  block,  completing  an
implementation  of  mySAP  Business
Intelligence.

While  the  ERP  approach  has  shifted  in
and out of favor – and back again — with
retailers  and  industry  watchers  since  Fusco
first  committed  to  SAP  in  1998,  he  says
his  allegiance  hasn’t  wavered. While  he

concedes  that  implementing  an  ERP  solu-
tion  requires  patience  and  a  willingness  to
stay the course, he feels his persistence has
been rewarded.

“There’s no question that this is an expen-
sive  project,  but  we  have  achieved  a  solid
ROI,” says Fusco. “Having an integrated IT
system  that  provides  consistent  data
throughout  our  retail  infrastructure  has
allowed  us  to  collapse  a  lot  of  redundant
business  processing.  By  taking  processes
out, we’ve succeeded in saving money and –
equally important – saving time.”

Fusco, along with CIOs from companies
such  as  OfficeMax,  Jo-Ann  Fabrics  and
PETsMART,  is  among  those  retailers  who
have embraced an ERP solution from SAP,
whose retail development team is located in
Chicago. The company has made consider-
able strides in the retail industry of late as a
result  of  its  willingness  to  sell  the  various
mySAP  Retail  modules,  without  requiring
retailers to buy into the company’s software
technology with a heavy hand.

Hunter  Harris,  vice  president  of  mySAP
Retail at SAP America, says that if a retailer
only  needs  financials,  SAP  can  provide  it
out  of  the  box. The  same  can  be  said  for
SAP’s  CRM  solution  or  its  Supply  Chain
Event  Management  application.  Each  can
be purchased as a standalone application or
connected  to  any  retail  system  without
other SAP modules in place. Still, he stress-
es that increased business process improve-
ment and cost savings are achieved by lever-
aging core SAP technology.

“The  mySAP  Retail  solution  provides  a
way for retailers to license all the technolo-
gy they require and deploy it as needed. So,
for example, a retail company might deploy
the  merchandising  system  the  first  year,
Financials  the  next  and  Business  In-
telligence  the  following  year,”  explains
Harris. “Basically the retailer can roll it out
when  they’re  ready  without  having  to  re-
license the software. With a modularized set
of software tools that can be deployed over
time,  it  can  save  money  in  terms  of  the
total cost of ownership.”
CORE BUILDING BLOCKS The mySAP Retail
applications have come a long way since the
early days of ERP. Early emphasis on finan-
cials has broadened into numerous industry
applications  with  functionality  that  Harris
insists  rivals  that  of  well-known  best-of-
breed retail solutions.

At the heart of the mySAP Retail solution
is a merchandising system, including a per-
petual inventory application. The company
also has a replenishment engine, a planning
solution, a data warehouse for consolidated

analysis  and  a  ware-
house management sys-
tem. Together,  Harris
describes  these  tools  as
the  core  building
blocks.

“The idea that we try
to  convey  to  retailers  is
to  keep  the  best  soft-

ware applications and eliminate the worst,”
explains Harris. “By deploying several SAP
modules,  a  retailer  can  ultimately  bring
down  the  total  cost  of  ownership  because
they’re  lowering  the  integration  costs
between  the  modules. We  take  care  of  the
integration,” he adds, noting that there are
huge  financial  incentives  to  stay  with  SAP
once a company is on board.

SAP made headlines in June 2002 with the
rollout of an enhanced version of its retail
software, part of SAP R/3 Enterprise. The
new version features upgraded merchandise
management and planning functionality,
along with more tightly integrated planning
processes within the inventory management
module. Using the tool gives retailers the
ability to integrate style and color planning
with other business indicators when deciding
on a mix of products to market.

“This  enhancement  helps  retailers  to
improve  the  quality  of  their  data  and  to
realize  higher  levels  of  operational  effi-
ciencies,” explains Harris. “There are a lot
of heterogeneous IT infrastructures in the
retail  industry. The  mySAP  technology
ties  together  information  and  processes
across  technical  and  organizational
boundaries.”

By integrating
data across
the supply
chain, J. Crew
has achieved
better control
of costs and
operations.

In  August,  SAP  announced  that  its  SAP
Advanced  Planner  &  Optimizer  (SAP
APO) had successfully completed the final
phase  for  Collaborative  Planning,  Fore-
casting  and  Replenishment  (CPFR)
Interoperability Compliance Testing, spon-
sored by the Uniform Code Council. That
means that other SAP solutions incorporat-
ing  SAP  APO,  including  the  supply  chain
management tool, also conform to industry
standards  for  interoperability.  By  providing
total  supply  chain  visibility  and  collabora-
tion,  industry  experts  feel  that  SAP’s  ERP
gains a slight edge over the competition.

From  where  Harris  sits,  SAP’s  competi-
tive  edge  is  the  byproduct  of  ceaseless
research  and  development  efforts.  “This
year  we  will  spend  $1  billion  in  R&D,
with  SAP  Retail  representing  a  portion  of
that. The  level  of  investment  is  a  big  part
of the value of this brand,” he insists. “Our
business  plan  is  based  on  delivering
improved  functionality  that  helps  retailers
to master challenges.”
BUSINESS BENEFITS At J. Crew, Fusco says
the decision to deploy mySAP sprang from
the need to pull together information from
the various channels of business.

“As the company expanded from the cata-
log  business  into  retail  stores  and  e-com-
merce, bolting together all the home-grown
systems was becoming more and more of a
problem,” he explains. “We wanted all the
business processes to be under one IT system,
rather than siloed by business channel, which
is how we were organized a few years back.
We felt an ERP was the best way to go.”

One of the business benefits the company
has  realized  is  improved  purchase  order
management.  Before  SAP,  the  retailer  used
a  different  set  of  purchase  orders  for  the
bricks-and-mortar  portion  of  the  business
than were used for the direct channel, even
though the vendors who were being issued

the purchase orders and the products being
purchased were the same.

“Because of the way J. Crew grew its busi-
ness, the processes were different,” explains
Fusco,  whose  experience  parallels  many
retail  companies.  “Using  SAP,  we’ve  been
able  to  collapse  the  purchase  orders  into
one,  simplifying  the  process  and  yielding
greater  efficiency  along  the  way.  It’s  the
kind of thing that sounds easy, but it’s really
a very complex undertaking.”

Changes  in  purchase  order  management
have  also  helped  J.  Crew  to  adapt  its  pur-
chasing  cycle.  By  using  the  same  purchase
order for the retail and direct channels and
linking the purchase order module into the
company’s website, J. Crew has been able to

take two to three weeks out of its purchas-
ing cycle, says Fusco.

Using  the  inventory  management  and
reporting tools deployed thus far, the com-
pany’s buyers now have better visibility into
what’s happening at the store level than they
had in the past. Sales and inventory receipts
from every store are sent to J. Crew’s corpo-
rate  headquarters  every  night,  providing  a
precise  account  of  what  inventory  came
through the door and what was sold.

“It’s  so  important  when  the  buyers  and
merchandisers arrive each morning for them
to  have  a  complete  view  of  what  sold  the
previous day,” Fusco explains. “Today, we’re
polling  all  the  stores  nightly  and  delivering
results that reflect reporting by 98 percent of

our  stores  by  7  a.m.  the  next  morning.
Having a better view of yesterday helps our
merchants  to  make  more  informed  deci-
sions.” Previously it took two or three days
before full results from all stores were com-
piled,  which  left  merchants  flying  blind
when it came to daily decision-making.

While  most  of  the  business  benefits  are
on  the  back  end  of  the  business,  mySAP
has also positively influenced the front end
of J. Crew’s business. Using SAP, the com-
pany is able to update and download prices
to  the  stores  nightly.  “Every  store  in  the
chain  gets  the  pricing  updates  so  there  are
no  incongruities,”  says  Fusco,  who  feels
that  pricing  inaccuracies  undermine  a
retailer’s objectives.

Right  now,  Fusco  and  his  IT  team  are
working on adding color and size function-
ality (deployed as part of the SAP R/3E) to
J.  Crew’s  inventory  management  tools.
Work  is  also  ongoing  to
collapse  the  inventory
management  of  the  retail
stores and the direct chan-
nel,  a  project  that  Fusco
hopes  to  complete  within
a few months.

J. Crew’s allegiance to
the ERP approach hasn’t
wavered since commit-
ting to SAP in 1998.

Looking ahead, Fusco says he has a three-
year  plan  with  SAP.  His  short-term  vision
includes  adding  the  retail  warehouse  man-
agement system (WMS) and expanding the
human  resources  functionality  to  include
time  and  attendance  in  the  store  environ-
ment.  Sometime  in  2004  or  2005,  he
expects to add the store management mod-
ule to the mix.

“Our strategy is very simple,” says Fusco.
“We plan to stick with SAP until it’s ruled
out.” — Susan Reda
