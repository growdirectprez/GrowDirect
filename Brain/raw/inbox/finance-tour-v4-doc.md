Introduction


Hello, welcome to T&K, I am the Finance Director and would like to walk you through the significant improvements we have delivered in the Finance Department over the last two years through the implementation of SAP and re-engineering of appropriate processes.

Before these improvements our financial systems were inefficient and difficult to use, consequently, only the Finance Department had access to them and increasing effort was required by us to maintain service levels to the organisation as we required work arounds to compensate for poor systems.

Even when budget holders could get the information they needed it was never on time due to the admin of routing information requests through finance, and the effort required by us to write and run ad hoc reports.  These frustrations in the organisation caused some departments to keep their own records, which in turn lead to mixed messages and conflict over who’s books were right.

Furthermore, the department’s moral and profile in the organisation was low, as people’s only experience of us was frustrating either through information requests or when we enforced policy to restrict their actions.


Two years ago we implemented SAP and re-engineered the business to give us a lean, efficient and focused department, which has:

Reduced the people required for routine activity relating to compliance, such as VAT returns and statistical reports, and transactions processing, such as vendor invoice verification (see below).
Simplified and improved internal controls over key areas such as, budgetary control at commitment level, authorisations, rebate management (see below), and inventory accounting (see below).
Reduced the people required for our core tasks such as month-end closing (see below).
Allowed us to focus greater effort analysing results to improved management’s understanding of the organisation and drivers behind costs. (See financial and management reporting below).

Furthermore, the organisation now feels empowered as all budget holders have on-line access to the system and the information they need to monitor and control their budgets.  As a result, there is now one set of books that everyone uses and our relationship with the organisation has improved, as we are no longer a source of frustration associated with information requests.   In addition, our role as policeman has declined as the system now enforces policies on authorisations and budget over-runs.

We have also been able to simplify management control through functionality introduced by the new system.  For example we are now using “projects” within SAP to monitor and control the cost of opening new stores.  The project is split into a Work Breakdown Structure (WBS) based on budgetary control, which usually equates to a work discipline such as promotions, maintenance, security etc. In addition, Networks can be used within the project to schedule tasks and milestones.  This allows the project manager to closely monitor progress and even have a phased release of budget funds within the project.

All this has had a significant effect on the Finance Department’s costs, which are principally headcount driven. However, this has not had a negative impact on us, as the work profile of the group is less on routine work and more on analysis and value adding which has meant our job becoming more interesting and satisfying.







1.	VENDOR INVOICE VERIFICATION (including ERS – self billing)
The system has freed us from a lot of routine tasks relating to transaction processing and compliance, for example, now that we have an integrated system we no longer need to reconcile and maintain interfaces for sub-ledgers relating to debtors; creditors; inventory and fixed assets.

However, the main efficiency in this area has been the saving in resources required for invoice processing.  This has been achieved through automatic checking performed by the system which now matches vendor/contractor invoices to:
Purchase Orders (PO), to ensure the rate charged is correct.
Goods Receipt Note (GRN), to ensure payment is only made for goods/services received.

Where invoice details match the PO’s and GRN’s then payment is automatically scheduled to take place on the payment run which optimises the payment terms of the vendor/contractor as noted in the system master data.  This ensures the optimal use of funds.

FAILED MATCHING
Where the invoice is outside the tolerances set in the system for matching to PO and GRN then payment is blocked and a procedure to check invoice status is carried out

SELF-BILLING
New functionality includes the ability to use self-billing based on the GRN’s generated within T&K. Self- billing negates the need to do any matching as the value paid is based upon the GRN, this saves cost by automating the process and placing the onus of reconciliation on the supplier. The need to investigate invoices will also be reduced to exceptions only (i.e. no match).

3.	INVENTORY ACCOUNTING (including shrinkage)
Shrinkage is an issue at T & K as with any retail organisation. With our old system there was poor analysis of shrinkage as the inventory sub-ledger was separate from the financial books.  As a result, shrinkage was only calculated with any accuracy at store level or possibly department level within store.   However, there was no breakdown of how the shrinkage was made up, i.e. theft, damage, spillage, obsolescence, or by item or supplier etc.

 4.  MONTH-END CLOSING ROUTINE
We found that period end closing was a moving target. The finance department was tied up for two weeks each month, this left little to no time available for any performance analysis or indeed future strategic business modelling to enhance the value provided by the data gathered within the financial systems. T & K had to find a way to reduce this time frame, retail is a fast moving business and we had to spend more time understanding what our business was doing and where we were going.

5.	FINANCIAL AND MANAGEMENT REPORTING
With the old system, standard reports were huge and took too long to issue due to reworking information on spreadsheets, and ad hoc reports took even longer as they had to be specified in the organisation and then routed to Finance where they would be written and then run.

 The result was information overload with too many big reports and management found it difficult to clearly identify important issues requiring corrective action.  Where such issues could be identified the corrective action was usually too late.

With SAP we decided to use a number of standard reports in the system, and custom built further ones to show key information required by store, region and head office management.  By using exception reports management are no longer tied up wading through huge printouts but can identify relevant issues quickly.  Furthermore, budget holders can run their own reports and use drill down functionality to interrogate the system online to identify important issues as they occur and consequently take corrective action on a timely basis.

Through the integrated approach of this system, T & K are able to view a P & L at any point in the hierarchy. This may be from cost centre to total UK operations. From any of these views, T & K management is able to drill down through various data levels to show transaction data. This functionality removes the need for recompilation of information from outside the core system.
