C. Product/ Solution Functionality Description

The Solution
Evant offers the first multi-channel merchandise planning, demand forecasting and merchandise replenishment application designed to support retailers.  Evant’s comprehensive solution supports the complete range of merchandising-related processes including planning, replenishment, forecasting, merchandise transaction management and operational analytics.  Evant’s application is built with best-in-class Web-based technologies including Java and XML.   The Evant solution is based on open standards, delivered over the Internet through a web browser and follows a multi-tier architecture. Evant’s underlying architecture is scalable and built to handle the transactions volumes and number of users necessary to be successful in the retail environment.  


Enterprise Information Management (EIM)
Evant's Enterprise Information Management (EIM) tool provides a 'one-stop' location through which users can monitor and manage all product information critical to the daily operation of their business. Product information may be tracked across multiple business channels, including web, direct/catalog, point of sales and fulfillment systems. Users may customize information captured based on an unlimited number of user-defined product attributes, such as color, size, personalization, seasonality, or product dimensions.  The EIM application also provides a mass update capability, allowing for quick management of information on groups of products.


Merchandise Planning Module (MP)
The Merchandise Planning System offers powerful multi-channel inventory planning and forecasting capabilities that can help increase margins, improve turns, and increase sales. 

Evant’s software can plan and forecast sales and inventory requirements across multiple category levels, stores, catalogs, and time periods, all the way down to the SKU level. Forecasts can be developed using sales history, demand response rate curves or other projections.  When sales order and shipment information is available, forecasts can easily be revised, allowing merchants to take best advantage of unexpected hot sellers while minimizing exposure due to under performing products.

Evant provides a rich set of pre-defined templates to enable merchants and inventory planners to quickly implement a planning solution.  The templates offer the user flexibility in configuration by selecting from 5 best practice inventory calculations and planning by units, retail or cost. These best practices workflows quickly enable world class planning process to be integrated into an organization such as Lillian Vernon. Measure names, display values and symbols, as well as view orientation can also be configured based on user preferences.  The templates support tops down, bottoms up and middle out planning, as well as Pre Season and In Season planning processes fully integrated into a single plan view.  The template highlights are:

Demand Planning & Replenishment Module (DPR)
Evant’s Replenishment and Forecasting module is an advanced demand planning and inventory replenishment solution that addresses the complex challenges of managing demand variability, supply variability, customer service level expectations, and inventory requirements. The suite is designed to maximize business value by optimizing forecasting and replenishment processes at the distribution center and/or store level.   
Multiple Safety Stock Strategies to Manage Inventory and Service Goals The application offers four replenishment strategies for calculating safety-stock levels and meeting target service levels.  The first is a standard service percentage goal.  The next three use the power of optimization and simulation: 1) for a given safety stock investment, minimize the lost sales in dollars, 2) for a given safety stock investment, minimize the number of shortage occurrences, and 3) for a given safety stock investment, minimize the line “cuts” (unfilled items that keep an entire order from shipping).  With our system, service level targets and different safety stock strategies can be readily applied at the SKU (item/location) level, with the help of mass-maintenance tools.  You can use this to assign higher goals to critical or fast-moving products and lower goals to your slow-movers.  Once you choose your strategy and target, you can review the expected impact before actual implementation. This gives you the maximum return on your safety-stock investment—resulting in lower safety stock, higher inventory turns, and improved in-stock positions.  
Advanced Statistical Forecasting that Delivers Superior Results Evant’s robust and proven forecasting approach takes care of outliers, and accounts for trend, seasonality, and day-of-week factors. The methodologies used for demand cleansing, trend estimation, and seasonal profiling are proprietary and yield better forecasts than standard approaches. Evant’s approach is focused on three distinct areas:

Adaptive algorithms for updating forecasts. The application applies forecast error measure and adaptive/dynamic smoothing. Using a proprietary method for recalculating and adjusting the ‘alpha’ factor, our system applies heuristics built into the forecasting algorithm to update the forecast for the next period. The result is a more accurate forecast, less safety stock, reduced inventory investment, and greater buyer/analyst productivity due to fewer exceptions. 
Comprehensive demand management. The application’s forecasting capabilities address all types of SKUs including basic staple items, promotional demand, new products, slow movers, and short life cycle products. For example, Evant customer, DOT Foods uses our system to more accurately forecast its short-shelf life products (perishables such as dairy and yogurt)—resulting in a 25% reduction in product obsolescence. 
Advanced seasonal profiling.  Evant has developed an automated process, based on unique science, for determining seasonal profiles.  Highly efficient algorithms are used to cleanse historical demand data, extract measures of trend and seasonality, determine appropriate seasonal profiles for all SKUs, and establish an optimal cluster of core profiles.  The end result is a manageable, actionable set of core seasonal profiles that enhance the accuracy of short and long-term demand forecasts--reducing forecast error and resulting in lower inventory investment without a negative impact on in-stock performance.  

Superior Distribution Management that Gets the Right Product to the Right Place
The application provides extensive capabilities for transfer orders, factoring in all relevant financial factors (such as transportation costs, inventory costs, handling costs) before recommending a transfer order—so that transfers between locations are made only when economically advantageous.  Customers like O’Reilly Automotive, provide local stores with access to a much broader range of products on a same day delivery basis (for example, transferring from nearby stores with more product selection and stock, rather than from the distribution center).  Evant also provides unique allocation logic (e.g., for constrained supply), as well as extensive return recommendations capabilities.  

Grouping EOQs Leverages Vendor Discounts
The economic order quantity (EOQ) grouping leads to enhanced margins.  It provides item-based optimization and the subsequent grouping of EOQs together to meet vendor discount offers, leading to buy-side margin improvement.  The application enhances this concept by balancing ALL vendor items still to be ordered, not just those that are due to be ordered today, to achieve available discounts.

More Profitable Investment Buying 
The application offers superior, completely integrated replenishment and deal buying functionality, for forward buys, ASV buys, and speculative buying. Suggested orders address both normal replenishment requirements as well as deal buying opportunities, and are integrated into the buyer’s daily workflow. Our system also provides advanced investment and alternate source buying capabilities, including flexible controls to set objectives and add extra days of inventory at the SKU level versus only at the system level. For wholesale distributors across all industries as well as retailers engaged in deal-buying at the DC level, this functionality can become a significant competitive differentiator. 

Efficiently Managing Inventory for Complex Distribution Networks
The complexities in inventory management multiply when there are multiple echelons in a hierarchical structure.  The Replenishment and Forecasting multi-echelon capabilities ensure that forecasting, planning, and ordering occur at multiple levels in a synchronous fashion, providing optimal inventory and customer service levels at the enterprise level.  Without enterprise-level optimization, changes at one level result in magnified reactions at the next, generating a ”bullwhip” effect—by the time the “real information” reaches the top level, it is already too late and the result is overstocks or outages.  With our multi-echelon capabilities, changes in seasonal or promotion information at a lower echelon are automatically passed upward.  A Distribution Requirements Planning (DRP) model does not adequately address this problem as there is no linkage between echelon safety stocks, making inventory and service optimization impossible.

Transforming Data into Business Intelligence
Evant’s application provides extensive what-if analysis and simulation capabilities for replenishment planning, as well as unlimited SKU grouping capabilities for ordering and reporting, safety stock and service level targeting. All fields in the system are available for reporting, providing you with more accessible and actionable information for decision-making.  The system also provides very detailed inventory stratification/segmentation, with up to 27 data layers to differentiate between replenishment inventory, safety stock, promotional, forward buying and other levels of inventory at the SKU-level for each warehouse or store.  Furthermore, the suite provides enhanced graphical and historical analytical views, with powerful and highly flexible decision-making tools for determining which SKUs to keep in stock, identifying forecasting anomalies, resolving overstock situations or analyzing suppliers’ order-to-delivery performance.



Alerts Management
Managing change is important in the dynamic world of multi-channel retailing. To help companies cope with unpredictable events that might require immediate attention - and better monitor routine performance indicators - Evant's application suite is equipped to deliver automated business alerts, based on sets of user-defined exception criteria. Whenever information meets the criteria, the associated module will send users an alert notifying them of important events that can potentially impact business performance.   Alerts are delivered via Evant's Workbench, a personalized gateway to each module of the Evant solution. Designed as a fully customizable homepage, the Workbench enables user-defined access to a variety of tools, information and value-added E-services.  The advanced alerts capabilities form a natural business process workflow for users.  It turns each day into one of management by exception and proactive planning from one of traditional reaction.  This step-by-step approach to planning generates an environment focused on profitability for the business and not just feasibility of the plans.

Top-Line Business Strategy and Planning

Evant provides a data model inclusive of five star schema’s for decision support activities. Our selector tool functionality allows our client’s to monitor activity against 1500+ key performance measures.

Merchandising Plans, Goals, and Objectives

Merchandise plans can be created at any level of the merchandise hierarchy.  Our hierarchy is n-tiered, allowing for as many levels as required to run Lillian Vernon’s business.  The original or pre-season plan is locked and used to manage and track plan vs. actual performance, at the offer level.  Multiple offers can be planned within the same template, allowing for a full seasonal view.  When in-season, the forecast version of the plan is developed utilizing catalog % complete to original plan, reforcasting need based on the original budget.  As forecasts are modified, the new receipt and demand flow can be used to update purchase order quantities and timelines.

Merchandise and Assortment Planning

Assortment plans are developed by week by SKU leveraging over 380 key performanace measures.  Five versions of the plan are available inclusive of OP (original), FC (forecast), WI (what-if), PM (previous forecast), TY and LY (actuals).  As sales and transactional data post in-season, the users can develop and change forecasts based on actual to plan performance.  Plan rules are used to trigger alerts against these plans to monitor, plan vs. actual percent variance for sales, receipts, or inventory values.  Other rules may include Weeks of Supply or Gross Margin targets.  Each night, the plan rules are reviewed and triggered, allowing the user to react to only those areas where action is necessary.  Assortment to financial planning reconciliation takes place, where the user can identify which plan is in control.  The increase or decrease from assortment to financial is prorated to the other based on percent to total contribution of the existing plan.

Demand Forecasting – Inventory Planning and Optimization

Evant uses proprietary mathematics and science within our demand forecast and optimization engines.  A team of scientists continually improve our application to provide sophisticated forecasting and modeling techniques within the DPR module.  The economic order quantity (EOQ) grouping leads to enhanced margins.  It provides item-based optimization and the subsequent grouping of EOQs together to meet vendor discount offers, leading to buy-side margin improvement.  The application enhances this concept by balancing ALL vendor items still to be ordered, not just those that are due to be ordered today, to achieve available discounts.

Purchasing and Purchase Order Management

Evant offers a suggest purchase order quantity that can be transferred to legacy applications.  Assortment plans are used to generate promotional or planned demand within the DPR module that are later used to create suggested orders for identified product.  Deals and vendor discounts as well as product lead times can be accounted for in the purchase order process.  Product sample tracking and status is monitored within the EIM toolset.

Vendor Relationship Management

Evant does not currently support Vendor Relationship Management processing within our current or near term footprint.  Vendor Relationship Management tools and tracking processes are usually housed within a warehouse management application.  

Business Performance Reporting

Evant provides a data model covering catalog, web, plan, purchase, and sales & inventory flow data.  Over 1500 pre-defined measures are covered within the five star schema models for use in a decision support tool.  Lillian Vernon must decide on a decision support product and Evant will provide professional services to incorporate our data model into the selected tool (i.e. have experience with microstrategy and oracle express).

Product Information Database (Product Library)

Evant's Enterprise Information Management (EIM) tool provides a 'one-stop' location through which users can monitor and manage all product information critical to the daily operation of their business. Product information may be tracked across multiple business channels, including web, direct/catalog, point of sales and fulfillment systems. Evant’s Implementation Methodology Overview

There are three tightly integrated phases that make up the Evant implementation methodology.  These phases are referred to as Scope, Design/Develop, and Integrate/Implement.  An additional phase called Production Support covers ongoing operations such as implementation support and planning and executing upgrades.  An overview of each phase is provided below followed by details of activities that fall within or cross phases.

The following picture is an overview of the entire methodology:






























Scope Phase Overview
Scope is one of the most critical phases of the Evant’s implementation 
methodology.  A thoughtfully executed project scoping exercise will set a solid 
foundation for the team through implementation and subsequent support. Conversely a poorly executed project scoping effort will inevitably lead to conflicts, miscommunication and frustration for all team members, both client and Evant. 

Properly executing a project scope will mean working closely with the client's project management team to confirm and establish the project's approach, assumptions, and primary objectives. The core project team should identify the organization’s vision, strategy, direction, and its impact on the Evant implementation. The project scope must be defined in relation to the functional processes, Evant functionality, technical architecture, organization, interfaces, data conversion, and reports that are to be addressed during the project. Additionally, a gap analysis must be prepared to determine the variances between the legacy system and processes and the Evant Solution and related processes. As a result of this phase, an agreement should be reached with the client on the project requirements, gaps, deliverables, and work plan. A scoping document should be created to document progress, decisions, issues and next steps.


Design/Develop Overview
There is quite a bit of overlap between the design process and the development process. The process of design and development is often iterative; during the development process discoveries can be made that result in the need to quickly re-design. While ideally design should always precede development, optimum results often only occur as a result of iterative design and development. 


Integrate/Implement Overview
The key objective of this phase is to ready the Evant solution for production.  Activities include: system integration testing, completing cutover planning, executing conversion and cutover, and finally training delivery. 


Production Support Overview
Key activities in this ongoing support phase include post implementation support and plan/execution of upgrades. During this phase transition occurs from the implementation team to customer care and the client’s IT organization. 
Production Support Process:
A. Establish Technical Infrastructure
The Client Care team is responsible for provisioning a technical environment. Three environments will be created: implementation, production staging, and production. Assuming hardware and the appropriate software licenses have been procured and installed in advance, there should be little to do other than touch test application readiness for configuration. 

Within Technical Infrastructure activity, an overview of the environments and how they will be managed is provided. 

B. Detail Requirements & Identify System Gaps
During this activity the scoping team (inclusive of the core implementation team and internal Evant subject matter experts), will meet with key client users to look at current business processes. They will systematically review all processes that may impact the Evant implementation. The key objectives of this activity are to access the level of business fit, determine and communicate gaps to Evant, and prepare a scoping document that will include a work plan that presents how Evant will be delivered to the client. 

Prepare scoping document. At the completion of the scope phase, a scoping document should be prepared to document the decisions made. Typical agenda items include the following:
Project Overview – objective/benefits, scope, approach, timeline
Project Team Organization – organization structure, roles and responsibilities
Business Architecture – high level business process model, business architecture, application scope, high level solution summary
Technical Architecture – current technical architecture, proposed technical architecture, proposed interface design, data conversion strategy
Next Steps – Issues/key decisions, next steps

Gaps are defined as client system requirements not supported by the base Evant solution. Issues should be documented and prioritized for level of importance to guide efforts for resolution.  Having reviewed the gaps identified during the scoping phase, it is very important to communicate to Evant Product Management. Communicating gaps serves many purposes including: (a) alerting Product Management to demand for some new requirement/functionality, (b) provides project team with a sense of when gap may be included in base functionality, and (c) provides the opportunity to discuss optimal workarounds for the situation. It is preferable to present all gaps at once, but it is expected there will be times when there is a need to have multiple gap meetings if new gaps are found, or further clarification is needed.

Project management requires strong communication skills that are leveraged to manage client expectations and the delivery of every task for the project duration.  Key tasks are as follows:
Implement the Evant solution meeting time and budget expectations
Gather and document client requirements obtained through interviews, and client background research
Prepare professional, high quality client deliverables – Project Scoping Document, System Walk-thru Document, Training Materials, etc.
Communicate and manage work plan, tasks and schedules
Facilitate regular project status meetings that include direct and indirect project team members. 
Follow-through with clients to ensure timely resolution of client issues
Manage communication between client, project staff, product development, engineering,  and executive management.
Manage/oversee the implementation of the technical architecture, conversion and interfaces
Thoroughly test our Solution prior to go-live at client site 
Advocate the use and leverage of Evant’s implementation methodology. 
Develop and continually improve our methodology through learning from current client project that can be leveraged for future clients. E.g. Project Work plans, Target Business Processes, Project Scoping Document, Systems Walk-thru Document, Design Workbook, Training Materials, Issue Logs, Gap Logs, Interface and Conversion doc., etc.
Plan and execute cutover
Plan upgrade process (if needed). Communicate upgrade plans with Client Relationship Manager.
Develop solid understanding of interface design and construction, and Evant technical architecture.
Prioritize issues, enhancements, gaps, and other lessons learned from implementation with product management

D. Client Relationship Management
Like project management, client relationship management is a highly interactive communicative process. Key tasks are as follows:
Advocate client requirements with product management and product strategy
Track issues related to quality of product, performance, and future functionality
Manage contract addendums and change order procedures
Support the authoring and development of client satisfaction surveys
Actively encourage clients to participate in client satisfaction surveys

Work with Accounts Receivable on any issues surrounding a client account

E. Interface design and development
Design and development of interfaces is a very involved process and requires detailed accurate communication between functional resources and technical resources. The end goal is to build upgradeable interface programs to/from Evant and other auxiliary systems 

The following are a list of typical tasks that occur during interface development:
Code and review interface programs (following internal standards)
Develop unit test scripts
Conduct unit tests. Execute test scripts. Compare actual results with expected results
Document unit test results
Correct program errors based on unit test results
Complete program documentation
Develop technical documentation for client technical group

F. Conversion design and development
During this activity the data conversion strategy is defined, designed, developed, and tested. Data conversion is accomplished through manual and/or automated means and includes the creation of any data needed for new data records. It enables Evant to accurately reflect the current status of organizational data at the start of production. The term "data conversion" is used in a wider sense than the dictionary definition in that it is defined to include four separate elements: 
Purging/cleansing of existing data; 
Reconciliation/balancing of the old data and the old system/Evant; 
Creation of new/additional data; and 
Conversion of the old data to Evant to create master file data and opening balance data (where appropriate) for Evant. 

G. Application design and system prototyping (including reporting)
During this activity the application is setup and prototyped with users. As the prototype process is highly interactive, it is not unusual for new requirements to surface that require the application to be setup again. A system prototyping may also be referred to as conference room pilot (CRP). The end result of the application design and system prototyping process is the agreed configuration of the Evant solution as collectively envisioned by the user community. 

H. Training Strategy
It is important to document a training strategy well in advance of the go-live date. It could be brief, but it should address the following questions:
Who is going to be trained, and in what applications/modules?
When will training be scheduled?
At what location will training happen? Is there an Internet connection?
How formal/informal is appropriate?
What is the maximum class size?
What is the policy on users that miss a class? Do they schedule a makeover? Will they get system access before training?
Where are the users located? 
Will there be different levels of users? 
What sorts of skills will each user level have? 
Is resistance anticipated from any user level? 
What type of tests, if any should be employed in the training process?
What type of materials should be employed in the training process? Are they available?
 
I. Develop testing strategy and plans
Prior to system integration testing in the Integrate/Implement phase, a test strategy should be put together and test plans built. The following are a list of key activities that should be included in develop testing strategy and plans:
Prepare system test strategy document. The strategy should include the following:
System test scope – key functions of Evant that will be implemented and integrated with ancillary systems 
Key interfaces with other solutions. Identify scenarios
Volume considerations including network throughput
System test work plan
Test objectives for each process
Define test objectives. For each test objective, define the test conditions that should be tested. When all of an objective's test conditions are executed properly, the test objective should be satisfied. The types of test conditions that should be considered include valid and invalid data conditions, and system interface conditions.  
Outline system test work plan. Look at reusability of unit/system test data
Determine system test team and responsibilities. Most likely this will be the core project team.
Communicate with the client’s technical resources to plan availability of a dedicated and controlled system test environment. Most likely systems test will be done on the planned implementation environment (with a backup having been taken of the up-to-date application design) 
Develop system test plans. Using the test strategy as a basis, create test plans based upon client-specific scenarios, along with expected results. It may make sense to obtain some of the Evant QA team’s scripts – and leverage them to start the building of test plans. However the most important perspective the implementation team can bring to the test plans is how the client will use Evant. Referencing process maps built during Application Design and System Prototyping will also confirm that resources are being deployed most efficiently. 

J. Cutover Planning
This activity straddles the Design and Integrate phases of a project implementation. The project team should keep the Client Relationship Manager in the loop on this activity. Cutover Planning ensures the transition of the system to the Users is carried out in a controlled and secured manner. It will involve thinking about various options. For example, should the cutover be ‘big-bang’, phased by site, phased by module (e.g. (MP) Merchandise Planning,  (DPR) Demand Planning & Replenishment, or (EIM) Enterprise Information Management).   High-level planning activities required for cutover should be documented. This will include the development of the overall approach for the cutover planning process. It is not unusual for parts of the process to be already in progress. Pieces that are in progress should be inventoried as part of the plan. For example, training may have commenced earlier if there are large numbers of staff to be trained and at a number of different locations, or data conversion preparation may have started, as it is generally a long and difficult process and may have commenced earlier in the project.

K. Training Preparation
Training preparation ensures that the transition from a test environment to a production operations environment, and the rollout to the users, is completed in a controlled and methodical manner. The implementation team should plan to leverage any material Evant Education Services have created. The final deliverable from training development is user-training material. 
 K1. Develop Technical Documentation 
This task is necessary to successfully transition the ownership of the software from the project team to the Client Project Sponsors. The project team should ensure that the clients have adequate documentation on the new control environment. 

K2. User Procedures
User Procedures are created to guide functional and technical people in how to use, operate, or support the Evant solution. With customized training available on-line along with on-line help, functional user procedures are usually brief documents that act as quick “cheat sheets” to support the implementation learning process. 
Lastly, all user procedures should be put to the test through a ‘dry-run’ with the final user audience.  The best test is always to ask someone unfamiliar with the process to perform the tasks outlined in the procedures.  The team may revise the procedures, depending on the findings of the tests.

L. System integration testing
During system testing, the system test team combines all parts of the system and exercises them in varied combinations. Combinations include scenarios within Evant and between Evant and other systems. These combinations are built through incremental integration of system components, and verified through the use of test conditions and test data, which form the System Test Plan. This approach is used to ensure thorough testing of all likely business scenarios before live production.

Having prepared the testing strategy, and documented test plans, System integration testing will focus on execution. Execution will focus on normal functionality expected by the client and will also review delivery effectiveness through conducting a stress test which may lead to the need for some system tuning. 

M. Training Delivery
While having the Evant solution fully tested and ready to go is critical, no less critical is the key activity of training delivery. Prior to delivery, the proper training material has been created, logistics have been sorted, and trainees have been identified and scheduled. Clear effective communication is important during training delivery, but perhaps a surprising critical success factor may be successful change management. Why change management? It is expected our clients are not interested in investing in excessive resources to re-engineer their business, but at the same time are relying on the Evant solution to bring them embedded ‘best practices’. It is expected the client team will not be seeing or hearing most of the functionality in the Evant solution for the first time and training delivery can proceed without too many issues. 
N. Execute cutover
The objective of this activity is to ensure the transfer of Evant, from a test environment to a production environment, occurs in a controlled methodical and secure manner. Before cutover, the Project Manager should meet with the entire team to review for the final time the schedule, interdependences between tasks, people dependencies and any outstanding issues. The entire team should include the following: implementation consultants, client relationship manager, Evant and client technical resources, interface/conversion resources, and client team members/management as appropriate. All cutover tasks are executed during this timeframe as outlined on the detailed cutover timeline (prepared in cutover planning). As is practical, the Project Manager should mark tasks complete when they are finished successfully, and make it available, so that everyone (including client) can get an understanding of the progress being made.  Each step listed on the detailed cutover timeline should be executed, without exception, especially if, as recommended, the cutover has been ‘rehearsed’ (in the “mock cutover”). 
O. Plan and Execute Upgrades
Evant plans to offer production ready upgrades to the base product up to four times a year. The upgrade path will always be from the previous production version, i.e. client will not be able to do ‘double’ upgrades. Because of the rapid availability of new releases and there being no ‘double’ upgrade path, it will be very important to actively schedule upgrades. Hopefully, the upgrade process should be something that can be accomplished as part of weekend maintenance activities. However a series of communications must happen with the client to make them aware of the forthcoming upgrade and any implications it may mean. The following a preliminary list of tasks that need to be performed to manage the planning and execution of upgrades successfully:
Client Relationship Manager (CRM) communicates date of upgrade and key additional functionality to client.
CRM reads upgrade document and notes the following for communication to client users
Any Decision Support reports that may need to be rebuilt (including any custom calculations)
Any data that may be lost – e.g. saved searches, preferences
Any menu option or look-and-feel changes
CRM communicates and schedules additional training, or additional implementation (if needed) 
CRM communicates any change to interfaces necessitated as a result of the upgrade. Note, if this is the case, the upgrade may take longer than a weekend.

P. Implementation Support
After implementation, the core project team will need to begin relinquishing day-to-day control of the client project and transferring tasks and responsibilities to the client’s technical and user community. Ideally an Evant representative will visit the implementation site to help in the transition, but if this cannot happen, transition must happen remotely. 

Q. Upgrade Training
Specific training will need to be delivered on new functionality offered to client users. Leveraging upgrade training materials created by Education Services, it is hoped the training team will not have to make too many customizations and can deliver the ‘vanilla’ version. Implementation/upgrade consultants should review tasks within the training strategy, training development, user procedures, and training delivery activities outlined in this methodology to fine-tune the delivery of any upgrade training.
