BroadVision, Inc. One-to-One Application Suite
Datapro Summary
BroadVision has developed a suite of applications that let organizations
take advantage of the benefits the Internet offers. With its portfolio of
One-to-One Web applications it gives organizations the opportunity to build
businesses around relationships, which can exist within enterprises, between
employees, between enterprises and customers, or any combination of these.
Its applications provide not only the opportunity for businesses to engage
in electronic commerce, both business-to-business and business-to-consumer,
but also to offer a personalized gateway to corporate information for
employees and supply chain participants. Its One-to-One Commerce products,
consisting of Retail Commerce and Business Commerce, enable the creation of
personalized B2C and B2B environments. The One-to-One Financials application
is a self-service consumer banking application that lets financial
institutions conduct Internet transactions securely; and its One-to-One
Knowledge application lets groups of users organize information that is
personalized based on the users' roles, and then distribute it via Web
browsers. Its newest One-to-One Billing application accommodates the
delivery and receipt of bills online. As a credit to its product success,
the company has attained financial profitability.

Overview
BroadVision has developed a suite of applications that supports secure
personalized communications throughout the enterprise. It calls this
personalization one-to-one relationship management, and offers a portfolio
of applications that are targeted at different enterprise segments, and
which run on a single platform. The platform and applications are based on
an object-oriented architecture that lets businesses use the Internet as a
framework to conduct commerce and to collaborate with employees and supply
chain participants via the intranet and extranet. One-to-One Enterprise
System is the core platform on which all other applications run. Its
application suite consists of One-to-One Retail Commerce, One-to-One
Business Commerce, One-to-One Financials, One-to-One Billing, and One-to-One
Knowledge. These applications extend the capabilities of the One-to-One
Enterprise System, and focus on the needs of customers in specific
industries, by offering more specific one-to-one capabilities for extended
merchandising, retail financial services, bill payment, or knowledge
management. BroadVision also offers several tools to complement its
application suite: Command Center, Design Center, Publishing Center, and
Instant Publisher.

Analysis
With Release 4.1 of BroadVision's One-to-One applications, the company has
dramatically enhanced the functions for its Knowledge and Commerce
applications, including both Retail Commerce and Business Commerce. For its
One-to-One Knowledge software it improved its already good personalization
capabilities with the addition of what it calls Personalized Navigation. The
new feature collects content from a variety of sources and presents it to
users based on their profile, role, or interests. In addition to
personalizing the content, the new capabilities also personalize the user's
navigation experience via a series of messages and news items. In addition
to these new knowledge management features, BroadVision added enhanced
capabilities to its two commerce applications (Business Commerce and Retail
Commerce) to personalize high-transaction e-commerce sites. Its Business
Commerce application now includes more flexible payment options which
include a flexible configurable order flow that now can accommodate
different order flows. For example, it supports both credit card and cash on
delivery, each of which can be controlled within the application at run
time, and can have conditional behaviors within the order flow based on the
results of activities such as credit scoring or fraud detection. The new
release also added the opportunity for users to customize applications to
match their specific business processes through a JavaScripting process. It
also incorporated the capability to facilitate the creation and
personalization of extranet sites through use of a new administrator desktop
tool. The new tool lets merchants offer their contract customers special
pricing or merchandise offers.
BroadVision has implemented an n-tier architecture that separates
application, presentation, business, and data logic. The application logic
tier consists of a series of servers that provide basic system services. For
example, the One-to-One Enterprise component includes servers that manage
profiles, content and rules; and the One-to-One Commerce, Financial and
Knowledge applications include servers to manage order management, pricing,
payments, taxes, etc. The support of object-oriented design and the CORBA
standard lets the application logic be distributed across multiple logical
and physical processors. In the business layer of the architectural
framework, business managers and content professionals handle their specific
roles. For example, business managers define the business rules in order to
control the business behavior of the system, and the content professionals
manage the site's content including document publishing, workflow, and
approval. A third layer, the data tier, is the site for database and
external data sources, and is accessed by database accessors, which reside
on the application layer, and which can be either a BroadVision database or
an external database. The presentation tier manages interfaces to the Web
site via a series of templates that are dynamically rendered for each user.
BroadVision has endorsed component-based, object-oriented application code
based on the Common Object Request Broker Architecture (CORBA) standard. The
support for CORBA, which is the foundation of BroadVision's applications,
ensures compatibility with other CORBA technologies, most notably Java, and
also enables its n-tier architectural design. It also enables the software
to run on single-platform computers, as well as across a large network of
processors, in order to support high-volume performance required of the
applications.
In order to handle the dynamic nature of relationship management, which can
incur a large number of interactions among many Web visitors, BroadVision
has developed an interaction manager. The interaction manager resides
between each of the tiers of the architecture, supports interoperability,
and manages the applications components. It maintains the context between
visitors and sites so that all interactions receive a response that
corresponds to the objective of the visitor and the site provider. The
interaction manager also interprets the application objects and templates at
runtime and retrieves the appropriate profile data and business rules so
that it can generate HTML that creates content and interactions that match
the needs of the specific Web site visitor. Finally, the session manager
also allows the Web site providers to incorporate additional software
processes to their Web site as more site visitors log on to the site.
As an added feature to enhance performance beyond the interaction manager,
and to improve Internet application development, BroadVision has structured
its application architecture around the use of objects and application
templates. These technologies help users create libraries of reusable
components to reduce the cost of new application development. In addition,
users can also define new business rules through BroadVision's Command
Center tool on a realtime basis as a result of the use of object technology.

As a complement to its product offering and as a method to encourage the
increased use of relationship management via the Internet, BroadVision has
implemented a comprehensive range of professional services. It has
established a worldwide professional services organization that offers
consulting services to support the product line with services that cover
application knowledge, technology, and product expertise. Strategic Services
consulting helps customers define and plan online businesses. Interactive
Services consulting helps companies develop customized BroadVision
applications. Content Services consulting helps customers with workflow
processes and user-interface design relative to content creation.
Despite its increasing market share and profitability, BroadVision still
faces serious competition from a wide range of companies seeking to take
advantage of the phenomenal growth in the e-commerce market. In this still
early stage of the market, BroadVision faces threats from smaller companies
such as itself that are tapping into the growth of customer relationship
management and e-commerce markets. These companies include players, such as
Ariba, Open Market, Intershop, and InterWorld, among others. However, at the
same time, BroadVision faces perhaps its biggest challenge from large
software and service providers such as Netscape, Microsoft, Oracle, and IBM
each of which is already marketing comprehensive packages that support
personalized commerce of the Internet and intranets.

Competitors

Ariba
Ariba is a major player in the e-commerce marketplace, and therefore a
serious competitor to BroadVision. Ariba has assembled a series of
e-commerce solutions that include its ORMS (Operating Resource Management
System) application, its network platform, and the Ariba Internet Business
Exchange service. ORMS is Ariba's flagship product that provides an n-tier
design to handle business procurement processes; it has enhanced its ORMS
product and delivered its ORMX application, which delivers
business-to-business e-commerce applications via application service
providers. The Ariba network platform connects buyers that use ORMS with
their network of suppliers. As part of its network platform, Ariba provides
catalog and content management, and also supports a wide variety of
standards to facilitate content exchange. Its Internet Business Exchange
(IBX) service provides a hosting environment that enables the creation of
commerce communities among organizations such as Web portals, financial
services companies, and telecommunications companies.

InterWorld
InterWorld's Commerce Exchange e-commerce solution consists of applications
that enable companies, particularly its targeted manufacturers, distributors
and retailers, to sell online to customers. It has recently begun focusing
more intently on the business-to-business market. The product provides a
framework that enables InterWorld's customers to personalize the e-commerce
experience through its suite of role-based tools. Its application suite
consists of Product Merchandising which facilitates merchandising online
with features such as cross-sell, up-sell, and personalized promotions;
Order Management manages the order entry, payment, and shipping processes;
and Account Management provides customer self-service, user profiling, and
customer segmentation.

Open Market
Open Market, Inc., competes primarily on the sell-side of the e-commerce
market with a series of four modular products that accommodate the
requirements of large and small companies moving into the electronic
marketplace. Its product line offers a variety of e-commerce options
including high-volume transaction management, content management, and
customer relationship management. Transact is the company's commerce server
designed for sites conducting high-volume B-to-B and B-to-C transactions. It
provides a full-featured set of e-commerce services ranging from order
management, fulfillment, and payment. LiveCommerce is the company's catalog
management software that complements its Transact commerce server. Important
new features with Release 2.0 let organizations track user buying patterns
to facilitate marketing campaigns. ShopSite is Open Market's low-cost
product for companies experimenting with electronic commerce or starting the
activity slowly. ShopSite, which is hosted by the customer's ISP that
manages the Web server, lets the customer establish an electronic storefront
giving users the opportunity to shop via a Web browser. In 1999, Open Market
acquired the Internet Publishing System (IPS) from FutureTense. IPS is a
publishing and content management platform that will be integrated with Open
Market's catalog and transaction product line.

Netscape
Netscape, through its iPlanet brand with Sun Microsystems, provides the
CommerceXpert product suite that supports a comprehensive array of
e-commerce functions including commerce information exchange, procurement,
selling, and information services among suppliers, partners, and customers.
Commerce Information Exchange functions are provided by a series of two
applications. ECXpert connects internal business systems, such as ERP, EDI,
and back-end transaction processing systems, with supply chain members
including partners, suppliers, and customers. TradingXpert lets trading
partners manage transactions via a browser interface. Procurement functions
are handled by the BuyerXpert application which provides centralized
purchasing management, analysis, and control functions. Netscape provides
online selling functions with its SellerXpert and MerchantXpert
applications. SellerXpert manages the B-to-B sales process, and
MerchantXpert handles business-to-consumer functions. Both products link
enterprises with their customers and manage the entire spectrum on online
selling from product selection through order tracking. PublishingXpert
manages the sales and delivery of personalized content via the Internet.


Strengths

Open Architecture
BroadVision has gone to considerable effort to maintain an open architecture
at every level of its application framework to facilitate integration and
interoperability. It provides a wide range of application program interfaces
(APIs) to extend its application functions to support capabilities such as
session profiling, discussion groups, order fulfillment, and content
matching, among many others. In addition, each layer of the BroadVision
architecture is open for integration with third-party systems. For example,
a business-specific targeting function such as data mining, can be
integrated using standard interfaces to the business layer. The application
layer can be integrated with selected ERP products, with financial
applications including Security First to handle core banking, and with
CheckFree to manage bill payment. The application layer also integrates with
middleware products such as BEA Tuxedo, Tibco, and IBM MQSeries and CICS to
support connection to back-end systems.

Profitable Company
Profitability is unique among nearly all of the Internet start-up companies
of the past several years. However, BroadVision is one of a few companies
that has reached the next level of corporate growth to retain some of its
increasing revenue as profits. It reported net income of US$4 million in
1998 based on revenues of US$51 million. This net income increased for the
first nine months of 1999, when BroadVision reported profits of nearly
US$10.8 million on revenue of almost US$72 million.

Personalization
Personalization is becoming an increasingly important element in customer
relationship management, and is another strong capability of BroadVision's
One-to-One functions. This personalization is done at each visit on a
realtime basis. Personalization can be based on observed information, stated
(or profile) information, and transactional information (for example, goods
or services bought in the past). The software interactively captures Web
site visitor profile data and matches that profile data with Web site
content based on the business rules managed by the application server.
