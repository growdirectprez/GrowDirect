Vendor Rating: JDA Software Group

JDA is doing well in a tough market by selling more applications to its customers in retail
and manufacturing. But its portfolio is a patchwork of different technologies, which can
make it hard to implement and support.

on existing customers will help it understand that it needs to integrate these acquisitions into a seamless,

Corporate Viability: JDA Software Group (JDA) is the most global vendor of business applications
software in the distribution industry, with the exception of SAP. JDA was founded in Canada in 1979 as a
pure-play merchandising management vendor. The company provides a suite of solutions to the retail
and distribution market. It operates from 36 offices in 20 countries and services clients in more than 60
nations.

JDA proved itself
to be a visionary by anticipating the need to provide merchandise management,
optimization and collaboration solutions globally through a broad retail and distribution suite. But eight
acquisitions in five years have meant that building the suite carries challenges that make the execution
more difficult, like having to use solutions running on disparate technologies. The main challenges are in
technology and integration as clients — especially Tier 2 retailers (see Note 1) — become more willing to
buy an integrated solution from a single vendor.

Note 1

Tier 1 retailers have revenue of more than $5 billion. There are fewer than 100 worldwide.

Tier 2 retailers have between $100 million and $5 billion in revenue. There are more than 3,000
worldwide.

JDA claims the broadest product suite in the retail market. It also maintains that every product in its
portfolio can be implemented as a stand-alone solution and that its strategy is to keep it this way. JDA
wants to be able to address whatever tactical problem a customer may have, to ensure a rapid return on
investment (ROI).

JDA faces no major loss of market share or decrease in revenue, unless the retail segment fails to
stabilize or meet forecast growth of 3 to 5 percent in IT spending for 2003 (see “Market Forecast: Retail
and Wholesale Industry, 2000-2005”). Since its first acquisition five years ago, JDA has increased it
revenue consistently every year. It has also reduced its percentage of services revenue, which is good,
as this type of revenue generates lower margins for software vendors (see Table 1).

Market Offerings: A Broad Yet Loosely Integrated Product Offering

Customers often struggle to understand the breadth and depth of JDA’s solutions. JDA originally offered
a merchandise management product running on IBM AS/400. It extended its functional scope into the
collaborative replenishment planning area with the acquisition of E3, a well-known and widely deployed
stand-alone AS/400 solution. With this acquisition, JDA recognized the need for business-to-business
processes and collaboration between manufacturers and retailers.

JDA Portfolio is a patchwork of more than 50 products on different platforms and technologies (see Table
3). Users willing to deploy a broad solution from JDA Portfolio should make sure that the considered
products work seamlessly together. Recent releases of JDA Portfolio are starting to take care of
integration between the products but completion will take several years.
