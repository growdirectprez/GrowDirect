I.	Assortment Management
A.	Objectives
To drive sales and profit, increase customer satisfaction, and enhance PETsMART’s brand image through product selection and presentation.

Description
Assortment Planning is the process of determining an optimal product offering, within a category, that achieves consumer fulfillment and enhanced business results.  It recognizes that corporate image, financial expectations, space constraints, lead times and strategy all play a part in creating a local merchandise assortment, and that all these factors must be different facets of one corporate plan. It encompasses the principles of micro-marketing by recognizing that there is a need to define individual assortments for individual stores.
B.	Critical Success Factors
1.	Ability to impact presentation at the store level in a timely manner
2.	Ability to influence store inventory levels
3.	Ability to effectively support corporate events
4.	Ability to support PETsMART’s mission of great products at great prices
5.	Ability to fulfill the needs of PETsMART’s customers
6.	Ability to be the “first” with new products
7.	Ability to edit under-performing items from the assortment on a timely basis
8.	Ability to effectively communicate article information throughout the supply chain.
C.	Assumptions
1.	New Store layouts will be handled within the Site Management.
2.	New Store listing will result from planogram assignment.
3.	The planogram activity relating to prototype definition will take place in a space management software application.

D.	Performance Measures
Shopper surveys / questionnaires
Brand image
Category Measures (See Review Assortment & Define Annual Category Strategy)
E.	Issues
There is an issue with regards to the integration of a space management system (i.e. Intactix) with the assortment and listing functions in SAP.  The objective is to control listing (and thus replenishment) for all SKU’s in the stores through the use of planograms.

F.	Better Practices Recommendations
Assortment is developed within a category or department context that fulfills the objective of the category relative to pricing, promotion, and positioning.
Consumer, market, distributor and supplier performance is evaluated via scheduled and standardized measurements defined in a balanced scorecard.
Support a customer driven (versus a vendor driven) view of assortments.  The focus on the customer purchase process is the key to meaningful variety from duplication.  Accommodate customization of the merchandise assortment range for individual locations.
Assortment plans are tied to store fixture capacity and needs.
Assortment changes do not always coincide with the planning cycle and may take on varying levels of complexity.
For categories with stable assortments, extend the current assortment.  Results should be factored in bottoms-up plans.
Major changes in an assortment outside the planning cycle may require a plan revision.
Use product line level assortment plans as the basis of vendor business reviews that occur during the planning cycle.
To identify new product opportunities, testing is performed and evaluated against a category specific standard.
Integration of merchandise and assortment planning with store planning.
Integration of space planning (visual and marketing) with merchandise and assortment plans.
