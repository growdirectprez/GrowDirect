# Introduction
Objectives of the Tesco Operating Model
To represent the business operating processes of the Tesco business
To describe what we do, why we do it and how we measure it
To detail the processes and understand how we operate
Approach to define the Tesco Operating Model – Phase 1
A series of workshops to produce a draft operating model by area of business operation capturing ‘what we do’ (a level 1 process) and ‘how we do it’ ( a level 2 process)

# Forecast and Ordering - Executive Summary
Forecast and Ordering covers the following:
Forecasting of expected sales
Ordering of stock for stores and Distribution Centres from suppliers
Planning of volumes in the physical distribution network
The key benefits are that:
We maximise stock availability for customers
We  waste very little stock in store
We have the lowest possible level of stock in the store warehouse
In-store Replenishment costs are minimised
Processes in the Stores are simple and enable maximum availability of stock at minimum operating costs
Our main dependencies are with:
Commercial: to provide information on promotions,new product lines, one-off buys ; to maintain logistics data for products
Space, Range and Display: to provide shelf capacity and ranging information by store,to support stock clearance,
MIS: to provide actual store sales
Marketing: to provide  the details and dates of promotional events, forecasted sales volumes to allow for volume planning
New Store Team: to provide locations, dates and sales plans for new stores
Store Operations: to follow the processes to maintain an accurate stock record
Key decisions to be made include:
Decide what is best practice for managing stock levels during promotions.
What is the best way to develop expertise on systems and supply chain optimisation – centrally or locally?  - Decision is centrally.

# Forecast and Ordering - Benefits
Forecasting and Ordering –
       Having accurate forecasting and ordering is a critical factor in our competitive advantage. A key part of our offer is to ensure that as a customer “I can get what I want” and accurate ordering of stock and replenishment to store shelves at lowest cost and with minimal waste is one of the building blocks of our business model
When we forecast and order what we want accurately, we:
improve product availability, (.25% increase in sales for every 1% increase in availability)
reduce levels of aged stock allowing us to sell more stock at full price
reduce waste, (by as much as 40% compared to when we order manually)
reduce store warehouse stock, (by as much as 50% to when we order manually allowing us to run with smaller backrooms as a proportion of selling space)
reduce levels of stock in the supply chain (14 days stock overall in the UK vs. up to 40 days in Central Europe)
reduce in-store replenishment costs, (by up to 20% by increasing the amount of stock that goes on the shelf first time)
make it simpler for stores, (by no longer asking stores to calculate and review orders thus reducing staffing levels in stock control)

Forecast and Ordering is the process by which we ensure that our customers can get what they want ; by forecasting sales, ordering the correct levels of stock and moving it efficiently to the shelf to maximise availability at minimum cost

# Forecast and Ordering Capability
| What we do? | What does this mean? | Why do we do it? | How do we measure it? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Forecast expected unit sales | We forecast what we expect to sell of an item. So that when we order, we know how many we need. | If we forecast correctly what we expect to sell we will not order too much or too little, and therefore maximise availability and minimise stock holding/waste. | The error between what we forecasted to sell in units and what we actually sold in the store expressed as a percentage error. We also measure the accuracy of the store order forecast against the actual order. |
| Order Stock for Stores | We calculate orders so that customers can get what they want, we fill our shelves cheaper than our competitors by ordering what we need when we need it. | If we order correctly;- customers can always get what they want, our shelf displays are always presentable- our shelves are quick to fill because most of the stock fits on the shelf when the order arrives- we only have what we want in the store warehouse, so we can find what we’re looking for easily | We measure the number of gaps we have on our shelves, how much product we have to reduce or throw away, how much stock we have in our stores and how many days it will last |
| Order stock for Distribution Centre | We order some lines from suppliers that are stocked in the Distribution Centre and from which stores can order regularly | Store can order more frequently and on a shorter lead times and if we order correctly;- store orders can be met in full - there is always sufficient space in the Distribution Centre when a supplier delivery arrives | We measure whether our suppliers deliver to us on time and in full. Also that we have enough space in the Distribution Centre to store the goods when the delivery arrives. |

Ordering too much leads to waste and excess stock in store, ordering too little loses sales and customers

| What we do? | What does this mean? | Why do we do it? | How do we measure it? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plan Deliveries into Distribution Centre | We organise the timing of deliveries of ordered stock into the Distribution Centre | So that stock is available for stores when they need it and so that when the goods arrive we have space to store the goods and enough doors and people to receive the goods. | We measure Distribution Centre to store service levels We measure how much stock we have to delay into the Distribution Centre because capacity has been exceeded (called back-scheduling) |
| Forecast Network Volumes | We forecast the volumes of intake and despatch for the Distribution Centres. | We forecast volumes so that Distribution Centres can plan labour to meet demand at the lowest possible cost. | We measure the accuracy of the forecast by comparing actual to forecast volumes. |
| Manage Network Volumes | We manage the flow of stock into and out of a Distribution Centre to meet store orders and deliver stock to stores in time. | We smooth the flow of stock from a Distribution Centre to stores to maximise the stock a Distribution Centre can deliver and reduce the cost of handling it. We do this to even out the daily delivery to store profile and to ensure that we have full shelves at the start of the peak trading days in the week. We bring forward store demand where Distribution Centre capacity is exceeded (called Getting Ahead of Trade). | We measure the percentage of goods delivered to stores by day of the week. We measure the level of deliveries received at store on time and the level of Distribution Centre costs. |
| Pass Sales to supplier | We give information to our suppliers about the sales of their products | When suppliers receive this information they can anticipate and plan their production requirements better ensuring that stock is available to meet demand | We measure Distribution Centre to Store Service Levels |
| Order Fuel for Petrol Stations | We forecast, order and deliver fuel to the Petrol Filling Stations | Petrol Filling Stations must always have fuel available for our customers to buy as part of the overall Tesco offer | We reconcile daily the amount of fuel held at each PFS |

Not forecasting DC volumes accurately results in availability issues or in wasted costs
