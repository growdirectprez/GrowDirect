Introduction
<Link from Jeff the Store Manager: a) Saturated store base, look to alternate channels to expand. b) Labour costs and overheads in store look high on P&L compared to the Internet channel which has much lower operating costs. >
Hi, I am the Complimentary Channels Manager. It's my job to develop the alternate channels business, which started with our mail order catalogue. We did not have the capabilities required to run a catalogue, so we acquired a Mail Order Company two years ago. We have now integrated the mail order staff and management with the rest of T&K. My latest project has been to develop our Internet business.

I'd like to share with you some of the key things T&K has learnt while we got our Internet channel up and running.
Apart from keeping me employed, why are we developing our non-store based channel?
As Jeff said T&K has a saturated store base. New Channels to market allow us to reach a wider audience and grow the business.
Our base has also expanded, due to the global reach of the Web. [slide with a map of the world showing Internet usage]
On the Internet we are able to extend our core customer segment to include younger ABC1 professionals. [slide on statistics of which groups use the Web]
As Jeff said the lower operating cost of the Internet channel is a great opportunity, but it is also a threat. T&K have new competition from Web only companies. [slide showing operating costs for web vs. store retailer] [slide on what are the drivers of a web based companies operating model]
We researched competitor activity in Internet retailing, including the new players in retail. [slide from E-Business strategy showing competitor activity in Web retailing] [slide showing convergence in retail sector e.g. Barclays Bank selling refrigerators on the Web]
Here are a few examples of what our main competitors are doing at the moment [briefly show Web sites for T&K’s competitors, using list in CIS]. I will show you those in a bit more detail later.
To assist our planning and forecasting we joined the PwC Consumer Electronic Access study. This provided trend analysis and market forecasts for the new channels we were considering. [CEA sales video] [slide showing technology trends, alternate channel forecasts]
Luckily we have been able to enter the channel relatively inexpensively because: [slide on what companies are investing to create a Web business]
The Mail Order Company provided us with the necessary skills in home distribution, catalogue production, and call centres.
SAP is a central repository for all of our transactions and information. The benefits this brought to us were … [Martin Butler to assist]

So you can see there are some compelling reasons to develop the T&K Internet channel.
Extend our market base from our saturated store base, both globally and by selling to a new consumer market.
Lower operating costs, and a low cost of entry
Threat of new competition, who uses a different business model

But compelling reasons will not make this channel successful. We need to ensure that our proposition is right. So, What is our Offer?
Extra convenience and flexibility for our customers
The T&K Web site offers 24 hour x 7 days a week shopping and information.
There are a number of delivery options available on the Web site to suit urgency and budget.
Extended product range
For example in our stores we only stock a limited range of plates, on the Web we can offer the full range of sizes and colours.
By not requiring to have stock immediately available we are able to develop ‘virtual’ ranges with our suppliers who will ship direct.
Lower prices on more expensive items and free delivery with larger purchases
Consumers often expect lower prices on the Web. The problem retailers face is the need to charge for delivery, therefore the consumer feels they are being charged more.
T&K solved this problem by reducing our fulfilment costs via the Web to on average £4, compared to £14 via our stores. This was achieved by the infrastructure that we have in place for the Mail Order business, and our integrated SAP systems.
I hope that this shows that with a carefully though out proposition, complimentary channels can offer consumers what they want, without cannibalising store sales.

Continuing the theme of offering consumers what they want, I would like to tell you how our Customer Value Management initiative has enabled us to offer a better service to our customers on the Internet.
To give customers what they want, we need to find out about it first.
At the moment T&K have a number of tools to help us understand our customers better, and offer them a more personalised service. We have been capturing loyalty card transaction details for a few years, as well as information from the mail order service.
Recently we have been capturing web shopping transactions and web site usage [show analysing web site statistics using Microsoft Site Server ecommerce tool].
Using the knowledge we have extracted from our CVM systems, we are able to offer services such as personalised web pages, and special offers unique to customers.
On the T&K web site we offer personalised pages once the user logs in. Lets have a quick look at the T&K Web site [show T&K web site, highlight personalisation features].
More of the T&K web site later, lets see what else is happening on the Internet [show Amazon.com book mailing list, where your interest in an author provides you with news of his latest book releases. show banner adverts on the results pages which are related to the searches you do using Yahoo].
We have big plans for CVM, such as using Genetic Algorithms to predict customer behaviour such as which customers are likely to respond to direct mailings, or potential product purchases based on previous purchase details. For more information on these initiatives please speak to our CVM manager.


Now that we are on the Internet, I would like to show you some current best practices in Web retailing. [show CIS portal main screen]
You may have noticed that I keep using the same web site to find sites I have wanted to show you. I have not created this directory of Web sites it is all on a site called the Consumer Information Service. This web portal has a directory of retail web sites in the UK and Internationally, and offers information and news for on-line consumers. [give an overview demo of CIS]
I would like to show you these web sites in the order of a person shopping [slide with shopping process - Awareness, Thinking, Research, Browse, Shop Around, Buy, Receive, After-Sales].
First, let me show you the difference between shopping in a store and shopping on the Web [slide of time line comparing shopping processes – drive to store, park car, look for product, ask assistant, look for product, wait at till, pay, find car, drive home OR launch Web browser, jump to T&K store, search for product, compare products, click buy now button, enter delivery and payment details, receive receipt, receive goods]
