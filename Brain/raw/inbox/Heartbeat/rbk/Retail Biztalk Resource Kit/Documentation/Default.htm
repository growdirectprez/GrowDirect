The Retail Biztalk Resource Kit (RBK) will provide a Retail framework for extracting, transforming, and serializing transaction log data.  The data source will be IBM 4690 Point of Sale transaction log data and  will use Biztalk to  transform this data into ActiveStore RBI schema found on http://www.Biztalk.org.  This RBK will provide the user an example of how to integrate IBM 4690 transaction log data with other products and services using Biztalk.  The RBK is intended to be in PILOT form to decide future Retail integration opportunities using Biztalk Server.

What is Biztalk Anyway?

Biztalk is one of the most misunderstood products in the Microsoft .Net Server product line.  The following is an attempt to explain what Biztalk is from a very high level and is by no means a full description of its capabilities.  Biztalk can be explained by using a simple travel metaphor.  As an example, you are part of a five (5) person team each of you have to be in 5 major cities for client meetings.  Each meeting has its own agenda or topic that must be covered specific to that client.  Bob agrees to drop the five of you off at the airport but in this case we will call it a travel port.  At the travel port passengers have the option of traveling by various methods: car, train, bus, helicopter, or jet.  Each travel method has a set of requirements or rules that must be followed in order to go to the destination.  All passengers must be identified and must check in, answer any questions that might be required in order to ensure safe travel:  You present Identification and answer some questions:  What is your Destination?  What are you bringing with you?  What luggage do you have?  After you check in at the travel port, you stop at a data port and dial into your virtual private network and gather required information and resources about your customer so you can be prepared for your meeting.  You are now prepared and ready to meet with your customer and you arrive at your gate to catch your jet to go to your final destination.   

So how does this explain Biztalk?  The five (5) person team represents a package of data from a trading partner or data source.  The five major cities represent the destination addresses of each of the travelers.  Think of the Biztalk Framework as the travel port.  Bob, represents an external trading partner.  The process Bob uses to drop off people represents a file or Queue receive function which invokes the Biztalk Framework.  The check in process represents document identification or schemas  which are built using the Biztalk Editor.  The check in process also aids in identifying the destination and any transformation of the data that might occur; channels.  When you step into the data port to gather required information and resources you are changing and evolving yourself to meet the requirements of your destination partner; data transformation.  This is a metaphor for XSLT or the Biztalk Mapper.   The travel methods (car, train, bus, helicopter, or jet) represent message ports with SMTP, HTTP(S), MSMQ, File, or Application Interface Components as transport types.
