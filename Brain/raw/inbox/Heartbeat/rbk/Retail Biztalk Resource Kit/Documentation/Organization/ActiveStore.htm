What Is the ActiveStore Initiative?

The Microsoft® ActiveStore® initiative is a Microsoft-led, worldwide collaborative initiative that began in late 1996. ActiveStore aims to reduce the cost, time, and risk associated with integrating and deploying line-of-business (LOB) technology solutions in retail enterprises. The initiative is driven by a board of major retailers from all industry segments (the Customer Advisory Board), with technical direction from a core team of the industry's most influential independent software vendors (ISVs).

What Are the Benefits of ActiveStore?

ActiveStore is a collaborative effort aimed at developing a standards-based retail information system framework that:

  • Reduces integration costs associated with deploying retail LOB applications.
  • Reduces employee training costs, while enhancing employee productivity.
  • Reduces support costs.
  • Speeds time-to-market and lowers development costs for software vendors.

What Is Included in the ActiveStore Retail Technology Architecture?

The ActiveStore retail technology architecture covers numerous aspects of retail operations, including storing and retrieving data, business functions that operate on the data, and methods information systems can use to present the results of these operations to users.

The primary component of the architecture addresses application integration. Commonly referred to as retail business interfaces (RBIs), this aspect of the initiative works to define retail-specific interfaces and events to satisfy the industry's application integration requirements. RBI's are XML-based schemas, platform independent, and expand application integration across the retail enterprise from store systems to core retail systems to supply chain management systems.

Another component of the architecture is platform services, which addresses basic application interoperability functions that retailers need in a store environment, such as immediate crash recovery, application state protection, and centralized security. Previously, such functionality was provided by ISVs, often resulting in inter-vendor integration barriers because many software developers have different approaches to solving these common technology issues. ActiveStore platform services are designed to take advantage of the power of Microsoft Windows® technology, provide a robust task-centric workstation environment, and lower development and support costs for software vendors.

The final component of the architecture has to do with the user interface. A key top-of-mind issue facing retailers today has to do with employee turnover. This aspect of the initiative is focused on addressing retailers' costs associated with employee training and proficiency in using solutions deployed at store level. It includes defining standards to lock-down task-centric workstations—navigation, alerts, help, and application task area—while preserving the ISV's ability to differentiate.
