Marks & Spencer Transaction Polling Solution Case Study

The following section was taken from the Marks & Spencer Case Study.  Marks & Spencer did not use the exact technology but used the same idea in polling data from in-store POS devices to a central enterprise system. 

Background

Global retailer Marks and Spencer had 1999 revenues of $13.2 billion and is the United Kingdom’s premier clothing, food and financial services retailer, with 435 stores in the United Kingdom, Europe and worldwide, including Brooks Brothers and Kings Supermarkets in the United States. The company’s commitment to value, quality and service began nearly 120 years ago; Marks and Spencer is legendary in the retail industry for its supplier relationships and long-term thinking.

Business Challenge

Retail in Europe is in a period of especially intense competition, and Marks and Spencer needed to refocus and rethink its plans to respond. To support and drive its business, Marks and Spencer had to be able to access critical information quickly — areas vital to its success, such as customer relationships, service and innovation, depend on faster access to more information. In addition, Marks and Spencer saw an opportunity to leverage its uniquely strong integration with suppliers into a dynamic, event-driven, global supply chain that responds swiftly and efficiently to customer needs. To hone this competitive advantage, Marks and Spencer wanted to deploy a solution that could support the huge business and operational changes of the future, that would make it possible to move data anywhere within its worldwide operations or supplier base, and that would not need complex reworking any time a link in the chain was changed. In addition, the information flow needed to be in near real time to allow quick response to changing retail demands.

Solution

Marks and Spencer reviewed a number of technology options and decided the best solution to meet its needs was Microsoft BizTalk Server, scheduled for release next year. The initial solution aims to move transaction-level point-of-sale data from the stores to the current head office systems in near real time and is based wholly on Microsoft technologies:

  • In-store. Existing Microsoft Windows NT Server 4.0 operating system running Microsoft SQL Server 6.5. Addition of Microsoft Windows NT service and the Microsoft Visual Basic development system component to extract data, convert to BizTalk XML and post to MSMQ.

  • Network. MSMQ routes data from over 300 stores over Marks and Spencer’s wide area network to the data center.

  • Data center. MSMQ servers pass data on to a BizTalk Server farm, where data is read, transformed and routed based on contents to the appropriate destination, using MSMQ (to other Windows NT Server-based systems), MSMQ and MQSeries Bridge (to some S/390 applications) or COMTI (through to CICS and DB2 applications). The central environment will need to support up to 250 store transactions per second into BizTalk Server from stores and 250 transactions onward into central applications, handling up to 5 GB of traffic in peak trading per day.

At the same time, there are similar data flows into the stores for event-driven promotions, and pilots in distribution that will use the same technologies.

Following this, the vision is to create a flexible infrastructure service for the integration of business processes via defined message standards that can act as a scalable, future-proof information backbone for the entire business, extending to the supply base of over 500 suppliers.
