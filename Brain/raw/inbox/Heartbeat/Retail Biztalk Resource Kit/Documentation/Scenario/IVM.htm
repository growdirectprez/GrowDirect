Item Velocity Monitor

Background

 A serious challenge facing grocery retailers is out of stock items on store shelves that lead to dissatisfied guests and lost revenue.  A study by a major CPG organization found that 45% of grocery store consumers are dissatisfied with the availability of items at their grocery stores and that product availability is the number one consumer complaint.  On average roughly 8% of all items sold at a typical grocery store are out of stock at any given time, resulting in a 3-4% loss in revenue (after accounting for switching to other in-stock items).These numbers nearly double for items on promotion. 

There are several key benefits for supply chain participants, one is to reduce the rate of “Out of Stocks” at the point of purchase to near zero. This is a huge incentive for retailers and in turn for manufacturers. Another expected benefit is that participants in the supply chain may be able to reduce stockpiles by 50% through better forecasting and timely reports that allow better supply chain optimization. The ramifications of this occurring in the supply chain are staggering. If participants could reduce capital investment in inventory, warehousing and manual processes, they could save huge sums of money.

Three Retail grocery enterprises participated in an Out of Stock (OOS) pilot in early 2001.  The OOS Pilot’s objective was to evaluate the business impacts of OOS’s at these retailers, demonstrate a prototype system that identifies near real time OOS events and notifies store personnel of the event for replenishment, and determine the savings that would result.  The pilots tracked real time sales for the top 1000 dry grocery items.  It showed there was a serious out of stock problem in these chains, which averaged OOS rates of 6.3% and 7%.  The three retailers, like the industry, saw the rate of out of stock nearly doubling when items are on sale or promotion.  60 to 80% of the out of stock conditions seen at these retailers appear to be directly related to store operations.

The pilots showed that with minor changes to store operations, the information provided by the system allowed a 25% reduction in the OOS rate. In the future, as store personnel become more familiar with the system and develop additional techniques for reducing OOSs, and as the information provided allows logistics system improvements such as more accurate ordering for promotions, substantial additional OOS rate reductions are possible.
