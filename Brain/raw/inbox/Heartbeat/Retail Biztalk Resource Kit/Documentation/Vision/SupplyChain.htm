Re-architecting the Supply Chain:

Agile businesses thrive by quickly realigning to new market conditions. For retailers, the timely consolidation, transformation, and distribution of their sales data within and outside of their enterprise drives this capability. Internally, transaction data supports core operational systems like replenishment, merchandising, and financials, as well as business intelligence solutions like loyalty, shrink and loss prevention. Externally, manufacturers and suppliers rely on sales data to understand consumption trends, market share, and the impact of promotional activities. Thus transaction data is the fuel for channel collaboration.

Propagation of a real-time consumer demand signal throughout the supply chain will have far reaching impact for the participants. The basic premise is that participants will able to  “better optimize, plan, and execute decisions, while improving the efficiency and utilization of their existing resources”. In order to accomplish this goal, several fundamental situations must become pervasive. Below are some of these fundamental objectives: 

  • Implement standards that will allow decision support information to freely flow between the participants in the supply chain
  • Reduce the time it takes for the demand signal to propagate throughout the supply chain. The demand signal must be near real-time and continuous.
  • Modify existing systems from forecast based to consumer demand based, allowing for optimization with as little human intervention as possible.
  • Develop solutions that will scale to support the supply chain functions of the largest retailers and manufacturers in the world, to the smallest “mom & pop” retail outlet.

 While striving to achieve these basic goals; we recognize that we must interface with current business processes & technologies in a manner that is consistent with industry standards. Since we must interact with real business operations, we must do so in a non-obtrusive way that doesn’t endanger the productivity or profitability of the participants.
