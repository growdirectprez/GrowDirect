RIO Team Structure

A reporting team structure will be established at the client site, which is divided into 6 organizational clusters.  Each cluster will be made up of single or multiple people based on project size and scope. 

Program Management Cluster

Program management owns and drives the schedule, the features, and the budget for the project. Program management ensures that the right product is delivered at the right time.  As the owner of the schedule, program management collects all team schedules, validates them, and integrates them into a master schedule that is tracked and reported to the team and to stakeholders.  As the owner of the features, program management plays a role in defining which features will be delivered in order to meet the requirements outlined by product management. Program management owns the functional specification (what is to be built) and the master project plan (how it is going to be built) and facilitates their creation by getting input from each of the roles on the team.

Product Management Cluster 

The Product Management Cluster acts as the Customer Advocate and the Team Advocate.

As the customer advocate to the team, product management is responsible for understanding customer requirements, creating the business case, establishing the shared project vision between team and customer, and ensuring that any solution that the team develops meets the needs of the customer by solving their particular business problem.

As the team advocate to the customer, product management is responsible for high-level communications and managing customer expectations. High-level communications include public relations, briefings to senior management/customers, marketing to users, demonstrations, and product launches. Managing expectations is the key role of product management once the vision is set.

Development Cluster 

The role of development is to build a product that meets the specification and customer expectations. It is important that development focus not only on coding to the functional specification but also on meeting customer expectations. This is because functional specifications are written before any significant development or building take place, leaving them inherently incomplete. Therefore, development must innovate, but only to solve the customer’s problem, not just for the sake of implementing interesting features.

Testing Cluster 

The goal of testing is to make sure that all issues are known and addressed prior to releasing the product. An issue is anything that prevents the product from meeting its requirements. This could be a fault in the code that development writes, otherwise known as a bug, a deviation in the specification that program management owns, or a defect with the documentation that user education produces. 

User Education Cluster 

User education focuses on enhancing user performance so that users are as productive as possible with the product. To accomplish this, user education acts as the advocate for the end user of the product, much like product management acts as the customer advocate to the team.

As the user advocate to the team, user education participates in the design process to deliver a product that is useful, usable, and in need of as little performance support material as possible. This also lowers the costs of supporting the product in the operations/delivery channel. Where user performance support materials are still required, user education designs, builds, and tests materials that will enable easier use.

Logistics Management Cluster 

Logistics management serves as the advocate for the operations, product support, help desk, and other delivery channel organizations in its focus on smooth deployment and ongoing management. As the operations advocate to the team, logistics management participates in the design process to help ensure that the product is deployable, manageable, and supportable. Logistics management also is responsible for understanding the product’s infrastructure and support requirements and ensuring that installation sites have met those requirements prior to deployment.

Organization Structure 

The organizational structure of RIO Inputs will encompass two groups: Microsoft Consulting Services (MCS) and Customer personnel.  The Managing Consultant from MCS will provide high-level business status to the Customer Director.  The MC will also initiate and drive the business vision and make certain that it is clearly communicated to the entire team.  The MC will also own the risks associated with the project and will work with the Director in developing requirements and strategies going forward.  The Director will drive high-level business decisions and internal customer process.  The Director will also communicate high-level vision and requirements to the entire team.  The Managing Consultant and Director will act as ownership peers, partnering to navigate the project given return on investment requirements.

The Senior Consultant (MCS) will provide project-level status to the Customer Project Manager.    The Senior Consultant (SC) will also report project-level status to the Managing Consultant.  The Senior Consultant owns all project-risks and resources internal to Microsoft.  The SC must work with the Customer Project Manager to determine project requirements, resource requirements, while managing the Development Resources.  The Customer Project Manager will provide project-level requirements to the SC while reporting project status to their Director.   

Development Resources under MCS will report and provide status to the Senior Consultant (MCS).  Internal Customer Support Staff will aid and assist the MCS Consulting staff and report and provide status to the Customer Project Manager.

 

 

 

ASPEN Team Structure

The ASPEN Development Team consists one Managing Consultant, two Senior Consultants, four Consultants and an Associate Consultant.  The Managing Consultant will own the project, its risks, and drive the financial goals of ASPEN development.  The MC will also drive all status meetings and report status to superiors.  There are two Senior Consultants that will report to the Managing Consultant.  One Senior Consultant will lead the Biztalk development effort.  The other Senior Consultant will manage the Subscription and Retail Customer Billing Web Service as well as testing/User Training/Logistical Planning.  Both Senior Consultants share a Development DBA resource. 

Senior Consultants will decide all hardware and software scalability and infrastructure issues.

Four Software Development Consultants 

Biztalk Developer:  Develops plan for Biztalk implementation and develops necessary Biztalk Objects for project.  Reports to Senior Consultant.

COM Biztalk Developer:  Builds COM and other business objects including: Biztalk Preprocessors, Application Integration Components, and COM+ components for Web Services.

Development DBA:  Assists entire team in developing database schemas and XML schemas.  This person should also know data warehousing and XML implementation.

Web Development:  Develops Web interfaces and web services for the project.

Testing/Logistics/User Training:  Develops test plans and User Manuals.  Conducts training for turning over operations.  Handles all logistics and hardware and software procurement.
