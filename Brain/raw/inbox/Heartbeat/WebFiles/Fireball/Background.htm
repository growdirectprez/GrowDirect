Background

This offering is the result of a pilot / poof of concept engagement with consumer products giant Procter and Gamble and their Ultimate Supply Chain project codenamed "Fireball". 

 

Most participants in the supply chain today rely on forecasts of consumer demand rather than actual consumer demand to schedule and drive their individual activities. Because these activities often occur within silos, response to actual consumer demand is difficult to match and even more difficult to optimize. The resulting overall supply chain is not synchronized to consumer demand and in its current form, prevents the real-time flow of information to the supply chain partners, and adversely affects product replenishment flow to consumers.

 

To insure themselves against adverse consumer replenishment situations, suppliers, manufacturers and retailers have created extensive buffers of distribution centers and warehouses. These huge safety nets are by their essence inefficient because they consume enormous amounts of capital resources. Still with these investments in buffers, the consumer still experiences an inordinate amount of out-of-stock situations at the point of purchase. This is a lost revenue opportunity anytime consumers are unable to purchase product that is in the supply-chain, or worse yet not manufactured.

 

Procter & Gamble has developed a vision of how to better service their consumers, indeed all consumers, by re-architecting their supply chain from a forecast driven process into a consumer demand driven process. This will require significant advances in three distinct areas: 1) real-time information flow of the consumer demand signal throughout the supply chain partners; 2) optimization processes that return increasing value to each participant in the supply chain; and 3) the development of information exchanges that facilitate the real-time flow of the demand signal and the optimization process.

 

The single driving concept behind the Fireball project is that consumer demand, not forecasts must drive the entire supply-chain. In order to evolve the supply chain from a forecast driven to a demand driven system, the following basic capability must become pervasive:

the Supply Chain must have a real-time consumer demand driven signal that initiates the supply chain response.

Consequently, Procter & Gamble and Microsoft embarked on a joint learning expedition, to explore the business and technical issues around the capture and orchestration of the consumer demand signal. The result was the development of the Consumer Demand Signal Service.

 

A leading data mining and data warehousing company, Data Ventures provided the Demand Signal Algorithm which detects out-of-stock conditions in the store. Microsoft delivered the application integration infrastructure which include integration of the retailer's transaction, item, and promotional data as well as development of the notification system.  Pilots were delivered at two Meijer food stores and one Food Lion store.

Meijer is a Tier 1 grocery retailer with corporate headquarters in Grand Rapids, Michigan. The company operates over 100 stores. Their newest stores are as large as 250,000 square feet and carry over 120,000 products in over 40 departments and operate 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. The pilot monitored 1,000 items in their grocery department.  

Food Lion is a member of the Delhaize America Inc. family of supermarket chains with more than 1,100 stores in 11 states in the southeastern United States. They sell more than 24,000 different products and offer nationally and regionally advertised brand name merchandise.
