Budgeting/Planning Process
Best Practice Option
Best practice in budgeting/planning policy focuses on streamlining, simplifying, and organizing the flow of the process.  In addition, the attention has shifted to a more outward and proactive focus that addresses business changes constantly.  Analysis reveals the following best practices for the budgeting/planning process:

Streamline
Budget bottom-up with overlapping top-down budget development process
Bottom up iterative planning is balanced by clear corporate vision statement
Centrally defined budgeting policy
Top down budgets that provide strong corporate guidance
Integrate forecast and measurement processes
Integrate strategic planning with budgeting
Use integrated/common planning tools
Use rolling, flexible budget (5-6 quarters) instead of annual budget (for volatile business environments)
Revise forecasts and budgets each month with a rolling three year forecast updated quarterly
Budget once a year at the latest time possible prior to period being budgeted
Establish budget targets only once by senior management
Use variances from the previous year as input to the current year budgeting process
Budget less than 100 line items
Number of forecasting lines less than the number of accounting lines
Minimize the number of approval cycles to 2 or 3

Outward/proactive focus
Drive targets by a commitment based on the strategic plan rather than a detailed plan
Include more than financial information
Consider budgets to be an annual performance measurement setting exercise
Use budgets to reinforce the continual improvement mind set
Use benchmark information as an input into the budgeting/planning process
Spend the majority of the time on controllables
Provide targets that focus on market share and competition

Examples of organizations who have utilized best practices for budgeting and planning:

An international manufacturer restructured their budgeting and planning processes to identify critical success indicators.  The annual operating plan was restructured to reflect “a set of management programs for the year and is no longer created as a separate financial project.”  As a result, management feels more ownership over the process and the process has changed from a once a year activity to a continual procedure which guides and responds to day to day situations.

An electricity company converted to a rolling financial forecast that is externally, rather than internally focused, and addresses the changing business environment much more readily.

A telecommunications company replaced annual budgeting with a six quarter rolling forecast method.  This resulted in reducing the time to set targets from traditional timeframe of 2 to 7 months, reducing the cycle time from 137 to 60 days, and allowed drill down of key business drivers.

A large furniture manufacturer moved from annual budgeting to a continuous process that is used to align the company’s strategic goals with operational realities.  This change, driven by a need to be flexible and innovative in today’s business environment, led to a 3 month budget cycle that is intricately tied to the strategic planning and operational reporting functions.

Budgeting/Planning Technology
Best Practice Option
Best practice in budgeting/planning technology focuses on an integrated, easily accessed, and flexible budget system that is user friendly and encourages cross functional involvement at all levels.  Analysis reveals the following best practices for budgeting/planning technology:

Common planning tool that integrates planning, budgeting, and forecasting with G/L
Budgeting can be performed on-line, with the ability to support multiple views and what-if analysis
Client server technology in support of decentralized preparation and analysis
Ready access to common set of financial parameters, assumptions, and budget drivers
Organizations with budget responsibility input data using shared systems
System flexible to report at various levels of summarization based on rollup codes
Budget volume changes can automatically update revised budgets based on relationships established during the initial budget cycle

Examples of organizations who have utilized best practices for budgeting and planning:

A health insurance company restructured their planning and budgeting processes after identifying a gap between operations and planning.  Changes include a continuous budget, automated rolling forecast system, enhanced strategic/financial model, use of project management and reporting toll to manage process, implementation of common software and on-line “Help” functions.
