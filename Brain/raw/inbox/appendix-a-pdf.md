OneMethodology Overview

OneMethodology - is an activity based implementation approach that employs a collection of activities to
organize work, capture and resolve issues, and quickly make and implement decisions.

OneMethodology is the approach used by J.D. Edwards to implement our software solutions.

The methodology is founded on the concept that every task within the implementation must be associated to a specific
activity.

Each activity comes equipped with the intellectual property needed by the project team to complete all of the necessary
implementation activities. The intellectual property generally consists of:

Invitees (based on customer roles)

•  Process flow models
•  Objectives and deliverables
•  Agenda
•
•  Database of discussion items (including implications)
•  Autopilot scripts to load data
•  Scripts to validate test transactions
•  Customizable, role-based end-user training materials

Workshops and Action Labs

Within OneMethodology two types of activity are used to define the business process flow, and configure the
supporting system.
All activities have objectives and specific inputs/outputs and deliverables.

WORKSHOPS

Workshops are typically used to perform one of the following:

•  Design, customize, or build workshop deliverables
•  Knowledge transfer and/or training
•  Project management and change management

Steps of the Workshops may be performed over different periods of time, and may involve different participants.

A workshops comprises four distinct steps:

•  Prepare – Gather the designated materials to support the execution of the workshop.
•  Build – Develop the specific workshop deliverables with core project team members.
•  Present – Present the deliverables to the wider business audience.
•  Craft – Refine the deliverables to include improvements from the Present step.

ACTION LABS

Action labs are characterized by the presentation of a pre-configured solution or standard that is provided by J.D.
Edwards.

Action labs are designed to facilitate rapid decision-making. Therefore, the “conduct step” of the action labs are
performed over a continuous time period, and usually involve a single set of participants.

An action lab comprises three distinct steps:

•  Prepare – Gather the designated materials to support the execution of the action lab.
•  Conduct – Present and discuss the J.D. Edwards’ standards and/or pre-configured solutions to the

customer team

•  Craft – Execute the action lab decisions.

Education and Training

Education and training are key components of OneMethodology

For the customer’s project team and/or power users OneMethodology includes Process Flow training

Process Flow Training

Process flow training is designed to match the business process flows that a customer uses in their day-to-day
business.  One of the greatest advantages of J.D. Edwards’ education is the integration throughout our software
solutions. Users want to know how the various software features and functionality work in the context of their specific
business area, but they also need to understand how their decisions and actions will affect the processes that occur
before and after their business area. Process Flow Training provides this context and knowledge transfer.

Project Team Roles and Responsibilities

J.D.  Edwards  believes  that  having  the  right  people  engaged  in  the  implementation  process  significantly  impacts
successful adoption of the total solution. To help you identify the appropriate individuals, we’ve prepared the role and
responsibility descriptions below.

a)  Executive Sponsor

The Executive Sponsor is the senior customer employee who is ultimately responsible for the J.D. Edwards project.
The Executive  Sponsor will typically meet formally with the Steering Committee on a regular basis and has specific
responsibility for the overall success of the implementation.  This person:

•
•
•
•

Chairs the Steering Committee
Provides the vision and direction for the implementation team and is proactive in contact with the Project Manager
Resolves major issues and escalates problems to the Steering Committee when necessary
Commits the company to the appropriate dedication of resources with the appropriate knowledge

b)  The Steering Committee

The Steering Committee provides high-level direction for the project. Members usually include the Executive Sponsor,
Customer  Project  Manager,  prominent  business  unit/operations  staff,  and  the  J.D.  Edwards  Project  Manager.    The
Steering Committee:

•
•
•

Approves budget and scope of the project.
Sets the direction for the implementation and ensures that the direction is being carried out by the team.
Evaluates major scope/cost changes that occur during the project and approves the appropriate course of action.

c)  Customer Project Manager

This customer individual directs the day-to-day activities of the Project, coordinates the overall implementation effort
and assists in the preparation of status reports for the Steering Committee. This person also interacts with the team on
a daily basis and has specific responsibilities such as:

Ensures team members are spending the time they originally pledged to the team.
