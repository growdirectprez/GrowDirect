# The Challenge
To Define and Implement the most efficient organization model for the delivery of Appriss Retail Products
What is the organization model? What are the lines of reporting?
What are the lines of accountability from quote-to-contract-to-invoice? Things to consider as we develop this:
Who gets involved in underwriting a deal with a customer?
Who gets involved in defining what’s in the box and what’s outside the box (and therefore need specialized estimation of time and effort)?
Who defines timelines for delivery?
Who coordinates what goes into product releases (to the extent there’s stuff that’s customer-specific that’s needed)?
How do we manage quality and avoid another Ollie’s type incident?
How does standardization of data ingest and product itself (risk metrics, reports, etc.) modify the roles needed to implement?
How do we avoid needing 10 people in each meeting to get work done?
What can be done to automate some/most of the monitoring?
What are the escalation paths required such that we don’t end up creating highly manual processes where everyone is involved but nobody is accountable?
Based on demand forecasts, how many people do we actually need?
About 4 Verify/Incent projects
About 12 Secure projects between new accounts and conversions.
Assume that we have about 15 “delivery” projects and about $1M worth of SOWs spread in a proportion similar to what we have this year.
Based on the above, what is the right-sizing activity and how many people are affected? We also need to arrive at specific names such that we can model the budget impacts and work with Maryanne to determine the least disruptive method of making our transitions. As I’ve shared with you, we need to look for at least 10% efficiency in labor across the teams such that we can plough back half of it in future R&D activities.
Recommendations on timing – of when we’d make any org shifts, role shifts, etc.

# Infrastructure, ARDM and Project Management
All deployments of Appriss Retail Products require engagement with common core services within the business, these services are critical to the successful deployment of a solution
Infrastructure
The infrastructure team is responsible for providing the technical hardware and base software installations required to run products (Operating System, Databases, Virus Checkers etc)
For on-premise implementations the infrastructure team is responsible for providing customers with supportable hardware specifications
ARDM / Data Team
All structured data that is required for all products is ingested into the ARDM model by the Data Team
ARDM team provides data mapping services and delivers mapped data per data contract to each Appriss Retail application as needed
Project Management
All Appriss Retail projects require project management. Currently PM engagement styles are different between product lines which needs to be addressed
Throughout this presentation these teams are referenced across all process flows
