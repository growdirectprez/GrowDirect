2	Category Management
The concept of category management was first discussed in T&K’s Board Room following the D&H acquisition.
The senior management team felt that there were significant areas for improvement within the commercial department.  We had started to loose our way after two years of poor results and we really had to focus on getting the basics right.
The term Category Management means different things to every retailer.  Yes there are various formal definitions (for example the Partner Group approach).  But for us Category Management was a catalyst for educating our commercial teams in new and improved business methods and driving a change programme that included several new initiatives.
When we started (back in the summer of 1996) we were all very keen to understand what this concept of Category Management was and what it could mean to T&K’s. At that time I was the senior buyer for fashion.  The CEO tasked me with performing an operational review of our commercial department functions (buying, merchandising and inventory management), which I undertook supported by business consultants from PwC and senior managers from D&H.

As part of the Category Management initiative we have introduced a new business planning process.
Our buying teams are now required to demonstrate (via the merchandise plan) that the category offer will be capable of delivering T&K’s performance objectives.
The category controllers were keen supporters of this initiative as it places a degree of rigour on the planning process – and everybody works from the same base line.

 4	Range Development/Assortment Planning

At T&K’s we have refined our category offerings to reflect the demands of our customers.  We also have to ensure we meet our profit target, which is why it is so important to involve the category teams in the budget setting process.
The range development process does vary depending on the nature of each category and it’s positioning.

For Fashion:
Our aim is to provide an up-market own label ‘lifestyle’ brand for men, women and children.  We must link our offerings with the latest colours, fabrics and styles (recognising that our main target audience is in the 35-50 year age bracket – so only a limited percentage of our offering would be considered ‘high fashion’ by our competitors).  We aim to achieve an overall margin for the Fashion Category of 60% so we have to ensure we select and design the right types of products and minimise markdown whenever possible.
Like most fashion retailers we have to offer a wide variety of products throughout a season (Autumn/Winter and Spring/Summer).  We want to offer our customers variety and they expect to see new lines coming into our stores on a regular basis.
As a result, managing an ‘own-brand’ fashion category has some specific characteristics that need to be clearly understood when developing the range and assortments for our stores.

We are heavily involved in the design and manufacture of our product lines – we need to have very close links with our supplier base and visibility of our commitment to stock.
Over half of our lines are one-off designs that reflect (hopefully) a particular season’s trend with regards to colour, fabric and style – there will be a limited window of opportunity in which to sell these lines.
As a result, stock planning is critical.  If we under plan stock requirements we will miss out on good revenues, if we have too much stock (and/or get the fashion mix wrong) we may have to heavily mark down the retail price to ensure the stock is sold.
