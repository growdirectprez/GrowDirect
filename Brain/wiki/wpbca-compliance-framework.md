# Community of Abalone Cove (formerly WPBCA) Compliance Framework — MOC

This is the map-of-content for Community of Abalone Cove operational governance under California Davis-Stirling and the Nonprofit Mutual Benefit Corporation Law. Community of Abalone Cove (formerly West Portuguese Bend Community Association, "WPBCA") is one of three entities in the deliberately separated compensation structure; see [[foundation-legal-framework]] for the Foundation side and the CHOA / peer-HOA landscape.

All cards here are distilled working positions from 2026-04-19 advisor memos and from the community's current operational state. Final adoption requires a functional three-director board and HOA counsel review.

## Naming note

**Community of Abalone Cove** is the current legal name, filed with the California Secretary of State. The prior name, **West Portuguese Bend Community Association ("WPBCA")**, remains in historical references (1949 founding, 2009 Restated Declaration, 2012 Bylaws, prior-board history, legacy counsel). The rename was the previous board's last action, with quorum achieved on the governing vote, driven by the Portuguese Bend Abatement District forming — the community wanted its HOA name to disassociate from the landslide connotation. Immediately after the rename filing, the board turned over — the current 2-of-5 director situation is a consequence of that turnover.

Wiki filenames use the `wpbca-*` prefix for continuity and discoverability in the existing `Brain/wiki/` namespace. The content reflects the current legal name.

## Current State (as of 2026-04-19)

- **81 lots, ~81 voting memberships** (Tract 14649, 2009 Restated Declaration)
- **Originally incorporated 1949 as WPBCA**; bylaws amended & restated 2012 (77 pages, pre-Davis-Stirling recodification). Legally renamed **Community of Abalone Cove** with CA SOS by the previous board as its last action.
- **5 director seats per bylaws; only 2 currently filled** — board lacks quorum to vote on meaningful action
- **Dues < $20/month** — severely underfunded for modern operational needs
- **Two private ocean access easements (1952)** — rare and valuable asset; sensitive
- **Architectural Committee authority** preserved under 2009 Restated Declaration
- **CHOA participation**: board president confirmed participation in Rancho Palos Verdes Council of Homeowners Associations; CHOA is a peer forum (audience / partner / potential software sales target), not a governance body over Community of Abalone Cove
- **Threats:** Portuguese Bend landslide compliance (which gave rise to the Abatement District and the rename), Coastal Commission pressure, Builder's Remedy / Housing Element rezoning, underfunding

The board cannot adopt a binding new policy (payment, investment, anything) until it reaches quorum. That makes [[wpbca-third-director-recruitment]] the single gating blocker for every other track. Once quorum is restored, an early planned vote is the **Name-Change Acknowledgement Proposal** — a low-stakes governance-engine test vote that establishes the rename as the reference point going forward; see [[foundation-blockchain-proof-layer]].

## What the Foundation Does Not Do

The Abalone Cove Foundation does not:

- Exercise governance over WPBCA
- Hold, collect, or route WPBCA assessments
- Advocate for specific lot owners' positions on the ocean path easement
- Fund litigation on individual lot-level issues (e.g., 0 Clipper grading risk to neighboring walls)
- Replace WPBCA counsel or WPBCA board decisions

The Foundation does neutral documentation, inter-HOA convening, and procedural-support research. Clean separation is enforced through the [[foundation-bylaws]] and the conflict-of-interest policy.
