# COAC: RPV-Resident SCOTUS Petitions (1994, 1990s, 2001)

Three U.S. Supreme Court Petitions for Writ of Certiorari filed by Rancho Palos Verdes residents seeking review of California Court of Appeal rulings in favor of the City. Recovered from Internet Archive microfiche digitization. All likely denied cert (>99% denial rate is normal), but the **record of attempted challenges** is itself the documentation value.

These three petitions trace a 7+ year arc of RPV residents trying to use federal-constitutional vehicles to challenge city land-use authority — **prefiguring modern state-vs-city Builder's Remedy fights by 25 years**.

## The Three Cases

### 1. Stuart v. City of Rancho Palos Verdes (1994)

- **Filed:** May 31, 1994 (October Term 1993)
- **Petitioner:** Enrica Stuart, Narcissa Drive, RPV 90274 — **Pro Per**
- **Court below:** CA Court of Appeal, Second Appellate District
- **Questions presented (paraphrased from OCR):**
  1. Under Article VI [Supremacy Clause] of the U.S. Constitution, can local Rules of Court be used to limit intrinsic rights protected by the Constitution under Amendments [5, 7, 14]?
  2. Did the Court of Appeals err in denying Petitioner's review and permission to file notice-of-error correction?
  3. Did the CA Superior Court err in ignoring essential and substantive issues, agreeing by inference with Defendants who never stood for trial?
  4. Did the court err in basing its decision solely on issues submitted by Plaintiffs in the consolidated case, even though Petitioner had moved for bifurcation and pointed out divergent issues in opening?
  5. **Can local governments use the issue of litigation to hold secret meetings with private parties in order to devise [strategies] that could circumvent the law?**

The fifth question is striking — and **directly relevant to current Cove FPPC and transparency work** (cf. [`Cove/docs/archive/originals/litigation/2024-08-07-FPPC-Complaint-Cruikshank.pdf`](../../Cove/docs/archive/originals/litigation/2024-08-07-FPPC-Complaint-Cruikshank.pdf)). Stuart was raising the secret-meeting / collusion problem in 1994.

Petitioner address (Narcissa Drive) suggests Portuguese Bend / Vanderlip-Estate-area homeowner.

### 2. Teng Lee v. City of Rancho Palos Verdes

- **Filed:** Late 1990s or early 2000s (date OCR-mangled — verify against original)
- **Petitioner:** Teng Lee, Oceanaire Drive, RPV 90275 — **Pro Per**
- **Court below:** CA Court of Appeal, Second Appellate District, Division Six
- **Question presented (paraphrased):**
  > Whether [a] political subdivision of [a] state may maintain action under the Supremacy Clause [of] the United States Constitution [to] challenge state action that conflicts with federal law. Does [a] local government function [as a] Municipal Corporation [when it] violates its own Code, breaches legal procedure, refuses its duty…

Two-pronged: Supremacy Clause + own-Code-breach. Pro Per filing.

Petitioner address (Oceanaire Drive) is in the western coastal RPV area.

### 3. Echevarrieta v. City of Rancho Palos Verdes (2001)

- **Filed:** June 19, 2001
- **Petitioner:** Jon Echevarrieta
- **Counsel:** Robert Hampton Rogers, Sullivan, Workman & Dee LLP, 800 S. Figueroa St., 12th Floor, Los Angeles CA 90017
- **Court below:** CA Court of Appeal, Second Appellate District, Division Four
- **Questions presented (paraphrased from OCR):**
  1. Does the City of Rancho Palos [Verdes] enforcement [of its] view ordinance — which changes long-established state property and nuisance laws by requiring the destruction of thirty-year-old trees to grant a view to another property owner — constitute a **taking in violation of the Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments** of the United States [Constitution]?
  2. Does the City of Rancho Palos [Verdes] view ordinance reach **too far in its forty-year retroactive reach** and its intolerance for existing lawful conforming uses, thus violating the Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments [Due Process / Takings]?

The four-document bundle (Petition + Opposition Brief + Reply Brief + a 4th Petition copy) suggests this case got the most procedural attention of the three — possibly because it had counsel of record.

## Why This Matters for Cove

### Direct Linkage to Colyear 2024

Echevarrieta 2001 and *Colyear v. Rolling Hills Community Association* (B308382, March 1, 2024 — see [[cove-colyear-2024]]) are the **bookends of a 23-year arc of view-and-tree litigation on the Palos Verdes Peninsula**. Echevarrieta challenged a city ordinance; *Colyear* challenged a private CC&R. The two together delineate what's enforceable under each authority — and what isn't.

### Direct Linkage to Cove FPPC / Transparency Work

The 1994 Stuart "litigation-hold secret meetings between city and private parties" question is the same constitutional vector that the 2024 Cruikshank FPPC complaint and 2024-09-11 Civil Case (per `Cove/docs/archive/originals/litigation/`) advance.

### Supremacy Clause Foreshadowing of Builder's Remedy

Both Stuart and Teng Lee invoked the Supremacy Clause to argue **federal law limits city action**. Two decades later, the **state** is using the same federal-supremacy logic against the city via state Housing Element / Builder's Remedy frameworks. The structural argument is the same; only the party invoking it has changed.

### Pro Per Pattern

Two of the three petitions were filed Pro Per (Stuart + Teng Lee). This is significant for COAC: **the city's land-use posture has produced enough resident frustration to drive multiple Pro Per SCOTUS cert filings** — an expensive, low-probability move that residents made anyway. That historical record is part of the Foundation's narrative of community-vs-city tension predating Cove's current work.

## Strategic Use

1. **Restated Declaration recital framing**: Cite the 23-year arc (1994–2017+) of resident-vs-city friction as evidence that the city has not been a reliable steward of property-right interests, supporting the Foundation's role as an independent compliance and documentation authority.
2. **Litigation-strategy historical context**: Pull the Stuart and Echevarrieta full briefs before any view-ordinance / tree / takings filing. The CA Court of Appeal opinions they appealed from contain RPV-specific holdings worth extracting.
3. **Coastal Specific Plan and Zone 2 work**: Echevarrieta's takings arguments may inform any current Coastal Commission engagement on similar parcels.
