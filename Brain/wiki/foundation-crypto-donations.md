# Foundation Crypto Donations

The Abalone Cove Foundation can accept cryptocurrency donations as one supplemental funding stream, treated the same as any other non-cash gift (stocks, real property). It cannot become a "crypto-native" Foundation or tie donor influence to governance rights.

Distilled working position from a 2026-04-19 advisor memo. Adoption requires board approval of a Gift Acceptance Policy before the first crypto gift is received.

## Why This Matters Here

The founder is a long-time Bitcoin holder (mined in 2014 with cgminer on a Raspberry Pi). BTC long-term capital gains provide the personal runway that makes Foundation work possible at all. There is an authentic community of Bitcoin-aligned donors who support civic preservation and institutional-memory projects. This funding stream is real and worth capturing — carefully.

What it cannot do:
- The Foundation cannot promote Bitcoin
- The Foundation cannot function as a crypto project
- Crypto donations cannot be tied to governance rights, voting power, or influence over Community of Abalone Cove decisions
- Compensation from the Foundation cannot be tied to Bitcoin-related work

## IRS / Federal Tax Treatment

- Crypto is **property** for IRS purposes (Notice 2014-21, Rev. Rul. 2019-24)
- **Income recognition**: FMV on date of receipt (ordinary income to the Foundation)
- **Donor deductibility**: FMV if held >1 year, cost basis if ≤1 year; subject to standard 501(c)(3) percentage limits
- **Contemporaneous written acknowledgment** required (no goods/services in return)
- **Form 990 Schedule B** required if single-donor gift >$5,000
- **Form 990 Part VIII** for contributions
- **UBI concern** if Foundation holds crypto for investment and trades it regularly (not UBI if infrequent and not a trade or business)

## California State Treatment

- **FTB Form 199** reports contributions and gains/losses (California conforms to federal)
- **AG Registry of Charitable Trusts** — annual RRF-1 discloses non-cash contributions; large gifts may prompt AG inquiry on valuation + use of proceeds
- **Commercial fundraiser rules** — if a crypto payment processor/DAO platform could be viewed as soliciting in CA, registration may be required
- **Money transmitter / DFPI** — accepting and converting occasionally does not typically make the Foundation a money transmitter under Fin. Code § 2300; a recurring crypto donation portal + significant treasury holding would change that

## Mandatory Gift Acceptance Policy

Adopted by the board at the first organizational meeting, before any crypto gift is received. Required elements:

- **Board approval threshold**: gifts >$10,000 require board approval before acceptance
- **Mandatory conversion**: crypto converts to USD within 24–48 hours of receipt unless the board specifically approves holding for mission-aligned reasons (rare)
- **Valuation method**: professional valuation at receipt (Coinbase, Gemini, or qualified appraiser); FMV on receipt date
- **Processor**: route all crypto donations through a California-compliant processor that handles conversion to USD and issues proper acknowledgments — candidates: Coinbase Commerce, BitPay, The Giving Block
- **Conflict-of-interest review** for large donors (threshold TBD, e.g., >$50k)
- **Custody rules**: never hold significant crypto on Foundation wallets long-term; use licensed custodian or immediate-conversion processor

## Operational Safeguards

- **Separate bank account**: crypto-converted USD flows into the Foundation's EIN-tied business checking account (distinct from Community of Abalone Cove's and the LLC's)
- **Accrual-basis accounting**: FMV income recognized on receipt date; subsequent gain/loss on conversion tracked separately
- **Audit trail**: wallet addresses (redacted as needed for donor privacy), transaction hashes, conversion receipts retained for Form 990 and AG filings
- **D&O and cyber insurance review**: confirm coverage given crypto volatility and hacking risks
- **Donor communications**: IRS-compliant written acknowledgment; warn donor that crypto is treated as property for their own tax reporting

## What Crypto Proceeds Can Fund

Legitimate charitable uses aligned with the Foundation's exempt purpose:

- Library of Evidence (research, transcription, scanning, archival)
- cove.org and abalonecove.org hosting and editorial content
- Trail restoration agreements (if the Foundation takes on this stewardship role)
- Olmsted digital overlay work
- Creek conservation easement documentation (toward PVPLC)
- Fire Station 53 relocation planning research
- Deferred Governance Engine development (but only as Foundation-commissioned educational infrastructure, not private benefit to the LLC — see [[foundation-three-hat-compensation]] arm's-length licensing rules)

## What Crypto Proceeds Cannot Fund

- Community of Abalone Cove assessments, governance, or treasury (see [[wpbca-investment-policy]] — HOA treasury stays in insured stable assets)
- Private benefit to the founder or the LLC
- Bitcoin-advocacy work
- Ongoing speculative crypto holding

## Bylaws Language

The bylaws authorize acceptance at a high level:

> "The Corporation may accept gifts, donations, and grants of any form, including cash, securities, real property, and virtual currency, subject to a Gift Acceptance Policy approved by the Board."

The operational detail lives in the Gift Acceptance Policy (a separate board-adopted document).

## 1023-EZ Reference

The 1023-EZ narrative should mention: "The Corporation expects to receive gifts of various forms, including cryptocurrency, subject to a Board-adopted Gift Acceptance Policy that requires prompt conversion to U.S. dollars and professional valuation." This shows thoughtful oversight without committing to detailed operational terms that may evolve.

## Open Decisions

- Processor selection (Coinbase Commerce vs. BitPay vs. The Giving Block vs. another)
- Board-approval threshold (advisor suggests $10k; adjust if preferred)
- Whether to accept during the 501(c)(3) pending period before IRS determination letter
- Specific donor-recognition practices (tiered acknowledgment, disclosure policies)
