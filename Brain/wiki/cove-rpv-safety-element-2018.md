# COAC: RPV 2018 Safety Element — Annotated for Cove

Adopted September 2018. The City of Rancho Palos Verdes' current Safety Element, distilled for the provisions that bind or affect WPBCA parcels on Tract 14649 and the surrounding Portuguese Bend / Abalone Cove landslide complex.

Full extraction at [`Cove/docs/archive/originals/city-records/general-plan/X-safety-element.md`](../../Cove/docs/archive/originals/city-records/general-plan/X-safety-element.md) (48 pages, ~2,150 lines of OCR text).

## Document Provenance

- **Adopted:** September 2018 (cover page of element, page 5 of extraction)
- **Predecessor framework:** Cities of RPV and Rolling Hills Estates Joint Hazards Mitigation Plan (2004 original, 2014 update) per FEMA definition of hazard mitigation
- **Place in the GP:** Part of the 2018 General Plan suite; companion to the Conservation and Open Space Element (which contains the geologic-profile background referenced throughout)

## Why It Matters for Cove

The 2018 Safety Element is the city's current, authoritative map of its own hazard posture. Every conclusion in the 1975 GP + 1990/2001 HEs about landslide and coastal hazard was **reaffirmed and updated** here. For WPBCA this means:

1. The 1956 Portuguese Bend landslide and its active / dormant / possible / non-landslide delineations are mapped as **Figure 3 — Landslide Inventory**, with CGS 2007 as source.
2. The 40-foot bluff setback (Declaration One-A, 1950) is validated by §5.9 Coastal Cliff Retreat.
3. Fire Hazard Severity Zone overlays apply to the upland margin of WPBCA parcels (§3, Figure 1).
4. Emergency access obligations (§8.6 + Figure 5) apply to cul-de-sacs and narrow streets — Sea Cove, Packet, Barkentine, Clipper, Peppertree.

See [[cove-rpv-planning-framework-2026]] for how this integrates with the General Plan and Housing Element.

## §5 Geologic Hazards — The Core for Cove

### §5.1–5.2 Seismic Hazards and Active / Potentially Active Faults

Two faults on the Peninsula: **Palos Verdes Fault** (NW–SE, steep SW dip, reverse with minor right-lateral strike-slip) and **Cabrillo Fault** (potentially active, NW–SE, Cabrillo Beach to Peninsula center).

**Table 1 — Faults in the Region** (verbatim critical rows):

| Fault | Dist. (mi) | Max Magnitude (Mw) | Peak Site Accel. (g) | Modified Mercalli Intensity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Palos Verdes** | 1–4 | 7.3 | **0.691** | **XI** |
| Newport-Inglewood | 10 | 7.1 | 0.337 | IX |
| Puente Hills Blind Thrust | 19 | 7.1 | 0.264 | IX |
| Santa Monica | 22 | 6.6 | 0.181 | VIII |
| San Andreas | 57 | 8.0 | 0.152 | VIII |

Palos Verdes Fault — *1 to 5 mm/yr slip rate (USGS 1999); maximum credible magnitude 7.3 (Petersen et al. 1996). Maximum credible earthquake scenario: $30B in building damage, 80–1,050 deaths, 2,400–19,000 injuries across Southern California (OES 2007).*

**Strategic read:** The Palos Verdes Fault is the dominant seismic hazard. Modified Mercalli XI = "few (masonry) structures remain standing. Bridges destroyed. Rails bent greatly." This is the baseline the city acknowledges for construction in the immediate Peninsula area — including Tract 14649.

### §5.3 Landslides

**Two major landslide systems** on the Peninsula grouped as complex coalescing landslides:

1. **Portuguese Bend** — "the most studied and publicized landslide in the area, and perhaps in the Los Angeles Basin." Mapped as a large ancient complex from ridge top to ocean. The most recent movement began in 1956, apparently as the result of grading operations, and involved movement in about one-third of the system. Includes Abalone Cove and Portuguese Bend Landslides.
2. **South Shores** — last movement ~±16,200 years ago. Apparently at equilibrium but renewed activity may occur if conditions are modified. Tarapaca landslide (eastern flank) triggered by San Ramon Canyon erosion.

Source: City of Rancho Palos Verdes & CGS Landslide Inventory Map of the Palos Verdes Peninsula, 2007.

**The upper limit of the landslide is under debate** (verbatim finding): the Landslide Inventory Map places the ancient scarp at the **Valley View Graben adjacent to Crest Road**; earlier maps (Dibblee 1999 and others) placed it further downslope. This residual uncertainty is itself a citable fact for any argument requiring precise landslide-boundary delineation.

**Infrastructure exposure (verbatim):** "In the lower portion of the landslide, Palos Verdes Drive South traverses the landslide along with water and sewer lines. The roadway and pipelines are under constant scrutiny to determine areas in need of repair… The risk to the roadway and pipelines is significant should portions of the Portuguese Bend landslide accelerate."

### §5.4 Liquefaction

"The potential for liquefaction in Rancho Palos Verdes is very low since the local soil deposits are relatively thin and cohesive and groundwater is usually at depth. Liquefaction is not considered to be a significant hazard in the City." Mapped liquefaction zones (Figure 4) are east of the PV Reservoir, shoreline of Royal Palm Beach Park, and shoreline adjacent to some beach areas.

### §5.5–5.8 Debris Flows, Expansive Soils, Settlement / Subsidence

Standard content; debris-flow threshold of 10 inches seasonal rainfall or 2 inches in a 6-hour intense event. Flow velocities typically 10–20 mph, rarely >100 mph locally.

### §5.9 Coastal Cliff Retreat

The element acknowledges coastal bluff retreat as an ongoing hazard. **This validates the 40-foot bluff setback in Declaration One-A (1950) for Lots 1–5 of Tract 14649** ([[cove-legal-framework]]). The city's own Safety Element recognition of the hazard means any attempt to weaken that setback runs into the city's own document.

### §7.1 Falling from Coastal Cliffs

Signage and fence obligations adjacent to public trails. Private bridle-trail easement implications — see [[wpbca-ocean-path-easement]] (private governance matter).

## §3 Wildland Fires — Fire Hazard Severity Zone

Figure 1 maps the Fire Hazard Severity Zone (FHSZ). WPBCA parcels along the upland margin fall within **Very High FHSZ**. Obligations under §3 + Policy 4:

- **Defensible space** coordination with LA County Fire Department Prevention Services
- **Fuel modification** actively implemented and enforced on properties in Very High FHSZ
- **Stringent site-design and maintenance criteria** (§2 Policy 9) for high fire hazard areas
- **Expedited reviews** for reconstruction of fire-damaged structures (§2 Policy 5)
- **Emergency access** to all streets including cul-de-sacs (§2 Policy 11)
- **Water flow capabilities** with adequate back-up (§2 Policy 7)

**Strategic read:** fuel-modification obligations are legally enforceable on private property in Very High FHSZ areas. ARC decisions on landscape design in upland WPBCA areas should incorporate these criteria.

## §8 Emergency Services — Infrastructure Dependencies

- **§8.5 Fire Protection** (Table 6): LA County Fire Department service from Station 53 (adjacent to WPBCA per [[cove-pvplc-partnership]] reference)
- **§8.4 Police Protection** (Table 5): LA County Sheriff response times
- **§8.6 Disaster Preparedness** + **Figure 5 Disaster Routes**: Evacuation and return routing. PVDS is a primary route; its landslide vulnerability (§5.3) is the critical weak point
- **Water company** (§3 / §5.1): Electronic telemetric monitoring of reservoir capacity, pressure, and distribution. Emergency contingency center coordinates with LA County Office of General Management and Southern California Region Emergency Centers. Large generators transported to lift stations during power outages
- **§8.7 Emergency Communications**: Coordination standard

## Cove Strategic Implications

### 1. The 40-Foot Bluff Setback Is Aligned with Municipal Policy

Declaration One-A (1950) predates the Safety Element by 68 years. The Safety Element §5.9 validates the setback as consistent with municipal coastal-cliff-retreat findings. This is defensive asset: the private restriction and the city's own finding point the same way.

### 2. Fire Fuel-Modification Obligations Override ARC Aesthetic Preferences

Where the two conflict, fuel modification under Very High FHSZ wins. ARC decisions on landscape should defer to Policy 4 coordination with LA County Fire.

### 3. *Albers* Inverse-Condemnation Vector Remains Live

The element's admission that infrastructure in the landslide area is "under constant scrutiny" and that "risk to the roadway and pipelines is significant should portions of the Portuguese Bend landslide accelerate" keeps R9 from [[cove-case-law-lessons]] live: any public-improvement action that triggers further movement creates a fresh *Albers v. County* claim. The city is on notice.

### 4. The Upper Landslide Boundary Uncertainty Is a Protective Fact

The explicit acknowledgement that "the upper limit of the landslide has been under debate for many years" — Valley View Graben vs. lower-slope interpretations (Dibblee 1999) — means the city cannot quietly push the landslide boundary downslope to free up more parcels for upzoning. The element itself preserves the uncertainty.

### 5. Palos Verdes Fault Risk Is on the City's Record

Modified Mercalli XI at 0.691g PGA is the design baseline the city acknowledges. This is relevant for any seismic-hazard disclosure requirement, insurance posture, or member expectation-setting.
