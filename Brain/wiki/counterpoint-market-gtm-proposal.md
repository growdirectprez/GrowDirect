# NCR Counterpoint Market — GTM Proposal

## Summary

Three vertical pillars carry the Counterpoint market entry: **Lawn & Garden** (Armstrong as the lead proof case), **Farm / Ranch / Firearms** (Murdoch's-class regional chains), and **Regulated Beverage** (state-controlled liquor systems and large independent fine-wine operators). All three sit on NCR Counterpoint or its near-substitutes. All three share a common failure mode: complex assortment + multi-state regulation + aging back-office infrastructure + tribal knowledge that walks out the door each turnover cycle. ALX's value lands in every one — the role being the same (Virtual Store Manager), the domain knowledge vault being different.

The GTM motion is **VAR co-sell** through Counterpoint resellers (Rapid POS, Rapid Garden POS, Rapid Gun Systems, plus state-vertical specialists). The wedge engagement is the **four-week audit / health check**; the conversion is **transformation engagement** delivered by the SI partner; the resting state is **operating-system mode** with Canary as the retail backbone.

This document applies the three-phase consumer positioning playbook (PwC Guess?, Inc. 2001 model) at the market level, then identifies the three target archetypes that exercise the full capability set.

## Phase 1 — Customer / Competition / Core Merchandise (Market-Level)

### Core customer

The Counterpoint specialty SMB retailer is **private, regional, multi-store, vertical-deep, regulator-aware**. Annual revenue $5M–$400M (a wide band that the platform addresses across its three engagement modes). Almost always wearing every hat — the owner is also the buyer, the LP manager, the IT decision-maker, the finance lead, the workforce scheduler. They have NCR Counterpoint because they bought it five to fifteen years ago when it was the right answer for a chain like theirs and have not had the budget or the bandwidth to migrate.

The **decision unit** is small: owner-operator at the smallest end (sub-$10M, single-region), owner + ops director at mid scale ($10–50M, multi-store), owner + ops + IT director + CFO at the largest end ($50–400M, multi-state). VAR relationships are warm at every scale; the merchant trusts the VAR more than they trust software vendors.

The **psychographic** is consistent: practical, tradition-respecting, brand-loyal to vendors who have served them well, allergic to vaporware and consultant-speak. They have been pitched on every retail trend of the last twenty years — RFID, AI, mobile POS, omnichannel — and most of it did not land. The pitch that lands is **infrastructure displacement** ("the server in the closet goes away") more than feature parity.

### Core competition

The competition is not other retail platforms. The competition is **the status quo plus three years of patches**. Every Counterpoint customer is in soft conversation with the cloud-native disruptors (Square for Retail, Shopify POS, Lightspeed) but the migration cost — data, training, integrations, custom workflows — is the moat that keeps Counterpoint sticky. Canary does not compete with Square or Lightspeed for the same merchant; it competes with **doing nothing for another two years** and with **the next round of Counterpoint customization spend** that the merchant was about to authorize.

Within the Counterpoint installed base, Canary's competitive set is:
- **Counterpoint customizations** the VAR has already quoted (forecasting plug-ins, LP modules, BI overlays)
- **Generic SMB analytics** (Looker / Metabase / Domo dashboards bolted on)
- **Consulting engagements** that diagnose but don't deliver platform
- **Wait-and-see** as a default

ALX changes the conversation by replacing the Counterpoint backoffice rather than overlaying on it. The merchant gets **a platform, not another module**.

### Core merchandise

What the merchant pays for, in priority order:

1. **Loss prevention attribution** — shrink stops being a black box. Specific events, specific evidence, specific accountability. Highest pain at the largest scales.
2. **OTB enforcement as a wallet** — overspend becomes mathematically prevented, not policy-prohibited. Highest pain in chains with multiple buyers.
3. **Vendor scorecard automation** — chargeback recovery, missed-discount capture, co-op claim filing. Direct money on the table for thin-margin operators.
4. **Item authorization across regulatory zones** — firearms, alcohol, OTC pharma, plant quarantines. Foundational for any vertical with multi-state regulation.
5. **Receipt chain anchoring** — externally verifiable evidence for audits, insurers, lenders, courts. Material differentiator for regulator-heavy verticals.
6. **Domain knowledge vault** — 100+ years of vertical expertise in a queryable format. Stops the seasonal-staff knowledge bleed.
7. **Real-time KPIs surfaced as exceptions** — dashboards become exception queues, not weekly reports.

Different verticals weight these differently — the GTM positions to the dominant pain in each.

## Phase 2B — What the Customer Actually Wants (Compressed)

In the agent-assisted-delivery posture, Phase 2B compresses from 8 weeks to days. Direct primary research with the three lead-target prospects (Armstrong, Murdoch's, state liquor archetype) substitutes for traditional focus-group methodology.

What is consistent across all three target prospects when the conversation surfaces the actual pain:

1. **Backoffice elimination is the headline.** Every prospect has a server-in-a-closet they can't talk about and a quarterly-IT-fee they can't justify. Lead with **infrastructure displacement**, not feature parity.
2. **The audit-trail story lands hardest where regulators land hardest.** Liquor (state auditor) > Firearms (ATF + state ABC) > Garden (plant quarantine occasional). The receipt-chain-as-evidence pitch is differential, not universal.
3. **The "knowledge vault" story is uniquely powerful for vertical-deep operators.** Armstrong (136 years of horticulture) and Murdoch's (40+ years of farm/ranch/firearms operating expertise) get the appeal immediately. Generic SMB retailers don't — they don't have the depth to vault.
4. **The OTB-as-wallet story is universal and underweighted.** Every prospect has been burned by OTB overrun; nobody is asking for a software fix because they don't think one exists. Surface this; it converts.
5. **VAR-led delivery is non-negotiable.** Direct sale to the merchant has zero conversion. The merchant trusts their VAR; the VAR is the channel.

## Pricing Posture — Market-Level

Two-component model:

| Component | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Base subscription** | Per-store predictable monthly fee. Floor that covers the platform's required core (RaaS chain, agent topology, KPI surfacing, multi-tenant infrastructure). |
| **Meter on payroll-to-revenue ratio** | Variable component tied to operational improvement. When the ratio improves (fewer labor hours per dollar of sales, tighter scheduling, better shrink recovery, tighter OTB), the meter earns. When it doesn't, the meter doesn't run hard. |

**Why payroll-to-revenue:** It's the one ratio that touches every module. Labor scheduling (L), shrink recovery (Q), OTB efficiency (M), margin on receipts (V) — every module's work either improves or degrades the ratio. This aligns Canary's revenue with merchant operational efficiency. The platform only wins when the merchant wins.

**The franchise application:** Franchisors / corporate parents set the payroll/revenue target as a brand standard. The meter enforces it commercially. Units that hit the target pay the base plan. Units that beat it earn credits. Units that miss it pay the overage.

Specific tiers, base prices, and meter rates are negotiated per channel partner. Sub-$10M private retailer pricing is materially different from $400M-class enterprise pricing.

## VAR Channel — Why This Is Not a Direct-Sale Motion

The Counterpoint installed base is gated by VAR relationships. Cold direct-to-merchant outreach has zero conversion. Every commercial path runs through a Counterpoint VAR (Rapid POS, Rapid Garden POS, Rapid Gun Systems, AMS Retail, C&K Systems, Mariner POS, RBMS, NCC, regional independents).

The VAR motion structure:

| Stage | Canary role | VAR role |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead identification** | Provides vertical-specific positioning content + ICP archetypes | Brings the merchant relationship + warm trust |
| **Audit / health check** | Delivers the four-week diagnostic and the documented baseline | Co-introduces, sits on the kickoff, owns the merchant relationship |
| **Transformation engagement** | Provides the platform + the SDD library + agent topology | Owns delivery; Canary supports |
| **Operating-system mode** | Platform runs continuously | VAR retains the merchant relationship and the customer-success role |

**Commercial structure:** VAR receives a recurring revenue share on the platform subscription + delivery margin on the transformation engagement. Specific economics negotiated per VAR.

**Why VARs say yes:** they get a productized methodology (audit + transformation + operating system) with a known scope, known timeline, known platform. They convert their existing book of Counterpoint customers into a platform-supported recurring revenue stream. Their delivery margin on transformation engagements is high; their support burden on operating-system mode is low.
