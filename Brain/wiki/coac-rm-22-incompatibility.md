# RM-22 Incompatibility — Peninsula Precedent Record

**RM-22** is the City of Rancho Palos Verdes' multi-family residential zoning tool used to satisfy state-mandated density calculations in the 2021-2029 Housing Element. When applied to the 1-acre west-of-creek bench at 0 Clipper Road (APN 7573-006-024) via the April 16, 2024 City Council vote, it creates documented incompatibility with the underlying geology, the surrounding single-family fabric, and the original Vanderlip master plan. This card documents peninsula RM-22 precedents, the City's "it's only for calculations" assurance pattern, and why the reactivation is needed to correct the drift.

**Not legal advice.** Distilled from a 2026-04-19 external-advisor memo. For internal strategic reference.

## The April 16, 2024 Vote

On April 16, 2024, the RPV City Council unanimously adopted the Revised Final 2021-2029 Housing Element and advanced related zoning overlays, including:

- **0 Clipper Road** (APN 7573-006-024, Housing Element Site #16) rezoned from RS-4 (single-family residential, 4 units/acre) to **RM-22** (multi-family residential, up to 22 units/acre)
- Related zoning overlays across multiple Housing Element sites
- Despite geologic concerns raised by residents and documented in public comment
- 43 speakers recorded at the meeting
- Heavily influenced by state mandates, HCD pressure, and threats of Builder's Remedy litigation

The site is the 1-acre west-of-creek bench:

- **Surface expression of the fault-line shelf edge**
- 30° alluvial runoff from Upper Barkentine (tide pools + fossils at ~800 ft)
- Inside the Abalone Cove Landslide Abatement District (ACLAD) footprint
- Below the 2009 WPBCA Restated Declaration area (though in the adjacent Lot H burdened land)

**Pending applications:** 16-unit proposal filed under the rezoning. Pending planning-department review.

## Recent City Actions (2024–2026)

- Housing Element site retained despite geologic concerns — City cited fear of Builder's Remedy preemption as reason not to remove the site
- Related ordinances restricting new construction in high-risk landslide zones — but allowing repairs within existing footprints — have been discussed and in some cases adopted
- ACLAD continues operating ~22 dewatering wells
- City road-repair budget in the landslide complex: ~$1M annually
- Slide movement data (early March 2026): Abalone Cove decelerating to ~2.14 inches/week; Portuguese Bend ~1.37 inches/week

The tension is visible in the public record: the City simultaneously enacts moratoria or restrictions in some parts of the landslide complex while pushing RM-22 density on adjacent unstable parcels to satisfy Housing Element compliance.

## "It's Only for Calculations" — The Assurance Pattern

In past RM-22 or equivalent higher-density rezonings on the peninsula, the City has assured residents with framings like:

- "This is primarily for Housing Element compliance."
- "It won't actually result in that level of intensity."
- "The zoning is a density calculation; actual applications will be reviewed case-by-case."

**The track record contradicts these assurances** once applications are filed. State law (SB 330, Builder's Remedy, density-bonus provisions, SB 9) sharply limits local discretion after rezoning. Vested-rights claims by applicants further reduce the City's ability to deny or substantially modify projects on density grounds.

The assurance pattern has a predictable arc:

1. **Pre-rezoning** — City signals that rezoning is "just for calculations"
2. **Rezoning** — Council adopts the higher-density designation under state pressure
3. **Application filed** — Developer submits maximum-density proposal
4. **Review phase** — City's ability to deny on density grounds is constrained by state law
5. **Approval or litigation** — Project approved in whole or substantial part, or litigation follows
6. **Built environment** — Out-of-scale development that alters neighborhood character, stresses infrastructure, introduces new risks in geologically sensitive locations

The 1-acre shelf-edge bench is at stage 3 (application filed). Absent the reactivation, the arc continues to stages 4–6.

## Peninsula Precedents

### Palos Verdes Bay Club

A 240-unit condominium complex at 32821 Seagate Drive. Enabled by higher-density zoning overlays. Outcomes:

- **Bulk and scale** dwarfing surrounding single-family homes
- **Parking and traffic** impacts on narrow coastal roads
- **Architectural style** clashing with the peninsula's traditional low-profile, view-preserving aesthetic
- Marketed as "casual coastal living"; resident experience is a large, multi-story structure that fundamentally changed the character of its section of coastline

**Relevance to 0 Clipper:** A precedent for what happens when density overlays are applied to coastal peninsula land. The scale mismatch and aesthetic incompatibility at the Bay Club directly parallel what would occur at 0 Clipper under RM-22 density.

### Trump National Area Overlays

Zoning overlays and density calculations have been debated in the context of Trump National Golf Club properties. While Trump National itself secured open-space protections on portions of its land (e.g., the 2015 conservation-easement donation to preserve trails and habitat), the broader pattern around its boundaries shows that:

- Once rezoned, sites become magnets for maximum-intensity proposals
- Unit count takes priority over site-specific compatibility
- Conservation easements protect specific parcels but don't prevent intensification on adjacent land
- The City's assurance pattern (same as above) has been observed here too

**Relevance to 0 Clipper:** Even with nearby conservation protection (PVPLC-adjacent land, ACLAD footprint, Coastal Specific Plan Subregion 4), RM-22 density on the 1-acre bench can proceed because none of those protections directly bind the rezoned parcel.

### Silver Spur Canyon (Adjacent Precedent)

A 482-unit / up to 11-story proposal by developer Akhilesh Jha / Verdes Estates at/near 5323 Ironwood Street. Contested by **Neighborhood Voices of Silver Spur Inc.** (NVSS, `savethecanyon.net`) — 400–450 neighbors mobilized. See [[coac-yimby-opposition-landscape]] for the full NVSS context and YIMBY response.

**Relevance to 0 Clipper:** A parallel landslide-prone, geologically constrained canyon area in the same city. The NVSS experience shows:
- Neighborhood opposition can slow projects but often faces Builder's Remedy / YIMBY pushback
- The public zoning process (not the private covenant layer) is the terrain where the fight has been waged
- Absent a recorded private-covenant overlay, neighborhood opposition relies on public-law tools that state housing mandates increasingly constrain

The CoAC reactivation is designed to add the private-covenant layer that NVSS-style opposition alone cannot provide.

## Why RM-22 Is Specifically Incompatible at 0 Clipper

### Geologic

- Surface expression of the fault-line shelf edge
- 30° eroding hillside runoff from Upper Barkentine
- Inside the ACLAD abatement footprint
- Historical geologic reports (USGS 1946, Ehlig 1982, LGC Valley 2011) consistently identify this corridor as an avoidance area
- Intensification converts a natural avoidance corridor into a future public liability

### Zoning / Planning

- Violates the original Vanderlip master plan's single-family and drainage protections (surviving in Declarations 100/101)
- Violates the 1978 Coastal Specific Plan Subregion 4 spirit, even where technical compliance is claimed
- Creates visual and safety impacts incompatible with surrounding Tract 14649 single-family homes
- Bulk and scale dwarf existing neighborhood fabric (per Bay Club precedent)

### Legal

- Private equitable servitudes (revived via the Restated Declaration) run with the land and are not automatically preempted by state housing mandates (Nahrstedt v. Lakeside Village; Citizens for Covenant Compliance v. Anderson)
- The Restated Declaration's surviving covenants — single-family residential use, density caps, natural drainage preservation, setbacks, Art Jury review — apply concurrently with RM-22 zoning
- Any RM-22-enabled project must still comply with the revived private covenants
- Enforcement path: Trustee demand letters, ARC review denial, private litigation to enforce the equitable servitudes

### Cost / Public Liability

- Unchecked density on unstable terrain → future public remediation exposure (multi-million-dollar)
- Covenant-enforced open space + ACLAD partnership → lower long-term cost, higher long-term safety
- Public-access geologic interpretive overlook ("everyone can use") aligns with community benefit without increasing risk

## The Dual-Mandate Trap

California land-use in 2026 operates under a **dual mandate**:

- **Sacramento** — demands maximum housing production (Housing Element, Builder's Remedy, SB 9/10, density bonuses)
- **Local government** — claims "stewardship" (geologic safety, coastal resources, neighborhood character, public safety)

The City cannot credibly claim both when it rezones the 1-acre shelf-edge bench to RM-22 under state pressure while ACLAD operates ~22 dewatering wells on the same landslide complex.

Naive to expect the City to resolve the dual mandate in favor of stewardship without the private-covenant layer. The reactivation is the non-naive response — a recorded-title reset that adds an enforceable layer the state housing mandates do not preempt.

## What the Restated Declaration Recital Says About RM-22

Proposed language for inclusion in the Restated Declaration:

> "**WHEREAS** the City of Rancho Palos Verdes rezoned portions of the original Declaration 100 boundary, including APN 7573-006-024 (the '1-acre west-of-creek bench' at 0 Clipper Road), from single-family residential to RM-22 multi-family residential on April 16, 2024, under state Housing Element mandates;
>
> **WHEREAS** the surviving Declaration 100 and 101 covenants — including single-family residential use, density caps, natural drainage preservation, setbacks, and Art Jury review — run with the land as equitable servitudes and are not automatically preempted by state housing mandates (see Nahrstedt v. Lakeside Village (1994) 8 Cal.4th 361; Citizens for Covenant Compliance v. Anderson (1995) 12 Cal.4th 345);
>
> **WHEREAS** the parcels within the burdened land (as defined by the Boundary Recital) include parcels affected by the April 16, 2024 rezoning, including but not limited to APN 7573-006-024;
>
> **NOW, THEREFORE**, the Trustees affirm that the revived private covenants operate concurrently with — and independent from — the City's zoning designations. Any application for development within the burdened land must comply with both the current municipal zoning and the revived private covenants. Where the private covenants impose stricter requirements, the private covenants control as equitable servitudes running with the land."
