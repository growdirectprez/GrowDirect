# Declaration No. 100 — General Plan of Restrictions

Declaration No. 100 is the 1929 constitutional framework Palos Verdes Corporation used to impose a complete private municipal government across the land it then owned. Recorded October 7, 1929 (Book 9436, Pages 155-178, LA County Recorder). It predates any city incorporation on the peninsula by a decade or more.

> **What Declaration 100 actually covers is not settled.** The property-description page (page 155) is partially illegible on the county recorder's microfilm — the one page that defines exactly which land is bound. The working assumption, not a proven fact, is that Declaration 100 covers all Palos Verdes Corporation land as of 1929 *except* areas already under Declaration 1 (Palos Verdes Estates, Miraleste) and named tracts that had their own declarations (Rolling Hills and possibly portions of what became Rolling Hills Estates). The distances visible on page 155 (2,304.72 ft, 1,779.84 ft, 2,209.30 ft) suggest full-coastline coverage, consistent with a superset read — but "suggests" is not "proves." Determining the actual boundary is one of the motivations for the Abalone Cove Foundation 501(c)(3): to do the archival work and provide institutional cover while it is done. See [[abalonecove-org]] and [[foundation-legal-framework]].

- **Recorded:** October 7, 1929 (filed October 10, 1929, 8:31 AM)

- **Signed by:** Jay Lawyer (Vice-President) and Donald K. Lawyer (Assistant Secretary) of Palos Verdes Corporation, a Delaware corporation
- **Notarized by:** Nellie Grace Frantz, LA County (commission expiring May 8, 1932)
- **Filed at request of:** Security Title Insurance & Guaranty Co.
- **Recording fee:** $19.70, Copy #908

## Place in the Declaration Scheme

The Palos Verdes Corporation created a layered system of private land restrictions:

- **Declaration 1 (1923)** — Covered Palos Verdes Estates and Miraleste, the first areas developed and sold
- **Declaration 100 (1929)** — Covered the remaining land: Lot H, the undeveloped "white space" on the PVE development map. This is the document described here.
- **Declaration 101 and Declaration 150** — Implementing instruments under 100. They assign specific parcels to specific use classes (A through K) defined in Declaration 100's Article IV. Without 100, the class designations in 101/150 have no meaning.

Declaration 100 is the parent document. It defines the governance bodies, the zoning classes, the enforcement mechanisms, and the duration rules. The later declarations plug individual tracts into that framework.

## The Smeared Page Problem

Page 155 — the property description page — is partially illegible on the county recorder's microfilm. The county recorder was unable to produce a clean copy. This is the one page that defines exactly what land Declaration 100 covers.

Fragments visible on the damaged page:

- References to **Lot H** and the **Bixby partition** (Case 2373)
- Bearings and long distances (2,304.72 feet, 1,779.84 feet, 2,209.30 feet — distances suggesting the full coastline, not a subset)
- **Mean High Tide Line of the Pacific Ocean**, with a general easterly direction along said line
- **Triangulation Station "Redondo"**
- References to Rancho Palos Verdes and Malo Pinito Ranch
- An acreage figure (illegible)
- A "true point of beginning"

The significance: 22 of 23 pages are legible. The one page that would confirm the exact boundary of Declaration 100 — and therefore whether any specific parcel (0 Clipper Road, specific lots in what became Rolling Hills Estates, any number of others) falls within it — is the one that is damaged.

This is not a minor technicality. Until page 155 is recovered from an alternate source (clean microfilm from a different repository, a certified copy retained by a title company, a recorded reference in a later instrument that quotes the property description in full), every claim about which land is or is not subject to Declaration 100 is inference from surrounding evidence — signers' intent, later implementing declarations (101, 150), the 1930 Filiorum grant deed, and the geometric plausibility of the partially-visible distances. The Foundation's archival work is aimed in part at resolving this.

## Structure and Content

### Article I: General Basic Restrictions (Sections 1-16)

Thirteen substantive sections establishing baseline restrictions across all of Lot H:

- **Section 1** — Prohibited uses: saloons, foundries, cemeteries, tuberculosis facilities, detention homes, explosives manufacturing, oil refineries, fish canneries, slaughterhouses, and any noxious trade
- **Section 2** — Racial covenants restricting ownership and occupancy to white/Caucasian persons (void and unenforceable under Shelley v. Kraemer, 1948, and the Fair Housing Act, 1968; remains in the text as historical artifact)
- **Section 3** — Oil drilling prohibition: no derricks, no extraction of oil, gas, petroleum, asphaltum, or hydrocarbon products
- **Section 4** — Grants enforcement power to Community Association of Palos Verdes and the Art Jury
- **Section 5** — Livestock and poultry subject to Association regulation
- **Section 6** — New materials only; no secondhand construction; no moving buildings onto the property; diligent prosecution of construction required
- **Section 7** — Split lot ownership rules for resubdivision (minimum 50-foot average width)
- **Section 8** — Minimum cost of improvements tied to July 1, 1929 construction costs; mandatory 3% landscaping expenditure in residential and certain business districts
- **Section 9** — Corporation reserves right to grade streets and slope onto abutting lots
- **Section 10** — No privies; septic tanks require Association building and plumbing permits plus Certificate of Completion
- **Section 11** — Natural drainage preservation. No obstruction, diversion, bridging, or confining of existing storm water channels without Association approval. This section is directly relevant to the 0 Clipper Road drainage and creek arguments.
- **Section 12** — Clothes drying yards must be enclosed (Art Jury approval required)
- **Section 13** — Association right to enter vacant lots for planting, maintenance, and crop growing
- **Sections 14-16** — Health/safety/welfare rules, tree trimming authority, insect pest control with right of entry

### Article II: Community Association of Palos Verdes (Sections 1-5)

This article created a private governing body with powers equivalent to a city council, building department, and public works agency:

- **Section 1** — Plan approval: No building, fence, wall, tent, pole, or structure without Association approval, building permit, and Art Jury sign-off. Plans deposited for permanent record. No billboards or signs without written permission.
- **Section 2** — Subdivision approval: No subdivision map or additional restriction declaration recorded without Association and Corporation approval
- **Section 3** — Maintenance charges and liens: Annual assessment based on LA County assessed valuation, at a rate not exceeding the City of Los Angeles total tax rate. Continuous lien on property. One-quarter of collections reserved for parks and recreation.
- **Section 4** — Twenty-six enumerated powers (a) through (z), including:
  - (a) Parks, playgrounds, golf courses, swimming pools, bathing beaches, boat landings, hospitals, museums, community facilities
  - (b) Streets, roads, trails, bridle paths, lighting, ornamental features
  - (c) Water works, pumping plants, water distribution systems
  - (d) Sewer systems, storm water drains, utilities
  - (e) Lot maintenance with additional lien authority (max 2 mills per square foot)
  - (f) Street sweeping, garbage collection
  - (g) Fire and police protection
  - (h) Franchise grants for utilities
  - (k) Department of Buildings — building permits, construction inspection, building codes, housing codes, zoning regulations
  - (l) Employment of managers, engineers, technical consultants
  - (p) Planning Board, Park and Recreation Board, Health Board, Library Board
  - (q) Enforcement of state and county laws
  - (t) General welfare powers following procedures of 6th-class California cities
  - (u) Borrowing, mortgaging, pledging property
  - (v) Street excavation permits, bonding requirements
- **Section 5** — Fallback if Association fails: 15 property owners can call a meeting, elect 3 trustees with all Association powers. Voting weighted by acreage (one vote per one-third acre).

### Article III: Art Jury (Sections 1-7)

A five-member design review body with binding authority:

- **§III.5(a) Composition:** one nominee from Palos Verdes Corporation, one from the Association board (versed in art), and three persons engaged in the practice of fine arts. For the *first* appointment, the three fine-arts seats are selected from the membership of the **Southern California Chapter of the American Institute of Architects**. For vacancies in those three seats, successors are appointed from a list of three nominees supplied by the Chapter's Board of Directors — successors must come from AIA nominations, but the declaration does not require them to be AIA members themselves.
- **§III.5(b) Terms:** staggered (1, 2, 2, 3, 3 years initially; chosen by lot), then three-year terms for successors
- **§III.1–§III.3 Scope:** approval required for all construction, exterior alterations, color changes, signs, landscaping, and works of art
- **§V.4(2) Binding effect:** Art Jury approvals/decisions recorded by its secretary are "conclusive" as to matters within its jurisdiction; reinforced by Article II §4(n) which states "the decisions of the Art Jury shall be conclusive and binding upon the Association and shall not be set aside or changed by it."
- **§III.5(d) Treasurer:** the Art Jury "shall designate and appoint a trust company to act as its Treasurer and to act as Trustee of all property of the Art Jury"

### Article IV: Use Districts and Zoning (Sections 1-36)

A complete zoning code defining use districts, height districts, and architectural districts.

> **Source caveat:** Page 166 of the source microfilm is missing. That page contains the end of Art Jury Section 7 and the beginning of Article IV Section 1 definitions (a) through (m) — the foundational zoning definitions. Section 1 resumes at (n) on page 167. The class summaries and zoning tables below are reconstructed from the surviving portions of Article IV (sections visible on pages 167+) and from the implementing instruments Declarations 101 and 150. Read them as derived, not verbatim, until page 166 is recovered.

**Use District Classes:**
| Class | Name | Permitted Uses |
|-------|------|---------------|
| A | Single Family Dwellings | Detached single-family only |
| B | House Courts, Flats, Dwellings | House courts, two-family, flats, boarding houses, fraternities, single-family |
| C | Apartments and Other Dwellings | Apartments, hotels, private schools, clubs, dormitories, plus B and A uses |
| D | Retail Business, Offices, Dwellings | Retail, professional offices, plus A-C uses; extensive list of prohibited industrial uses |
| E | Business, Theatres, Dwellings | D uses plus theatres, dance halls |
| F | Public and Semi-Public | Schools, parks, polo fields, aerodromes, museums, libraries, firehouses |
| G | Religious Edifices | Churches, parish houses, single-family |
| H | General Business, Garages, Dwellings | D-G uses plus garages (6+ vehicles), auto repair, gas stations, dry cleaning |
| J | Wholesale and Material Business | H uses plus lumber yards, coal yards, warehouses, freight depots; no dwelling except one watchman |
| K | Hospitals and Institutions | Hospitals, sanitariums (excluding TB/mental), clinics, charitable institutions, plus A-C uses |

**Height Districts:**
- 1 Story: max 25 feet
- 2 Story: max 35 feet
- 2.5 Story: max 40 feet
- 3 Story: max 45 feet
- Slope exception: lots steeper than 1:6 may add one downhill story

**Lot Coverage:**
- Class A: max 30%
- Classes B, C, F, G, K: corner lots 70%, interior lots 60%

**Architecture Type Districts:**
- Type I: Mediterranean/Italian Renaissance inspiration, light-toned plaster or stucco, tile roofs max 30-35 degrees
- Type II: Greater latitude from Type I, more wood permitted, steeper pitches (40-45 degrees)
- Type III: Greater latitude from Type II, clay products, pitches up to 60 degrees; Type I buildings may not be approved
- Type IV: Corporation-controlled group design and color scheme with Art Jury approval; uniform facade, roof lines, gables required

Detailed area requirements specify rear yards, side yards, inner and outer courts, and setback minimums by height district and use class.

### Article V: Duration, Enforcement, Amendment (Sections 1-13)

- **§V.1 Duration:** In force until January 1, 1965, then automatically renewing in 20-year periods. Renewal is defeated only if owners of more than one-half in area (exclusive of streets, parks, and open spaces) record a modification agreement within the six months prior to each 20-year boundary. Currently in force through at least January 1, 2045 (the 2025-2045 period). No evidence of any modification agreement being recorded.
- **§V.2(a) Amendment of basic restrictions:** requires mutual written agreement of Palos Verdes Corporation (or its successor holding the reversionary rights) + owners of record of not less than 80% in area + not less than 70% of all owners of record title + the Association.
- **§V.2(b) Amendment of local restrictions:** Association + 2/3 by area of the affected property + 2/3 by area of all lands in private ownership within 300 feet + Palos Verdes Corporation (or successor) + a public hearing convened by the Association.
- **§V.3 Modification procedure:** applications must be endorsed by the Association secretary before circulation for signature; signatures expire 6 months after endorsement (extendable once by another 6 months); denied applications cannot be reconsidered for one year.
- **§V.6 Reversion of title:** Breach causes property to revert to Palos Verdes Corporation or its successor in interest as owner of the reversionary rights, with right of immediate re-entry. Restrictions run with the land. Bona fide mortgages and deeds of trust protected.
- **§V.7 Summary abatement:** Corporation or Association may enter and summarily abate violations at the owner's expense.
- **§V.8 Nuisance:** Every violation constitutes a nuisance, abatable by Corporation, Association, or any lot owner subject to the Association's jurisdiction. Remedy is cumulative, not exclusive.
- **§V.5 Annexation:** Adjacent property owners may voluntarily subject their land to Declaration 100 by agreement with Corporation and Association, approved by the Art Jury.

## Why This Matters: The City Before the City

Declaration 100 established a complete private municipal government in 1929 — a decade before Palos Verdes Estates incorporated (1939), nearly three decades before Rolling Hills (1957), and four decades before Rancho Palos Verdes (1973). *Wherever it applies*, this private framework still runs parallel to the later municipal governments, and in some places imposes stricter standards. The open question of exactly where it applies is the point of ongoing research, not a footnote.

The Association under Declaration 100 had powers equivalent to:
- A city council (general governance, rule-making, enforcement)
- A building department (permits, inspection, codes)
- A public works department (streets, water, sewers, storm drains)
- A parks department (parks, recreation, beaches, pools)
- A police and fire department
- A planning commission
- A health department
- A library board

When the cities later incorporated, they inherited a population already living under this private regulatory framework. The declaration scheme did not disappear — it runs parallel to municipal government, and in some cases imposes stricter requirements than city zoning.
