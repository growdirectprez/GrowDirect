# South Bay Wiki — Content Architecture

**Wiki:** [[Brain/projects/Angel|Angel]]

A living neighborhood knowledge base for the Palos Verdes peninsula and South Bay, built as the content backbone for Angelique Lyle's real estate brand. Starting point: Angelique's static two-page neighborhood guide, reimagined as a crawlable, updatable, voice-driven content system.

## The Concept

Angelique's printed guide covers 7 categories in two pages. The South Bay Wiki expands this into a living content platform that positions her as the definitive local voice — someone who doesn't just sell houses, she knows every corner of the peninsula. The content is structured for SEO, social, and direct client engagement, with a freshness engine that keeps it current without manual effort.

---

## Two-Tier Geography

Content splits into two geographic scopes with different depth and update cadences:

**Tier 1 — PV Peninsula (hyperlocal, deep)**
Schools, neighborhoods, community events, parks/trails, local governance, geology/nature, community organizations. This is "I live here, I know every street" content. Covers RPV, PVE, Rolling Hills, Rolling Hills Estates, Lomita.

**Tier 2 — South Bay (lifestyle, curated)**
Dining, coffee, desserts, shopping districts, nightlife, fitness, wellness, arts/culture. This is "where we eat, drink, and play" content. Covers Hermosa Beach, Manhattan Beach, Redondo Beach, Torrance, El Segundo, plus PV peninsula venues. Curated top-list format — opinionated, not encyclopedic.

## Angelique's Voice

The content isn't generic real estate copy. It reads like a knowledgeable friend who's lived on the peninsula for years and knows which table to request at Nelson's.

**Voice characteristics:**
- **Warm authority** — she knows, she doesn't guess. "The best sunset spot isn't Point Vicente (too crowded) — it's the bench at the end of Paseo Del Mar where the trail drops into Lunada Bay."
- **Insider language** — uses neighborhood names locals use, not the formal names. Mentions specific intersections, not zip codes.
- **Opinionated but generous** — has clear favorites, explains why, doesn't trash competitors. "I love Yellow Vase for Saturday morning, but if you want a quiet weekday coffee, Reverent is perfect."
- **Lifestyle framing** — every recommendation connects back to living here. Schools aren't just rated — "this is where your kids will make their lifelong friends." Restaurants aren't just good — "this is where I take clients when I want them to fall in love with the area."
- **Casual elegance** — not stuffy, not slangy. Reads like a well-written personal email, not a brochure.

**Voice don'ts:**
- No "nestled" or "boasts" or "vibrant community" or any real estate clichés
- No passive voice ("is known for") — active voice ("everyone knows")
- No generic superlatives ("world-class," "premier") — specific details instead
- No listicle voice ("Next up on our list...") — conversational flow instead

## Content Management Strategy

### Sourcing Model

Each content category has different source types and refresh cadences:

| Category | Primary Sources | Crawl Method | Refresh |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neighborhoods | Redfin, Zillow, county assessor | API + scrape | Quarterly (prices) |
| Schools | GreatSchools, PVPUSD, Niche | API | Annually (ratings) + event-driven (enrollment) |
| Events | City calendars, PVPLC, PV Art Center, Eventbrite | RSS + scrape | Monthly scan, event-driven updates |
| Parks/Trails | RPV.gov, PVPLC, AllTrails | Scrape | Annually + event-driven (closures, fires) |
| Dining | Yelp API, Google Places, OpenTable, Infatuation | API + scrape | Quarterly (new openings/closings) |
| Coffee/Desserts | Yelp, Google, local blogs | API + scrape | Semi-annually |
| Shopping | Google, chamber of commerce | Scrape | Annually |
| Golf | Course websites | Manual | Annually |

### Freshness Engine

The system needs a way to detect when content is stale and flag it for update:

**Automated signals:**
- Yelp/Google "Permanently Closed" flag → immediate removal from lists
- Median price change > 5% from published figure → flag neighborhood article
- New GreatSchools rating differs from published → flag school article
- New event found on city calendar not in events list → flag for addition

**Manual signals:**
- Angelique visits a new restaurant → quick-add workflow
- Reader/client feedback ("that place closed") → correction workflow
- Seasonal rotation (summer events, holiday events) → calendar-driven

**Update workflow:**
1. Freshness scanner runs weekly (crawls sources, compares to published content)
2. Generates a "stale content" report with specific items to update
3. Angelique (or content assistant) reviews and approves updates
4. Updated articles get republished with "Last updated" timestamp

## Technical Architecture

### Where Content Lives

The South Bay Wiki is a structured data layer, not just markdown files. Each piece of content is:

1. **A data record** — name, category, location, ratings, URLs, last_verified date
2. **A voice article** — Angelique's written take, in her voice
3. **A distribution package** — formatted for each channel

This suggests a simple database-backed system (could be the Angel app, could be a standalone tool) with:
- Content records (structured data, crawl-updatable)
- Voice overlays (Angelique's written commentary, manually authored)
- Template system (generates website articles, social posts, email blocks, PDF guides from the same data)

### Crawl Pipeline

```
Sources (Yelp, Google, Redfin, city sites)
    ↓
Crawler (scheduled, per-category cadence)
    ↓
Raw Data Store (structured records with timestamps)
    ↓
Diff Engine (compares to published content)
    ↓
Stale Content Report (weekly digest)
    ↓
Editorial Review (Angelique approves/modifies)
    ↓
Published Content (website, social, email, PDF)
```

### MVP Scope

Don't build the full pipeline. Start with:

1. **Seed the wiki** — take the crawled data from this session, write 3-5 articles in Angelique's voice as proof-of-concept
2. **Define the data schema** — what fields does a "restaurant" record need? A "neighborhood" record? A "school" record?
3. **Build one crawl script** — Yelp or Google Places for dining, since that changes most often
4. **Generate one print guide** — quarterly PDF from the data, replacing the static two-page guide with a richer, data-driven version

### Key Technical Decisions

- **Voice overlays stored as Python dicts** in `voice_overlays.py`, not in DB. Moves to a `neighborhood_content` table when we need a CMS.
- **Slug-to-taxonomy mapping**: URL slugs use hyphens (`lunada-bay`), DB taxonomy uses underscores (`lunada_bay`). Conversion in route.
- **SEO routes registered at app root**, not under blueprint prefix, so `/sitemap.xml` and `/robots.txt` are where crawlers expect them.
- **School data dual-sourced**: Structured dicts in route for the school guide page + `local_entities` rows for neighborhood cross-reference. Authoritative source is the route dict (`PVPUSD_SCHOOLS` in `seed_content.py`).
