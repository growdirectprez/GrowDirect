# Baughey Article — Rancho Palos Verdes 1784–1947

A newspaper article written by Col. Robert M. Baughey of the Palos Verdes Corporation for the Palos Verdes News Tenth Anniversary Edition (1947). This is a primary source — PV Corp's own staff historian writing the official corporate history 34 years after Vanderlip's purchase.

## Key Facts (Verbatim from Source)

### Chain of Title

| Date | Event | Acreage |
|------|-------|---------|
| 1784 | Juan Jose Dominguez petitions Governor Fages for cattle grazing permit. 63-year-old Spanish Royal Army veteran. First private land use grant in California. | 43,119 ac (Rancho San Pedro) |
| 1821 | Jose Dolores Sepulveda enters the picture after Mexican Revolution | — |
| 1827 | Mexican Governor Figueroa issues provisional grant to Sepulvedas for Rancho Palos Verdes, described as "the place situated towards the Embarcadero" | 31,629 ac |
| June 4, 1846 | Pio Pico (last Mexican governor) grants formal decree to Sepulvedas — 23 days before Commodore Sloat raised US flag at Monterey | — |
| Dec 20, 1853 | US Land Commission confirms Sepulveda title | — |
| 1882 | Partition suits award rancho to Jotham Bixby | 16,002 ac |
| 1912 | Frank A. Vanderlip purchases — "closed the deal without first having seen the property" | 16,002 ac |
| 1920 | Vanderlip sells ~3,200 acres to developers of PV Estates and Miraleste. PV Corporation founded. | ~12,800 ac retained |

### Cabrillo (1542)

Baughey describes Cabrillo seeing a "westward-jutting, rolling promontory, partially obscured by slowly moving layers of smoke." Named the area "The Bay of Smokes." The smoke was attributed to fires set by Indians — either rabbit hunting or a welcoming signal. Baughey quips: "Cabrillo, among other 'firsts,' was the first visitor to our shores to be greeted by smog."

### Dominguez Grant (1784)

Baughey calls it "the first conception in our state of the use of sovereign land for private use — of land privately owned and operated" and "the first 'soldier's bonus' ever granted in California." The boundaries were determined "by methods that, were they used today, would fill our courts with litigation in volume beyond imagination."

Original rancho: 43,119 acres (~67.5 square miles), from the LA River basin westward to the sea, including present-day Redondo Beach, Gardena, Torrance, Lomita, part of Compton, San Pedro, Wilmington.

### Sepulveda Conflict

The Dominguez and Sepulveda families "didn't get along very well." Cristobal Dominguez concluded the Sepulvedas "were taking in too much territory... were running too many cattle, and registering too many cattle brands." By 1827 the Sepulvedas had established their claim well enough for a provisional grant.

### Vanderlip Purchase (1912)

Baughey confirms Vanderlip "conducted all of the negotiations and assumed full responsibility in the purchase of Rancho Palos Verdes, closed the deal without first having seen the property." This from PV Corp's own officer.

## Significance for 0 Clipper

- Confirms the complete chain of title from Dominguez (1784) through Sepulveda through Bixby through Vanderlip
- Written by PV Corp staff — the same corporation that recorded Declarations 100/101 and the Filiorum deed
- Establishes that Vanderlip and PV Corp viewed themselves as stewards of a planned community, not speculative developers
- The 1947 date means this was written before WPBCA was founded (1949), before Tract 14649 was recorded, and before the landslide began (1956)
