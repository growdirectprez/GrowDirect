# RPV Planning Framework — What Controls WPBCA (2026 Synthesis)

Cross-cutting read of the RPV planning instruments that actually bind **Tract 14649 / the 81 lots / Abalone Cove / Portuguese Bend** as of April 2026. Synthesizes [[coac-1975-general-plan-eir]], [[coac-rpv-housing-elements]], the 2018 General Plan + Safety Element, the 2024 Housing Element adoption cycle, and the 2024 Builder's Remedy analysis into one map of "what applies, what's strict, what's soft."

This is not a source summary — see the individual wiki articles for those. This is the operational answer to "what does the city say about our land and who enforces it?"

## 2018 Safety Element — What it says about Abalone Cove / Portuguese Bend

Adopted September 2018. Full page-level extraction at [`Cove/docs/archive/originals/city-records/general-plan/X-safety-element.md`](../../Cove/docs/archive/originals/city-records/general-plan/X-safety-element.md). A dedicated wiki article exists at [[cove-rpv-safety-element-2018]] for the full catalog. Summary here:

- **§3 Wildland Fires** — Fire Hazard Severity Zone overlay with figures (Fire Hazard Severity Zone map). WPBCA parcels along the upland margin fall within Very High FHSZ; siting and vegetation-management obligations follow.
- **§5 Geologic Hazards**
  - 5.1 Seismic Hazards
  - 5.3 Landslides — Figure 3 (Landslide Inventory), Figure 4 (Landslides & Liquefaction). The Portuguese Bend / Abalone Cove landslide complex is the central mapped feature.
  - 5.9 Coastal Cliff Retreat — applies directly to the 40-foot bluff setback established in [[cove-legal-framework]] Declaration One-A (1950) for Lots 1–5.
- **§7 Other Hazards**
  - 7.1 Falling from Coastal Cliffs — fence / signage obligations.
- **§8 Emergency Preparedness** — coordination with LA County Fire, building-and-fire-code adoption, expedited reconstruction of fire-damaged structures, stringent site-design criteria in high-fire zones.

**Strategic read:** The Safety Element is *the city's own document* mapping our parcels as inside overlapping hazard zones. Same posture as the 1990/2001 Housing Elements calling moratorium land "not buildable." Any attempt by the city to upzone or green-light development in these overlays contradicts its own Safety Element findings. Document the overlap when/where it matters.

The moratorium is now permanent — which is both a defensive asset (the city cannot quietly let it lapse) and a tension point (state RHNA pressure does not defer to local moratoria).

## 2024 Housing Element + Builder's Remedy — the current fight

The 6th-cycle Housing Element was adopted April 16, 2024. The 2024-07-02 staff report analyzes Builder's Remedy applicability during any gap in certification.

**What changed from the 1990/2001 posture:**

1. **State can no longer treat moratorium land as automatic zero-capacity.** HCD pushed sites inventory methodology to include more parcels.
2. **Builder's Remedy (Gov. Code §65589.5(d))** applies whenever the city is without a certified HE — letting developers propose projects that meet affordability thresholds even on non-compliant zoning. The 0 Clipper threat operates in this window.
3. **The estoppel argument still runs** — the city's own 1990/2001 HEs specifying "Not buildable" for Portuguese Bend are citable against any current upzoning in those same areas. See [[coac-rpv-housing-elements]] for the specific inventory language.

**What this means for WPBCA:**

- The *city's* planning posture toward Portuguese Bend is in visible conflict: Safety Element says "hazard zone," old Housing Elements say "not buildable," 2024 Housing Element under HCD pressure accommodates more development.
- *Private CC&R enforceability* is an independent track (see [[cove-case-law-lessons]]) — state housing law preempts **government zoning**, not **private covenants** ([[cove-legal-framework]]).
- 0 Clipper Road is the test case ([[cove-0-clipper]]).

## Five things the 2018 GP + Safety Element change for WPBCA construction

For any ARC review or member remodel on Tract 14649:

1. **Fire Hazard Severity Zone** — parcels in Very High FHSZ have vegetation-management and siting obligations beyond pre-2018 practice. Cross-reference the Safety Element map before approving landscape plans.
2. **Coastal Cliff Retreat** — the 40-foot bluff setback in Declaration One-A (1950) is **validated** by the 2018 Safety Element §5.9. That private restriction is now aligned with municipal finding — not in tension with it.
3. **Landslide overlay** — Figure 3 of the Safety Element is the map of record. Any ARC application in a RM-4 / RM-5 area (Active Landslide / Old Landslide Area per 1975 GP) should cite both the 1975 RM overlay and the 2018 Safety Element landslide inventory.
4. **Emergency access** — Safety Element §8 imposes emergency-access obligations on narrow streets. Sea Cove Dr + Packet / Barkentine / Clipper / Peppertree geometries are at the margin; verify before approving additions that affect street width or driveway spacing.
5. **Reconstruction in fire-damaged structures** — expedited review pathway exists in §8. ARC should know this exists before a member is surprised by it.

## Coastal Specific Plan (S4) — operational summary

The Coastal Specific Plan (1978, created by GP Amendment 3 → Resolution 78-81, December 19, 1978) is the vehicle by which Coastal Act compliance is integrated into RPV regulation. Subregion 4 covers Abalone Cove. See [[cove-coastal-specific-plan-s4]] for full article. Key 2026 read:

- **View corridors** — the 1975 GP RM-7 marine resource overlay is operationalized here. View protections run with the land under municipal authority, independent of private CC&Rs.
- **Beach access** — the private bridle trail + walkway easement from Lot 1 to the ocean (1949 Declaration of Easements) is a *private* instrument; the Coastal Specific Plan's public-access policy runs parallel and is the Coastal Commission's leverage point.
- **Habitat area** — the 0.57-acre creek/flood hazard area at 0 Clipper identified as "habitat area in excellent condition" in the Coastal Specific Plan is a direct citable anchor for the PVPLC partnership ask ([[cove-pvplc-partnership]]).

## How this framework interacts with WPBCA private instruments

See [[cove-legal-framework]] + [[cove-case-law-lessons]] for the CC&R side. Rule of thumb:

- **The city controls** what can be **permitted** (zoning, subdivision, building permit, grading, coastal development permit).
- **WPBCA controls** what can be **built by members** on Tract 14649 lots under the recorded declarations (architectural review, setbacks, height, materials — enforceable under [[cove-legal-framework]]).
- **Neither preempts the other.** A city-approved project can still be blocked by CC&Rs; a CC&R-compliant project can still be blocked by zoning. This is the foundation of the 0 Clipper defense posture.
