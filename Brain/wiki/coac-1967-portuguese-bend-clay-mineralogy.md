# COAC: 1967 Portuguese Bend Clay Mobility (Kerr & Drew)

**The geological taproot of the Portuguese Bend / Abalone Cove landslide policy regime.** Predates the 1975 RPV General Plan, the 1978 Portuguese Bend Club litigation, the formation of the Abalone Cove Landslide Abatement District (ACLAD), the 2013 RPV LRPMP, and the 2024 FEMA $42M voluntary buyout program. All subsequent regulatory and engineering frameworks descend from the science laid out in this 1967 USAF/Columbia paper.

## Abstract (Verbatim)

> The south slope of the Palos Verdes Hills west of San Pedro, California, in the area known as Portuguese Bend contains an unusual landslide. Since the site is one of the choice residential areas of the southwest and **130 homes have been reported to have been removed as a result of the slide**, the locality has attracted wide interest.
>
> The slide movement takes place in members of the **Monterey Shale** which contain bentonite; particularly the **Portuguese Tuff**. Samples of bentonite from the tuffaceous rocks are rich in **Ca-montmorillonite** which has definite **thixotropic properties**. When dry the clay is stable, but with the adsorption of water it swells and may ultimately flow. Overlying shale masses although substantial in themselves are slowly rafted down slope on the underlying bentonite-lubricated slip plane. Movements have frequently been measured in terms of a small fraction of an inch per day, but during a year cumulative movement in places may amount to **10 to 30 feet**.
>
> The physical properties of the clay have been studied in considerable detail and reveal a mobile material. Field observations indicate that enlargement of the slide area is also in constant progress. **No field control of this situation has as yet been established.** However, granted **adequate drainage on the surface and along the slip plane below, plus impregnation by stabilizing additives**, it is suggested that a pattern of control might be developed. However, **divided ownership inherent** [in the slide area limits coordinated control].

## Why USAF Funded This

1960s USAF research interest in soil mechanics was tied to military construction and missile-site stability. The Nike missile site referenced in the 1975 RPV GP sits in the same Peninsula area. AFCRL had a broader portfolio of geophysical and geotechnical research relevant to base siting and structural engineering — Portuguese Bend was a high-profile, well-instrumented case study with widely reported residential losses.

## Cove 501c Relevance — Five Strategic Uses

### 1. The Geology Vocabulary for Restated Declaration Recitals

When the COAC Restated Declaration is recorded ([[coac-reactivation-framework]] Phase 2), the recitals defining the **creek/fault-line avoidance corridor** should use the technical vocabulary established by Kerr & Drew:

- **Monterey Shale** — host formation
- **Portuguese Tuff** — failure-horizon member
- **Ca-montmorillonite bentonite** — the active mineral phase
- **Thixotropic** — solid when dry, fluid when sheared / saturated
- **Bentonite-lubricated slip plane** — mechanical model

Citing 1967 primary-source science in the recitals strengthens the recording against later challenges that the avoidance corridor is "speculative."

### 2. "Divided Ownership" → COAC Reactivation Premise

Kerr & Drew's closing-paragraph observation is uncannily aligned with the COAC reactivation thesis:

> "However, divided ownership inherent [in the slide area limits coordinated control]."

The 1967 authors identified divided private ownership as **the structural obstacle** to coordinated remediation. Reactivating Declaration 100 with three Trustees + a Restated Declaration is the exact legal mechanism that **consolidates governance authority across divided ownership** for the purpose of coordinated landslide response.

This is a 60-year-old, peer-reviewed, USAF-funded finding that the COAC strategy directly addresses. It belongs in the Foundation founders narrative.

### 3. Estoppel Against "Unforeseeable Risk" Claims

Any city, developer, or regulatory party arguing post-2024 that the Portuguese Bend slide risk is somehow novel, surprising, or recently understood, can be cited against this paper. The science was published in 1967, distribution unlimited, USAF-cleared. Risk has been on the public record for **59 years**.

### 4. Engineering Control Framework — Predecessor to ACLAD

Kerr & Drew suggested the control framework: **surface drainage + slip-plane drainage + stabilizing additives**. ACLAD's dewatering-well program implements the first two; the third (chemical stabilization) has been investigated in subsequent decades but not deployed at scale. The 1967 paper is the engineering predecessor citation.

### 5. FEMA Buyout Documentation

The 2024 FEMA $42M voluntary property buyout program presupposes a long-term, well-documented hazard. Kerr & Drew's "130 homes have been reported to have been removed as a result of the slide" (as of 1967) is part of the historical baseline against which buyout justification rests.
