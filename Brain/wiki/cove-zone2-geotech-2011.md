# Zone 2 Geotechnical Study (LGC Valley, 2011)

City-commissioned geotechnical study prepared by LGC Valley, Inc. as Appendix D of the EIR for the Zone 2 Landslide Moratorium Ordinance Revision. Dated March 29, 2011, Project No. 103002-01. Prepared for Rincon Consultants, Inc. Signed by Matthew Hawley (CEG 2122) and Basil Hattar (GE 2734).

## What This Document Is

A literature-review-based geotechnical assessment of 47 undeveloped lots in Zone 2 of the Abalone Cove Landslide Abatement District (ACLAD). LGC Valley reviewed decades of archived reports from the City of Rancho Palos Verdes, performed a site visit, and synthesized findings into a geologic and geotechnical history to support the EIR for relaxing the landslide moratorium on those 47 lots.

## Zone 2 Physical Description

- Approximately 112 acres in a gated community north of Narcissa Drive / PV Drive South intersection
- 111 total lots: 64 developed, 47 undeveloped/underdeveloped
- Located within the Portuguese Bend Community Association area
- Private streets maintained by the PBCA
- Largest developed lot: Portuguese Bend Riding Club (pre-1973 incorporation)
- Situated entirely within the City's Landslide Moratorium Area (LMA)

## Geologic Setting

Zone 2 sits on the southern flank of the Palos Verdes Peninsula, a northwest-trending dome-shaped ridge (9 miles x 5 miles). The peninsula rose from the sea during Early Pleistocene time, creating at least thirteen marine-cut terraces. It rises to approximately 1,480 feet elevation.

**Bedrock:** The Altamira Shale Member of the Monterey Formation (Middle Miocene to Early Pliocene). Three lithofacies: Portuguese Tuff (50-75 feet thick, altered ash tuff to bentonite clay -- the critical weak layer), Cherty Lithofacies, and Phosphatic Lithofacies. Bedding inclined approximately 20 degrees toward the south/ocean.

**The critical fact:** Zone 2 sits entirely within the approximately 900-acre Ancient Portuguese Bend Landslide (APBL) complex. It is not adjacent to a landslide -- it IS on the landslide. Initial landsliding occurred approximately 120,000-500,000 years ago.

## Landslide Mechanics

The APBL moved as a translational-type landslide along pre-existing weak bentonite clay layers inclined toward the ocean. The mechanism:

1. Inclined bedding becomes unsupported at the coast due to wave erosion
2. Toe material fails into the beach zone
3. Upslope material loses support and becomes susceptible
4. Fractured landslide material allows more water infiltration, adding weight and buoyancy
5. Beach erosion at the toe prevents natural buttressing
6. Result: a series of landslides that "shingle" upslope toward the anticline crest

The landslide structure varies by position: severely broken rubble at the toe, transitioning to large relatively intact blocks separated by extensional tears, scarps, and grabens further upslope. The uphill blocks are stronger and provide structural support to areas above them.

## Adjacent Active Landslides

### Abalone Cove Landslide (ACL) -- 85 acres
- Abuts Zone 2 immediately to the south
- Reactivated 1974, significant movement through 1985
- Dewatering began early 1980, movement nearly stopped by end of 1985
- GPS monuments (1994-2006): toe displaced 1.9 ft, mid-section 0.8 ft, head 0.6 ft
- Annual slip rates: 1.9 in/yr (toe), 0.8 in/yr (mid), 0.6 in/yr (head)
- Movement occurs in pulses correlated with rainfall, not steady

### Portuguese Bend Landslide (PBL) -- 260 acres
- Reactivated 1956 (earlier movement possibly 1931-1947)
- Moves at approximately 3 feet per year
- Caused by overburden fill and cut operations for Crenshaw Blvd extension
- Destroyed approximately 134 of 160 affected homes
- Basal rupture surface on sheared bentonite clay bed 30-40 feet above Portuguese Tuff
- Five semi-independent subslides (Ehlig, 1992)
- Peak movement reached 1.5 inches per day after high rainfall
- Never successfully stabilized despite: 23 steel-reinforced concrete caissons, dewatering wells, gabions, drainage improvements, earth redistribution

## Zone 2 Movement Data

GPS monument data (1994-2006) shows Zone 2 moving at approximately **0.3 inches/year** (3 inches per decade). This is characterized as creep-type movement, not sudden failure. Movement is attributed to loss of support from slow downslope movement of the adjacent ACL and/or PBL. Results in nuisance-type cracking and higher maintenance costs, not life-safety threats.

## Groundwater -- The Controlling Factor

Every report reviewed agreed: groundwater is the single most important factor controlling landslide stability. Robert Stone & Associates (1979) identified three mechanisms:

1. Water increases plasticity of clay gouge along the slide surface, reducing frictional resistance
2. Buoyancy from water accumulation reduces the weight of solid material on the slide surface
3. Fluid pressure in the direction of slide movement provides additional driving force (like water behind a dam)

RSA (1979) concluded the driving force from groundwater head is THE controlling factor. LGC Valley agrees. Historical sources of excess groundwater: cesspools, septic systems, leach fields installed during original residential development. Current primary source: rainfall, plus infiltration from adjacent canyons and broken pipes.

## Factor of Safety

Disputed among professionals:
- At or just below 1.0: GeoKinetics (2007)
- Greater than 1.0 but less than 1.5: Cotton Shires (2001)
- Greater than 1.5: Leighton (2001, 2006)

LGC Valley's position: likely between 1.0 and 1.5, aligning with Cotton Shires. The disagreement stems from analytical methods and interpretation of GPS monument data, not from the fundamental geologic parameters.

## Seismic Hazards Summary

- NOT in an Alquist-Priolo Earthquake Fault Zone
- NOT in a Seismic Hazards Zone for Liquefaction
- IS in a State of California Seismic Hazard Zone for landslides
- IS in a Seismic Hazard Zone for earthquake-induced landslides
- Palos Verdes Fault: approximately 4 miles (6.5 km) from site
- Probability of seismically-induced landslides: at least moderate risk
- Expansive soils: medium to high expansion potential

## Conclusions and Development Conditions

LGC Valley concluded that development of 47 lots would not negatively impact gross stability IF conditions were met:

1. Conform to RPV Landslide Moratorium Ordinance (Municipal Code Chapter 15.20)
2. Less than 1,000 cubic yards grading per lot; max 50 cubic yards imported fill
3. Mandatory participation in ACLAD
4. Geotechnical report required before building permit issuance
5. All houses connect to public sanitary sewer (no septic)
6. Storm drainage improvements to reduce infiltration
7. All lot drainage deficiencies corrected; pools/ponds/sumps subject to City review
8. Runoff collected and directed to street or approved drainage course
9. All relevant building code requirements met

**Critical caveat:** The study explicitly warns that if significant movement occurs in adjacent landslides (ACL or PBL), the loss of support could result in "significant structural damage within Zone 2."

## Moratorium History

- **1978:** Landslide Moratorium Ordinance adopted in response to ACL movement (1974-1985)
- **1979:** Robert Stone & Associates investigation; recommended against development until ACL stopped, water table lowered, drainage improved
- **2011:** This study commissioned as part of EIR for moratorium revision (to allow development of 47 lots with conditions)
- **2025:** Moratorium made permanent by the City of Rancho Palos Verdes
