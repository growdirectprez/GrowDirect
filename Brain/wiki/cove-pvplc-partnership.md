# PVPLC — Partnership for Sub Region 4 Implementation

The Palos Verdes Peninsula Land Conservancy (PVPLC) is the natural partner for the open space and habitat preservation components of the Sub Region 4 proposal. They already manage the land adjacent to 0 Clipper Road, hold the conservation easement on Abalone Cove Reserve, and have the institutional capacity to absorb the creek/flood hazard area into their existing conservation framework.

## Who They Are

- Founded 1988 by Bill Ailor as a 501(c)(3) nonprofit
- Manages 1,700 acres of open space across the peninsula
- Habitat Manager for the 1,400-acre Palos Verdes Nature Preserve (owned by City of RPV)
- Holds conservation easements in perpetuity
- 50-year management agreement with RPV (2011)
- NCCP/HCP adopted by RPV City Council (2019)
- Completed $30 million "Go Wild for the Peninsula" campaign (2025)

## Managed Reserves Adjacent to Abalone Cove

| Reserve | Acreage | Relevance |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| **Abalone Cove Reserve** | 64 acres | State Ecological Preserve, conservation easement held by PVPLC, 20.3-acre restoration in progress (Phase 4, 2022), Conservation Center opened 2024 |
| **Portuguese Bend Reserve** | 399 acres | Largest reserve, directly adjacent, PV Blue Butterfly habitat, trails closed due to active land movement |
| **Filiorum Reserve** | 191 acres | Wildlife corridor linking Three Sisters and Portuguese Bend. Named after the Filiorum Corporation — the same entity in the 1930 grant deed (Book 10226, Page 170) that incorporated Declarations 100 and 101 |
| **96-acre Wildlife Corridor** | 96 acres | $30M campaign, connects coast to 1,000-acre upland preserve, completed 2025 |

## The Filiorum Connection

The Filiorum Reserve is named after the Filiorum Corporation — the Vanderlip family's land holding entity. The 1930 grant deed from PV Corp to Filiorum Corporation transferred three portions of Lot H and expressly incorporated "each and every provision, condition, restriction, reservation, lien, charge, easement and covenant contained in Declarations 100 and 101."

The family's land speculation vehicle became a nature preserve. The private city's implementing deed became the name on a wildlife corridor. The arc from 1930 to 2009 (when Filiorum was acquired) is the arc of the entire story — Vanderlip restrictions protecting land that eventually returned to open space.

## Species Recovery

PVPLC has assisted recovery of four at-risk species on the peninsula:
- **Palos Verdes Blue Butterfly** (*Glaucopsyche lygdamus palosverdesensis*) — the rarest butterfly in the world, found only on the peninsula. Recovered on PVPLC-managed habitat. First captive-reared release into breeding grounds on Palos Verdes Nature Preserve in 2020. Up to 700 butterflies per day observed in 2008.
- Coastal California gnatcatcher
- Cactus wren
- El Segundo blue butterfly

The PV Blue Butterfly is listed as endangered under the Endangered Species Act. Any development at 0 Clipper would need to address habitat under Section 7 (16 U.S.C. §1536). PVPLC is the entity doing the actual recovery work.

## Why This Partnership Works

### What PVPLC brings:
- Conservation easement expertise and legal framework
- Existing adjacency — Abalone Cove Reserve borders the community
- NCCP/HCP compliance structure (already adopted by RPV)
- Species recovery track record (PV Blue Butterfly)
- Relationship with US Fish & Wildlife Service ($12.6M in federal grants)
- Relationship with California Dept of Fish & Wildlife ($4.8M in state grants)
- Relationship with RPV city government (50-year management agreement)
- $30M fundraising track record

### What the Foundation brings:
- The research and public record documenting why the creek must be preserved
- The legal analysis connecting Declaration 100/101, Coastal Specific Plan, and Wong subdivision
- Community advocacy capacity
- Tax-deductible donation vehicle for the survey and legal opinion

### What WPBCA brings:
- CC&R enforcement standing
- Annexation mechanism (Coastal Specific Plan Policy 4)
- 81-lot membership representing the adjacent community
- Beach path easement control

## The Ask

Add the 0.57-acre creek/flood hazard area at 0 Clipper Road to the Abalone Cove Reserve conservation easement. This is:
- Contiguous habitat (the drainage course at the eastern boundary, identified as "habitat area in excellent condition" in the Coastal Specific Plan)
- Within the PV Blue Butterfly range
- Adjacent to the existing 64-acre Abalone Cove Reserve
- Consistent with the NCCP/HCP framework already in place
- A natural extension of the Wildlife Corridor connecting coast to upland preserve

## Has PVPLC Taken a Position?

The article notes: "The Palos Verdes Land Conservancy, which manages the adjacent Portuguese Bend Reserve, has not commented publicly on the ESA implications."

This is an open question. PVPLC's mission is habitat preservation. Their managed land is adjacent. The species they recovered lives here. But they also have a working relationship with the city that rezoned the parcel. Whether they take a public position on 0 Clipper is a political question, not a scientific one.

The Foundation's role is to make the scientific and regulatory case so clear that PVPLC can act on it without political risk.
