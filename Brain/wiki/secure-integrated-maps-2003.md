# Integrated Maps — Retail Hardlines Solution Landscape (2003/04)

## Summary

A single-deck **Solution Map for Hardlines Retail 2004**, dated 12 September 2003. Maps the retail-technology landscape of the time across three axes:

- **SAP / Point Solution** — SAP-anchored products for discrete functional domains
- **ISV Point Solution** — third-party best-of-breed products
- **IBM Point Solution** — IBM-branded retail products

Organised by process area (HR, Store Operations, Supply Chain, Merchandising) with overlays for **SAP Process Transformation**, **Core R/3 Process Area**, and **SAP Enterprise Transformation**. A reference artefact from the IBM retail consulting practice circa 2003 showing how the then-active retail tech stack was categorised for client conversations.

## Solutions Named in the Map

**HR / People:**
- Payroll, Training & Development, Organisation Effectiveness
- Hourly Recruiting (Unicru)
- e-Recruiting
- Benefits Administration
- Performance Management
- Time Management
- StaffWorks Centralized Workforce Deployment (management, forecast scheduling — non-union)
- Time & Attendance Centralized

**Store Operations:**
- SAP Store Portal Content
- SAP Retail Store
- IBM On Demand Workplace for Store Management
- IBM POS Software / Hardware
- Task Management (StorePerform Reflexis)
- IBM Performance Advisor

**Supply Chain:**
- IBM CRP Service (Vendor Managed Inventory)
- Vendor Managed Inventory (generic)
- Collaborative Planning & Forecasting (Syncra)
- Open to Buy
- Promotion Management
- Master Data

**Merchandising:** Merchandising core + adjacent planning/promotion

## Why This Matters

A snapshot of what the retail software stack looked like in 2003 *as an IBM consultant would explain it to a client*:

- **SAP centrality** — the map is organised around SAP R/3 as the process backbone, with IBM + ISV products filling gaps. SAP was the dominant enterprise retail platform before the modern SaaS era.
- **Vendor-name archaeology** — several products named here (Unicru, Syncra, Reflexis) had significant retail presence at the time but shifted or consolidated in the decades since. Useful reference when interpreting earlier engagement docs that reference these vendors.
- **The IBM Point Solutions column** — shows how IBM positioned its own products (CRP, On Demand Workplace, POS, Performance Advisor) inside a mixed-vendor landscape rather than as a full-stack replacement. That pragmatic positioning informed how IBM retail consulting teams (including Jeffe's) operated.
