# COAC: Palos Verdes Endangered Species & Habitat — Scientific Foundation

Five peer-reviewed scientific papers ground Cove's coastal-habitat conservation posture in citable primary literature. Together they establish the **biological-resource baseline** that the 1975 RPV General Plan's RM-7 (Marine Resource), RM-8 (Wildlife Habitat), and RM-9 (Natural Vegetation) overlays were designed to protect, and that the modern **PVPLC partnership** + **NCCP Subarea Plan** frameworks operationalize.

For Cove 501c, this is the science vocabulary the Foundation can cite when:
- Engaging USFWS on Section 7 consultations
- Defending Coastal Specific Plan compliance positions
- Justifying conservation easements and open-space designations
- Documenting the public-good rationale for Foundation activity

## The Five Papers

### 1. Coastal Cactus Wren Site Fidelity (Dalkey 2016)

**Citation:** Ann Dalkey, "Site Fidelity of a Coastal Cactus Wren (*Campylorhynchus brunneicapillus*) on the Palos Verdes Peninsula," Bull. Southern California Acad. Sci. 115(3):141–145, 2016.

**Author affiliation:** Palos Verdes Peninsula Land Conservancy, 916 Silver Spur Road, Suite 206, Rolling Hills Estates CA 90274 (the very PVPLC that partners with WPBCA / Cove).

**Key findings:**
- Coastal cactus wren inhabits prickly pear (*Opuntia littoralis*, *O. oricola*) and coastal cholla (*Cylindropuntia prolifera*) thickets in coastal sage scrub and chaparral
- PV Peninsula population isolated, threatened by habitat loss and reduced genetic diversity
- 5-hectare Palos Verdes Nature Preserve is the core habitat
- USGS sampled 620 wrens 2012-2013 across Ventura, LA, San Bernardino, Riverside, Orange, San Diego counties (Barr et al. 2015)
- Notable individual demonstrated multi-year site fidelity in the Preserve

**Source:** [`2016-dalkey-coastal-cactus-wren-pv-peninsula-site-fidelity.pdf`](../../Cove/docs/archive/originals/city-records/parks-conservancy/scientific-papers/2016-dalkey-coastal-cactus-wren-pv-peninsula-site-fidelity.pdf)

### 2. Palos Verdes Blue Butterfly — Rediscovery (Mattoni 1994)

**Citation:** Rudi Mattoni, "Rediscovery of the endangered Palos Verdes blue butterfly, *Glaucopsyche lygdamus palosverdesensis* Perkins and Emmel (Lycaenidae)," J. Research on the Lepidoptera 31(3-4):180–194, 1992 (publication actual ~1994).

**Author affiliation:** UCLA, Department of Geography.

**Key findings:**
- PVB believed extinct for 11 years
- Rediscovered by accident **March 10, 1994** at the Defense Fuel Support Point (DFSP), San Pedro, by Rick Rogers, Timothy Dahlum, and Rudi Mattoni
- Subspecies originally described by Perkins and Emmel (1977) from LA County
- Listed federally endangered before this rediscovery (presumed-extinct species can remain listed)
- Paper outlines reintroduction, revegetation, and monitoring recovery plan

**Source:** [`1994-mattoni-pv-blue-butterfly-rediscovery.pdf`](../../Cove/docs/archive/originals/city-records/parks-conservancy/scientific-papers/1994-mattoni-pv-blue-butterfly-rediscovery.pdf)

### 3. PVB Mass Rearing (Mattoni, Longcore, Krenova, Lipman 2003)

**Citation:** Mattoni R., Longcore T., Krenova Z., Lipman A., "Mass rearing the endangered Palos Verdes blue butterfly (*Glaucopsyche lygdamus palosverdesensis*: Lycaenidae)," J. Research on the Lepidoptera 37:55–67, 1998 (publication actual ~2003).

**Author affiliations:** Lepidoptera Research Foundation; Urban Wildlands Group; University of South Bohemia (Czech Republic); Institute of Ecology, University of Georgia.

**Key findings:**
- 8-year mass rearing program for insurance against extinction + reintroduction stock
- 168–968 pupae produced per season from a small initial stock
- Major losses: first instar mortality + microsporidian infection
- Predation during pupation significant in semi-natural confined conditions
- Cylindrical cages over potted plants AND outdoor tent cages successful for natural mating without hand pairing
- Artificial diet trialed; cage design refined

**Source:** [`2003-mattoni-et-al-pv-blue-butterfly-mass-rearing.pdf`](../../Cove/docs/archive/originals/city-records/parks-conservancy/scientific-papers/2003-mattoni-et-al-pv-blue-butterfly-mass-rearing.pdf)

### 4. PVB Pupae Salvage as Mitigation (2005)

**Citation:** "Salvage of Individual Pupae as Mitigation Measure for Loss of Palos Verdes Blue Butterfly Habitat," Journal of the Lepidopterists' Society 59(2):116–117, 2005.

**Key findings:**
- DFSP is the only consistently occupied locality for the PVB
- Annual brood since 1994 estimated < 300 adult butterflies, sometimes < 50 (Longcore and Mattoni 2003)
- PVB located on **U.S. Navy-owned housing development adjacent to DFSP** during pre-disposal surveys
- HUD recipient of surplus property + USFWS reached protection agreement
- Mitigation framework: locate and salvage pupae under deerweed (*Lotus scoparius*, larval foodplant) before site transfer
- Documents the Section 7 Formal Consultation between HUD and USFWS for "Disposal and Reuse of the Palos Verdes and San Pedro Navy Housing Areas, Los Angeles County, CA," September 4, [year]

**Source:** [`2005-pv-blue-butterfly-pupae-salvage-mitigation.pdf`](../../Cove/docs/archive/originals/city-records/parks-conservancy/scientific-papers/2005-pv-blue-butterfly-pupae-salvage-mitigation.pdf)

### 5. Late Pleistocene Molluscan Faunas (Marincovich)

**Citation:** Louie Marincovich, "Late Pleistocene Molluscan Faunas from Upper Terraces of the Palos Verdes Hills, California."

**Key findings:**
- PV Hills bear a sequence of **13 emergent Pleistocene marine terraces**
- Study of upper terraces (5, 7, 9, 12) shows essentially the same character as the better-known lower terraces (1–4)
- Entire terrace sequence considered late Pleistocene age
- Mollusks lived in rocky-shore habitats ≤30 m water depth
- Local marine hydroclimate cooler than today's PV coastline
- Many southern rocky-shore species suggest a local warmer-water site or periodic warm counter-current introduction
- 47 mollusk species in PV Hills Pleistocene deposits not previously reported

**Cove relevance:** Documents the **physical terrace structure** that defines the Peninsula's coastal habitat zonation — the substrate on which both the PVB and cactus wren habitats are mapped, and the geological taproot of the bluff/shoreline geometry the Restated Declaration recital language describes.

**Source:** [`marincovich-late-pleistocene-molluscan-faunas-upper-terraces-pv-hills.pdf`](../../Cove/docs/archive/originals/city-records/parks-conservancy/scientific-papers/marincovich-late-pleistocene-molluscan-faunas-upper-terraces-pv-hills.pdf)

## Strategic Use for Cove Foundation

### 1. Public-Voice Educational Material

Foundation 501c3 educational publications can cite:
- Dalkey 2016 (PVPLC's own scientist) → Foundation–PVPLC partnership credibility
- Mattoni 1994 + 2003 → multi-decade species recovery narrative for community engagement
- 2005 paper → HUD/USFWS coordination framework as model for civic land-disposition processes

### 2. Restated Declaration Recital Citations

When the Restated Declaration is recorded, the recitals defining habitat-protective language can cite this body of literature directly. "The community recognizes the federally endangered Palos Verdes blue butterfly (*Glaucopsyche lygdamus palosverdesensis*), the coastal cactus wren (*Campylorynchus brunneicapillus*), and the coastal sage scrub habitat documented in [citation list] as protected resources within the Declaration boundary."

### 3. NCCP Subarea Plan Compliance

The NCCP framework requires participating jurisdictions to maintain habitat for covered species. Cove's documentation of these papers in its public archive demonstrates compliance posture.

### 4. Coastal Specific Plan Compliance

The 1975 GP RM-8 (Wildlife Habitat) and RM-9 (Natural Vegetation) overlays, plus the 1978 Coastal Specific Plan (GP-A3), are operationalized in part through habitat protection for these species. Cove citations to this literature support its standing in any city/Coastal-Commission interface.
