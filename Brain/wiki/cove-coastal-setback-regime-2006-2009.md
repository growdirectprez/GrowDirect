# Coastal Setback Regime — 2006–2009

Internal article of record covering the regulatory chain that fixed the methodology for locating the Coastal Setback Line on individual properties in the Rancho Palos Verdes coastal zone, with attention to the two cases on record at 7 Sea Cove Drive and 24 Sea Cove Drive that bracketed the change. Drafted in the calm legal register; no individual names per the volunteer-protection rule and the editorial restraint default. Citations to recorded instruments and case numbers throughout.

## Summary

Between 2006 and 2009, the regulatory framework for locating the Coastal Setback Line on individual coastal-zone properties in Rancho Palos Verdes shifted under pressure from a single after-the-fact variance application. The 2006 baseline was the certified 1978 Coastal Specific Plan, applied case by case. By late 2009, city staff had attempted, by code amendment, to bind the 1976 Earth Sciences Associates (ESA) maps as the controlling instrument and to remove the long-standing right of property owners to impeach those map lines with site-specific scientific testing. The West Portuguese Bend Community Association, in official capacity through its then-president, filed a six-page numbered objection on November 23, 2009, and other commenters filed in parallel. The available record indicates that the November 2009 staff proposal to lock the ESA Maps in did not survive intact; by June 2010, the operative methodology had shifted to a centerline-of-Coastal-Setback-Line overlay against the city's official Zoning Map. The 1976 ESA Report itself, the December 19, 1978 Certified Coastal Specific Plan Map at full resolution, and the property owner's engineer's CAD demonstration that impeached the ESA Map lines as non-congruent with the certified Coastal Zone Map are all referenced in the record but are not yet in the Foundation archive.

## I. The 2006 Baseline (Case No. ZON2006-00113, 7 Sea Cove Drive)

The August 29, 2006 Hearings Officer staff report for the property at 7 Sea Cove Drive approved a coastal permit, grading approval, and site plan review for a new 4,225-square-foot two-story residence with a 690-square-foot deck, spa, and 613 cubic yards of grading on a vacant lot. A timely appeal was filed September 13, 2006, and withdrawn October 23, 2006. The decision became final October 23, 2006. The property is located in a non-appealable portion of Subregion 4 of the certified Coastal Specific Plan; the California Coastal Commission retained no appellate jurisdiction.

The required coastal permit findings, on the face of the staff report, invoke the **Coastal Specific Plan** as the controlling instrument. The ESA Map is not invoked as binding. Subregion 4 Policy No. 1 ("require all new housing within the confines of Subregion 4 to be of a single-family nature") is quoted as governing law. Subregion 4 Policy No. 2 ("require development abutting the natural drainage course to maintain the character of the course") is quoted as governing law and triggered NPDES review of the natural drainage course adjacent to the property. The OC-2 (Socio-Cultural) and OC-3 (Urban Appearance) overlay control districts are noted as covering the entire coastal zone with performance criteria.

The case file contains a January 29, 2006 personal-letter packet to the twenty closest neighboring property owners with certified-mail tracking, which constitutes a partial reconstruction of the community member roster for that section of Tract 14649 at that date.

## II. The 2007–2009 Fracture (Case Nos. ZON2007-00046, ZON2007-00253, ZON2009-00416, 24 Sea Cove Drive)

An after-the-fact pool, spa, and outdoor chimney barbecue within the Coastal Structure Setback Zone at 24 Sea Cove Drive triggered the chain. The chronology, on the face of the city's own staff narrative in the November 24, 2009 staff report:

- **May 24, 2007** — The property owner files a request for formal Interpretation (Case No. ZON2007-00253) regarding the basis for locating the Coastal Setback Line on the property and on properties within the city's coastal zone generally.
- **June 21, 2007** — The Director of Planning, Building and Code Enforcement issues a formal Interpretation concluding that, in cases of ambiguity regarding the location of the Coastal Setback Line, the city's "ESA Maps" prepared in 1976 are to be used.
- The Director's Interpretation is appealed to the Planning Commission; the appeal is tabled to permit the appellant to explore other issues.
- **September 8, 2009** — The Planning Commission considers the appeal. On a 4-2-1 vote (two dissents, one abstention), the Commission adopts a revised Interpretation stating that the location of the Coastal Setback Line on properties shall be based on **a site-specific geology study and the Coastal Specific Plan Land Use Map rather than on the ESA Maps until the issue can be further clarified by an amendment**.
- **September 23, 2009** — A new appeal is filed seeking to overturn the Planning Commission's revised Interpretation and reinstate the June 21, 2007 Director's Interpretation that elevated the ESA Maps.
- **October 20, 2009** — The City Council considers the appeal, hears public testimony, continues the appeal to December 15, 2009, and directs Staff to begin the process of amending the Municipal Code regarding which map controls.
- **November 5, 2009** — A 1/8-page public hearing notice is posted in the Peninsula News, and notices are mailed to the 77 property owners traversed by the Coastal Setback Line, the applicable Homeowners' Associations, and the California Coastal Commission.
- **November 17, 2009** — A public comment letter is filed with the Planning Commission arguing that the Certified Coastal Specific Plan Map (December 19, 1978) is unambiguous, on file with the California Coastal Commission in Long Beach and within the city's own files, and corresponds precisely with the Official Zoning Map. The letter notes the existence of an "oversized wall hanging Zoning Map" that "city Staff chose to remove from public view."
- **November 20, 2009** — Staff publishes the proposed code amendment text. Two business days before the Planning Commission hearing.
- **November 23, 2009** — The West Portuguese Bend Community Association files a six-page numbered objection letter, signed in official capacity by the then-president, in Case No. ZON2009-00416. The letter cites *Monks v. Rancho Palos Verdes*, demands an Environmental Impact Report, distinguishes legislative from quasi-judicial fact-finding, argues that the proposed code amendment would constitute an unconstitutional taking under *Monks*, critiques the ESA Report on its own scientific terms (no site-specific testing, only five area-wide references from circa 1972–1973), and objects to the inadequacy of two-business-days' notice.
- **November 24, 2009** — The Planning Commission holds the hearing on the proposed code amendment (ZON2009-00416), considering staff's recommendation to amend Chapter 17.88 of Title 17 to specify use of the ESA Maps in determining the Coastal Setback Line on individual properties, and to amend Chapter 17.90 to remove the conflicting interpretive procedure.
- **November 25, 2009** — A Notice of Public Hearing is issued for a re-noticed City Council hearing on the underlying after-the-fact variance (originally denied by the City Council on June 2, 2009, with the resolution not yet adopted as of November 25, 2009), set for December 15, 2009.

## III. The November 2009 Code Amendment — and What the Operative Methodology Became

The November 24, 2009 staff proposal would have inserted Section 17.88.030(E) into the Municipal Code with the following text (paraphrased from the draft ordinance in the case file): *for purposes of establishing the location of the Coastal Setback Line on individual properties, the location shall be based upon the ESA Maps which accompany the report titled "Geologic Factors Related to a Coastal Set-Back Zone for the City of Rancho Palos Verdes, California" prepared by Earth Sciences Associates (ESA) in 1976 and referenced in the appendix to the City of Rancho Palos Verdes' Coastal Specific Plan, on file in the office of the Director of Planning.* It would have simultaneously amended Chapter 17.90 to remove the existing interpretive procedure that allowed property owners to impeach the line with geotechnical or soils reports.

On the available record, this proposal does not appear to have been adopted in the form recommended.

A June 1, 2010 City Council staff report on the Residential Development Standards Steering Committee Code Amendment (Planning Case No. ZON2007-00377; distinct case file from ZON2009-00416) describes the operative methodology in force at that date as follows: *"the location of the Coastal Setback Line on properties shall be based on the City's official Zoning Map, by overlaying an enlarged portion of the City's Zoning Map onto a property and identifying the centerline of the Coastal Setback Line."*

That methodology is materially different from the November 2009 staff proposal. It does not bind the ESA Maps as the controlling instrument. It does not eliminate site-specific impeachment. It is closer to what the September 8, 2009 Planning Commission revised Interpretation (4-2-1) and the November 17, 2009 comment letter were arguing for: that the city's official Zoning Map (the legal-effect map produced by the certified 1978 Coastal Specific Plan) controls, with the line's centerline applied to the individual property.

The exact ordinance number and adoption date that locked the centerline-of-Zoning-Map methodology in still requires direct verification through the city's ordinance records or Municode. The available evidence indicates that the November 2009 staff proposal to make the ESA Maps the binding instrument did not survive the public comment record intact.

This disposition matters for two reasons. First, it means the foundational instrument for the Coastal Setback Line on any individual lot in the coastal zone — including 0 Clipper Road, 7 Sea Cove Drive, 24 Sea Cove Drive, and the lots holding the 1952 ocean access easements — is the city's official Zoning Map (which traces back to the certified 1978 Coastal Specific Plan), not the unadopted-and-uncertified 1976 ESA maps. Second, it means the September 8, 2009 Planning Commission revised Interpretation language — that the location shall be based on **a site-specific geology study and the Coastal Specific Plan Land Use Map** — may remain operative or partially operative, and any current development analysis that relies on the ESA Maps as binding is operating against an instrument that the available record does not support.

## IV. The Foundational Documents

### The 1976 Earth Sciences Associates Report

Title: *Geologic Factors Related to a Coastal Set-Back Zone for the City of Rancho Palos Verdes, California*. Prepared by Earth Sciences Associates. Used in preparing the 1978 Coastal Specific Plan, which was certified by the California Coastal Commission in 1979. The report defines geologic Categories 1a, 1b, 2, 3, and 4 in textual narrative and includes maps that show a 25-foot Coastal Structure Setback Zone landward of the Coastal Setback Line.

The November 23, 2009 WPBCA objection establishes on the record that:

- The ESA Report cites only five "references," all area-wide surveys, all dating from approximately 1972 and 1973.
- The report contains no site-specific geology testing.
- City staff conceded before the City Council that they had been unable to locate any site-specific geology to support the lines on the ESA Map.
- Category 1a, by ESA's own definition, prohibits virtually all uses including "human passage by foot, horseback, or off-road vehicle." Applied literally, the Category 1a designation in the ESA Map covers the entire coastal shore including portions of Abalone Cove Shoreline Park and the existing public and private coastal access paths.
- The ESA Report itself was never formally adopted by the City Council. Only the maps were used administratively.

The report itself is not in the Foundation archive. Acquisition path: California Coastal Commission Long Beach office; city Planning Department under public records request; possibly the Coastal Specific Plan appendix on file with the Director of Planning. The Zone 2 Landslide Moratorium Ordinance Revisions EIR Section 4.5 Geology document on the city website cites the report and may include partial extracts.

### The 1978 Certified Coastal Specific Plan and the December 19, 1978 Map

Adopted by the City Council in 1978 and certified by the California Coastal Commission in 1979. The November 17, 2009 comment letter describes the December 19, 1978 Certified Coastal Specific Plan Map as on file with the California Coastal Commission in Long Beach and within the city's own files, oversized and in color, corresponding precisely with the city's Official Zoning Map. The same letter references an "oversized wall hanging Zoning Map" that city staff "chose to remove from public view." Subsequent Planning Commission interpretation (September 8, 2009, 4-2-1) and the post-2009 operative methodology both treat the certified plan and its associated map as the controlling instrument for individual-property line location.

A clean reproduction of the December 19, 1978 map is not in the Foundation archive. Acquisition path: California Coastal Commission Long Beach office (highest probability of full-resolution); city Planning Department.

### The 2015 Coastal Specific Plan Update

The certified Coastal Specific Plan was updated in 2015. The updated PDF is available on the city's Maps & Misc. Documents page. Whether the 2015 update produced a more detailed map than the 1978 original, or merely amended policy language while leaving the foundational map intact, requires direct review of the 2015 document. As of the original 1978 plan, the November 17, 2009 commenter described the map as "in color, oversized, and very easy to further enlarge to show the Coastal Setback Line location on Individual properties."

A 2010 resolution (Res. No. 2010-87) modified policies and language for the Nantasket subdivision portion of the certified plan; this is an isolated, site-specific amendment, not a comprehensive map revision.

## VII. Through-Lines

**Same regulatory regime, two cases.** Both the 2006 and the 2007–2009 cases concern lots within Subregion 4 of the certified Coastal Specific Plan, governed by the same Coastal Setback Line, the same OC-2 and OC-3 overlay districts, and the same Subregion 4 policies. The 2006 case treats the Coastal Specific Plan as the controlling instrument on its face. The 2009 staff proposal would have substituted the ESA Map for the Coastal Specific Plan as the operative locating instrument for individual lots; the substitution does not appear to have survived the public comment record.

**Engineering record.** The 2006 case relies on the city's NPDES review of the natural drainage course. The 2007–2009 case relies on the property owner's own engineer to demonstrate, by CAD, that the ESA Map is non-congruent with the certified Coastal Zone Map. Both produce site-specific engineering work that bears on the corridor as a regulated environment. The owner's CAD report is the only contemporaneous site-specific impeachment of the ESA Map for any lot in the immediate area on the public record, but is not yet in our archive.

**Absent foundational documents.** The 1976 ESA Report and the December 1978 Certified Coastal Specific Plan Map are the two foundational instruments to which both cases refer. Neither is yet in our archive. Until they are, every downstream analysis — the 0 Clipper Road analysis, the Triangle Lot analysis, the corridor and easement analysis, any current development proposal in the coastal zone — operates against staff summaries of the foundational documents rather than against the documents themselves.

## VIII. Subregion 4 Policy No. 1 and the 0 Clipper Road Question

The 2006 staff report at page 3 quotes Subregion 4 Policy No. 1 of the certified Coastal Specific Plan as imposing on the city the obligation to "require all new housing within the confines of Subregion 4 to be of a single-family nature." The policy is invoked, in 2006, as governing law on a single-family permit. If the 0 Clipper Road parcel sits within Subregion 4 — a geographic question that requires verification against the certified plan map — then any 16-unit proposal at 0 Clipper Road is in apparent tension with the city's own certified Coastal Specific Plan policy as the city itself has invoked it. This is a record finding; it is not a litigation theory.
