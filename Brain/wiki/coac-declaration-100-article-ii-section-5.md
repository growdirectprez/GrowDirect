# Declaration 100 Article II §5 — Self-Executing Reactivation Mechanism

Declaration 100 Article II §5 is the **only procedural "reset button"** the Vanderlip drafters built into the original Lot H framework. It is dormant by design, obscure by design, and self-executing once its condition precedent is met. This card documents what the mechanism says, why it still works, and what it triggers.

**Not legal advice.** Distilled from a 2026-04-19 external-advisor memo. HOA counsel review required before invocation.

## The Clause

Article II §5 of Declaration 100 provides that if the Community Association of Palos Verdes fails to meet or carry on its functions for **90 consecutive days**, **15 owners of record title** of **15 separate parcels** within the original Declaration 100 boundary may:

1. **Publish notice** of a meeting — 3 times in a Los Angeles daily newspaper + 1 time in a San Pedro newspaper
2. **Call and hold** the meeting
3. **Elect a Board of three Trustees** by majority vote (votes weighted by acreage per the original declaration — but see [[coac-article-v-equity-modernization]] for modernization)

The three Trustees then automatically step into the shoes of the original Association and inherit all its enumerated powers under Article II §4 (26 powers — see below).

## Why the Condition Precedent Is Satisfied

The Community Association of Palos Verdes has been **dormant or dissolved for decades**. The 90-day failure-to-meet requirement was satisfied long ago and has remained satisfied continuously.

This is not an accidental gap. The advisor's synthesis captures the intent:

> "The 90-day 'failure to meet and carry on' clause in Declaration 100, Article II, Section 5 was deliberately written as a dormant reactivation mechanism, not a routine operating rule. The Vanderlip drafters never intended the Community Association of Palos Verdes or its Art Jury to function as an active, day-to-day body for the entire Lot H coastline. Those entities were intentionally left private, obscure, and latent — to be invoked only when the private layer needed to be revived against external threats."

The map-book references to Declarations 100 and 101 do not appear under modern tract names in routine title searches. They surface only through:

- The original metes-and-bounds survey notes for Lot H
- The 1930 Filiorum grant deed (Book 10226)
- Handwritten annotations in private archives of early peninsula residents
- Deep cross-referencing of the 1939 PVE booklet, 1949–1950 instruments, Wong subdivision map, and Coastal Specific Plan

The obscurity is protective. It preserved the mechanism for the moment residents who know about it choose to use it.

## What the Trustees Inherit (Article II §4 — 26 Enumerated Powers)

Once elected, the three Trustees exercise the full Article II §4 authority. Key powers:

- Enforcement of recorded restrictions (including Declaration 100/101 covenants)
- Assessment liens
- Art Jury interpretation and approval authority
- Zoning and building-code functions (within the private layer)
- Park, street, water, sewer oversight
- Contract authority
- Recording of amendments, restatements, and operating instruments

These are not advisory or recommendatory powers. They are the full delegated authority of the original Community Association of Palos Verdes, now held by the three elected Trustees.

## What the Trustees Do First

The first substantive act at or after the meeting is to **record a Restated Declaration of Covenants, Conditions & Restrictions** (modeled on the 2009 WPBCA restatement). That instrument:

- Expressly incorporates surviving, non-void provisions of Declarations 100 and 101
- States the negative-space Lot H boundary (see [[coac-negative-space-lot-h-boundary]])
- Modernizes Article V equity/vote-share (see [[coac-article-v-equity-modernization]])
- Adds contemporary recitals (creek/fault-line avoidance corridor; ACLAD partnership; 1980 Wong subdivision; 1978 Coastal Specific Plan; 1946 USGS report; 1982 Ehlig paper; 2011 LGC Valley study)
- Reaffirms reversionary interest and enforcement rights

See [[coac-restated-declaration-blitz-plan]] for the full execution sequence.

## Why This Is NOT the High-Threshold Amendment Path

Article V §2(a) of Declaration 100 sets a high amendment threshold: **80% by area + 70% of all owners + Association approval + Corporation (or successor) consent**. That would be impossible to assemble in 2026 and is not required here.

Article II §5 is a different path. It is a **constitutional-crisis reset**, not an amendment. The 15-owner trigger is a low-threshold on-ramp the Vanderlip drafters built for precisely the situation the peninsula faces today: a failed private association + external pressure from a larger jurisdiction (then the County; now the City under state housing mandates).

Courts treat properly noticed, recorded restatements as valid ratifications of the original covenants (2009 WPBCA restatement precedent; Nahrstedt v. Lakeside Village (1994) 8 Cal.4th 361; Citizens for Covenant Compliance v. Anderson (1995) 12 Cal.4th 345).

## What the 15 Owners Must Prove

- They hold **record title** to **15 separate parcels**
- Each parcel lies **within the original Declaration 100 boundary** — i.e., inside the master Lot H metes-and-bounds minus the recorded negative-space exclusions (see [[coac-negative-space-lot-h-boundary]])
- The Community Association of Palos Verdes has failed to meet or act for >90 consecutive days (trivially true; decades of dormancy)
- The notice was published as required (3× LA daily + 1× San Pedro)
- The meeting was properly held
- The three Trustees were elected by majority vote

Nothing more. No 80%/70% supermajority. No City approval. No Coastal Commission pre-review. No public hearing before recording.

## Operational Gates Before Invocation

Separate HOA counsel must verify:

- **Exact notice publication requirements** — confirm the current reading of "Los Angeles daily newspaper" + "San Pedro paper" and whether any replacement publications satisfy the original language
- **Certified copy of Declaration 100 page 155** — the metes-and-bounds page is smeared on the county recorder's microfilm; pull a cleaner copy from the Vanderlip Papers or a title-plant search
- **Reversionary rights successor analysis** — PV Corp and Filiorum are dissolved. Under California escheat law, the reversionary rights may have passed to adjoining owners or the State. The Trustees' recording can recite the Article II §5 mechanism as supplying the necessary consent.
- **2023 rename continuity** — confirm the rename from WPBCA → Community of Abalone Cove filed with CA SOS does not affect the Association's standing under Declarations 100/101 (same legal entity; continuity of history)

## Risks and Defenses

**Risk:** The City or a developer (e.g., the Clipper Road applicant) sues to declare the old declarations unenforceable.

**Defense:** The Restated Declaration is prepared with a full evidentiary packet:
- 1939 PVE Civic Problems booklet
- 1949 Declaration of Easements
- 1950 Lot H Declaration
- 1980 Wong subdivision map
- Coastal Specific Plan Subregion 4
- USGS 1946 geologic report
- Ehlig 1982 landslide guidebook
- 2011 LGC Valley Zone 2 geotechnical study

The record becomes the defense. Non-abandonment is demonstrated by continuous reference in later deeds and the 1978 Coastal Specific Plan.

**Risk:** Changed-conditions defense (the covenants no longer serve their original purpose).

**Defense:** Geologic, drainage, and single-family covenants directly map to today's documented landslide complex, ACLAD dewatering operations, and the creek/fault-line alluvial shelf edge. The conditions have not changed — they have intensified. See [[coac-aclad-partnership]].

**Risk:** Racial covenants in the original declaration.

**Defense:** Expressly struck from the Restated Declaration, exactly as the 2009 WPBCA restatement did. Shelley v. Kraemer (1948) and the Fair Housing Act (1968) already render them void; the Restated Declaration acknowledges that cleanly.
