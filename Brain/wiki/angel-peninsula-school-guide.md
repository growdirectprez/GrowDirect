# Peninsula School Guide — Content Pool (Raw)

**Purpose:** One canonical reference for everything Angel content needs to say
about schools on the Palos Verdes Peninsula. Replaces the per-neighborhood
Schools sections (which have been removed from `angel-*-raw.md` profiles —
assigned elementary is a logistics question Angelique answers case-by-case,
not a content asset). Middle and high school on the Hill are district-wide
stories, so they belong in one place.

**Scope:** PVPUSD public schools (10 elementary, 3 intermediate, 2 comprehensive
high schools, 1 continuation), the out-of-district permit options that let
non-residents attend PVPUSD, the on-Hill private schools, and the parochial /
church-affiliated K-8 schools.

**Out of scope:** Specific test scores and rankings per school — those age fast
and live in the per-neighborhood raw profiles where they're more useful as
color. This guide is structural: which schools exist, how assignment works,
how out-of-district families get in, and what the schedule / culture
differences are between the two high schools.

---

## 1. TL;DR — What Angel Content Needs To Know

- **Elementary is boundary-based** but your assigned school depends on capacity,
  not just your address. Families sometimes get routed to the next-closest
  campus when their home school is "impacted."
- **Cornerstone Elementary has no boundary** — it's the district's Spanish
  dual-immersion program, open by application and lottery to any PVPUSD
  resident (Lunada Bay gets priority as the host campus).
- **Middle school (called "Intermediate" in PVPUSD)** is boundary-assigned
  across three schools — PV Intermediate (PVE side), Miraleste (eastern RPV),
  and Ridgecrest (RHE / western RPV). Lots of families juggle carpools,
  private transport, or intra-district transfers depending on which side of
  the Hill a parent works.
- **High school is open choice district-wide.** Any PVPUSD resident can enroll
  at either PV High or Peninsula — they are not boundary-assigned. The
  schools have meaningfully different schedules and cultures (see §5).
- **Out-of-district families have real paths in.** The grandparent permit,
  Allen Bill (parent employment inside district boundaries), and the Eastview
  optional attendance area are the three most relevant to peninsula buyers.
- **Private and parochial schools on the Hill matter to a meaningful share of
  buyers** — Chadwick, Peninsula Heritage, Rolling Hills Country Day, St.
  John Fisher, and Christ Lutheran are the names families actually discuss.

## 2. PVPUSD — The Public District

### The Basics

Palos Verdes Peninsula Unified School District serves the four peninsula
cities (Palos Verdes Estates, Rancho Palos Verdes, Rolling Hills, Rolling
Hills Estates) plus unincorporated parts of the Peninsula. Roughly
**10,300 students across 17 schools.** District office is at 375 Via Almar,
PVE (the original Malaga Cove School site, opened 1926).

## 3. How Elementary Assignment Works

### The Default Path

Enter your address into the PVPUSD School Locator and it tells you your
assigned elementary. The district's stated goal is to place you there.

### The "Impacted" Caveat

Assignment is only finalized after enrollment is complete. If your home school
is "impacted at the time enrollment is completed," PVPUSD can place the
student at "the closest school with available space." For popular boundary
schools (Montemalaga, Lunada Bay, Silver Spur), this is a real risk in a
hot enrollment year.

### Cornerstone — the Dual Immersion Option

Cornerstone Elementary has no attendance boundary. Any PVPUSD-eligible student
can apply. Program specifics:

- **Model:** Parent-participation + dual language immersion.
- **Language:** Spanish (first DLI iteration — majority of day and all core
  content taught in Spanish; PE in English).
- **Application window:** March 1 – March 31 via Aeries parent portal.
- **Notifications:** End of April.
- **Priority:** Lunada Bay residents first (Cornerstone is the Lunada Bay
  campus host site). Lottery for all other district students if seats remain.
- **No waitlist** for denied applications.

### Intra-District Transfers (want a different elementary than your assigned one)

- Parents can list **up to 2** alternative schools in order of preference.
- **One application window only:** May 15 – June 15. Notifications by Aug 3.
- Applications are processed via **random lottery**, not first-come.
- **Accepting a transfer means giving up your current placement.** The district
  explicitly says "do not participate if you are unsure."
- Sibling priority applies only to *currently enrolled* students — not to
  new applications in the same window.

### The Eastview Optional Attendance Area

Eastview (eastern RPV adjoining San Pedro) is technically zoned for LAUSD.
PVPUSD designates it an **optional attendance area** — TK–12 students who
live there may choose PVPUSD instead. This is a unique, Hill-specific path
and should be called out in any Eastview-adjacent content.

## 4. Middle School (Intermediate) — Boundaries and Logistics Juggling

### Boundary Assignment

Unlike high school, intermediate school is boundary-assigned across the three
campuses. Rough pattern:

- **PVIS** — PVE-side neighborhoods (Malaga Cove, Valmonte, Monte Malaga, Lunada
  Bay, Montemalaga feeder).
- **Miraleste** — eastern RPV (Miraleste, Mira Catalina, PV Drive East and
  North, Mediterrania / Mirasol).
- **Ridgecrest** — RHE, Silver Spur, Rolling Hills, western RPV, La Cresta.

### The Real-World Juggle

This is where the Hill's geography matters. Parents who work at opposite ends
of the peninsula, carpool across the hill, or have kids at two different-side
schools (e.g., one at Lunada Bay, one at Miraleste) regularly use the
**intradistrict transfer window** (Feb 16–28, notifications March 3) to move
a kid to the more-logistical school.

Angelique sees this constantly: a family buys on the RHE side but has work or
family ties on the PVE side and wants PVIS. Approvals are space-dependent,
not guaranteed, and accepting a transfer means giving up the boundary
placement. It's a real workflow consideration during a home search.

### What Drives Middle School Choice When Transfer Is Possible

- Commute direction (home-to-parent-workplace).
- Sibling at the same campus (sibling priority for currently enrolled).
- Specific elective or program strengths (PVIS is known for language/arts
  electives; Ridgecrest has a reputation for advanced math/science; Miraleste
  draws families who want smaller scale on the east side).
- Friend-group continuity from the feeder elementary.

## 5. High School — Open Choice and Why the Two Schools Are Different

### The Most Important Fact

**Any student residing in PVPUSD may attend either PV High or Peninsula High.**
High school is not boundary-assigned — it's open enrollment for district
residents. Enrollment is done directly at the high school campus; the 2026–27
window opens February 2, 2026.

That means the "which high school is your kid going to" question isn't
determined by where on the Hill you buy a house. It's a real choice the
family makes — and the two schools genuinely feel different.

### Palos Verdes High School (PVHS) — Block Schedule

- **Address:** 600 Cloyden Rd, Palos Verdes Estates (half a block from the
  Pacific — the "ocean-side" campus).
- **Schedule:** Block. **Three 115-minute block periods per day**, plus an
  optional Zero Period (55 minutes, daily). Two-semester calendar.
- **Implications of block:** Fewer classes per day, deeper single-subject
  immersion, more substantial homework blocks per subject, fewer transitions.
  Works well for students who focus better in longer stretches; can be tough
  for students who prefer variety and frequent resets.
- **Scale:** Smaller than Peninsula. Feels more intimate; some families
  describe it as the more "boutique" of the two.
- **Culture keywords (to vet with Angelique):** beachy, surf-culture undertone,
  tight-knit, arts-forward — see [[Brain/wiki/card-pvhs-drama|Card — PVHS Drama]] for the program nicknamed "Best Entertainment Value in LA."

### Palos Verdes Peninsula High School (PVPHS) — Traditional Schedule

- **Address:** 27118 Silver Spur Rd, Rolling Hills Estates (the "hilltop"
  campus).
- **Schedule:** Traditional. **Six periods per day**, roughly 44–61 minutes
  each, plus a 35–39 minute lunch.
- **Implications of traditional:** More subjects per day, shorter bursts,
  more frequent transitions. Students see every teacher every day. Closer
  to the typical public-high-school rhythm.
- **Scale:** ~2,000 students — the bigger of the two. Historically "Rolling
  Hills High School" before merging with PV High's name lineage.
- **Culture keywords (to vet with Angelique):** larger-school energy, broader
  elective catalog, more comprehensive sports program.

### How to Frame It in Content

- **Don't imply one is better.** Both are high-performing, 5-star, top-125ish
  California high schools. The delta is fit, not quality.
- **The schedule difference is the most decision-relevant fact.** Block vs.
  traditional is a real academic-lifestyle choice.
- **Mention it's open choice.** Families relocating from out of state often
  assume high school is boundary-assigned like elementary. It's not.

## 6. Getting Into PVPUSD From Out of District

This is the section that matters most for buyers who own (or are considering)
a home outside PVPUSD boundaries but want access to the schools — or buyers
who come from out of state and don't realize non-resident paths exist.

### The Five Real Paths

**1. Grandparent Permit** (the most commonly used out-of-district path)

- Available for **TK through 12** — covers all grade bands.
- Student must have at least one grandparent residing inside PVPUSD
  boundaries. LAUSD-zoned portions of RHE and RPV (notably parts of Eastview)
  don't qualify as "inside PVPUSD boundaries."
- **Application window:** April 1 – June 1 (annual).
- **Documents required:**
  - Student's official birth certificate.
  - **Parent's** official birth certificate (proves the parent–grandparent
    lineage). This is the piece most applicants forget.
  - Two different utility bills showing the grandparent's PVPUSD-boundary
    address.
  - Recent report card or transcript.
  - IEP or 504 plan if applicable.
  - An interdistrict permit release from the student's home district.
- **Online enrollment quirk:** When filling the district portal, enter the
  home address as literally `1 Grandparent Permit` — *not* the student's
  actual home address and not the grandparent's address.
- **Space-dependent.** Approval is not guaranteed. District recommends
  concurrent enrollment in your home district as a fallback.
- **Placement:** Intermediate and high school placements are communicated at
  the time of permit approval.

**2. Allen Bill (Parent Employment)**

A parent works **at least 10 hours per week** at a business physically located
inside PVPUSD boundaries. Governed by Administrative Regulation 5.111.12.
Covers K–12. Space-dependent like the grandparent permit.

**3. Active-Duty Military**

Children of active-duty U.S. military parents qualify for an interdistrict
permit. Separate, expedited process.

**4. Bullying Victim Transfer**

Under California Education Code 46600, students determined to be bullying
victims at their district of residence (or at a proposed district of
attendance) receive priority consideration for interdistrict permits.

**5. Eastview Optional Attendance**

Not a permit — a zoning fact. Eastview RPV is LAUSD-zoned but can opt into
PVPUSD. No lineage, employment, or military tie required.

### Also on the Books

- **Child of PVPUSD alumni** — separate pathway.
- **PVPUSD employee children** — separate pathway.
- **Homeless and foster youth** — covered under state and federal placement
  rules.

### Insider Notes for Agents (vet with Angelique before publishing)

- The grandparent permit is **widely used** on the peninsula. It's one of the
  quieter reasons multi-generational families stay in PVPUSD across moves.
  Buyers from South Bay / South LA with a parent still living on the Hill
  should be reminded it's a real option.
- **Interdistrict releases from the home district are the common failure
  point.** LAUSD, for example, has its own release process that takes time
  and doesn't always approve. Build that into expectations.
- **Permits are not portable across children** — each kid files separately.
- Allen Bill is underused. A buyer who works out of a Peninsula Center office,
  Golden Cove, or any PVE / RPV / RHE business has a viable path.
- Families sometimes **coordinate jobs or small side businesses** to physically
  locate inside PVPUSD for Allen Bill qualification. This is legitimate but
  requires real substance (the 10-hour minimum is verified).

## 7. Private Schools on (and Adjacent to) the Hill

These are the private schools peninsula families actually consider. All
tuition figures below are approximate and move every year — verify against
the school's admissions page before citing.

### Chadwick School — K–12

- 26800 Academy Drive, Palos Verdes Peninsula (Rolling Hills side).
- **K–12.** ~860 students, 8:1 student–teacher.
- **Tuition:** ~$43,740–$51,240 (varies by grade).
- Founded 1935 by Margaret Lee and Commander Joseph Chadwick. Moved to PV in 1938.
- Outdoor education K–12; global studies and community service grades 7–12.
- Niche: #4 private K–12 in LA, #7 in California, #19 private high school
  in America (2026).
- The default name families mean when they say "Hill private school."

### Peninsula Heritage School — K–8

- Rolling Hills Estates. Founded 1961.
- Small (around 12–15 students per class).
- Emphasis on individualized education, warm / small-community feel.

### Rolling Hills Country Day School — K–8

- Palos Verdes Peninsula.
- K–5 elementary plus 6–8 middle school divisions.
- Small classes, subject-specialized teachers.

### Rolling Hills Preparatory — 6–12

- **Located in San Pedro** (1 Rolling Hills Prep Way), not on the Hill, but
  serves peninsula families heavily.
- **Tuition:** ~$44,800.
- Academically rigorous, known for strong learning-support program.
- 11 interscholastic sports.

### Homeschool Co-ops

- **South Bay Christian Homeschool Academy** — K–8 co-op based in RPV for
  Christian homeschooling families.

*Montessori schools have been moved to §9 since most are early-childhood
focused. See [Montessori cluster](#montessori-cluster) below.*

## 8. Parochial, Religious, and K–8 Church Schools

This is the section the peninsula content market tends to under-cover. A lot
of families — including families who could afford Chadwick — choose a
religious K–8 for values reasons, smaller scale, or simply proximity. The
peninsula is well-served by Catholic and Lutheran options, with the Lutheran
schools clustered just over the PVE boundary along PV Boulevard.

### Catholic

**St. John Fisher School — K–8**

- 5446 Crest Road, Rancho Palos Verdes. Part of St. John Fisher parish.
- **~183 students.** Small classes (≤20 per class). 14:1 student–teacher.
- **Tuition:** ~$7,780 (parishioner rate) up to ~$11,600 (non-parishioner)
  per public sources. Confirm current year directly.
- Academically rigorous prep for high school per parent reviews; some
  critiques around extra-fees / parent-participation hours ($20/hour charge
  for unfulfilled participation).
- The peninsula's default Catholic K–8.

**Mary Star of the Sea — K–8 and 9–12 (two separate schools, same parish)**

Technically in San Pedro, but close enough to the RPV/Western Avenue boundary
that peninsula families — especially on the Eastview, Miraleste, PV Drive
East, and Mira Catalina side of the Hill — regularly consider it.

- **Mary Star of the Sea Elementary** — PK–8. 717 S Cabrillo Ave, San Pedro
  90731. Founded 1922 by the parish. Catholic. Financial-aid and
  sliding-scale tuition available.
- **Mary Star of the Sea High School** — 9–12. 2500 N Taper Ave, San Pedro
  90732. Founded 1954. Co-educational, Roman Catholic. Sits up on the bluffs
  above the harbor, near Miraleste Plaza — just down the hill from the RPV
  boundary, which is why it shows up in peninsula families' consideration
  sets even though it's technically San Pedro.

Mary Star High is the **only nearby Catholic high school option.** All three
PVPUSD high schools are secular and Chadwick is secular independent, so Mary
Star is the parochial-high-school answer for families who want faith-based
continuity after a K–8 like St. John Fisher.

### Lutheran — The PV Boulevard Cluster

Three Lutheran K–8 schools are relevant to peninsula families. Two sit just
over the PVE western boundary along Palos Verdes Boulevard (which, despite
the name, is technically Torrance and Redondo Beach right where PVE ends)
and one is on Western Avenue on the eastern RPV boundary. Together they're
the biggest religious K–8 option on/near the Hill after the Catholic schools.

**Riviera Hall Lutheran School — PK–8**

- **330 Palos Verdes Boulevard, Redondo Beach** 90277 (right on the PVE /
  Hollywood Riviera / Redondo Beach boundary — feels like the Hill even
  though it's technically Redondo).
- 242 students, PK through 8th grade.
- Founded 1952. Affiliated with Resurrection Lutheran Church.
- The closest religious K–8 for buyers on the PVE / Valmonte / Lunada Bay side.

**First Lutheran School — PK–8**

- 2900 W Carson Street, Torrance 90503 (Torrance side of the Hill, along the
  PV Boulevard / Hawthorne corridor — an easy drive from Valmonte, Malaga
  Cove, and the Riviera).
- 293 students, TK through 8th grade.
- **Tuition:** ~$8,420 top grade.
- Founded 1957. Owned and operated by First Evangelical Lutheran Church of
  Torrance. WASC-accredited.

**Christ Lutheran Church & School — EC–8**

- 28850 South Western Avenue, Rancho Palos Verdes (eastern RPV boundary,
  serves the Mira Catalina / Eastview / PV Dr East side of the Hill —
  essentially the east-side counterpart to the west-side PV Blvd cluster).
- **Early childhood through grade 8.** Lutheran Christian.
- Smaller, community-oriented. Christ-centered curriculum.

Insider note: the "PV Boulevard Lutheran cluster" is one of those things
Hill-native families know about and out-of-area buyers don't. If a family
wants a values-based K–8 and lives on the PVE / Valmonte / Lunada Bay side
(where Christ Lutheran on Western is too far), Riviera Hall and First
Lutheran are the real options.

## 9. Preschool & Early Childhood on the Hill

The peninsula has an unusually deep preschool market — and many of the
social / word-of-mouth feeder networks into both PVPUSD elementaries and
private K–8s run through these preschools. Four clusters matter.

### PVPUSD Early Learning Academies

The district runs two Early Learning Academies. These are district-operated
preschool programs — relevant because they feed directly into PVPUSD TK/K
enrollment.

- **Miraleste Early Learning Academy** — east side of the Hill.
- **Valmonte Early Learning Academy** — PVE / west side.

### Montessori Cluster

The peninsula has four Montessori options. Most are early-childhood focused
but some extend into elementary years.

- **Rolling Hills Montessori** — 26825 Rolling Hills Road, Rolling Hills
  Estates. 18 months through 6 years (PK, K–1). ~100 students, 6:1 ratio.
- **Palos Verdes Montessori Academy** — 28451 Indian Peak Road, Palos Verdes
  Peninsula. Serves PVE, RPV, RH, RHE, and Torrance. Enrichment in music,
  dance, Chinese, Spanish, sports. Full hot lunch and breakfast included.
- **Peninsula Montessori School (Palos Verdes Campus)** — Rancho Palos Verdes.
  18 months through 5th grade — the one Montessori that extends meaningfully
  into elementary. ~48 years in operation.
- **Silver Spur Montessori** — 656 Bart Earle Way, Rolling Hills Estates.

### Church Preschools

Several Hill churches run preschool programs. These function as both
faith-community programs and as the social onramp into local elementary
school cohorts.

- **St. Peter's by the Sea Preschool** (Presbyterian) — 6410 Palos Verdes Dr
  South, RPV. Ages 2–5. 9 AM–12 PM base day, extended day to 3 PM. Enrichment
  in Spanish, art, music.
- **Rolling Hills Covenant Church Preschool** — 2222 Palos Verdes Dr North,
  Rolling Hills Estates. Ages 2–5. Mon–Fri 8 AM–5 PM. Play-based Christian
  curriculum. Covers cognitive, gross motor, creative, language, spiritual.
- **Rolling Hills United Methodist Church Preschool (RHUMPS)** — 26438
  Crenshaw Boulevard, Rolling Hills Estates. Ages 2–5. Half-day and full-day
  options, 2/3/5-day schedules. Early care from 7:30 AM, extended day to
  3 PM. Also operates **RHUMPS@Walteria** as a second site.
- **Riviera Hall Lutheran Preschool** — attached to the K–8 above. 330 PV
  Blvd, Redondo Beach. Ages 3–5.
- **First Lutheran Church Preschool** — attached to the K–8 above, 2900 W
  Carson, Torrance.
- **Christ Lutheran Early Childhood** — attached to the K–8 above, 28850 S
  Western, RPV.
- **Neighborhood Church** (Rolling Hills Estates) — community programs.

### Temple / Jewish Programs — the Crestridge Corridor

**Congregation Ner Tamid of South Bay** (Conservative Jewish) sits on
Crestridge Road in RPV — in the Indian Peak / Highridge / Crestridge corridor
the user referenced. It hosts two education programs that pull from across
the peninsula:

- **Congregation Ner Tamid** — 5721 Crestridge Road, Rancho Palos Verdes
  90275. The peninsula's primary Conservative synagogue.
- **The David Preschool** (at Ner Tamid) — bilingual English/Hebrew early
  childhood center built around three pillars: Limmud (learning), Kehillah
  (community), Chesed (kindness). Fosters multilingual language development,
  artistic creativity, early math/science. Mon–Fri 8 AM–5 PM. One of the
  city's most respected early childhood centers.
- **Ner Tamid Religious School** — supplementary / Hebrew school for
  Conservative Jewish peninsula families.

### The Crest Road Cluster

**Crest Road** runs along the top of RPV / Rolling Hills and is home to
several early-childhood programs plus St. John Fisher K–8. For a buyer on
the top-of-the-Hill ridge neighborhoods (Mesa Palos Verdes, Crest area,
Island View, parts of La Cresta), almost all their preschool and K–8 options
are within a five-minute drive along Crest.

- **Hilltop Nursery School** — 5702 Crest Road, RPV. Ages 2–6. Mon–Fri
  8 AM–3 PM. Play-based, experienced teachers.
- **CHALK Preschool** — 5702 Crest Road, RPV. Mon–Fri 8 AM–5 PM. Boutique
  preschool with fewer classrooms and indoor/outdoor learning focus.
- **St. John Fisher School** (K–8) — 5446 Crest Road. (Already listed under
  Catholic.)

### Other Early Childhood Centers

- **Discovery World Early Education Center** — Rolling Hills Estates. Infant
  center and preschool.
- **New Horizons Preschool** — Palos Verdes Peninsula. Early childhood
  education.
- **Broadacres Pre-School** — Palos Verdes.
- **ALC Preschool** — peninsula area.

## 10. After-School Enrichment, Tutoring, and STEM Programs

The peninsula's commercial centers are saturated with after-school enrichment.
This is a distinctive feature of the Hill's education market — academic
intensity doesn't stop at the school bell, and **essentially every commercial
plaza on the peninsula hosts at least one enrichment program.** Families
treat this layer as part of the schooling picture, not as optional add-on.

### The Two Main Commercial Clusters

**Peninsula Center (RHE / Deep Valley area — "Peninsula Mall")**

This is the peninsula's densest enrichment cluster. Walking distance from
Peninsula High. Concentrates STEM, math, and language-arts tutoring.

- **Code Ninjas Palos Verdes** — 60 Peninsula Center, RHE. STEM coding
  program for kids — problem solving, critical thinking, coding projects.
- **Mathnasium of Rolling Hills Estates** — 805 Deep Valley Drive. K–12 math
  tutoring, from elementary through Calculus. In-person and virtual sessions.
- **Kumon Math and Reading Center** — 627 Deep Valley Drive, Suite A. The
  franchise math/reading program. Open Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri 10–7 (closed Wed).
- **Peninsula Academy** — 734 Silver Spur Road, RHE. General tutoring.

**Lunada Bay commercial (PVE coastal side)**

The west-side equivalent. Smaller footprint but serves PVE / Valmonte /
Montemalaga / Malaga Cove families who don't want to drive across the Hill.

- **Study Hut Tutoring — Lunada Bay** — 716 Yarmouth Road, Ste 101/102/104/
  105, PVE 90274. The peninsula flagship location. Offers a formal
  **After-School Homework Club** with small-group supervision, focus on
  executive-functioning skills, subject-specific support, and test prep.
  Aimed at elementary and middle-school students.

### Plus a Third — Along PV Boulevard / Redondo Riviera

Study Hut also operates a Redondo Beach Riviera office serving the western-
edge families. This is the same PV Boulevard corridor where Riviera Hall
Lutheran sits.

### The Pattern Worth Calling Out

- Every Hill commercial plaza — Peninsula Center, Golden Cove, Miraleste
  Plaza, Malaga Cove Plaza, Lunada Bay, Silver Spur plaza — hosts at least
  some enrichment (tutoring, music, martial arts, art, STEM, or language).
- The density reflects demand: PVPUSD academic expectations plus a large
  share of dual-income professional households equals strong baseline spend
  on enrichment.
- For a real estate buyer, this matters because **"what's in the nearest
  plaza?"** is effectively a logistics answer about weekly routines, not
  just a retail question.

### Private Tutoring Beyond the Named Centers

Beyond the franchised centers, the peninsula has a large informal private
tutoring market — college students from USC/UCLA, teachers doing side work,
former PVPUSD instructors, and academic coaches. This isn't publicly
mapped but is a real part of the scene. Angelique almost certainly has
recommendations families trust; worth capturing those in a follow-up.

### Music, Art, and Non-Academic Enrichment

The Hill also has deep music/art/athletic enrichment infrastructure worth
noting (piano and violin studios in every plaza, PV Art Center, PV Ballet,
South Bay Music Conservatory, etc.) — these aren't "school" per se but
belong in any comprehensive content piece about peninsula kid-life. Could
be a sibling content asset to this guide.

## 11. Insider Scoop — For Angelique and Angel Content

### What School Choice Actually Looks Like on the Hill

- **Elementary is a logistics decision.** It's driven by boundary,
  "impacted school" risk, and whether the family is willing to fight for a
  Cornerstone DI slot. Angelique can talk about the specific elementary for
  any given address.
- **Middle school is when logistics get real.** PVIS / Miraleste / Ridgecrest
  are all well-regarded. The transfer window is quietly one of the most
  important calendar items for Hill parents — Feb 16–28.
- **High school is the culture question.** Families who care about schedule
  philosophy (block vs. traditional) make an active choice. Some families
  deliberately split — an older kid at PV High, younger at Peninsula — based
  on fit.

### Grandparent / Allen Bill Framing

- Leading with "you might still qualify" for out-of-district buyers is a real
  rapport-builder. Most non-local buyers don't know about the grandparent
  permit or Allen Bill at all.
- **The grandparent permit is a multi-generational legacy lever** — it's how
  a family who moved "down the hill" in the 1990s can still get grandkids
  into PVPUSD today.

### Private-vs-Public Mindset

- The Hill is unusual in that **its public schools are as strong as its
  privates** for most families. The "why go private" reasons tend to be:
  - Class size (Chadwick small-cohort culture).
  - Values / faith (St. John Fisher, Christ Lutheran).
  - Learning differences (Rolling Hills Prep's learning-support reputation).
  - K–12 continuity (Chadwick).
- Not performance-per-se. That's a useful framing when working with out-of-
  area buyers who assume they need to write $50K checks to get "the best."
