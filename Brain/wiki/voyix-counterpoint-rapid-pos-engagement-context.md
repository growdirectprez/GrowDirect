## NCR Voyix — backstory

**Founded 1879** as the National Manufacturing Company in Dayton, Ohio. James Ritty invented the first mechanical cash register the same year. Bought in 1884 by John H. Patterson and renamed the **National Cash Register Company** — the original NCR. Spent the 20th century pivoting from cash registers to mainframe computing to retail and banking systems.

**The 2023 split.** On Sept 16, 2022, NCR announced a planned separation of its Digital Commerce business from its ATM business. Names were unveiled July 24, 2023: **NCR Voyix** for Digital Commerce (retail, restaurant, digital banking software/POS/payments), **NCR Atleos** for ATM. Split completed Oct 16, 2023. Stockholders received one share of Atleos common stock for every two shares of NCR.

**NCR Voyix today.** Headquartered in Atlanta, Georgia. NYSE: VYX. Operates in 35+ countries. Three segments: Retail, Restaurants, Digital Banking. Strategic positioning: "global platform-powered leader in unified commerce for shopping and dining."

**"Unified commerce" is Voyix's strategic pillar.** Not a product — a platform thesis. The pitch: bring POS, ecommerce, payments, kiosk, mobile, and back-office onto one platform, with one identity layer, one inventory ledger, one customer record. Voyix uses the term across its retail, restaurant, and digital-banking marketing and is hiring against it. The article Geoffrey shared is one of Voyix's anchor pieces on this theme.

## Counterpoint — the platform involved here

Counterpoint is the **specialty-retail product line** inside Voyix Retail. Originally an independent product (Counterpoint SQL, dating to the 1980s-90s as a Synchronics product), acquired by Radiant Systems in 2007, acquired by NCR in 2011 with Radiant. Survived the 2023 split as part of NCR Voyix Retail.

**Target customer.** Mid-sized specialty retailers in North America. Voyix lists the verticals explicitly: **apparel, gift, lawn & garden, sporting goods, wine & liquor, and beyond.** Lawn & Garden is a named vertical — the H&G prospect maps directly onto Voyix's stated specialty-retail go-to-market.

**Capabilities.** POS, inventory management, customer loyalty, automated purchasing, configurable reporting, ecommerce hooks. Open architecture for customization. Mobile and back-office front ends. The NCR 2017 announcement of a "next-generation Counterpoint channel software solution" signaled the modernization push that's still active under the Voyix banner.

**Channel-led, not direct-sold.** Counterpoint is sold through a partner channel, not direct by Voyix. This is critical for the engagement model — Voyix is the platform owner, but the customer relationship and the implementation contract live with the VAR.

## Rapid POS — the VAR involved here

**Rapid POS** is one of the most prominent NCR Counterpoint VARs in North America, operating across the USA, Canada, and Mexico. Headquartered in San Diego (per LinkedIn). They're a certified Counterpoint reseller, systems integrator, support provider, and trainer.

**Where Rapid POS plays in the channel.**
- **Sales.** They sell the platform license, hardware, and their own services as a bundle.
- **Implementation.** Software/hardware install, configuration, training, go-live support.
- **Customization.** They build their own enhancements — ecommerce connectors, tax/compliance integrations, vendor integrations, ERP bridges, loyalty modules.
- **Specialized verticals.** They run **Rapid Gun Systems** as a Counterpoint-on-firearms-retail division. The vertical-specialization pattern matters: it suggests Rapid POS will spin specialized Counterpoint configurations for verticals where they see scale, and Lawn & Garden is one Voyix already markets.
- **Training.** They operate **Counterpoint University** — a knowledge/training site for the Counterpoint user community. Running "the university" gives them disproportionate channel mindshare beyond their share of installs.

**Implication for the engagement.** Rapid POS treats the customer relationship as theirs, not Voyix's. They will resell us to the H&G chain, not introduce us. They'll want a clean scope boundary, repeatable methodology, and predictable margin. They will value an engagement methodology that lets them take the work to other Counterpoint customers afterward, not just the H&G chain.

## Store as Mini Data Center

An NCR Counterpoint deployment is on-premise by default. The store runs Counterpoint on a Windows Server inside the building — the same server that holds the POS database, the transaction history, the inventory ledger, and the customer records. There is no mandatory cloud dependency for the core retail operation.

This is Canary's deployment advantage. The edge agent runs on hardware that already exists, connects to a database that is already running, and operates on a network that is already in place. No new infrastructure budget required. No cloud migration required. The store is already a mini data center — Canary plugs in.

**What the store hardware typically includes:**

| Component | Typical spec | Canary use |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| Windows Server (on-prem) | 2016/2019, 16–32 GB RAM | Edge agent Docker container |
| Counterpoint SQL Server | SQL Server 2016+ | Module T direct adapter |
| NAS or local storage | 2–8 TB | SQLite event buffer + local Fox evidence store |
| Camera system | DVR/NVR, 4–16 cameras | Module Q video feed (Phase 2+) |
| Store LAN | 1 Gbps | NATS JetStream local event bus |
| WAN / internet | Variable quality | Sync path to cloud ALX — degradation-tolerant |

**The offline contract:** The store must run without internet. Counterpoint does. Canary must also. Cloud connectivity enables fleet telemetry, model updates, and cross-chain LP patterns — but the store-level detection, Fox case creation, and work dispatch all run locally. Internet outage is a sync delay, not an outage.

**Counterpoint's REST API surface (CPAPI):** The public API is the clean integration path. The Module T adapter reads the PS_DOC transaction schema directly from the local SQL Server — faster and richer than the REST API, and available offline. The REST API is used for cloud-side operations (account management, remote config, audit) where latency is acceptable.

## Field Org Framing — Agent Hierarchy Mirrors LP Reality

The Canary agent hierarchy is not an abstract technical design. It is the digital mirror of how LP organizations are actually structured in multi-store retail.

| Human LP role | Agent equivalent | Scope | Primary signal |
|--------------|-----------------|-------|---------------|
| Store LP Lead | Store ALX (edge) | Single location | Real-time transaction anomalies, known offender patterns, local Fox cases |
| Regional LP Manager | Regional ALX (Phase 2) | 5–15 stores | Cross-store patterns, organized retail crime, vendor fraud across a district |
| LP Director / VP LP | Cloud ALX | Full chain | Fleet telemetry, chain-wide shrink benchmarks, model updates, strategic LP policy |

**The insight for the retailer:** The store LP lead knows every cashier, every SKU, every shrink pattern in their store. They do not need to call the director every time a void happens. They make the call themselves. That is what the store agent does — it has the local context to act without escalation.

The director sees patterns the store lead cannot — an organized ring working three stores in a district simultaneously. That is what the cloud agent does — it aggregates the signal the store agents cannot see individually.

**The SMB version:** A 5-store specialty retailer cannot afford an LP director, a regional manager, and a store lead at each location. They have one person wearing all three hats. The agent hierarchy gives that one person the full LP organization as software — store-level real-time detection, regional pattern recognition, and chain-wide LP direction, all running without headcount.

**Why this framing matters for the VAR conversation:** The VAR has sold Counterpoint to hundreds of specialty retailers. Most of them have zero dedicated LP staff. Framing Canary as "the LP team you cannot afford to hire" lands immediately — it maps to a real pain point every Counterpoint VAR customer has, regardless of vertical.
