# ACLAD Partnership — Same-Side Public-Private Defense

The **Abalone Cove Landslide Abatement District (ACLAD)** is the public-safety counterpart to the Community of Abalone Cove's private covenant overlay. ACLAD operates ~22 dewatering wells and coordinates with the City on road-repair and geologic-monitoring work in the Abalone Cove / Portuguese Bend landslide complex. This card documents the "same-side" strategic frame, the coordination mechanism, and the Restated Declaration recital language.

**Not legal advice.** Distilled from a 2026-04-19 external-advisor memo. ACLAD coordination signal should be established before Day 0 of the reactivation blitz.

## What ACLAD Is

The Abalone Cove Landslide Abatement District is a public special district created to manage landslide hazards in the Abalone Cove / Portuguese Bend area. Its mandate:

- **Public safety** — stabilization of the active landslide complex through infrastructure
- **Slope stabilization** — operation of a network of dewatering wells that extract groundwater to reduce slide movement
- **Open-space preservation** — management of areas within the abatement footprint as geologic avoidance corridors
- **Coordination with the City** — ongoing relationship with the RPV City Council and Public Works on road repairs, emergency declarations, and permitting within the complex

ACLAD operates approximately 22 dewatering wells (some sources cite 16–22 across different points in time). The City contributes approximately $1M annually to road repairs in the complex. As of early March 2026, Abalone Cove was decelerating to ~2.14 inches/week and Portuguese Bend ~1.37 inches/week, attributed to the wells and waning winter rains.

The 2023 WPBCA → Community of Abalone Cove rename was driven in part by the formation (or formalization) of the abatement-district structure: the community wanted its corporate name to dissociate from the Portuguese Bend landslide connotation. See [[wpbca-compliance-framework]] for the rename context.

## The "Same-Side" Frame

The strategic framing:

- **ACLAD** — public funding, engineering, regulatory authority
- **Community of Abalone Cove (revived Declaration 100/101 covenants)** — recorded private equitable servitudes running with the land, not automatically preempted by state housing mandates (Nahrstedt; Citizens for Covenant Compliance)

**Together = layered defense.** Public abatement wells + private covenant enforcement = permanent protection of the documented landslide complex against density intensification that would create future public liability.

The user's framing captured it directly:

> "We need to be partnering somehow and get on the same side because they have the money to actually develop this piece of land into something everyone can use."

The 1-acre west-of-creek bench at 0 Clipper Road becomes the **demonstration project**:

- Surface expression of the fault-line shelf edge
- 30° alluvial runoff from Upper Barkentine (tide pools + fossils at ~800 ft)
- ACLAD's geologic responsibility
- CoAC's private covenant authority (post-reactivation)

Outcome: the parcel remains undeveloped or is converted to a **public-access geologic interpretive overlook** that "everyone can use" — exactly as the 1939 PVE Civic Problems booklet sought to reclaim community property for shared benefit.

## Why This Partnership Works

### 1. Structural alignment of mandates

ACLAD's mandate (public safety, slope stabilization, open-space preservation) is directly aligned with the surviving Declaration 100/101 covenants (single-family use, density caps, drainage, setbacks, Art Jury review, geologic avoidance corridors). Neither entity benefits from RM-22 density on unstable terrain; both benefit from coordinated protection.

### 2. Legal complementarity

- **Public law side** — ACLAD operates under California special district law; has authority over abatement infrastructure and some permitting within the complex; cannot directly override state housing mandates but shapes what can be built through safety constraints
- **Private law side** — recorded equitable servitudes on the chain of title; not subject to Housing Element / SB 330 / Builder's Remedy preemption absent extraordinary changed-conditions proof

One side shapes what's buildable through safety; the other shapes what's buildable through title restrictions. Together they eliminate most of the density-expansion pathway.

### 3. Cost-benefit alignment for taxpayers

- **Unchecked density** → future public liability for landslide remediation (multi-million-dollar exposure)
- **Covenant-enforced open space + ACLAD partnership** → one-time stabilization investment + permanent avoidance-corridor protection at far lower long-term cost

The partnership frames the economic case in a way that reaches beyond the immediate community to broader RPV and County stakeholders.

### 4. Credibility with the Coastal Commission

The California Coastal Commission weighs public safety, coastal access, and natural-resource protection. A coordinated public-private record — ACLAD's engineering data + CoAC's recorded covenants + the Coastal Specific Plan Subregion 4 — creates a far stronger record than either side alone.

## Coordination Mechanisms

### Option A — Informal Partnership Signal (Recommended Initial Posture)

Before Day 0 of the blitz, a CoAC Board representative (or, in the absence of CoAC quorum, a designated representative of the 15-owner network) has an off-the-record conversation with ACLAD leadership:

- "We are preparing a private covenant overlay consistent with your landslide abatement mission."
- "The Restated Declaration will include a recital acknowledging ACLAD's public role and committing to coordinate on geologic matters within the burdened land."
- "We are not asking ACLAD to endorse or co-sign. We are letting you know."

Outcome: ACLAD is not surprised on Day 0. Absent objection (which is unlikely given structural alignment), the partnership frame holds.

### Option B — Formal MOU (Post-Recording)

Within the first 30–90 days after the Restated Declaration is recorded, the Trustees (or the CoAC Board) initiate discussions with ACLAD about a formal Memorandum of Understanding:

- **Scope** — coordination on geologic, drainage, and abatement matters within the burdened land
- **Data sharing** — ACLAD monitoring data available to Trustees; ARC reviews informed by ACLAD technical input
- **No assessment commingling** — CoAC covenants do not impose ACLAD assessments; ACLAD's district funding is not redirected
- **Joint Coastal Commission posture** — coordinated submissions on projects within the Coastal Zone that implicate both geologic safety and private restrictions

An MOU is not required for the reactivation to succeed. It formalizes the partnership if and when both sides find value in doing so.

### Option C — Parallel Operations Without Formal Coordination

Even absent informal signal or formal MOU, the partnership works as long as:
- ACLAD continues its public-safety mission
- CoAC Trustees enforce the private covenants
- Both sides refrain from publicly contradicting each other

The revived covenants and ACLAD's wells operate in the same physical geography with compatible objectives. They do not require formal coordination to produce layered effect.

## Restated Declaration Recital Language

The Restated Declaration includes a dedicated recital (proposed language):

> "**WHEREAS** the Abalone Cove Landslide Abatement District (ACLAD) exists to protect public safety through landslide abatement infrastructure, dewatering-well operations, and coordination with the City of Rancho Palos Verdes on geologic matters within the Abalone Cove and Portuguese Bend landslide complex; and
>
> **WHEREAS** the surviving covenants of Declarations 100 and 101 include single-family residential use restrictions, density caps, natural drainage preservation, and setback and architectural-review requirements that are structurally aligned with ACLAD's public safety mandate;
>
> **NOW, THEREFORE**, the Trustees recognize ACLAD as a natural complement to the private covenant overlay restated herein. The Trustees and the Association shall:
>
> 1. Coordinate in good faith with ACLAD on geologic, drainage, and abatement matters affecting the burdened land;
> 2. Defer to ACLAD's technical expertise on groundwater management, slope stability, and abatement infrastructure within the complex;
> 3. Support ACLAD's coordination with the California Coastal Commission and the City of Rancho Palos Verdes on projects implicating both public safety and the private covenants;
> 4. Refrain from imposing private restrictions that would impair ACLAD's exercise of its public mandate; and
> 5. Seek to document shared interests in a formal Memorandum of Understanding at such time as both parties find it appropriate."

## The 1-Acre Bench as Demonstration Project

The 1-acre west-of-creek parcel at 0 Clipper Road (APN 7573-006-024) is the natural focal point:

- **Geologic reality** — surface expression of the fault-line shelf edge; 30° alluvial runoff from Upper Barkentine; tide pools + fossils at ~800 ft
- **Zoning reality** — RM-22 under the April 16, 2024 Housing Element vote
- **Application reality** — 16-unit pending proposal
- **ACLAD reality** — inside the abatement complex; public safety infrastructure already operating
- **Covenant reality (post-reactivation)** — inside the master Lot H boundary after negative-space exclusions; burdened by revived Declaration 100/101 covenants (single-family, drainage, setbacks, Art Jury review)

**Proposed long-term use (post-recording):**

- Preserved as permanent open space or a low-impact **public-access geologic interpretive overlook**
- Shared infrastructure between ACLAD (geologic monitoring) and CoAC (covenant-compliant public access)
- Possible co-funding: ACLAD geologic-education outreach + 501(c)(4) advocacy-arm capital campaign (see [[501c-advocacy-arm]])
- Parallels PVPLC conservation-easement work on adjacent peninsula parcels

This reframes the site from "density site the City rezoned" to "shared geologic safety valve the community protects." The narrative is naturally aligned with ACLAD's mandate and with 1939 PVE Civic Problems precedent.

## Risk: ACLAD Declines to Partner

If ACLAD leadership signals they cannot or will not coordinate — for political, legal, or institutional reasons — the reactivation still proceeds. The Restated Declaration's ACLAD recital is **non-binding on ACLAD**; it reflects the CoAC's commitment to coordinate, not ACLAD's reciprocal commitment. The partnership can be:

- Pursued later when ACLAD leadership changes or institutional reasons evolve
- Replaced with parallel-operations mode (Option C above)
- Supplemented by PVPLC conservation-easement coordination if ACLAD cannot formally engage

The partnership strengthens the defense but is not a precondition.

## Risk: ACLAD Objects Publicly

Low-probability but high-consequence. Mitigation:

- **Informal signal before Day 0** (Option A) reduces surprise and gives ACLAD time to voice concerns privately
- **Non-binding recital language** in the Restated Declaration respects ACLAD's institutional independence
- **Coordination, not directive** — the recital commits CoAC to coordinate, not to command ACLAD
- **Counsel review** — HOA counsel reviews the recital for any language that could be read as claiming authority over ACLAD

If ACLAD does object publicly, the response is factual: "The Community of Abalone Cove's revived covenants operate on the private chain of title and do not direct ACLAD. We welcome ACLAD's continued public-safety work and will coordinate where appropriate."
