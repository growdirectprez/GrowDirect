# LRPMP — Shoreline Park Parcel (APN 7573-007-900)

The Successor Agency to the Rancho Palos Verdes Redevelopment Agency Long-Range Property Management Plan (LRPMP) is a state-mandated document reviewed and approved by the California Department of Finance. Pages 23-26 cover the Abalone Cove Shoreline Park parcel at the southeast corner of Sea Cove Drive and Palos Verdes Drive South.

This parcel is part of the same Parcel 106 remainder strip as 0 Clipper Road. Same assessor book (7573). Same title history back to the 1920s. Same drainage course (the 24-inch pipe / open creek). The city's own admissions about this parcel apply to the entire strip.

## The Parcel

- **APN:** 7573-007-900
- **Location:** Southeast corner of Sea Cove Drive and Palos Verdes Drive South
- **Size:** 52,351 square feet (1.20 acres)
- **Current Use:** Open space and passive parkland, part of Abalone Cove Shoreline Park
- **Address:** None
- **Buildings:** None
- **Current Zoning:** RS-2 (residential, single family, max 2 lots per acre, minimum lot size 20,000 sq ft) with Socio-Cultural (OC-2) and Urban Design (OC-3) Overlay Control Districts and Open Space — Hazard
- **General Plan Designation:** Residential 1-2 dwelling units per acre and Hazard Area, with a socio-cultural control district

## The CC&R Admission

The city's own language (page 24, verbatim):

> "The parcel is also subject to various Covenants, Conditions, and Restrictions (CC&Rs). In addition to other restrictions on the property discussed in the 'Summary of Properties' section of this Plan, those CC&Rs could limit the type of development or use that is permissible on the parcel."

The city acknowledges CC&Rs exist on Parcel 106 land. The city told the state they exist. The city says they "could limit the type of development or use." The city does not name the specific CC&Rs. Declarations 100 and 101, sitting at the county recorder's office, were never referenced.

## Title History

> "The parcel has a long title history, dating back to the 1920s. Various easements are recorded against the parcel, including road easements, a slope easement, sewer and storm drain easements, and utility easements (water, telephone, and electric)."

The 1920s. Declaration 100 was recorded in 1929. The Filiorum deed in 1930. The title history the city references is the same decade as the Vanderlip declarations.

## Chain of Acquisition

- **Original:** Part of Lot 106 / Parcel 106, carved from Lot H of Rancho Los Palos Verdes
- **County of Los Angeles** held the property
- **November 10, 1987:** Transferred from LA County to the RDA as one of four parcels in the Abalone Cove Beach property conveyance, per Section 7 of the Horan Agreement
- **Horan lawsuit:** Filed by private property owners whose properties had been damaged by movement of the Abalone Cove Landslide. The RDA agreed to accept the property "as-is" from the County, including property restrictions agreed to by the County in the past (e.g. Ecological Reserve)
- **2011:** Park and infrastructure maintenance easement agreement between RDA and City of RPV (May 3, 2011), granting the City an easement in perpetuity for park operation
- **2014:** Successor Agency conveyed to City by grant deed (the redevelopment dissolution conveyance documented in [[Brain/wiki/cove-rpv-redevelopment-conveyance|RPV Redevelopment Conveyance]])

## The Drainage

> "Drainage facilities, which convey runoff via a 24-inch pipe running under Palos Verdes Drive South, are located on this parcel."

This is the same watercourse that runs through 0 Clipper Road. The 24-inch pipe goes under PV Drive South; on the 0 Clipper side, the creek is open. Same water. Same drainage course. The city documents it as a physical feature on Parcel 106 land.

## State Park Bond Restrictions

> "Various improvements have been made to Abalone Cove Shoreline Park, including the parking lot and restrooms, which were funded in part by grants totaling more than $200,000 (BB-19-349) under the 1986 and 1988 State Park Bond Acts. The grants include restrictions on the use and conveyance of the properties. Some of the grants require that the properties will be maintained in perpetuity in accordance with the uses specified in the grants, and others provide that the use of the properties can only be changed with the approval of the State Legislature."

Perpetuity. State Legislature approval required to change use. This is a harder lock than any CC&R.

## NCCP Reserve

> "This parcel is within the City's proposed NCCP Reserve, and uses inconsistent with the parcel's Reserve status would not be permitted under the NCCP. The Reserve conserves regionally important habitat areas and provides important habitat linkages between patches of conserved habitat."

The city itself says development inconsistent with the reserve wouldn't be permitted. This is the same Parcel 106 strip. The same habitat. The same linkage.

## Coastal Commission Jurisdiction

> "The parcel is within the jurisdiction of the California Coastal Commission. Any changes to zoning of this coastal property would require approval from the California Coastal Commission, which would be unlikely as the parcel provides coastal views to the public in an area where little public access exists."

The city told the state that Coastal Commission approval would be needed for any zoning change. And that approval would be "unlikely."

## Overlay Control Districts

- **Socio-Cultural (OC-2):** Designates the property as a socio-cultural control district "which shall preserve, protect, and maintain land and water areas and improvements that have significant historical, archaeological, or cultural importance to the public"
- **Urban Design (OC-3):** Preserves, protects, and maintains "land and water areas, views and vistas, and unique scientific and educational values"

## Why This Matters for 0 Clipper

APN 7573-007-900 and APN 7573-006-024 (0 Clipper Road) share:
- The same Parcel 106 origin
- The same assessor book (7573)
- The same title history back to the 1920s
- The same open creek / 24-inch pipe drainage course
- The same CC&Rs the city acknowledges but doesn't name

The city's own state-reviewed document admits:
1. CC&Rs exist on this Parcel 106 land and could limit development
2. The title history dates to the 1920s (the decade of Declaration 100)
3. The drainage course is a physical feature documented on the parcel
4. NCCP Reserve status would prohibit inconsistent uses
5. Coastal Commission approval would be needed for zoning changes
6. State Park Bond restrictions require perpetual maintenance as parkland

Under *Russell v. Palos Verdes Properties* (1963), equity will enforce covenants against grantees who acquired with notice. The LRPMP is the city's own written notice — to the state — that CC&Rs constrain Parcel 106 land. The city was on notice. The state was on notice. The document is part of the public record.

Then the city rezoned the adjacent Parcel 106 parcel to RM-22.
