# Foundation Bylaws

The Abalone Cove Foundation must adopt its own standalone bylaws. California Corporations Code §§ 5150–5152 and IRS Form 1023-EZ instructions require every nonprofit to have its own bylaws governing internal affairs, board structure, officer duties, meeting procedures, and conflict-of-interest rules. The Foundation cannot borrow or operate under the 2012 Bylaws (drafted under the legacy WPBCA name; now governing Community of Abalone Cove).

Distilled working position from a 2026-04-19 advisor memo. Final drafting requires separate 501(c)(3) counsel.

## Why Separate Bylaws

the 2012 Bylaws (drafted under the legacy WPBCA name; now governing Community of Abalone Cove) were adopted for a mutual-benefit corporation governing an 81-lot common-interest development. They contain Davis-Stirling-specific provisions (secret ballots, inspector of elections, assessment quorums, architectural committee rules) that do not apply to a public benefit 501(c)(3). Sharing bylaws would:

- Blur corporate separateness and expose both entities to commingling findings
- Raise IRS red flags during 1023-EZ review
- Complicate 501(c)(3) compliance (private benefit, private inurement)
- Weaken the Foundation's ability to act as a neutral convening body
- Expose the Foundation to Community of Abalone Cove governance disputes or member challenges

The Foundation's bylaws should be short (10–20 pages vs. the 2012 Bylaws' 77), mission-focused, and protective.

## Required Sections

### Name and Purpose

Mirror the charitable purpose statement from the Consolidated Design Spec:

> Preservation of the historical documentary record of Abalone Cove and the Palos Verdes Peninsula's layered governance and land-use history; public education on California common-interest-development law, coastal planning, and private governance instruments; stewardship support for scenic, ecological, and civic assets held in trust by peninsula HOAs and land trusts.

### Board of Directors

- Minimum 3 directors (recommended 5–7 for resilience)
- Qualifications, terms (staggered), election, removal, vacancies
- Duty of care (Corp. Code § 5231), duty of loyalty (§ 5233)
- Conflict-of-interest policy attached (mandatory IRS expectation)

### Officers

- President, Secretary, Treasurer (CFO) at minimum
- Duties aligned with 501(c)(3) oversight
- Dual-role prohibitions (e.g., President and Treasurer typically separate)

### Meetings

- Annual + special meetings
- Notice requirements
- Quorum rules
- Voting (simple majority unless supermajority required for major actions — bylaw amendments, major asset sales, dissolution)

### Committees

Optional standing committees:
- Research (Library of Evidence)
- Inter-HOA Convening
- Future Governance-Engine Oversight (deferred)

### Indemnification and Insurance

Standard nonprofit D&O protections. Coordinate with D&O carrier to confirm coverage of the Foundation's specific activities (research, convening, deferred governance facilitation).

### Amendment of Bylaws

Board vote + optional member ratification (Foundation is unlikely to have voting members in the traditional sense; board-adopted amendments with supermajority are typical).

### Dissolution

Assets distributed to another 501(c)(3) with similar purposes (required for tax-exempt status). Candidates: PVPLC (Palos Verdes Peninsula Land Conservancy), Palos Verdes Library District Foundation, or a similar CA historic-preservation nonprofit.

### Specific Authorizations (Tailored to the Spec)

Explicit language authorizing:

- **Publication of research** and the Library of Evidence
- **Acting as neutral convening body** for peninsula HOAs on shared matters (trails, landslide compliance, coastal policy)
- **Providing procedural templates and facilitation support** for Declaration 100 Article II §5 reactivation (without the Foundation itself exercising governance)
- **Acceptance of non-cash gifts, including cryptocurrency**, subject to a separate board-approved gift-acceptance policy — see [[foundation-crypto-donations]]
- **Supplemental cryptographic timestamping and record-signing tools** for §V §5 record-keeping, subject to inspector-of-elections oversight and Davis-Stirling compliance — see [[foundation-blockchain-proof-layer]]
- **Reasonable compensation of directors and officers** for actual services rendered to the Foundation's exempt purposes, subject to the Compensation and Conflict-of-Interest Policy — see [[foundation-three-hat-compensation]]

### Founding-Period Protective Language

> "The Board may approve reasonable compensation for services performed by a director or officer during the pre-incorporation and organizational phase, provided that (i) a summary of tasks and supporting logs are presented, (ii) the compensation is reviewed and approved by disinterested directors, and (iii) the amount is determined to be reasonable based on the nature and scope of the work performed for the Corporation's exempt purposes."

### Explicit Prohibition on Governance of Other Entities

> "The Corporation shall not exercise governance powers over Community of Abalone Cove (a California Nonprofit Mutual Benefit Corporation formerly titled West Portuguese Bend Community Association), any other homeowner association, or any other common-interest development. The Corporation's role is limited to research, documentation, neutral convening, and procedural-support services in furtherance of its charitable and educational purposes."

This reinforces the "support, not supplant" boundary.

## Conflict-of-Interest Policy (Attached Exhibit)

Required by IRS for 501(c)(3) recognition. Must include:

- Written disclosure of each director's outside roles (including the founder's LLC and Community of Abalone Cove positions)
- Recusal procedures for votes on compensation, contracts, or transactions involving an interested party
- Annual signed conflict-disclosure statements by all directors
- Documentation requirements (board minutes reflect the disclosed conflict, the recusal, and the disinterested-director decision)

## First Board Meeting Adoption Checklist

1. Adopt bylaws
2. Elect officers
3. Adopt Conflict-of-Interest Policy
4. Adopt Compensation Policy (see [[foundation-three-hat-compensation]])
5. Adopt Time Allocation Methodology (see [[foundation-time-logging-policy]])
6. Adopt Gift Acceptance Policy (see [[foundation-crypto-donations]])
7. Authorize bank account opening
8. Authorize Form 1023-EZ filing
9. Ratify pre-formation Q1 work (if compensation is to be paid for it)
10. Record all in official minutes
