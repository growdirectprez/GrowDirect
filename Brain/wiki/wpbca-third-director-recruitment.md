# Community of Abalone Cove (formerly WPBCA) — Third-Director Recruitment

The 2012 Amended & Restated Bylaws (drafted when the entity was still named West Portuguese Bend Community Association / WPBCA; now governing the renamed Community of Abalone Cove) provide for **5 director seats**. Currently **only 2 are filled** — the founder's wife is one. Without a third director, the board cannot achieve quorum for most meaningful action. The 2-of-5 situation is a direct consequence of the board turnover that followed the name-change filing: the previous board's last action was the CA SOS rename; then the board rolled over and most seats emptied. This card documents the blocker, the path to fix it, and why every other Community of Abalone Cove track is gated on this.

Distilled working position from a 2026-04-19 advisor context and Cove/CLAUDE.md. Not legal advice.

## Why This Is a Hard Blocker

Per the 2012 Bylaws (and general California HOA governance):

- **Section 6.6** — Quorum for general business: 1/3 of members; for assessments: 1/2
- **Section 8.2** — 5 directors; must reside in tract 35%+ of year
- A 2-person board lacks quorum for binding action on nearly everything (adopting policies, authorizing contracts, setting annual budget meaningfully)

What the 2-person board cannot do until a third director is seated:

- Adopt the WPBCA Payment Policy — see [[wpbca-payments-platform]]
- Adopt the WPBCA Investment Policy Statement — see [[wpbca-investment-policy]]
- Authorize a cooperation resolution with the Foundation
- Approve the HOA-specific payment-platform contract
- Take binding positions on inter-HOA convenings
- Set annual assessments
- Enforce CC&Rs through any action that requires a board vote
- Approve a new insurance carrier, new bank, new registered agent

The board can do some things as individual directors acting in good faith — informal coordination, member outreach, information gathering — but nothing that requires a vote.

## Path to Quorum

### Option 1 — Fill from Current Membership

- Identify residents in the 81-lot community who would serve
- Target: residents who understand the current dysfunction and are willing to put in modest time
- Ideal profile: respected, neutral, with bandwidth
- Approach: direct conversation first (not mass email), explain the situation and the minimal time commitment

### Option 2 — Appoint Interim per Bylaws

If the Bylaws provide for appointment of an interim director to fill a vacancy until the next annual meeting, the 2 current directors may have authority to appoint 1 more (this makes quorum) subject to later election. Check the exact bylaw language before relying on this. Section 8.12–8.14 likely relevant.

### Option 3 — Call a Special Meeting + Election

If appointment is not an option, calling a special meeting and running an abbreviated election is the route. Challenges: member engagement in an under-communicating HOA, proper notice requirements.

### Option 4 — Coordinate with the Broader Community

The Foundation (once formed) can support member outreach in a neutral educational way — explaining the legal framework, the quorum problem, and the ask — without itself exercising governance. See [[foundation-legal-framework]].

## Member-Outreach Approach

- **One-to-one first** — personal conversation with 3–5 potential candidates before any broad messaging
- **Clear ask** — "the board needs a third director to legitimately govern. The time commitment is modest. Here is what's at stake."
- **Avoid recruitment via the sensitive issues** — do not frame the ask around the ocean path easement or Parcel 106 or 0 Clipper. Those are separate private governance matters.
- **Focus on enabling action** — once we have quorum, we can adopt policies, fix the payment platform, communicate with members, etc. Stable governance, not a political agenda.
- **Respect what the existing 2 directors have been doing** — they have been working under constraints; the third director isn't replacing them, it's unlocking them

## Candidate Qualification

From the 2012 Bylaws:

- Must be a member of the Association (deed to a lot in Tract 14649)
- Must reside in the tract 35%+ of the year
- No felony / disqualification restrictions
- Willing to be named publicly as a director

## Documentation After Seating

Once a third director is seated:

- Update Statement of Information with CA Secretary of State (ballpark annually or when officers/directors change)
- Update Registered Agent info if affected
- Update banking and insurance records (directors on file)
- Update any platform / vendor records (HOA software, email, etc.)
- Reconvene regular board meetings on a scheduled cadence
- Begin adopting the policies that have been waiting on quorum — see [[wpbca-compliance-framework]]

## Ocean Path Easement Note

The third director should be someone who can be trusted with the sensitive governance matters, including the 1952 ocean access easements. Recruitment conversations should not lead with that topic. Board conversations after seating should treat it as private governance — see [[wpbca-ocean-path-easement]].

## Connection to the Foundation

The Foundation is a parallel track and does not depend on WPBCA's third director. The Foundation can be formed, adopt bylaws, and begin operations with its own 3 directors (distinct from WPBCA's board). When the Foundation's first board meeting occurs, the founder presents pre-formation work logs for ratification (see [[foundation-three-hat-compensation]]). WPBCA's recovery and Foundation's launch run on independent timelines, deliberately.

## Open Decisions

- Target candidates (list of 3–5 residents to approach, in order)
- Timing of the asks — coordinate with current 2 directors
- Whether to pursue appointment (Option 2) vs. election (Option 3)
- How to communicate the recruitment to the wider 81-lot membership (no big email blast until a candidate is ready to say yes)
- Whether to engage HOA counsel on the exact bylaw procedure before taking any recruitment action
