# Article V Equity / Vote-Share Modernization

The original Declaration 100 Article V ties voting power and enforcement authority to **proportional equity / acreage** under the pre-subdivision PV Corp master holding. In 2026, with only a subset of parcels reactivating and negative-space exclusions having fragmented the original acreage, the acreage-weighted model is impractical and unworkable. The Restated Declaration must **modernize Article V intent** by shifting to a practical per-parcel (or equal-share) voting model while preserving the original protective purpose.

**Not legal advice.** Distilled from a 2026-04-19 external-advisor memo. HOA counsel review required before the Restated Declaration is recorded.

## The Original Article V

Declaration 100 Article V established:

- **Voting weighted by acreage** — one vote per one-third acre (or similar) under the unified PV Corp master holding
- **Duration** — in force until January 1, 1965, then automatically renewing in 20-year periods (currently in force through at least January 1, 2045)
- **Amendment threshold** — 80% by area + 70% of all owners + Association approval + Corporation (or successor) consent for basic restrictions; 2/3 by area within affected property + 2/3 by area within 300 feet for local restriction changes
- **Reversion of title** — breach causes property to revert to Corporation (or successor holding reversionary rights), with right of immediate re-entry
- **Enforcement** — Corporation and Association right to summarily abate violations at owner's expense
- **Annexation** — adjacent property owners can voluntarily subject their land to Declaration 100 by agreement with Corporation and Association

The acreage-weighted voting reflected the Vanderlip drafting context in 1929: a unified master holding where proportional equity made sense. The drafters could not have anticipated that by 2026:

- PV Corp would be dissolved
- Filiorum would be dissolved
- The master holding would be fragmented across dozens of tracts
- Negative-space exclusions would carve out most of the original acreage into separately-governed subdivisions (PVE, Tract 14649, etc.)
- State housing mandates (SB 330, Housing Element, Builder's Remedy) would create density pressure the 1929 framework could not have imagined

## The Problem for a 15-Owner Reactivation

The Article II §5 reactivation uses a different threshold — 15 owners of 15 separate parcels — as the trigger. But once the three Trustees are elected and begin exercising Article II §4 powers, the governance structure must function on some voting basis for future decisions:

- Amendments to the Restated Declaration
- Further assessments
- ARC decisions
- Enforcement votes
- Expansion or contraction of the burdened land

Using the original acreage-weighted Article V for ongoing operations creates problems:

- **Impossible math** — negative-space exclusions mean the original acreage figures no longer map to current ownership
- **Administrative paralysis** — every vote would require reconstructing the 1929 acreage shares across 95 years of subdivisions
- **Inequity between small and large parcels** — a 10-acre owner would have 30× the vote of a 1/3-acre owner, which has no modern justification in a residential governance context
- **Vulnerability to challenge** — opponents could argue the acreage figures are unprovable, making any Trustee decision attackable

## The Modernization

The Restated Declaration adopts **one parcel, one vote** (or equivalent equal-share model) for all future Association/Trustee actions among participating reactivated owners. The original protective purpose of Article V — ensuring fair governance among burdened parcels — is preserved. The acreage-weighted mechanic is retired as obsolete.

This does **not** alter:
- Underlying Declaration 100/101 covenants (single-family use, density caps, drainage, setbacks, Art Jury review)
- The reversionary interest
- The Association's enforcement rights
- The duration of the Declaration (through at least January 1, 2045 per automatic renewal)
- The negative-space boundary

It **only** modernizes the voting/equity mechanic to make governance workable.

## Proposed Recital Language

The Restated Declaration includes a dedicated recital and operative section:

> "**WHEREAS** the original Article V equity and vote-share provisions of Declaration 100 were intended to ensure fair, proportional governance among all burdened parcels under the Vanderlip master plan; and
>
> **WHEREAS** only a subset of parcels is now reactivating via the self-executing Article II Section 5 mechanism, and the negative-space exclusions recorded over the past 95 years have fragmented the original acreage holdings such that acreage-weighted voting is no longer administratively workable; and
>
> **WHEREAS** Courts have consistently upheld reasonable modernizations in restated declarations that carry forward the original protective intent (see Nahrstedt v. Lakeside Village (1994) 8 Cal.4th 361; Citizens for Covenant Compliance v. Anderson (1995) 12 Cal.4th 345);
>
> **NOW, THEREFORE, the Trustees hereby modernize the intent of Article V** by adopting one vote per parcel (or, where multiple parcels are held in common ownership, one vote per separately-assessed parcel) for all future Association and Trustee actions under this Restated Declaration. This provision preserves the protective purpose of the original Article V without altering the underlying covenants, the reversionary interest, or the enforcement authority vested in the Association and its Trustees under Article II Section 4 of the original Declaration 100."

## Why This Is Defensible

### 1. Consistent with original protective intent

The purpose of Article V was to ensure fair governance among burdened parcels. One-parcel/one-vote achieves the same purpose with 2026 demographics and subdivisions. It does not weaken protection; if anything, it strengthens it by making enforcement votes easier to achieve.

### 2. Nahrstedt / Citizens for Covenant Compliance precedent

California courts uphold reasonable modernizations in restated declarations that preserve original intent. The 2009 WPBCA Restated Declaration is a direct peninsula precedent for this approach.

### 3. Does not alter substantive covenants

The modernization is purely procedural/governance. Single-family use, density caps, drainage, setbacks, Art Jury review — all unchanged. No changed-conditions defense is available because no substantive restriction is being altered.

### 4. Does not alter the reversionary interest

The reversion of title to the Corporation (or successor) for breach remains intact. The Trustees do not claim or alter the reversionary interest; they simply govern ongoing Association actions.

### 5. Parallels public-entity modernization

Municipal charters, state constitutions, and even federal voting structures have been modernized from original 18th/19th-century proportional schemes to per-capita or per-entity models. The private governance layer follows the same modernization logic.

## What This Does NOT Do

The modernization does **not**:

- Expand the burdened land (the boundary stays as defined by the negative-space master minus exclusions — see [[coac-negative-space-lot-h-boundary]])
- Revive void racial covenants (those are struck expressly per Shelley v. Kraemer and the 2009 restatement precedent)
- Eliminate the Article V Section 2(a) 80%/70% amendment threshold for amending the Restated Declaration's basic restrictions (that threshold still applies to future substantive changes, just not to this procedural modernization which is authorized by the Trustees under Article II §4)
- Create new restrictions beyond those surviving in Declarations 100/101
- Assess new members outside the burdened land

## Edge Cases to Resolve

### Multiple parcels in common ownership

The recital provides: "one vote per separately-assessed parcel." A single owner with three separately-assessed APNs inside the burdened land has three votes. This mirrors the structure many modern HOAs use and is defensible.

### Partial interests / tenants in common

The Restated Declaration should specify: a single vote per parcel, cast by the owner(s) as they agree; if owners disagree, the vote is not counted. (Standard CC&R practice.)

### Subdivided parcels post-recording

If a parcel inside the burdened land is later subdivided (e.g., under SB 9 or by private lot-split), the Restated Declaration should specify whether each new parcel acquires a vote. Default recommended: yes, one vote per separately-assessed resulting parcel, provided the subdivision complies with the surviving covenants.

### New annexations

Article V §5 of Declaration 100 allows voluntary annexation of adjacent land. If post-recording annexations occur, each annexed parcel acquires one vote on the same basis.

## Interaction with the 15-Owner Trigger

The Article II §5 15-owner trigger is **separate from** the ongoing Article V governance. The trigger was used to reactivate the Association. Once the Trustees are in place, all future votes operate under the modernized one-parcel/one-vote rule — including among owners who were not part of the original 15.

Additional owners inside the burdened land (or who voluntarily annex under Article V §5) become participating members of the Association and vote on the modernized basis going forward.
