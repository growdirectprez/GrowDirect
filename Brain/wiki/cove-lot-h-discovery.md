# Lot H Discovery

> **CORRECTION (April 10, 2026):** Prior research overstated the scope of the Lot H Declaration. The 1950 Lot H Declaration contains **only racial exclusionary language** — no architectural restrictions, no building type limits, no ARC authority. The architectural controls (single-family only, minimum square footage, ARC review, setbacks) come from the **1949 Declaration No. One**, which applies to Tract 14649 (the 81 WPBCA lots), NOT to the broader Lot H parcel. The confusion arose because the 2009 Restated Declaration combined four founding documents into one, stripping the racial language and keeping the architectural rules — making it appear that all protections traced to a single source. This correction is material to all theories about using Lot H to block development at 0 Clipper. See [[Brain/wiki/cove-legal-framework|Cove Legal Framework]] for the document-by-document breakdown.

## What is the Lot "H" Declaration?

Recorded January 25, 1950 (Book 32160, Page 26, LA County Recorder) by Palos Verdes Corporation. Covers everything PV Corp owned in the Portuguese Bend area that was NOT already part of a named tract.

**Critical limitation:** The Lot H Declaration's only substantive restriction was a racial occupancy covenant (now void under California Civil Code § 12955). It does NOT contain building type restrictions, architectural controls, ARC authority, or development standards. Those restrictions exist only in Declaration No. One (1949), which covers Tract 14649 specifically, and from which the Lot H parcel is a separate instrument.

## The Document Confusion

The four founding instruments and their actual scope:

| Document | Date | Scope | Contains |
|----------|------|-------|----------|
| Declaration No. One | April 29, 1949 | Tract 14649 (81 lots) | ALL architectural controls: single-family only, 1,250 sq ft min, ARC authority, setbacks, no mining/drilling |
| Declaration of Easements | May 5, 1949 | Tract 14649 | Utility easements, bridle trail, drainage corridors |
| Lot H Declaration | January 25, 1950 | Lot H (broader parcel, excepts named tracts) | **Only racial covenant** (now void) |
| Declaration No. One-A | October 30, 1950 | Lots 1-5 of Tract 14649 (oceanfront) | Additional bluff setbacks (40 ft), vegetation limits |

The **2009 Restated Declaration** merged Declarations No. One, One-A, and Easements into a single document, removed the racial language, and kept the architectural rules. This made it look like one unified set of restrictions when in fact the building controls only ever applied to Tract 14649 — not to the broader Lot H parcel.

## What This Means for 0 Clipper

APN 7573-006-024 (0 Clipper Road) is NOT one of the 81 lots in Tract 14649. It does not appear in the 2009 Restated Declaration's APN list. It is not in any of the 11 excepted tracts. The 1985 assessor map shows it as a blank, unsubdivided parcel with the handwritten note: "250 ft no plumb add on other restriction a variance."

The parcel falls within the Lot H boundary — but the Lot H Declaration's only restriction (racial occupancy) is void. **The architectural controls from Declaration No. One do not apply to this parcel.**

This significantly weakens the CC&R-based defense against development. The remaining arguments are:
1. The assessor's "other restriction a variance" notation — what restriction was the assessor referencing?
2. Whether PV Corp recorded any other instruments covering this parcel
3. The geological/environmental/coastal arguments (which stand independently)
4. Whether the 2009 Restated Declaration's scope was intentionally expanded beyond Tract 14649

## Duration and Renewal Status

- Original term: January 25, 1950 to January 1, 1975
- Auto-renews for successive 10-year periods
- Currently in Renewal 6: January 1, 2025 through January 1, 2035
- Termination requires written agreement from owners of more than half the covered area
- No termination agreement has ever been recorded

## Why Nobody Caught It

Three factors:

1. **RPV didn't exist.** The city incorporated in 1973 — 24 years after the declarations were recorded. The planning department has no institutional memory of the PV Corp declaration scheme.

2. **Title companies missed it.** They see a wall of similar-looking PV Corp documents from 1949-1950 and assume they're duplicates. The Lot "H" Declaration looks routine but its geographic scope is massive.

3. **PV Corp is probably voided.** Stock sold to Great Lakes Carbon Corp in 1953 for ~$9M. Corporate chain: Great Lakes → American Industrial Partners (2001) → Rain Industries (2007). PV Corp itself likely voided in Delaware for non-payment of franchise taxes. If voided, no entity can easily release the restrictions — making them effectively permanent.

## Enforcement Strategy

Three immediate steps (total cost under $500):

1. **Title search on APN 7573-006-024** — confirm Lot H coverage
2. **Delaware search on PV Corp** — confirm corporate status ($10-20)
3. **Board resolution** — authorize investigation and enforcement

Full legal defense estimated at $50,000-$150,000 through trial. Split across 81 lots: $600-$1,800 per household. Attorney's fees may be recoverable under the CC&Rs' enforcement provisions.

Recommended firms: Tinnelly Law Group (CC&R enforcement, FindHOALaw.com), Kulik Gottesman Siegel & Ware (PV-area real estate), Richardson Ober DeNichilo (HOA litigation, Torrance).

## Peninsula-Wide Implications

The Lot H boundary encompasses potentially hundreds of parcels. If enforced at 0 Clipper, the precedent applies everywhere PV Corp land was not otherwise restricted. This could be a defense template for thousands of California HOAs with pre-1970 CC&Rs facing Housing Element densification.
