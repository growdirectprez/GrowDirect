# The CIO Leave-Behind Wedge

## The reframe

Consulting leaves behind a deck, a roadmap, and a change-management
handoff. We leave behind a running plugin, a running SaaS instance, and
a CIO. The instance perpetuates the method. The CIO perpetuates the
judgment. That is why this offer is CIO-priced, not engagement-priced.

I am offering myself as the leave-behind.

## The three moves

**1. Methodology — certified, 2003.**

IBM Retail Consulting certification. Two portfolio deliverables exercise
it at real-client scale: Clarks Retail Diagnostic (2006) and Morrisons
IT Architecture (2006). The structural frames from those engagements are
preserved in two companion wiki articles and carried forward as the spine
of the consulting plugin. This is not nostalgia. It is the certified
method under the portfolio evidence that the method works.

**2. Productized — 2026.**

`consulting:retail-diagnostic` and `consulting:it-architecture-options`
ship as a Claude plugin that carries the certified method. Any merchant
can plug their evidence in — financials, POS, systems inventory,
workshop notes — and the plugin enforces the seven-section diagnostic
frame or the eight-section architecture frame that Big 4 Partners still
use, at agent speed, with evidence traceability on every slide. The
methodology does not change. The delivery economics do.

**3. Proven — on ourselves.**

The first client was us. We ran the retail diagnostic on GrowDirect's
own operation, built Canary against the findings, operated Canary
through to near-beta, and the artifact of that loop is a repeatable
model. We can point to a running SaaS that exists because the method
worked on its author. No Big 4 firm has a comparable proof.

## The offer

Engagement + plugin + SaaS instance + me as the embedded CIO.

The client gets the diagnostic done. They get the plugin that runs the
method on their data going forward, not just for the engagement. They
get a tenant SaaS instance configured to their operation — the Canary
pattern applied to their vertical, not a generic dashboard. And they
get me in the room for a defined term as the CIO / Chief AI Officer who
teaches the plugin, defends the method, and runs the tenant.

Big 4 sells the promise of agentic advisory. We ship the artifact, the
platform, and the operator — one invoice.

## The Big 4 wedge

Deloitte has Zora. PwC has ChatPwC. EY has EY.ai. KPMG has KymChat.
Accenture has AI Refinery. Every one of them is pitching the same idea
to the same client base in 2026: AI-augmented delivery, human-in-the-loop
advisory, agentic consulting. They are selling the concept because the
concept is real.

Three things they do not have that I do:

- **Certified method with portfolio evidence.** IBM 2003; Clarks +
  Morrisons 2006. Portable. Published. Not a brochure.
- **A working plugin.** The method already runs. Not roadmapped. Not
  previewed. Installable.
- **An operating proof case.** GrowDirect / Canary. First client was us.

A Big 4 Partner's best day is a demo. My worst day is a live tenant.

## Why this is honest

Three checks:

**Does the method actually work?** Clarks + Morrisons are the receipts.
Structural rigor, evidence traceability, £ low/high prize quantification,
side-by-side option evaluation — those were the 2006 deliverables and
they are the 2026 plugin outputs. Same shape.

**Can one operator really carry it?** Look at GrowDirect. One founder.
Canary near-beta, Cove + Angel in active dev, Seacove and Secure
running alongside. The platform is the force multiplier; the operator
is the judgment layer; the client gets both.

**What happens when I leave?** The plugin stays. The SaaS tenant stays.
The methodology wikis stay. A new CIO can inherit. The leave-behind is
the leave-behind, not a person you can't replace.
