Diagnostic Execution Primer

v1 · 2026-05-04 · operational scaffold

A diagnostic engagement examines a business in detail — its customers, operations, systems, people, and decisions — and produces a clear, organized picture of how the business actually runs today. The transformation team picks up that picture and uses it to design and deliver the changes the business needs.

What it produces

System map, stakeholder map, KPI baseline, customer base, operating processes, SOPs, compliance posture, cultural realityschema-fragments, one per facet
Synthesized narrativesper-facet markdown in engagement repo
Decision logappend-only JSON
Audit trailappend-only NDJSON
Handoff packagefolder + bus snapshot, signed

Chain of command

Five layers. Authority flows down, escalations flow up. Each layer's authority is bounded; the imprint header spec enforces it.

Every artifact carries a typed envelope. Validated at write. Rejection on schema violation.

Phase structure

Phase 1 establishes who the business serves and how it positions. Phase 2 establishes how the business operates. Phase 3 is the transformation team's; the diagnostic ends at handoff.

Run-loop per imprint dispatch

  1. Imprint loads with declared inputs.
  2. Capsule pulls inputs from engagement record.
  3. Agent issues bus queries (vector recall) for additional context within scope.
  4. Agent calls MCP tools per declared authority.
  5. Agent emits finding plus audit-events.
  6. Capsule indexes finding into bus.
  7. Issue tracker marks the dispatch closed.
  8. Synthesizer imprint dispatches when its inputs (the findings) are present.
  9. Synthesizer writes schema-fragment.
  10. Wiki card for the facet updates from the fragment.
  11. Open-question issues attached to the facet close or remain open.

VSM overlay

One process-mapper dispatch per value stream. Typical streams: order-to-cash, procure-to-pay, hire-to-retire, idea-to-product, customer-to-renewal, return-to-restock, complaint-to-resolution.

Per-step taxonomy

Method overlay — Merchandise fit test

Per assortment cluster, score the fit against the core customer. Surface gaps (under-served jobs) and stretches (clusters that don't belong). The fit-test agent reads the customer fragment and the assortment, emits a per-cluster score.

Method overlay — TIME quadrant scoring

Each application is placed on McFarlan's strategic grid — Strategic / Turnaround / Factory / Support — driving the disposition (invest, modernize, maintain, sunset). The quadrant agent reads the app portfolio and the strategic capability needs, emits a quadrant + disposition per app.

Break ARR by acquisition cohort. Plot retention curves. Surface which cohorts drive net retention and which drag. The cohort agent reads subscription history and acquisition metadata, emits cohort retention + NRR + drag attribution.

Value Case

v1 · 2026-05-04 · draft

We run three structured engagements that produce a complete, decision-ready picture of your business: a positioning engagement, an information-systems planning engagement, and a SaaS engagement. Each can run on its own. Run together, they produce the transformation summary — the integrated business, technology, and operational view leadership needs to set direction and act.

What each engagement produces

EngagementWhat you walk away with
Strategic positioningCore customer, brand promise, competitive position, channel and merchandising recommendations, activation roadmap.
Information systems planningBusiness and technology alignment, current and target architecture, prioritized project portfolio, roadmap with investment plan.
SaaSCommercial reality (customers, recurring revenue, churn), product and technology reality (architecture, codebase, technical debt, integrations, security), and operational reality (team, go-to-market, customer success, finance, compliance).

What all three produce together — the transformation summary

Run together, the three engagements produce one integrated picture: who you serve, what you stand for, where you win; the technology and capabilities that support that strategy; and the commercial, product, and operational reality the strategy must work within. Leadership leaves with a single decision-ready record covering business direction, technology plan, and execution reality — sequenced, costed, and traceable to evidence.

The leave-behind — your decision brain

When the engagement ends, you keep more than a deck. You keep a working knowledge organ — your structured business record, organized by topic, queryable in plain language, kept current as your business changes. Every claim in the record carries its source. Every decision carries its rationale and the people who signed it. Every change you decide to make pushes back through the same connectors that captured the original picture.

The brain stays useful long after the engagement closes. It is the substrate your team operates against — for the next planning cycle, the next board pack, the next investment decision, the next acquisition diligence — without rebuilding the foundation each time.

What it does for you

  • Better informed. Every choice on the table has evidence behind it, with the source one click away.
  • Faster to decision. The structure removes the back-and-forth of gathering, reformatting, and reconciling.
  • Faster to execution. The future-state design pushes back through the same connections that captured today's reality. No rebuilding integrations to act.
  • Faster to market. Positioning, channel mix, and activation are already organized when you need to move.
  • Resilient to direction change. The brain holds the structured truth no matter which path you choose. A pivot does not mean starting over.

What we ask of you

  • Access to your systems under scoped, audited credentials.
  • Time on calendar with the people who actually know the business — owners, executives, key operators.
  • Decisions when they reach you. We escalate; you sign.
  • Trust to let the work run in the open. Every action is logged and visible while it happens.

Per-topic structured recordsCustomer base, market position, system map, KPIs, processes, SOPs, compliance posture, and more — each in a defined shape

The bottom line

Standard consulting leaves you with a deck and a relationship that ends. This work leaves you with a decision substrate that compounds. Whatever you decide to do next — invest, pivot, acquire, divest, scale, restructure — you start from a structured picture of your business, not from scratch.
