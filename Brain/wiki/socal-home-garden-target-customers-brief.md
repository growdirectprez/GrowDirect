## Where Canary fits — three-actor structure

Per the engagement-context wiki, the engagement model is:

```
End retailer  ←→  Rapid POS / Bart's whitelabel sub-VAR  ←→  NCR Voyix
                              ↑
                  GrowDirect subcontracts here
                  (Canary spine integration + delivery methodology)
```

GrowDirect is **NOT** a peer of Rapid POS; we're a specialist subcontractor. The retailer doesn't see us directly — they buy a bundle from Rapid POS that includes our integration work. Rapid POS owns the customer relationship.

**Implication for the Bart conversation:** the proposal isn't "let us sell Canary to your customer." It's "let us deliver the spine integration scope as part of YOUR bundle to YOUR customer; you resell, you keep the customer relationship, you take the margin you choose."

## Where Canary fits — competing vs. complementing

### vs. Rapid POS — COMPLEMENT (this is the partnership shape)

Rapid POS sells Counterpoint + their customizations + training + support. They DON'T sell back-office analytics + LP + multi-store merchandising as a deep capability. Canary fills that gap inside their bundle.

Specifically:
- Canary doesn't replace Rapid POS's ecommerce connector, vendor integrations, or ERP bridges — those stay theirs
- Canary doesn't replace their customer relationship — that stays theirs
- Canary doesn't replace their POS deployment / training / support work — that stays theirs
- Canary ADDS analytics + LP + module surfaces (T R N S F derived from Counterpoint substrate; Q rule catalog; native module options for L W payment-rails) that Rapid POS would have to build themselves otherwise — and they don't

For Bart specifically (whitelabel sub-VAR): Canary is a differentiator inside HIS offering vs. competing whitelabel resellers selling plain Rapid POS. He gets a sharper pitch + larger contract size + stickier customers.

### vs. NCR Voyix — COMPETE in the analytics/LP layer; ALIGN with the unified-commerce thesis

This is the nuanced position:

**Compete:** Voyix has its own LP/analytics surface (heritage from Sysrepublic / Secure-era acquisitions; current Voyix Retail offerings include analytics dashboards). Canary plays in that layer. The pain-points wiki shows Voyix's analytics support is weak; that's the gap Canary fills. But Voyix would prefer customers buy their analytics, not ours.

**Align:** Voyix's strategic pillar is "unified commerce" — bring POS + ecommerce + payments + back-office onto one platform. **Canary IS the customer-side implementation of unified commerce for Counterpoint retailers.** That positioning makes Canary feel like part of Voyix's strategic thesis, not a competitor against it. Voyix can't easily oppose customers buying integration tools that align with their own platform direction.

**The right phrasing for Bart (and any NCR-facing communication):**
> "Canary delivers the customer-side integration spine that the unified-commerce thesis requires. We're not a parallel platform; we're how a Counterpoint retailer actually USES the platform's full surface."

This matches the canonical positioning ("multi-store merchandising and store ops for SMB on top of the Counterpoint API backbone") and gives Voyix-aware audiences (Bart, his contacts at Voyix, future direct Voyix conversations) a frame that's hard to reject.

### vs. workforce / store-ops vendors (Homebase, Deputy, When I Work, Beekeeper, YOOBIC)

This is where the **full SMB offering** decision comes in (next section). Today these are external vendors customers might add separately. Tomorrow, Canary's native modules (option d / e per memory `project_canary_native_labor_module_opportunity.md`) could displace them inside Canary's footprint.

## Edge integration — what's the architecture?

NCR Voyix has TWO platform layers worth distinguishing:

| Layer | What it is | Canary integration |
|---|---|---|
| **NCR Counterpoint** (legacy specialty-retail product) | Windows on-prem POS. Data lives in customer's SQL Server + TLD. REST API server = the "edge" component, runs on customer Windows host (port 52000 default). | Canary connects to **customer's on-prem Counterpoint API server** via HTTPS REST. Customer authority for access. Per `Brain/wiki/ncr-counterpoint-connection-runbook.md`. |
| **NCR Voyix Edge** (newer cloud-edge platform) | Voyix Commerce Platform — cloud-native API platform + edge software infrastructure. Virtualized apps + containerized microservices managed from one cloud control plane. Hardware-agnostic. | Different integration shape — likely cloud-API-first rather than per-customer on-prem. Counterpoint customers may eventually migrate to Voyix Commerce Platform; if so, Canary's adapter pattern shifts to cloud-API consumption rather than on-prem polling. |

**For Bart's customer base today (Counterpoint via Rapid POS whitelabel):** the edge IS the customer's on-prem Counterpoint installation. Canary integrates over the public REST API. **No NCR Voyix Edge involvement needed.**

**For the future (Voyix Commerce Platform migration):** customers moving from Counterpoint to Voyix Commerce Platform will have a different integration surface. Canary will need to support both. This is a **2027+ concern**, not Phase 1-5 of the current build plan.

**The Voyix Commerce Platform — January 2026 announcement:** Voyix unveiled an AI-accelerated suite of applications on the Voyix Commerce Platform (per the BusinessWire press release earlier this year). This signals their direction. Worth tracking the platform's roadmap — Canary's integration may need to follow customers as they migrate.

## Are we building a full SMB offering — store ops + asset management + VSM module?

**Strategic answer: YES, that's the long arc. But sequenced.**

|---|---|---|
| T (Transactions) | Direct via Document family | Phase 1 |
| C (Customer) | Direct via Customer family | Phase 1 |

**Phases 0-5 ship: 11 of 13 spine modules.** L (Labor) and W (Work Execution) are absent from Counterpoint REST.

### VSM module — what is it?

User mentioned "VSM module" as part of the full SMB offering. Two possible interpretations:

1. **Visiting Senior Manager** (from memory `project_alx_alxjr_naming.md`) — future ALX persona that interacts externally as a human-form (Alex / Alejandro). This is an AGENT capability, not a retail-domain module. Future feature, not yet active.

2. **Virtual Store Manager / Value Stream Manager** (retail-domain) — a Canary-native module that acts as a store manager's right-hand AI: daily ops dashboard, KPI alerts, anomaly callouts, suggested actions, agent-driven workflows. Sits ON TOP of CRDM events; consumes Modules T R N S F Q L W collectively. Existing wiki at `Brain/wiki/canary-vsm-diagnostic-mode-requirement.md` suggests this is a real Canary concept.

**Most likely user-meant interpretation (retail-domain):** Virtual Store Manager. This is Canary's "executive view" — a synthesizing agent that gives the store GM what they actually want from data: not dashboards but ANSWERS. "What needs my attention today?" "Why is this store's margin softer than the others?" "Which employee should I shadow this week?"

**Where VSM fits in the offering:**
- Phase 1-5 builds the substrate (CRDM populated from Counterpoint)
- Phase 4c builds Module Q (rule-driven detections)
- Phases 6+ build native modules (L, W, payment rails)
- VSM is the **synthesizing layer** that makes all of the above usable for a non-technical GM

VSM is essentially the agent-facing surface over everything below. It's natural to position it as a Phase 7 deliverable (after L and W are built) but it could be Phase 5.5 (alongside cutover) for the H&G chain if their GM-experience is the differentiator they're buying.

**Don't promise the full offering on Monday.** Promise the spine + Q + the methodology behind delivering it. The native-module roadmap is a "Phase 2 conversation" with Bart — once trust is established, then the wedge expansion is a co-investment discussion.
