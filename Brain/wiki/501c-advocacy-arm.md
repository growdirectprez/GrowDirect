# 501(c)(4) Advocacy Arm — New Entity Decision (Parking)

The 2026-04-19 reactivation-playbook advisor memo recommends a **new 501(c)(4) social welfare organization** — distinct from the existing Abalone Cove Foundation 501(c)(3) — to carry the advocacy/viral arm of the reactivation strategy. This card parks that decision, summarizes the rationale, and outlines the next steps for separate counsel engagement.

This is a **parking card** — the decision is not made. Content here is design-space, not adoption.

**Not legal advice.** Distilled from a 2026-04-19 external-advisor memo. Formal decision requires separate 501(c)(3) / 501(c)(4) counsel review.

## Why a 501(c)(4) Is Being Considered

The existing **Abalone Cove Foundation** (California 501(c)(3) Nonprofit Public Benefit Corporation, forming) is structured for charitable/educational activities: research, Library of Evidence, editorial sites (cove.org, abalonecove.org), inter-HOA research/convening partnership, neutral procedural support for §V §5 processes. Its lobbying capacity is constrained by IRC § 501(c)(3) limits and Rev. Rul. 2004-6 / Form 990 Schedule C reporting.

The reactivation playbook requires activities that push against those limits:

- **Viral 2026 Civic Problems campaign** — public-facing advocacy against specific rezoning and Builder's Remedy applications (see [[coac-2026-civic-problems-memorandum]])
- **"Same-side" ACLAD partnership messaging** — advocacy framing, not pure education (see [[coac-aclad-partnership]])
- **Opposition to specific YIMBY-aligned pressure** — potentially crossing into direct advocacy on local housing decisions (see [[coac-yimby-opposition-landscape]])
- **Coordinated press outreach** — framing specific sites and specific City actions

A **501(c)(4) social welfare organization** has substantially more latitude for:

- Substantial lobbying (federal and state)
- Advocacy on specific local housing and land-use decisions
- Limited political campaign activity (subject to IRC rules)
- Direct issue campaigns (like the 2026 Civic Problems memorandum)

Contributions to a 501(c)(4) are **generally not tax-deductible** to donors, but the entity itself is exempt from federal income tax (and California corporate tax if properly qualified).

## What a Companion 501(c)(3) Could Do

If tax-deductible donor funding is critical for specific activities — e.g., geologic-education materials, interpretive-overlook capital, archival scanning — a **companion 501(c)(3) public charity** could be formed separately, distinct from both the Abalone Cove Foundation (which is focused on the Foundation's own charitable mission) and from the 501(c)(4) advocacy arm.

Proposed name: **Abalone Cove Geologic Education Fund** (or similar).

Purpose: charitable / educational / scientific — e.g., geologic education, landslide awareness, preservation of natural avoidance corridors "everyone can use."

Structure: the 501(c)(3) Geologic Education Fund makes grants to or partners with the 501(c)(4) advocacy arm and with ACLAD on joint education projects. Strict separation of funds and activities required.

**Alternative:** use the existing Abalone Cove Foundation 501(c)(3) for the education work if the charitable-purpose statement covers it. Counsel review required to confirm scope.

## Why Not the Existing Foundation for Advocacy

Running the viral campaign + YIMBY-landscape navigation + coordinated press outreach through the existing Abalone Cove Foundation 501(c)(3) would:

1. **Strain lobbying limits** — Rev. Rul. 2004-6 and Form 990 Schedule C reporting constrain substantial lobbying; measured advocacy is possible via the § 501(h) election but full viral campaigning typically exceeds (c)(3) comfort zone
2. **Risk private-benefit challenges** — advocacy on specific developer sites can look like private benefit to the founder or other specific parcel owners, especially without the (c)(4) structure
3. **Damage donor credibility** — mission-aligned (c)(3) donors (preservation, historic, educational) expect restrained activity; advocacy-heavy activity changes the donor profile
4. **Entangle the neutral research mission** — the Library of Evidence and inter-HOA research work depends on the Foundation being seen as a neutral convener; advocacy activity complicates that posture
5. **Create entanglement with CoAC operational governance** — the Foundation's charitable mission is intentionally distinct from CoAC's mutual-benefit governance; advocacy muddies that boundary

The 501(c)(4) carves out the advocacy work cleanly, preserving the Foundation's charitable integrity.

## Formation Path

Recommended sequence (pending counsel):

1. **Separate 501(c)(4) counsel** engaged — distinct from HOA counsel, distinct from Foundation 501(c)(3) counsel
2. **Purpose statement** drafted — social welfare, geologic-safety advocacy, public education, partnership with public-safety infrastructure (ACLAD), preservation of historic private covenants
3. **Articles of Incorporation** filed with California Secretary of State
4. **Form 1024-A** filed with IRS for 501(c)(4) recognition (or legal reliance on self-declared (c)(4) status pending approval)
5. **California Attorney General registration** (charitable trust registry)
6. **FTB Form 199 + Form 3500** for California tax-exempt status
7. **Bank account + books** opened separately
8. **Board** confirmed (can include overlap with Foundation or CoAC boards with COI disclosures)
9. **Gift Acceptance Policy** — no crypto (keep crypto at the 501(c)(3) per [[foundation-crypto-donations]])
10. **Platform integration** — dedicated (c)(4) section on `dev.abalonecove.org` with visual and legal separation from Foundation and CoAC sections

Fastest path if speed matters: **fiscal sponsor** for initial 30–90 days while formation is pending, OR individual founder acting in a personal capacity with clear disclaimers until (c)(4) is formed.

## Reasonable Compensation Through the (c)(4)

If the founder is compensated by the 501(c)(4) for advocacy work (viral campaign, press outreach, platform management), the same principles from [[foundation-three-hat-compensation]] apply:

- Formal board adoption of a compensation policy
- Comparable-compensation data (executive directors of similar-size California 501(c)(4) advocacy nonprofits)
- Disinterested-director approval (founder recuses)
- CICD + email logs as the evidence base (see [[foundation-time-logging-policy]])
- Annual re-approval
- Written disclosure of founder's roles across the 501(c)(3), 501(c)(4), LLC, and CoAC Board

The three-hat compensation model becomes **four-hat**: LLC / Foundation 501(c)(3) / 501(c)(4) Advocacy Arm / CoAC volunteer. Each hat has distinct allocation rules. File-path allocation (see [[foundation-three-hat-compensation]]) extends to include:

- **501(c)(4) hat (paid advocacy):** `Brain/wiki/coac-2026-civic-problems-memorandum.md`, advocacy campaign assets, press releases, YIMBY-navigation memos, political-analysis notes
- **Foundation 501(c)(3) hat (paid charitable/educational):** existing allocation — research, Library of Evidence, Foundation legal framework, editorial sites as charitable output
- **LLC hat (paid commercial R&D):** existing allocation — Canary, Cove platform, Angel, etc.
- **CoAC hat (volunteer unpaid):** governance, Board service, recruitment, enforcement work, `dev.abalonecove.org` operational admin
