# Community of Abalone Cove (formerly WPBCA) — Ocean Path Easements — Private Governance Matter

Tract 14649 contains two recorded 1952 ocean access easements running from Seacove Drive to the Pacific. These are rare, valuable, and legally sensitive private rights. This card captures the working position on how the Foundation and Community of Abalone Cove handle them — with emphasis on what **not** to do publicly.

Distilled working position from a 2026-04-19 advisor memo. Sensitive. Treat as private WPBCA governance matter. HOA counsel review required before any public communication touching this topic.

## Why This Card Is Tagged Sensitive

The founder put it directly: "I have been reluctant to point that out specifically because it is somewhat controversial that we have it to begin with, think Hollister Ranch and the Bixby fight there. Well we have the same situation sort of. Some of the lots in tract 14649 have lot lines that are beyond the mean high tide line, they like it that way. It is exceedingly rare, in terms of assets valuable, it makes a k-shaped economy right inside our own HOA."

Publicly emphasizing these easements could invite:
- California Coastal Commission staff interest
- Media coverage framing the HOA as restricting public coastal access
- Activist-group pressure (Surfrider, coastal-access advocates)
- Internal member conflict between lots that have the easement and lots that do not
- Opportunistic litigation

The precedents:
- **Hollister Ranch** (Santa Barbara County) — decades of litigation over public vs. private coastal access
- **Bixby / Gaviota Coast** disputes — similar private-access-vs-public-policy tensions

WPBCA's situation is not identical but is analogous enough that the same playbook of external pressure could apply.

## The Instruments

From the 2009 Restated Declaration + Declaration of Easements (1949/1952 instruments):

- **1949 Declaration of Easements** — reserved the two ocean access easements and associated utility/drainage rights; recorded servitudes running with the land
- **1952 specific easement grants** — formal recording of the Seacove Drive → Pacific access paths
- **Tract 14649** — the 81-lot tract; not all lots share the same relationship to these easements
- **Lot lines beyond Mean High Tide Line** — exceedingly rare along the California coast post-Coastal Act; existence is a pre-Coastal-Act artifact

## Foundation Role — Limited to Neutral Documentation

The Abalone Cove Foundation's role on this topic is **research and documentation only**. Not advocacy. Not policy. Not public litigation support.

Permitted Foundation activity:
- Transcribing and indexing the 1949 Declaration of Easements and 1952 grants as part of the Library of Evidence
- Publishing the recorded chain of title (the facts of what was recorded when) on cove.org or abalonecove.org in a restrained, factual register
- Cross-referencing related historical instruments (Declaration 100, Declaration 1, Tract 14649 map, 2009 Restated Declaration)
- Preserving the documentary record

**Not** permitted Foundation activity:
- Advocating for or against specific lot owners' positions
- Publishing commentary on the public/private coastal access policy debate
- Supporting or opposing Coastal Commission positions on this specific tract
- Funding or organizing litigation on the easement
- Using the easement as a fundraising hook ("help us defend our beach access")

The distinction matters. Neutral documentation is core 501(c)(3) educational activity. Advocacy on this specific contested asset is private-benefit territory and could endanger exempt status.

## WPBCA Role — Private Governance

Everything beyond neutral documentation belongs with WPBCA as the mutual-benefit HOA:

- Legal defense of the recorded easements (if challenged)
- Inter-HOA coordination with neighboring associations on shared coastal issues
- Member communication about easement rights and responsibilities
- Enforcement actions for encroachment on the easement
- Strategic decisions about how public to be about the easement's existence

This work is done by the WPBCA board (once quorum is restored — see [[wpbca-third-director-recruitment]]) using WPBCA's counsel (not Foundation counsel) and WPBCA's treasury (not Foundation funds).

## Why Separation Protects Both

If the Foundation were to advocate publicly on the easement:
- **501(c)(3) risk** — private benefit to the specific lot owners; political/lobbying activity potentially beyond educational purpose
- **Public-pressure amplification** — the Foundation's public voice draws more attention than individual member voices; that can invite the exact pressure the founder is reluctant to trigger
- **Entanglement with WPBCA governance** — blurs the clean separation the Foundation structure depends on
- **Donor-credibility damage** — mission-aligned funders expect preservation/education, not coastal-access litigation

Keeping the Foundation in neutral-documentation mode:
- Protects the 501(c)(3) status
- Keeps the rest of the Foundation's mission (100-year story, Library of Evidence, inter-HOA convening) credible and funded
- Lets WPBCA handle the sensitive governance privately through proper channels
- Avoids the Hollister/Bixby pattern of inviting external pressure

## Public Voice Calibration

On cove.org / abalonecove.org, the easement can be referenced the way any other recorded instrument is — as a fact in the record:

> "The 1949 Declaration of Easements reserved two ocean access easements from Seacove Drive to the Pacific Ocean, running with the land of specified lots within what is now Tract 14649."

That is a true statement of the recorded record. It is not a policy position, a defense, or an invitation to argue.

What to avoid:
- Framing language like "our beach," "private access we earned," "public vs. private"
- Maps that feature the easement as the focal point
- Fundraising copy that implies the Foundation is defending the easement
- Social-media amplification of the topic

## Member Communication

Inside WPBCA (members-only, not public):
- Board can communicate with members about easement rights and responsibilities
- Legal counsel can advise on specific disputes
- Annual meeting discussions can address strategy
- This is private governance — not Foundation activity

## Open Decisions (WPBCA, not Foundation)

These belong to the WPBCA board once quorum is restored:

- Legal posture toward any specific encroachment or challenge
- Engagement (or non-engagement) with Coastal Commission inquiries
- Coordination with other peninsula HOAs with analogous easements
- Whether to pursue a declaratory judgment confirming the easements (pro) vs. keeping a lower profile (con)
- Insurance coverage specific to easement defense

## Founder-Level Personal Note

The founder's lot may or may not be among those with easement rights. The Foundation's posture on this topic is the same regardless. This is specifically to avoid any appearance that the Foundation is a vehicle for advancing the founder's (or any individual member's) personal coastal-access interests. See [[foundation-three-hat-compensation]] on keeping personal interest distinct from charitable-purpose work.
