The Decision Substrate

A closed-loop consensus mechanism. Control through transparency. Every action carries its source, every decision carries its evidence, and the loop closes — capture, organize, decide, act — so the substrate compounds with use.

closed loop · transparent center · every stakeholder sees the same evidence

Who · How · Why

Who

Leaders making decisions where being wrong is expensive. Founders, CEOs, owners, and boards considering acquisition, divestiture, transformation, scale, pivot, or succession. Investors evaluating a software or specialty-retail target. CTOs aligning technology with business strategy.

How

Three structured engagements — positioning, information systems planning, SaaS — each run by a focused team under scoped authority. Every action is logged. Every claim has its source. The output deposits into a queryable knowledge layer that stays with you and stays useful after the engagement closes.

Why

Standard consulting walks out with the relationship and the slides. The decision substrate stays. Whatever you decide to do next, you start from a structured picture of your business — not from scratch.

Method · Strategic Positioning

Who you serve. What you stand for. Where you win against competitors. What to do next to bring the strategy to market.

Data points produced

  • Core customer profile with target segments and fit-scores
  • Brand promise and value proposition grounded in customer evidence
  • Competitive position and the white space your brand can claim
  • Channel and merchandising recommendations with rationale
  • Activation roadmap sequenced by dependency and impact
Example finding A specialty retailer learns that 30% of its assortment doesn't fit its core customer. The merchandise-fit-test surfaces which clusters to cut and which to extend. Three months later, gross margin is up four points and the brand statement finally matches what's on the shelf.

Method · Information Systems Planning

How your technology supports the business today. What it should look like to support the business tomorrow. The portfolio and roadmap to get there.

Data points produced

  • Business strategy alignment mapped to technology capability
  • Application portfolio with each app on the strategic grid (invest, modernize, maintain, sunset)
  • Target architecture across applications, data, technology, and governance
  • Project portfolio with priorities, dependencies, and net-present-value
  • Roadmap and investment plan sequenced by year
Example finding A multi-store retailer discovers half of its application portfolio is in the Support quadrant — keeping the lights on but not building advantage. The roadmap shifts the budget toward two Strategic platforms and sunsets four legacy apps over twenty-four months.

Method · SaaS

The commercial, product, and operational reality of a software business — for investment, partnership, transformation, or transaction decisions.

Data points produced

  • Customer base and ARR cohorts with retention curves and net revenue retention by segment
  • Codebase health, architecture, and technical debt categorized by impact and remediation cost
  • Integration map with break-impact scores
  • Security and compliance posture mapped to the next certification
  • Unit economics and operational pattern across team, GTM, customer success, and finance
Example finding A buyer evaluating a SaaS target finds 40% of net revenue retention comes from a single segment running on a tech stack the buyer cannot support post-acquisition. The deal reframes from acquisition to partnership.
