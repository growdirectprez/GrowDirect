## What he asks for that we say no to

- "Modernized SaaS multiple of 8-10x" — no. He's selling a legacy services business; we're paying the legacy multiple. The modernization premium accrues to us because we're doing the work.
- "All-cash close, no earnout" — no. Customer continuity is load-bearing for our economics; earnout aligns interests.
- "Keep team intact through Y5" — yes for senior engineers via retention package; no for guarantees on full team. We need flexibility to repoint the team toward growth work via A4.
- "Maintain Counterpoint as the default front-end forever" — yes for the legacy book; no for new customers. New onboardings default to DriftPOS but Counterpoint stays available.

- DriftPOS partnership unwinds or slips materially

## Strategic context — RapidPOS as Patient Zero

This deal is the platform investment for the VAR roll-up thesis. The methodology, the skill scaffold, the agentic ops fabric, the cost-model — all of it gets built once on RapidPOS and amortizes across VAR #2, #3, #4. Each subsequent VAR deal is materially cheaper to execute.

## What's in our favor we shouldn't oversell

- He doesn't know about ISMS as a continuous-monitoring discipline (Q8 hypothesis). When we name the standard, he learns. We don't lecture; we let the standard speak.
- His suitor pool is not as big as he thinks. PE rollup buyers care about EBITDA + multiples, not about modernization narrative. NCR Voyix-direct interest is theoretical. We're realistically the most credible buyer in his market.
- The DriftPOS partnership means we don't need RapidPOS to provide a modern POS. We're acquiring his *customer book* and *team*; the modern POS comes from Bart.

## What we should be honest about with ourselves

- The cost-model demo run shows negative net 3-year at archetype-default values. This is because the T3 GCP ramp ($17M+/year by Y3) requires anchor revenue much larger than the $5M placeholder. Real anchor-account ARR for a Global-50 retail-spine subscription is in the $50M-$200M range. With realistic anchor sizing, the math works. **Without the anchor, T3 doesn't have to ramp** — we stay at T2 steady-state and the math also works (~$7.6M net 3-year).
- Phase A burn-down execution is the dominant risk. If wiki coverage doesn't reach 60% by month 6, the whole flywheel stalls and Bart's-team labor mix doesn't shift, and we end up running an expensive break-fix shop.
- Customer-side discovery may show heavier per-customer customizations than the archetype suggests (Q12 hypothesis). CRDM mapping difficulty could rise from the $1.45M expected to $2-3M, which materially compresses margins.
- 20-year owner-operators don't always behave rationally about acquisition pricing. Be prepared for emotional anchoring at numbers that don't reflect the business reality.
