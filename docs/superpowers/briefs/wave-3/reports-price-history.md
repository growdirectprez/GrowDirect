**Primary role:** MGR; BYR; LP  
**Entry points:** Primary sidebar nav (Reports section); item detail Pricing tab → view history

## Key Elements

### Price Change Table
Columns: Date, Item #, Description, Category, Store, Old Price, New Price, Change ($), Change (%), Changed By, Reason, Reference (PO # if cost-driven, promotion ID if promotional).

**Changed By:** Every price change is attributed to the user who made it. Canary captures the actor, timestamp, and previous value — the audit trail that CP doesn't have. "Who changed this price and when?" has a definitive answer.

**Reason:** Free text or reason code entered at the time of the price change (from item edit or pricing rule). Required for price decreases above a configured threshold.

**Reference:** If the price change was generated by a promotion or pricing rule (Wave 4 additions), the rule or promotion ID appears here as a link. If it was triggered by a cost update (last PO cost increased), the PO # appears. Manual changes have no reference — they're naked price adjustments.

**LP read:** An unauthorized price reduction — no promotion reference, no cost justification, changed by a cashier-level user — is a potential fraud event. LP can filter: Changed By = non-MGR users, Direction = Decrease, no Reference → shows unsanctioned price reductions.

**Empty state:** "No price changes in the selected period."

## Interaction Flows

1. **Vendor cost passthrough audit:** BYR opens price history → filters by Reference = PO # → verifies that cost increases from last vendor PO were passed through as price increases → 3 items missing the update → adjusts
2. **LP unauthorized price change investigation:** LP filters Changed By = cashier accounts, Direction = Decrease → 2 price reductions made by cashier #C-0042 without a promotion reference → neither change has a reason entered → flags for investigation; opens alert
3. **BYR seasonal pricing review:** BYR opens price history → last 90 days → Direction = Both → reviews all pricing decisions for the season → evaluates whether increases or decreases correlated with sales volume changes

## UX Callout

In CP, changing an item's price overwrites the previous price with no record of what it was. If a price is wrong, there's no audit trail to understand when it changed or who changed it. "Why is this item $14.99?" is an unanswerable question in CP. In Canary, price history is a first-class report because pricing decisions are consequential — they affect margins, sales velocity, and (for LP) may represent unauthorized manipulation. The LP use case (unauthorized price reduction by non-MGR users) is the edge case that motivates building the audit trail as a report rather than just a log. Normal users will rarely look at price history; LP investigators will look at it whenever a discount anomaly needs explanation.
