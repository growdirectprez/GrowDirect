Promotion / Event Management
Process Scope and Description
This process includes all aspects of planning promotions/events encompassing event calendars; media selection, production and distribution; and merchandise selection. The process facilitates execution by triggering actions (e.g., ordering merchandise) when critical calendar dates are reached. The process monitors the results of the promotion/events and supports accounting for all promotional funds due.

Promotions / Event Management Process Recommendationsi
General Promotional/Event Plans should be developed from the following inputs:
Marketing Plan
Merchandise Plan
Assortment Plan
The general promotional plan should outline
Overall promotions calendar
Themes for each event
Type of promotion
Type of ad
Projected results
As the lead-time for each event is reached, specific detailed plans for the event should be set. These include:
Type of promotion
Type of ad
Merchandise for the promotion is specified
Critical dates are set, e.g., ad approval date, ordering date, in-store date, etc.
Expected results, i.e. sales, margin and sell through
Critical dates should be fed to promotions monitoring and control. Promotion monitoring and control should provide both before-the-fact notice, the task to be done, and after-the-fact notice when it is not done when required.
Promotions / Event Management Process Recommendations (Continued)r
Based on the critical dates, merchandise should be selected and presentation requirements set and prepared for communication to the stores.
Ad/Promotional materials should be specified and turned over to production (internal or external).
Ad Production and Distribution are both tracked by promotions monitoring and problems are reported for appropriate action.
Critical dates drive order generation, orders may be generated from:
Incremental replenishment buys
Promotional and Seasonal buys
Purchase orders follow the normal process approval through store receiving except that all events in the supply chain are monitored against critical dates. PO tracking reports estimate the impact of status on arrival dates. Problems are reported for appropriate action.
Purchase Orders issued and received support the accounting for promotional allowances and rebates. Promotional allowances feed sales, margin and inventory accounting as well as the financial system to support reporting and collection of billbacks and rebates.
All elements of the promotion and the sales and inventory results feed promotion effectiveness monitoring which not only report current results, but also maintains history for subsequent analysis.p

Key Changes
Versus Current Practice *
Supports more comprehensive planning of promotions and events.
Captures pre-event forecasts of performance.
Supports execution through before-the-fact screening of tasks and after-the-fact reminders of tasks not done or started on time.
Facilitates capturing promotion/event conditions to be used in evaluation and forecasting.
Enhances performance reporting so that ad effectiveness can be tracked and corrective actions can be taken.
Reduces the effort in accounting for allowances and facilitates their collection.
Versus Best Practice
Benefits
Promotions/Events management provides complete planning execution and monitoring support
Planning can be more complete
Execution will be more controlled in all areas
There will be better evaluation of effectiveness which in turn will yield better plans
Overall there should be improvements in the following:
Sell-through (and inventory productivity)
Less effort required to set up a promotion
Fewer missed dates because of missed orders and deliveries
Improved accounting for promotional money due which will yield better recovery
