Recommended Price Management Process Architecture
Price Management Process Recommendation
Initial prices and planned price moves should be generated from plans (i.e., Merchandise Plan, Promotional Plan). Reactive price changes should be based on actions taken from monitoring competitive prices and from monitoring sales, margin and inventory performance.
Price changes should be tested (simulated), where appropriate, and evaluated before they are approved and released. Evaluation should employ historical price volume relationships to project the impact of changes. For example, period sales, margin and inventory performance for the class or sub-class level can be projected based on the proposed price changes.
Price changes outside of certain pre-set parameters should be approved before transactions are generated and transmitted.
Price changes that take selling price below cost require write downs to the inventory of the product.
Store attributes facilitate both the establishment of zones and the execution of the pricing strategy. Attributes are needed below the total store level (e.g., store department, sub-department). Attributes needed for all store clusters to be used in addressing pricing issues.
Transactions should be generated by store for transmission to each location using store attributes and pricing zones.
Price Management Process Recommendation (Continued)
Pricing transactions should be managed at any level of the timing of the effective date, for example:
Immediate
Between set dates
Progressive application based on intervals, e.g., first markdown at 30% off and after 2 weeks the discount is increased to 50%
Price lookup supports POS. Approved price overrides should be used based on rules with appropriate audit trails.
Although outside of the Price Management process, sales audit provides the first line check on price overrides. The results of sales audit feed loss prevention and sales, price and margin monitoring.
Monitoring should track the following related to price changes:
Top line sales
Price variances with plan
Margin changes and sales below margin targets
Monitoring should provide the basis for taking corrective action, for example
Suggested price changes
Correction to previously issued changes
