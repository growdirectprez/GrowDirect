Process Scope and Description
Planograms and floor plans provide the key link between assortment management and store execution of merchandise presentation. They also provide links between presentation and inventory management and labor management.
Staples Processes
Planogram (reference no. 9)
To develop consistent, implementable visual displays of merchandise that will facilitate sales (make the category more shoppable), sustain inventory productivity and reduce the labor required to implement planograms/floor plans and stock the planogram/floor plan.

Recommended Planogramming / Floor Planing
Planogramming / Floor Plan Process Recommendations
Periodically space needs to be reallocated. Typically it should be done annually, when there are major changes in the merchandise assortment or new prototype stores.
The space allocation should be derived from product sale, inventory requirements and profitability analysis. The physical design of the space(new prototype and / or existing space) is also a major consideration.
Fixtures are specified and floor plans are generated according to the space allocation parameters. New floor plans feed into store construction and remodeling.
Proposed fixture / area presentation plans are developed from the floor space allocation and fixture data:
Sales and profitability analysis
Inventory and labor analysis
Product affinities
The assortment
Product movement
Physical attributes of products
The objective of the display development sub-process is to balance physical characteristics, affinities, inventory productivity and labor requirements of individual store types while maximizing sales and profitability through presentation.
Planogramming / Floor Planning Recommendations (Continued)
Inventory productivity goals help determine minimum and maximum shelf allocations; as well as the use of full case vs. broken case quantities.
Productivity goals consider the use of full case, inner packs and eaches. The impact on the store as well as labor is also considered.
The process should be set up to accept physical characteristics data from vendors. Initially unreliable vendors should be excluded from 
downloads
Digitized images of the product should be used to facilitate communication and reduce the number of manual preview sets that need to be made.
Manual planogram sets may be made and evaluated before the development of the final presentation.
Store communication supports sending the planogram electronically, as well as producing all of the shelf labels and point of purchase signage.
For slow moving items, the final minimum and maximum quantities for space allocation are used by store replenishment
The timing of store communication and the timing of shipment of new planogram products to the stores should be coordinated.

Versus Current Process

Benefits
Improve overall productivity of space by reducing the impact of lower productivity areas in the store.
Reduce total elapsed time to produce a planogram or revise one.
Reduce total hours required to produce a planogram or revise one.
Make planograms easier to set in the store and reduce the number of labor hours required.

Organization / Policy Implications
In the target process environment, merchandising would drive more of the POG reviews and production of POGs as a result of implementing comprehensive merchandise planning including the use of category management, as required.
As a result, the production of POGs could be more frequent and therefore require more specialists.
In addition store hours may have to be increased during peak times. (Hopefully, the improved efficiency would help offset the total time required.)i
