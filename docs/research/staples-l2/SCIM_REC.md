Supply Chain Inventory Management 
Process Scope and Description
Logistics Planning: Logistics Planning incorporates evaluating vendors and the mode of buying and flow for products to determine how to improve their contribution to profit margin. The process also includes logistics capacity analysis. 
Forecasting: Forecasting provides projections of sales in support of all methods of generating orders and in support of merchandise planning and logistics planning.
Review Management: Review Management works in conjunction with parameters set by logistics planning to route products to the appropriate order generating methods (e.g., replenishment) based on time and event conditions (e.g., low inventory).
Replenishment (Pull): Replenishment is the process by which stores and distribution centers are restocked based on sales, inventory position and service level targets.
Allocation (Push): Allocation is the process by which bulk orders are fairly distributed to individual stores based on a variety of drivers, e.g., store plan. Allocation models (by store type) also may be used to generate orders in support of promotional and event buying.
Investment Buying: Investment Buying is the process by which incremental buying quantities are suggested when there is an opportunity to acquire products where there is a temporary increase in gross margin.

Investment Buying Process Architecture Chart
Investment Buying Recommendations
When there is an investment opportunity, the Suggested Order Quantity should be generated by using merchandise contribution margin analysis and a set of guidelines on return on inventory investment.
The opportunity is created by:
Deal pricing that may not be passed on to the consumer
Rebates available at a specific volume level
Buying in anticipation of price increases
Getting extra payment dating
The merchandise contribution margin analysis finds the incremental time supply where the contribution margin after increased handling cost (if any) and inventory carrying cost equals the current value, subject to the result yielding at least the guideline return on inventory investment.
For example, adding 10 weeks of supply to inventory may yield a backroom contribution margin, but the contribution margin return on inventory investment may only be 90% ($0.90 per $1.00 invested in inventory) while the target is 130%.
The input triggering the analysis is Review Management (event-driven).
Investment Buying Recommendations (Continued)
The following also support the analysis:
Item management (e.g., current cost, order generation method)
Vendor Management (e.g., terms)
Inventory - On-hand and On-order
Sales history
Initial order that was generated. (Typically the investment buy is incremental to the initial quantity) 
The suggested order should be passed to Vendor Management.
Orders generated are evaluated to determine if the economic benefits are achieved. If not, adjustments should be made.
Promotion and Event Buying Process Architecture Chart
Promotion and Event Buying Recommendations
Promotion / Event Buying should be triggered by Review Management and supported by Item Management and Vendor Management.
The review trigger is the lead time to buy merchandise for an upcoming promotion or event. (The date may be established in promotion and event management process)
Promotion / Event suggested order quantity is generated from the model quantity and the available inventory (if any).
The Promotion / Event quantity is developed from the basic models or may use allocation models by store type. (The reverse can also be done, i.e., Allocation can use Promotion / Event models by store type as an allocation driver)
Promotion / Event suggested order quantity may be passed to Vendor Order Management or may be subject to an investment buy, if the opportunity exists.
Models should be generated from history and Promotional / Event conditions. Modeling varies from simple to very complex as illustrated in the following progression:
Average sales as a function of:
Market / Store type
Promotional conditions such as media type, featuring, etc.
Product category
Statistical relationships that identify strengths of 
Neural networks
Evaluation (using Promotional and Event Monitoring) enables sell thru to be analyzed. Models can be adjusted, in effective models should be eliminated and new models should be added.
The recommendation is to start with simple average and move as fast as possible to statistical methods. Use of neural networks should be deferred until they become more fully developed as tools.
Distribution Resource Planning Process Architecture Chart
Distribution Resource Planning Recommendations
The Distribution Resource Planning suggested order quantity is triggered from Review Management (usually a weekly process) and uses item data (e.g. relative importance), and vendor data (e.g., planning horizon).
Distribution Resource Planning is an advanced technique for Supply Chain Ordering typically for the following conditions:
Partnership vendors for high volume products where production is closely tied to retailer forecasts
Private label products
Forecasts for future sales should be shared with the supplier:
Minimum coverage period is the current production cycle plus at least the raw material procurement cycle
Suppliers
The pipeline model and quantity developed from it represent the aggregate needs of the pipeline in terms of an aggregate order-up-to-level or time supply (both include a provision for safety stock).
DRP suggested order quantity passes through Vendor Order Management and becomes a PO which triggers shipment from the supplier. When the selling dynamics warrant it, the need to fill the pipeline with shipments to individual DC
s may not be determined until the order is ready to ship. At that point, an allocation is made to the DC based on the DC inventory model which is appropriate for the mode of flow (e.g., for flow through, aggregate store order-up-to levels less available inventory).
Distribution Resource Planning Recommendations (Continued)
Once received and based on mode of flow, the quantity may be:
Allocated to stores
Part - DC holdback, determined by the model
Stocked and replenished to stores
DRP suggested order quantities are evaluated for effectiveness and adjustments are made to models and forecasting in order to improve performance.
The conventional forecasting replenishment and allocation processes should be used within a DRP conceptual structure. s

Vendor Order \ Store Order Management Recommendations
The total process primarily covers vendor orders (or store drop ship orders). Not all process components apply to store orders for DC stock.
Raw suggested order quantities should be adjusted for item level minimums (if any).
Vendor imposed
Internal supply chain (store presentation quantity)
The next adjustment is for pack size (full case or inner pack in the case of store orders to a DC).
Orders are adjusted for unit loads:
Pallet layer quantity
Tier rounding
Full pallet rounding
For joint orders (multiple items from one vendor), minimums or concession level may be met or 
Minimum order size, e.g., for prepaid freight (for drop shipment)
Minimum to obtain a concession level, e.g., purchase volume for a rebate
Transportation rounding with tolerance (min, max levels):
Truckload
Car load (paper)
Container (imports)
Multiple transportation loads may be generated including rounding of fractional loads to full loads subject to rounding rules.
Transportation rounding may require the use of reaching for a minimum tolerance. Reaching adds time supply across the products that need replenishment up to the limits provided. If the time supply exceeds the allowed limit, no order is produced and it must be generated manually if the order is really required.y
Vendor Order \ Store Order Management Recommendations Continuedl
Date coordination is used primarily for back haul (collect freight) programs to build full trailers by shipping lane. Pick up dates may be set equal to allow the pick up. If coordination adds time supply, the SOQ is adjusted to avoid potential stock out conditions.
The final SOQ is subjected to exception rules to determine if the order needs manual review. The rules should include:
Time supply (minimum and maximum)
Minimum quantities ($ and units)
Maximum quantities ($ and units)
Orders that fail edits are cycled for manual review. Orders that pass edits go on to become purchase orders (EDI format), assuming they pass open-to-buy review.
