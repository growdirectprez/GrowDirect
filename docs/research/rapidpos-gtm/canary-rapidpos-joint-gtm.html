The Market Moment

Why the Counterpoint installed base is actionable now, and why DriftPOS changes the strategic frame

The Counterpoint installed base — approximately 9,000 SMB specialty retail stores across all VARs, ~1,200 of them garden-center format — is at a structural inflection. Three forces converge:

ForceWhat it meansThe window
Aging infrastructure The server in the closet is 8–15 years old. Every Counterpoint merchant who bought on-premise in 2010–2015 is in a hardware end-of-life window. Upgrade conversations are already happening between VAR and merchant. VAR is already in the room. The question is whether the upgrade is Counterpoint-only or platform-enabled.
Cloud-native disruptors Square for Retail, Lightspeed, Shopify POS are taking greenfield and small-chain accounts. They won't take the 15–40 store specialty operators — the migration cost is the moat — but they are raising the expectation for what a modern system looks like. Merchants are fielding unsolicited demos. The VAR who shows up with an enterprise-layer answer owns the next five years of that relationship.
DriftPOS in motion Rapid POS is no longer only a Counterpoint VAR. DriftPOS — their own .NET + Angular + Android platform with per-tenant Postgres, modular architecture, and Azure DevOps backend — is in active development. Every Counterpoint customer in the Rapid POS book is a future DriftPOS migration candidate. The partnership must be durable across both platforms. Canary positioned as the enterprise spine — agnostic across Counterpoint and DriftPOS — holds value through the migration rather than becoming a casualty of it.
The strategic reframe DriftPOS requires

This conversation is not VAR recruitment. It is a vendor-to-vendor partnership. Rapid POS is building a platform. GrowDirect is building a platform. The question is how two platform companies with complementary — not overlapping — capability sets go to market together without stepping on each other's roadmap or commercial model.

Why Merge Forces

The structural case for a joint motion over independent GTM

The asymmetry is clean and stable: Rapid POS has what Canary lacks (the merchant relationship, vertical domain depth, Counterpoint implementation expertise, 18 years of trust inside the book); Canary has what Rapid POS lacks (the enterprise analytics and LP spine, the CATz engagement methodology, the agent layer, the evidentiary infrastructure). Neither side competes with the other. Each side's value compounds through the other.

Without the otherGapWith the other
Canary alone Cold direct-to-merchant outreach. Zero conversion. No channel. No Counterpoint configuration expertise. No vertical domain depth per merchant. Canary via Rapid POS channel: warm merchant introduction, VAR-credentialed delivery, Counterpoint schema knowledge, customer trust already established.
Rapid POS alone Merchant transformation conversations end at "more Counterpoint customizations." No enterprise-layer answer. Increasing exposure as cloud-native disruptors raise expectations. DriftPOS gaps in LP, OTB, distribution, labor remain unfilled. Rapid POS + Canary: the CATz methodology converts a VAR renewal conversation into a transformation engagement. Recurring platform revenue on top of implementation margin. DriftPOS gaps filled by Canary modules before they become competitive liabilities.
DriftPOS alone No Loss Prevention module. No Distribution / OTB module. No Labor module. Same gaps as Counterpoint. The platform ships without the enterprise-layer features that justify premium pricing at the 15–40 store tier. DriftPOS + Canary as the enterprise layer: the module gaps (LP, OTB, Distribution) become "powered by Canary" rather than roadmap debt. Canary's span across Counterpoint and DriftPOS provides migration continuity for every Rapid POS merchant.
The migration continuity story — the single most durable commercial argument

A Rapid Garden POS customer running Counterpoint today can install Canary as the enterprise layer. When that customer migrates to DriftPOS — on Rapid POS's timeline, not Canary's — their Canary continuity persists. Transaction history, LP rules, OTB configuration, domain knowledge vault: none of it resets. The merchant migrates their POS; they don't re-onboard their intelligence layer. This is a retention argument Rapid POS can sell before the migration happens.

The CATz discipline that makes the audit convert

The four-week audit does not close without five gate conditions: vision signed by the merchant, business case signed (benefits + cost + NPV), Phase II scope defined, change-readiness assessment complete, executive sponsor confirms proceed. Compressing the audit by skipping the sign-off is a documented failure mode. Rapid POS's relationship is what makes the gate achievable — they hold the executive sponsor, not Canary.

Integration Architecture

Counterpoint adapter (now) · DriftPOS adapter (bridge) · dual-track strategy across both platforms

Canary's integration posture is enterprise layer alongside, not inside. It does not live in the POS codebase. It does not require schema changes to the merchant's Counterpoint installation. It reads; it does not write (except where the merchant explicitly enables it for plan execution). This posture is what makes the "zero disruption" pitch credible and what makes the migration-continuity story durable across Counterpoint → DriftPOS.

DriftPOS Integration — Four Scenarios

✓ Recommended default
Scenario B — Alongside, reading Postgres
Canary runs as a separate service. Reads the tenant's DriftPOS Postgres via logical replication or sanctioned read-replica. Decoupled deploys, language-neutral, mirrors the Counterpoint posture exactly. Requires Rapid POS to sanction the read pattern at the partnership level.
Medium-term direction
Scenario C — DriftPOS API consumer
Canary calls DriftPOS Catalog, PointOfSale, Sync, and Reporting endpoints. Cleanest contract; future-proof as DriftPOS schema evolves. Depends on DriftPOS exposing a stable API surface — open question to confirm.
Phase 2 — plan execution
Scenario D — Canary writes to DriftPOS
Canary's plans (POs, transfers, distribution recs) write into DriftPOS via API. DriftPOS executes; Canary monitors the result. The "plan in Canary, execute downstream" UX. Dependent on Scenario C; requires idempotency and write-authority agreement.
Park unless proposed
Scenario A — Canary as DriftPOS module
DriftPOS.Module.Canary ships in modules.json, runs in-process. Architecturally elegant; commercially constraining. Couples Canary's roadmap to Rapid POS's release cycle. Only viable if both companies align fully on joint roadmap governance.

Commercial Model

Three engagement modes, VAR margin structure, platform subscription, meter on operational improvement
Engagement Mode 1
Audit / Health Check
Four weeks. Fixed-fee. Delivers the CATz diagnostic deck, LP attribution baseline, OTB position, vendor scorecard. Exit: signed finding + conversion decision. Rapid POS charges the merchant; GrowDirect is the delivery engine. Designed to be priced at the VAR's discretion — GrowDirect provides the methodology and the platform; Rapid POS owns the commercial terms with the merchant.
Engagement Mode 2
Transformation Engagement
Six to twenty weeks depending on scope. SOW-based. Activates modules per the diagnostic findings. Rapid POS owns delivery and collects the delivery margin. GrowDirect provides the platform, SDDs, and technical escalation. This is the high-margin engagement for the VAR — the CATz methodology provides a known scope and timeline that makes the SOW defensible to the merchant.
Engagement Mode 3
Operating System Mode
Ongoing. Per-store recurring subscription. Base platform subscription (predictable floor) + variable meter on payroll-to-revenue ratio improvement. Rapid POS retains the merchant relationship and owns tier-1 support. GrowDirect collects the platform subscription. Revenue share structure negotiated at partnership level — not disclosed to the merchant.
The Meter Rationale
Payroll-to-Revenue as the Alignment Mechanism
Every Canary module — LP (Q), OTB (J + C), Distribution (D), Labor (L) — either improves or degrades the payroll-to-revenue ratio. When the ratio improves, the meter earns. When it doesn't, the meter doesn't run hard. This aligns GrowDirect's variable revenue with merchant operational improvement. The platform only wins when the merchant wins. Rapid POS wins when the merchant renews — same alignment.
Why VARs say yes

Rapid POS converts their existing Counterpoint book into a platform-supported recurring revenue stream. The CATz audit is a high-margin, known-scope engagement they can quote and deliver confidently. The transformation margin is higher than a Counterpoint customization quote. The operating-system recurring share is additive to the maintenance contract they already collect. And they own the customer relationship throughout — Canary is never in direct contact with the merchant without Rapid POS's involvement.
