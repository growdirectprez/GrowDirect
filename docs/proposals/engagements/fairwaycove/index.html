What we heard between the lines

You have been burned before. Your RFP language around “maintainability,” “knowledge transfer,” and “portable artifacts” reads as a team that has lived through bespoke point-to-point integration projects that became unsupportable two years after cutover. You want a method, not just a project.

Your audit committee is paying attention. [Merch System] is the record of truth for assortment, pricing, vendor terms, and inventory. That data is high-stakes — financial, contractual, regulatory. The lineage and immutability requirements in the RFP read as audit-driven, not technology-driven. We treat that as a first-class design constraint, not a compliance afterthought.

Your IT team is small relative to the engagement. The RFP describes a delivery model that augments rather than displaces internal staff. The team you retain after we leave matters more to you than the team you rent during delivery. We design the engagement around that.

You are aware that the next decade of retail operations is AI-augmented. The RFP asks for “modern API contracts,” “machine-readable integration definitions,” and “extensibility for future tooling.” That is the language of a buyer who does not want to plumb this system once for today and re-plumb it in three years for AI agents. The Canary Method is built to that forward shape from day one.

What we’re solving for

  1. Plumbing fidelity. Every system speaks one canonical retail model, mapped explicitly, contract-tested at the boundary.
  2. Auditability. Every record's lineage is hash-anchored on arrival, immutable in storage, and provable on demand — without depending on a single vendor's database to remain authoritative.
  3. Reversibility. At any phase, you can fall back to the legacy system without data loss or surprise. No bridge is burned until you sign off.
  4. Portability. Your team retains the Integration Accelerator — the canonical model, OpenAPI contracts, MCP connectors, runbooks. We leave. The capability stays.
  5. Future-readiness. The integration substrate is MCP-native, which means tomorrow's AI tooling and agent operations plug in without a re-implementation cycle.

What this is not

This is not a hand-rolled, point-to-point integration project. It is not a re-implementation of [Merch System]'s out-of-the-box connectors with custom adapter code. It is not a project that produces tribal knowledge in the heads of three people who leave when we exit. If that is what you wanted, we are not the right respondent.

Section 05

The framework for delivery

The Canary Method delivers merchandising-system integrations in six phases across approximately 40–48 weeks, with explicit go/no-go gates between every phase, four accountability rails enforced through every deliverable, and a governance cadence designed for joint operation with your internal team. The structure is the same across engagements; the content of each phase is parameterized against your specific environment, captured in the engagement profile during Phase 0.

Phases & gates

Phase detail

Phase 0 ~4 weeks

Discovery & Profile

Map current-state data flows across every system. Document target-state with [Merch System] at the center. Build the engagement profile: scale, channels, jurisdictions, vendors, freeze windows. Set canonical-model scope. Exit criteria: profile signed, canonical scope agreed, governance stood up.

Phase 1 ~6 weeks

Canonical Model Mapping

Map each existing system to the canonical retail model. Map [Merch System]'s API surface to the canonical model. Identify gaps, conflicts, transformations. Stand up the DMZ landing zone & hash-anchoring infrastructure. Exit criteria: mapping complete, DMZ live, anchoring tested end-to-end against a sample payload.

Phase 2 10–14 weeks

Pass-Through Plumbing

Build OpenAPI 3.0 endpoints and MCP connectors for every system pair. Read paths first — reversible, low-risk. Write paths second, with contract tests at every boundary. Continuous canonical-model conformance verification. Exit criteria: all read paths flowing, contract tests passing, write paths gated by retailer sign-off.

Phase 3 ~8 weeks

Master Data & Migration

Item master, vendor master, location master, customer master harmonized against the canonical model. Legacy data migrated with hash-anchored lineage end-to-end — every record traceable from origin to destination. Reconciliation against the canonical contracts. Exit criteria: master data reconciled, migration audit report signed, cutover plan approved.

Phase 4 ~8 weeks

Cutover & Hyper-Care

Phased cutover by store cluster or channel — never big-bang. Real-time monitoring through canonical-model lens. Documented rollback procedures usable at any cutover sub-stage. 90 days of declining-intensity hyper-care covering both systems and people. Exit criteria: all channels live, hyper-care metrics in green, no open severity-1 issues.

Phase 5 4–6 weeks

Steady State + Accelerator Handoff

Documentation handoff. The Integration Accelerator transferred to your team with practitioner certification. Decision point: ongoing Canary platform service (canonical model + DMZ + audit anchoring as a hosted service) or self-host the kit. Exit criteria: Accelerator delivered, certification complete, steady-state operating model signed.

Four accountability rails

The Canary Method enforces four rails through every phase. They are not periodic audits — they are the operating discipline of the engagement. Each rail has an explicit measurement, an explicit threshold, and an explicit escalation path.

Rail 01
Operational

No unknown loss across system boundaries. Every record that enters the canonical model is accounted for — in transit, at rest, and in downstream destinations. Reconciliation is continuous, not retrospective.

Rail 02
Financial

Phase-fixed pricing with documented gate criteria. No scope creep without a formal change order. No surprise overruns — if a phase trends off-target, it surfaces in weekly governance, not at month-end.

Rail 03
Evidentiary

Every record's lineage is hash-anchored on arrival in the DMZ landing zone. Storage is immutable. Provenance is provable on demand without depending on any single vendor's database remaining authoritative.

Rail 04
Vendor SLA

Active SLA discipline against [Merch System] and downstream cloud providers. Measured throughput, latency, and availability against contractual commitments. Exit options retained throughout.

Why these four, why this order

Operational and Financial are table stakes — any credible SI commits to them. Evidentiary is what makes this engagement audit-grade rather than just well-run. Vendor SLA is the rail most often skipped by SIs because it requires us to push on platforms we don't own; we treat it as a first-class deliverable because the long-term cost of weak vendor accountability exceeds the integration cost itself.
