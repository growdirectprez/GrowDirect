## The Insight

Everyone in Bitcoin mining is selling the coins. GrowDirect keeps them.

When a miner wins a block, the standard play is to sell the reward for fiat, cover electricity, and repeat. The business is commodity extraction: convert hash power to coins, convert coins to dollars, hope the margin holds.

GrowDirect does something different. The mining reward — 0.1 BTC earned from F2Pool by the founder — does not get sold. It stays on the chain. Each of those 10 million satoshis carries a unique ordinal number: a permanent serial identifier on the Bitcoin time chain. When inscribed with the El Jeffe notarization protocol, those ordinal numbers become permanently addressed slots in a canonical verification namespace. The Bitcoin does not leave. It transforms — from passive savings into productive infrastructure that generates revenue every time anyone needs to prove something happened.

Mining does not produce coins. Mining produces write access to the most permanent ledger ever created. GrowDirect is the first company to treat it that way.

## The Window

The cost to inscribe a Merkle root notarization on the Bitcoin time chain is approximately 200 satoshis today — less than twenty cents. Fee rates are at cycle lows. Post-halving inscription speculation has subsided. Frequent blocks clear at 1 sat/vbyte.

This window is closing. Each halving cycle compresses the fee floor upward as miners rely more heavily on transaction fees. Each new protocol competing for block space tightens availability. Under moderate adoption assumptions, sustained fee rates reach 30–100 sat/vbyte within 24 months. At that level, a bootstrapped competitor cannot economically establish a comparable inscription range from zero.

The clock is the fee market. The urgency is mathematical. Every month GrowDirect inscribes at current rates is a month where the cost to replicate the position increases for any follower.

## The Claim

Ordinal inscription ranges are to the Bitcoin verification layer what IP address ranges were to the early internet.

In 1993, ARIN allocated IP address blocks for trivial administrative fees. The addresses were just numbers. By 2021, those same blocks traded at $50 per address — a /8 block worth over $800 million. The scarcity was not the numbers. It was the position: established early, integrated into infrastructure, compounding in utility with every device that connected.

GrowDirect's Genesis Pool is 10 million ordinals inscribed with the first canonical notarization protocol, at the earliest available block heights, funded from the founder's own mining reward. The provenance is on the chain: mining effort, block reward, founder wallet, inscription transaction. A competitor can inscribe later ordinals with a later protocol from a market-purchased UTXO. They cannot replicate the position. The block heights are mined. The timestamps are written. The provenance is permanent.

Three counterarguments and their answers: Ordinals are infinite — but positional scarcity within abundance is what made early IP allocations valuable. There is no central registry — but the chain is the registry, and protocol precedent is the authority. The protocol can be forked — but the fork carries no history, no timestamp advantage, and no founder provenance. You can copy the code. You cannot copy the block height.

## The Timing

The world is entering a trust collapse. Generative AI has reduced the cost of producing convincing false evidence to near zero. Synthetic identities, deepfake transactions, and AI-generated documents are proliferating faster than any verification system can adapt. Every institution that relies on mutable records — banks, courts, insurers, regulators — is exposed.

The only verification that cannot be argued with is one anchored to the Bitcoin time chain. You cannot fake a block timestamp. You cannot unwrite an inscription. You cannot produce a Merkle proof for an event that was not in the tree.

The progression is visible: courts will require cryptographic timestamps for digital evidence. Insurers will demand immutable proof-of-loss records. Identity verification will shift from credential-based to proof-based. The demand curve for trustless verification is being manufactured by the same AI technology that is destroying trust in existing systems.

GrowDirect is building the infrastructure layer for this verification future at the lowest cost it will ever be. The window closes as the crisis becomes obvious and the market responds with fee pressure and competition for the namespace.
