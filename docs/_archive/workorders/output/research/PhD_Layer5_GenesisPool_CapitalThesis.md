## 2. The Capital Allocation Argument

### 2.1 Why Converting Mining Rewards to Inscriptions Is Superior to Selling Bitcoin

A founder who earns a Bitcoin mining reward has three options:

**Option A: Sell the Bitcoin.** Convert to fiat. Use the proceeds for operational expenses. The Bitcoin leaves the founder's custody. The mining reward becomes a one-time liquidity event. Standard practice for 99% of miners.

**Option B: Hold the Bitcoin.** Store as treasury reserve. Appreciate with BTC/USD price movement. The asset remains, but it generates no revenue. It is a savings strategy, not a business model.

**Option C: Inscribe the Bitcoin.** Convert the mining reward into a permanent inscription range on the time chain. The Bitcoin does not leave the chain. The satoshis still exist. But they now carry inscribed protocol data — the El Jeffe notarization schema — that enables perpetual validation revenue. The asset transforms from passive (held) to productive (revenue-generating) without leaving custody.

GrowDirect chooses Option C. The reasoning:

| Dimension | Sell (A) | Hold (B) | Inscribe (C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bitcoin leaves custody | Yes | No | No |
| Generates revenue | One-time (fiat) | None (appreciation only) | Perpetual (validation sats) |
| Creates competitive moat | No | No | Yes (timestamp, provenance) |
| Balance sheet treatment | Income recognized, BTC gone | Asset held, no revenue | Asset held AND revenue-generating |
| Founder narrative | "Sold mining reward" | "Hodling" | "Deployed proof-of-work into permanent infrastructure" |

Option C is strictly superior on every dimension that matters to an investor: the asset remains on the balance sheet, it generates perpetual revenue, and it creates a moat that compounds over time.

### 2.2 The Accounting Treatment

Every satoshi spent on inscription fees is a capital expenditure that produces a permanent asset. The inscription exists on the Bitcoin time chain for as long as Bitcoin exists — which, given that the network has maintained 99.98% uptime since January 3, 2009, is the most durable infrastructure in the history of computing.

**Cost basis:** The inscription cost at current fee rates (see Fee Window Model). At 1–3 sat/vbyte, the total cost to establish the namespace across 20 protocol-defining inscriptions is approximately 4,000–12,000 sats. The Genesis Pool itself (the 10M satoshis) is already on the balance sheet as a Bitcoin holding.

**Asset classification:** The inscribed Genesis Pool is a digital infrastructure asset — analogous to IP address space, spectrum licenses, or domain name portfolios. It does not depreciate. It does not require maintenance. It generates revenue through the validation gate (Layer 3) with zero marginal cost per validation.

**Revenue model:** Each validation request against the canonical record generates a micropayment in satoshis via the Lightning Network (L402 protocol). The revenue is perpetual — every event ever notarized through El Jeffe can generate validation revenue at any point in the future, for any reason, by any party.

**The critical insight for investors:** There is no operating expense associated with maintaining the inscribed range. It exists on Bitcoin. Bitcoin does not charge rent. The inscription is permanent. The only ongoing cost is the inscription of new Merkle roots as event volume grows — and that cost is borne by the fee market, which GrowDirect manages through intelligent queue timing (see Fee Window Model, Section 6).

Traditional SaaS: customer pays monthly. Revenue stops when the subscription ends. Customer acquisition cost must be recovered within the contract term.

There is no subscription to cancel. There is no contract term. The event is on the chain. The proof is permanent. The validation gate is permanent.

## 3. The Founder Origin Story — Why It Matters to Investors

### 3.1 Provenance Is the Moat

The Genesis Pool's provenance is recorded on the Bitcoin time chain with cryptographic certainty:

1. **Mining reward.** 0.1 BTC earned from F2Pool. The coinbase transaction is on the chain. The block height is recorded. The proof-of-work is verifiable.

2. **Founder wallet.** The reward was sent to Geoff's personal wallet. The UTXO is traceable.

3. **Inscription transactions.** The protocol inscriptions are funded from this UTXO lineage. Every inscription carries the provenance chain: mining effort → block reward → founder wallet → inscription.

This provenance cannot be manufactured retroactively. A competitor can purchase Bitcoin on an exchange and inscribe their own protocol. But the provenance story is fundamentally different: market purchase → exchange wallet → inscription. The UTXO lineage traces to Coinbase or Binance, not to a mining pool. The origin story is commercial, not foundational.

### 3.2 Why Founders Who Mine Are Different

In the Bitcoin ecosystem, proof-of-work is the genesis act. Satoshi mined the first blocks. The coinbase reward is the purest form of Bitcoin acquisition — earned by contributing hash power to the network, not by purchasing from a counterparty.

Geoff's decision to mine, hold the reward, and deploy it into permanent infrastructure carries a narrative weight that cannot be purchased:

- **Alignment:** The founder earned the Bitcoin through work, not speculation. He is deploying it into the foundation of the company, not holding it for appreciation.
- **Commitment:** The mining reward could have been sold for immediate liquidity. Instead, it is being converted into the most permanent form of capital deployment possible — an inscription on the Bitcoin time chain.
- **Credibility:** To a Bitcoin-native investor, the distinction between "bought BTC to fund inscriptions" and "mined BTC and inscribed it" is the difference between a financial transaction and a founding act.
