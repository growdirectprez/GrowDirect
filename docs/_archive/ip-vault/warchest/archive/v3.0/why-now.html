Six Forces Converging

This product could not have been built five years ago. It will cost ten times more to build five years from now. The convergence is narrow and it is happening right now.

1. Square's API maturity. Production-grade webhooks with HMAC signature verification. Robust OAuth 2.0 authorization. Real-time event streaming from the point of sale. The plumbing that makes merchant data accessible — securely, reliably, at scale — is ready. Five years ago, the APIs were incomplete. The webhook infrastructure did not exist. Today, Square's platform is the distribution channel and the data source in one.

2. Shrink is at a ten-year high. Average shrinkage hit 1.6% of revenue in 2024, up from 1.4% in 2020. For a merchant doing $500,000 in annual revenue, that is $8,000 per year walking out the door. Small merchants feel this more acutely than large retailers — thinner margins, zero tools, no loss prevention department. The pain is real and growing.

3. AI changes the delivery model. Enterprise loss prevention requires analysts to interpret data — specialists who translate statistical outputs into business actions. That model works at scale but is inaccessible to small merchants. Canary's Chirps use AI to collapse the interpretation layer entirely. The system detects anomalies and delivers plain-language alerts to the merchant's phone. No analyst required. No training. No BI literacy. The product IS the analyst.

4. No competition in the segment. Zero dedicated loss prevention products exist for Square merchants with 1 to 50 locations. The $4.7 billion LP industry serves enterprise retailers with 500+ locations. The market below that threshold is not contested — it is empty. There is no incumbent to displace. There is only white space to fill.

5. The trust collapse is happening now. AI-generated everything — deepfakes, synthetic identities, synthetic transactions. When trust in digital records collapses, the only verification that cannot be argued with is the one that happened on the Bitcoin time chain. You cannot fake a block timestamp. You cannot unwrite a Bitcoin inscription. The instrument that solves the trust problem exists now. First canonical authority on the time chain owns the answer.

6. The fee window is open. Large-batch inscription at current fee rates is economically feasible — under twenty cents per inscription. That window closes as Bitcoin adoption accelerates and block space demand rises. The genesis pool must be minted before the window narrows. The cost to establish this position today is trivial. The cost to replicate it later is prohibitive.
