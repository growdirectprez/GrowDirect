# The Fee Window
*Spine: ACT 6C | Manifesto: VI.3*

## Mint the Range Before It Closes

The window to establish a canonical inscription range at marginal cost is open today and closing measurably. This is the internal clock GrowDirect races against — and the urgency argument for investors.

Inscription costs are a function of block space demand. At current rates, a minimal inscription costs approximately 200 satoshis — under twenty cents. A thousand Merkle root inscriptions cost roughly 200,000 satoshis — about $170. The window is wide open.

It will not stay open.

## The Escalation Drivers

Three structural forces push inscription costs upward:

**Halving cycle compression.** Bitcoin block rewards halve approximately every four years. The 2024 halving reduced the reward to 3.125 BTC. The 2028 halving will reduce it to 1.5625 BTC. As block rewards decline, miners rely increasingly on transaction fees. The fee floor rises structurally with each halving — not as speculation, but as economic necessity.

**Adoption-driven demand.** Bitcoin is moving toward its role as a settlement layer. Layer 2 networks, institutional custody, Lightning settlement — all of these increase demand for base-layer block space. The block size is fixed. Demand is not. Fees rise as the gap closes.

**Inscription competition.** If the notarization use case gains traction — if other protocols recognize the value of Bitcoin-anchored event verification — fee pressure from inscription-class transactions rises. Success closes the window for followers.

## The Break-Even Threshold

At 1 sat/vbyte, a bootstrapped competitor can inscribe essentially without limit. At 10, they are viable but constrained. At 50, they are significantly constrained — about 1,170 inscriptions per year on a $10,000 annual budget. At 100, they cannot compete on throughput. At 200, establishing a competing notarization protocol from zero is economically non-viable.

The break-even threshold sits at 50 to 100 sat/vbyte sustained average. Above that level, a new entrant without existing treasury and pool relationships cannot economically replicate what GrowDirect has already built.

## The Point of No Return

The point of no return is not a single fee level. It is the intersection of three curves: fee escalation rising structurally, GrowDirect's accumulated notarization history growing daily, and the network effect from each merchant adding validation demand. By 12 to 24 months from genesis, the combination of these three forces makes establishing a competing Bitcoin-native notarization protocol from zero economically irrational.

The blocks are already being written. The window is open now. The argument to investors is simple: the cost to do this later is structurally higher than the cost to do it today, and the cost to replicate it once done is prohibitive.
