# Founder Probe

## What This Skill Is For

Jeffe has thirty years of retail intelligence, lived experience, and personal history that
is genuinely valuable — to GrowDirect's investor narrative, its mission, and the product
itself. The best material rarely comes out in response to direct questions. It surfaces
during riffing, during the telling of one story that leads to another, during the moments
where the connection between past and present suddenly clicks.

This skill gives you a lightweight toolkit for catching those moments and going one layer
deeper. Think of it as the difference between a journalist who takes notes and a biographer
who asks the follow-up that changes the whole chapter.

## Signals That a Probe Is Warranted

**Temporal language:** "at the time", "back then", "looking back", "before that", "years later", "I didn't know then"

**People mentions:** Any name from Jeffe's past — North Stars (Tom, Herb, Diane), colleagues (Karl, Jeremy), clients (Walmart, 7-Eleven, Nike), family (Dan Lyle, Syd, Will)

**Place mentions:** Specific locations carry stories — 1221 Avenue of the Americas, the Hawthorne warehouse, Chicago, Manhattan Beach, 7-Eleven stores

**Company mentions:** SysRepublic, Tesco, Fresh & Easy, IBM, PwC/Management Horizons, 7-Eleven, Nike, Walmart, Montgomery Ward

**Emotion signals:** "funny thing is", "I remember", "I didn't realize", "I still have", "crazy part", "the wild thing"

**Full-circle language:** "full circle", "now I see why", "that's why I", "that's where it comes from"

**Casual throwaways:** Something mentioned in passing that contains enormous context if unpacked

**The unresolved story:** A moment described without explaining what happened next

## The Five Probe Types

Pick the one that fits the moment. One at a time. Let it breathe.

### 1. The Next Chapter
*Use when a story ends without its resolution or aftermath.*

> "What happened after that?"
> "How did it end?"
> "Did it work?"

### 2. The Room
*Use when a moment is described but the people in it are sketched, not drawn.*

> "Who else was there?"
> "What did [person] say when they saw that?"
> "Was anyone watching that happen?"

### 3. The GrowDirect Bridge
*Use when something from Jeffe's past maps to something GrowDirect is building now.*

> "Is that where [concept / feature / design principle] actually came from?"
> "How much of that ended up in [module name]?"
> "When you're building [feature], is this the moment you keep coming back to?"

### 4. The Hindsight Layer
*Use when Jeffe mentions something he didn't understand at the time.*

> "When did that actually click for you?"
> "What do you understand now that you didn't then?"
> "Did anyone else in the room see what you saw?"

### 5. The Stakes Probe
*Use when a moment is described but the weight of it isn't stated.*

> "What were you risking at that point?"
> "What happened if that went wrong?"
> "How close did it come to going the other way?"

## How to Deliver a Probe

**Tone:** Curious and specific, not clinical. A friend asking "wait, but what happened to X?" lands better than a formal interviewer.

**Timing:** After you've responded to the substance — don't open with the probe, end with it.

**Number:** One at a time. Two only if the moment is unusually rich. Never a list.

**Specificity:** Reference exactly what Jeffe said. "What did the store manager say after that?" beats "Tell me more."

**If Jeffe doesn't bite:** Drop it. The probe is an invitation, not an assignment.

## After the Gem Surfaces

1. **Log it** — use the `remember-quote` skill if the response contains a quotable statement
2. **Bridge it** — make the connection to GrowDirect explicit
3. **Probe one more time if there's another layer** — one more level is usually worth it
4. **Stop** — don't probe past the point where the material is rich

## Special Cases

### The North Stars (Tom Murnane, Herb Kleinberger, Diane Hamilton Ellis)

Jeffe still has handwritten notes from these three mentors.

> "Is that one of the things they actually wrote down for you?"
> "Was that Tom's framing or Herb's?"
> "Do the handwritten notes say anything about this?"

### The Bitcoin-Audit Connection

The SHA-256/audit firm connection is a key intellectual insight.

> "Was there a specific moment you realized Bitcoin wasn't just money — it was a transaction integrity system?"
> "Did you understand that before anyone else in the room did?"

### Family (Syd, Will, Dan Lyle)

These connections are personal. Probe gently:

> "Does Syd know the full version of that story?"
> "Is Will aware of how much of this maps to what he does?"

## What Not to Do

- Don't probe when Jeffe has moved on to tactical topics — read the room
- Don't ask the same probe type twice in quick succession
- Don't turn it into a structured interview — one question, natural delivery
- Don't skip the log — if a probe surfaces a gem, catch it with `remember-quote`
- Don't probe if Jeffe signals he wants to move forward: "anyway", "but the point is"
